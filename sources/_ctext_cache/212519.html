《鳥獸類下 Birds and Beasts, Part Two》

《鳥獸類下·龜 Tortoises》

1
○龜藏六而運任脈，其息以耳。雌雄尾接，或與蛇交。春夏出蟄脫甲，秋冬藏穴導引，故藥取敗版以養陰云。其上甲中直三，圍十二，外圍二十四，中之文曰千里，其首橫文第一級接於千里者，龜王也。元龜貴大，而張世南雲神龜老如錢，遊於蓮葉，以油管噀而取之，夫神龜猶畏香油邪？香油抹眼，入水不沈。其溺善入，取之者以沙缽盛之，復以一缽蓋其上而縛定之，微開一孔進煙，外用水缸覆之，內以樟木燒煙，其溺即出。蘄州龜小而長，綠毛，或剔甲搗韭以封之，一再對剔，綠毛自生。中通曰：以尋常龜用生姜汁塗背上，待乾，又用污泥塗之，如此數次，即生毛。暄曰：或云荊有綠龜，細紋如毛，腹骨中斷，前後捏之則合。別一種臭者，可烹食。
○ The tortoise withdraws its six [extremities] and circulates the conception vessel, breathing through its ears. Male and female join at the tail, or it mates with snakes. In spring and summer it comes out of hibernation and sheds its shell; in autumn and winter it hides in holes and practises daoyin; so in medicine the cast plastron is used to nourish yin. On its upper shell there are three in the middle line, twelve around them and twenty-four on the outer ring; the pattern in the middle is called a thousand li, and the one whose first transverse line at the head connects with the thousand li is the tortoise king. Great tortoises are valued for size, yet Zhang Shinan says the divine tortoise when old is the size of a coin and swims on lotus leaves, and is caught by spraying it from an oil tube — does even the divine tortoise fear sesame oil? A tortoise with sesame oil smeared on its eyes does not sink in water. Its urine penetrates well; those who collect it put it in an earthenware bowl, cover it with another bowl and bind them, make a small hole to let in smoke, cover the outside with a water vat, and burn camphor wood inside for smoke, and the urine comes out at once. The tortoises of Qizhou are small and long with green hair; or scrape the shell, seal it with pounded chives, scrape again once or twice opposite, and green hair grows by itself. Zhongtong says: Smear the back of an ordinary tortoise with raw ginger juice, let it dry, then smear it with dirty mud, and after doing this several times it grows hair. Xuan says: Some say that Jing has green tortoises with fine lines like hair, whose belly bones are broken across the middle and close when pressed front and back. Another stinking kind can be cooked and eaten.

《鳥獸類下·蟹 Crabs》

1
○山谷曰：寒蒲束縛十六輩。謂對蟹也。旁行郭索，故曰螃蟹。入海輪芒，未嘗驗之；執稻隊行，則果然矣。取者以燈照捕之。兩頭銳曰白蟹。本草曰：蟳即蝤蛑，八月能與虎鬥。小曰蟛螖，即爾雅之螖蠌，非蔡謨所食之蟛蜞也。至小曰沙狗，不可食，然今皆酢而食之。食鱔中毒，食蟹即解。化漆為水，亦可醒膠。郭憲言善苑國有百足蟹，殼可熬膠。外紀言大蟹長丈餘，螯斷人頭，其殼如屋。張宅人曰：漳泉海蟹橫尖曰蝶，太而圓曰蟳，圓如盆而正赤者夜有光，性暖，小蟹如錢，八九十月來，山東海皆有之。
○ Shangu says: "Sixteen fellows bound with cold rushes." This refers to paired crabs. They walk sideways, guosuo, hence the name pangxie. That they go into the sea and pay tribute of awns I have never verified, but that they march in file holding rice stalks is indeed so. They are caught by lamplight. Those pointed at both ends are called white crabs. The Materia Medica says: The xun is the swimming crab, which in the eighth month can fight tigers. The small one is called pengyue, the yueze of the Erya, not the pengqi eaten by Cai Mo. The smallest is called sand dog and is inedible, though nowadays all are pickled and eaten. Poisoning from eels is cured by eating crabs. It turns lacquer to water, and can also revive glue. Guo Xian says the country of Shanyuan has hundred-legged crabs whose shells can be boiled into glue. The Record of the Outer Regions says that great crabs over a zhang long can cut off a man's head with their pincers, and their shells are like houses. Zhang Zhairen says: Of the sea crabs of Zhang and Quan, those pointed sideways are called die, large and round xun; those round as basins and pure red shine at night and are warm by nature; small crabs like coins come in the eighth, ninth and tenth months; they are found in all the seas of Shandong.

《鳥獸類下·鱷 Crocodiles》

1
○南海記曰：鱷生子百數，為鱷者十二，餘或為黿為鱉。虛舟子曰：鱷頭似虎，能上岸行，殺鱷而懸其首，其齒自生。鱷畏黃蠟，取珠者以蠟裹體。述異記曰：魚虎化鮫。薦叔曰：鯊魚化鱷，蛟不成龍，亦化為鱷。東坡言潛蛟制渴虎。外紀瓦刺而多，亦鱷類也。瓦刺而多有足，登陸食人畜，吐涎以滑物而啖之，但行甚遲，口用上頦動，冬月不食物，見人遠則哭，近則噬，通身鱗甲，惟腹有耎處，仁魚以此制之，此鱷亦畏乙苟滿鼠。
○ The Records of the South Sea say: Crocodiles lay a hundred or so eggs, of which twelve become crocodiles and the rest become soft-shelled turtles or turtles. Xuzhouzi says: The crocodile's head is like a tiger's, and it can go ashore; if a crocodile is killed and its head hung up, its teeth grow by themselves. Crocodiles fear yellow wax, so pearl divers coat their bodies with wax. The Record of Strange Things says: The fish-tiger turns into a shark. Jianshu says: Sharks turn into crocodiles, and jiao that do not become dragons also turn into crocodiles. Dongpo speaks of the hidden jiao controlling the thirsty tiger. The lagarto of the Record of the Outer Regions is also of the crocodile kind. The lagarto has feet, goes ashore and eats people and livestock, spits slaver to make things slippery and swallows them; but it moves very slowly, moves its upper jaw, does not eat in winter, weeps when it sees people far off and bites when they are near; its whole body is scaled except for a soft place on the belly, by which the benevolent fish subdues it; this crocodile also fears the ichneumon.

《鳥獸類下·白小 Whitebait》

1
○有數種：南通州以白小疊成如片，長寸者，皆為銀魚。北方寶坻大數寸。近海者為龍頭魚，一名水晶魚，入湯惟剩一脊。觜長者名針魚。龍泉蘇魚長二三寸，乃鰷魚也。廣之恩州出鵝毛脡，蓋鱊鯞魚也。桐城麥魚亦鵝毛脡也。
○ There are several kinds: in Nantongzhou whitebait pressed together into sheets, an inch long, are all silver fish. In the north, those of Baodi are several inches long. Those near the sea are dragon-head fish, also called crystal fish, which leave only a spine when put in hot water. The long-beaked ones are called needle fish. The su fish of Longquan, two or three inches long, are tiao fish. Enzhou in Guang produces goose-feather ting, which are the yuzhou fish. The wheat fish of Tongcheng are also goose-feather ting.

《鳥獸類下·章花魚 Octopus》

1
○章花魚有大者，其諸足長尺餘，能上岸縳豕而下水。
○ Some octopuses are large, their legs over a chi long, and can go ashore, bind pigs and drag them into the water.

《鳥獸類下·斗魚 Fighting Fish》

1
○大如指，長二三寸，花身紅尾，善鬥，人家盎畜之，呼丁斑魚。張世南云：三山溪產小魚，里中豢之，角勝為戲。
○ As big as a finger, two or three inches long, with patterned body and red tail, they are good fighters; households keep them in bowls and call them dingban fish. Zhang Shinan says: The streams of Sanshan produce small fish which the villagers keep to fight for amusement.

《鳥獸類下·四足魚 Four-Legged Fish》

1
○魶魚有足，緣木，音如兒啼。周益公記宜興洞有四足占。張舜民記黃州四足占。全義之西南盤龍山乳洞有金沙龍盤魚，皆四足脩尾丹腹，狀若守宮。泰和鄉有四足如螭，有時上岸，見人則入水，不傷人。鱉無裙而尾長，亦謂之魶。游子六曰：閩高山源有黑魚，如指大，其鱗即皮，四足，可調粥治小兒疳。
○ The na fish has legs, climbs trees, and cries like a child. Lord Zhou Yi records four-legged catfish in a cave at Yixing. Zhang Shunmin records four-legged catfish at Huangzhou. In the stalactite cave of Mount Panlong southwest of Quanyi there are golden-sand dragon-coil fish, all four-legged with long tails and red bellies, shaped like geckos. At Taihe village there are four-legged creatures like chi dragons, which sometimes come ashore and on seeing people go into the water, harming no one. A soft-shelled turtle without a skirt and with a long tail is also called na. You Ziliu says: At the sources of the high mountains of Min there is a black fish the size of a finger, whose scales are its skin, with four legs, which can be mixed into gruel to treat infantile malnutrition.

《鳥獸類下·魚獸 Fish-Beasts》

1
○陸璣蟲魚疏曰：象彌魚服，此海中魚獸，似豬，皮斑，腹青，可為弓鞬步義。海將潮，天將雨，其毛皆起；潮還天晴，毛腹如故。周益公言周麟之使北塞，糟牛魚歸，牛魚皮應風潮。中履曰：陸璣字元恪，非士衡，監本爾雅及時珍皆誤作機。
○ Lu Ji's Commentary on the Insects and Fish says: The "ivory bow-ends and fish-skin quivers" are of this fish-beast of the sea, like a pig, with spotted skin and green belly, which can be made into bow cases and quivers. When the sea is about to rise and it is about to rain, its hair all stands up; when the tide recedes and the sky clears, its hair and belly are as before. Lord Zhou Yi says that Zhou Linzhi, on an embassy to the northern frontier, brought back pickled ox-fish, whose skin responds to wind and tide. Zhonglü says: Lu Ji's style was Yuanke, not Shiheng; the Directorate edition of the Erya and Shizhen both mistakenly write ji with another character.

《鳥獸類下·白鯗 White Dried Fish》

1
○即石首也，出寧波健跳者上，爵溪者次。白下鮮者，冰留以來，謂之黃魚。
○ It is the croaker; that from Jiantiao in Ningbo is best, and that from Juexi next. The fresh ones of Baixia, kept on ice when brought, are called yellow fish.

《鳥獸類下·鯪 Ling Fish》

1
○閩人取石鯪，鋪歷頭石鎮之，鯪即伏而易取。
○ The people of Min catch the stone ling by spreading lime and pressing it with stones, and the ling lie still and are easily caught.

《鳥獸類下·畜魚 Raising Fish》

1
○沙池宜鯔魚，土池宜鯖、鰱、鱸、鯉之類。有飛魚者，善出水，宜畜之。辟獺而魚不淰。下霧則鯉魭者飛。有鱉則魚不去，故鱉曰神守。池欲淺而向陽，環洲使游，中一區深，積摻過冬。最忌桐油、茶餅、橄欖、芫花、雞糞。若池穢致浮喁者，碎芭蕉葉於池，自止。藥毒者宜新水，新水發又有魚貫之患，宜開竇而編竹洩之。白青魚食草，而食青魚之穢者，細頭鰱、金絲鱮、大頭庸也，必宜並畜，三月至八月必採稚草飼之。其黑青魚則食螺螄，亦併畜之。凡初秧宜小池，飼以細米粉，或風颺水面，令其食盡，日可二次，俟五六寸可入大池，一年一尺，次年二尺。陶朱曰：二月上旬庚養鯉，易長。又取道中棄胞，盆覆之，埋池四角，魚永不失。鰲魚、烏魚、大占皆食魚，不可畜。烏魚埋土數月，得水復活。墨子曰：端午取鯉魚子塗席上，陰乾，六旬取置水中，糞箕覆之。南海諸郡八九月采魚子著草，懸於灶煙上，至二月雷發時，以草浸池塘，旬日出如科斗，謂之米蟻。後山曰：魚隨陽，春夏浮而溯流，秋冬沒而順流。智按：冬寒則蟠泥成窩，眾魚聚伏不動，當冬不食。
○ Sandy ponds suit mullet; earthen ponds suit black carp, silver carp, perch, common carp and the like. There are flying fish that leap well out of the water, which should be raised. They ward off otters, and the fish do not scatter. When fog comes down, carp and yuan fly. If there are soft-shelled turtles the fish do not leave, so the turtle is called the divine guardian. The pond should be shallow and face the sun, with islets around to swim among, and one deep section in the middle with heaped cover for overwintering. It most dreads tung oil, tea-seed cake, olives, genkwa and chicken dung. If the pond is foul and the fish come up gasping, break banana leaves into the pond, and it stops. For poisoning, fresh water should be used; if fresh water causes the fish to swim in file, open a sluice and let it out through woven bamboo. White and grass carp eat grass, and those that eat the grass carp's waste are the fine-headed silver carp, golden-thread xu and big-head yong, which should be raised together; from the third to the eighth month young grass must be gathered to feed them. The black carp eats snails, and is also raised together with them. Fry should be kept first in small ponds and fed fine rice flour, or scattered on the water surface in the wind so they eat it all, twice a day; when five or six inches they can go into the large pond, growing a chi in a year and two chi the next. Taozhu says: Carp put in on the first geng day of the second month grow easily. Also, take afterbirths discarded on the road, cover them with basins and bury them at the four corners of the pond, and the fish are never lost. Ao fish, snakeheads and large catfish all eat fish and should not be raised. Snakeheads buried in earth for several months revive on getting water. Mozi says: At Duanwu take carp roe, smear it on mats, dry it in the shade, and after sixty days put it in water covered with a dung basket. In the commanderies of the South Sea, in the eighth and ninth months they gather fish roe attached to grass and hang it over the stove smoke; in the second month when the thunder breaks they soak the grass in ponds, and after ten days they come out like tadpoles, called rice ants. Houshan says: Fish follow the yang, floating and going upstream in spring and summer, sinking and going downstream in autumn and winter. I, Zhi, note: When winter is cold they coil in the mud making hollows, the fish gather and lie still, and do not eat through the winter.

《鳥獸類下·養金魚法 Methods of Raising Goldfish》

1
○物理所曰：金魚三尾五尾者，跌子時大蝦蓋之，無蝦則尾如常魚。岳珂桯史曰：金鯽為上，鯉次之，有三尾、九尾、白中朱者，取闤渠小紅蟲詞，凡百日皆變，初白如銀，次漸黃，久則金矣。單尾大者，鯉也。海澱養數千頭，皆長三尺。養金魚缸，擦生芋，注水，苔生而水易浮。魚瘦生白點者名虱，投楓樹皮則愈。中通曰：或用新磚入糞桶中浸一日，取出令幹，投水中。中履曰：魚本有五色，吉州有一身具五色者，名手巾魚。暄曰：建陽銅山古井有五色魚，長四寸，如龍尾豎四爪，旱以祈雨，置河中，自歸井。
○ The Studio of the Principles of Things says: Goldfish with three or five tails are produced by covering the spawn with large shrimps when they drop their roe; without shrimps their tails are like those of ordinary fish. Yue Ke's Notes from Ting says: Gold crucian carp are the best and carp next; there are three-tailed, nine-tailed and white with red, fed with little red worms from the market drains, and in a hundred days all change, at first silver-white, then gradually yellow, and in time gold. The large single-tailed ones are carp. At Haidian several thousand are kept, all three chi long. For goldfish vats, rub them with raw taro and fill with water, and moss grows and the water easily floats [things]. Lean fish with white spots are said to have lice; put in sweetgum bark and they recover. Zhongtong says: Or soak a new brick in a dung bucket for a day, take it out, dry it and put it in the water. Zhonglü says: Fish originally have five colours; Jizhou has one with all five colours on its body, called the handkerchief fish. Xuan says: In an old well at Tongshan in Jianyang there are five-coloured fish four inches long, with tails upright like dragons and four claws; in drought they are taken to pray for rain and put in the river, and return to the well by themselves.

《鳥獸類下·河肫 Puffer Fish》

1
○河肫愛五色縷，人困以取之。諺云：鮧鯸三月大，四月小。善怒。其肝血毒，然以橄欖、蘆荀煮者亦可食。東齋錄曰：梅聖俞於范希文席上賦河肫，有楊花荻芽之句，蓋河肫食柳絮而肥，南人與荻芽為鱠而食之。
○ Puffers love five-coloured threads, and people trap them with these. The proverb says: Puffers are large in the third month and small in the fourth. They are easily angered. Their liver and blood are poisonous, but cooked with olives and reed shoots they can be eaten. The Records of the Eastern Studio say: Mei Shengyu at a banquet of Fan Xiwen wrote a rhapsody on the puffer with a line on willow catkins and reed shoots, because puffers fatten on willow catkins, and southerners eat them minced with reed shoots.

《鳥獸類下·龍蝦 Lobsters》

1
○龍蝦尾吐紅子，色奪榴花，有長二尺者，人家淘空其殼如船，燈挂佛前。海南有蝦子蚙。
○ The lobster's tail emits red roe whose colour outdoes pomegranate flowers; some are two chi long, and households hollow out their shells like boats and hang them as lanterns before the Buddha. Hainan has shrimp-roe ling.

《鳥獸類下·海粉母 The Sea-Powder Mother》

1
○吐海粉者曰海粉母，色綠，如荷包，飲從此入，溲從此出，今之海粉即所溲也。子六曰：如墨魚形，大三四寸，冬養之家，春種之海邊田內。或曰：插竹枝田中，其母上竹枝吐出。
○ What spits out sea powder is called the sea-powder mother, green in colour, like a purse; it drinks through this opening and excretes through it, and the present sea powder is its excrement. Ziliu says: It is shaped like a cuttlefish, three or four inches long; in winter it is kept at home and in spring planted in fields by the sea. Some say: Stick bamboo twigs into the fields, and the mother climbs the twigs and spits it out.

《鳥獸類下·寄生 Commensals》

1
○劍蚌腹有小蟹，任昉述異記謂之箸。又有寄生蛤螺。又有蝞，生南海，如蝦，寄生龜殼，食之益人顏色，即瑣蛣、海鏡之類也，人以火迫之，出則蟹，大如豆，黃而頭足俱備。大沙魚本胎生也，其小魚常出入其腹中。又有魚寄生枯螺中，載之以行，形似蝦，有四足兩螯。
○ In the belly of the sword clam there is a small crab, which Ren Fang's Record of Strange Things calls zhu. There are also commensal clams and snails. There is also the mi, which lives in the South Sea, like a shrimp, lodging in tortoise shells; eating it improves the complexion; it is of the suoji and sea-mirror kind; when people drive it out with fire, what comes out is a crab the size of a bean, yellow, with head and legs complete. The great shark is viviparous, and its young often go in and out of its belly. There are also fish that lodge in dead snail shells and carry them about, shaped like shrimps, with four legs and two pincers.

《鳥獸類下·淡水龍 The Freshwater Dragon》

1
○其形似蛇，洋船用之，置鹽水中，則水自淡。至若鹽龍，則蕭注破南蠻所得，養之盤中，其鱗生鹽，食之益陽者也。
○ It is shaped like a snake; ocean ships use it, and put in salt water, the water becomes fresh of itself. As for the salt dragon, it was obtained by Xiao Zhu when he defeated the southern barbarians and kept in a dish; its scales produce salt, and eating it strengthens the yang.

《鳥獸類下·禦龍 Warding Off Dragons》

1
○桃花浪，黑雲起，知為龍，則焚鱟殼等諸臭物卻之，或洒青礬，尖浪起，洒犬血。劉薦叔曰：海舵師知龍起，但雷金鼓，知颶在二月後四月前，故四月後開洋。洪芻怪洋記亦言鉦鼓、弓矢、叫噪卻龍事。魚龍畏鐵，鐵辛害目，故橋堤鑄鐵牛、鐵蜈。武功章秋治水，以鐵柱貫釜，塞水源，遂成平陸。南昌沙地，故以鐵樹、鐵鎖填之。
○ When peach-blossom waves and black clouds rise, one knows it is a dragon, and it is driven off by burning horseshoe-crab shells and other foul-smelling things, or by sprinkling green vitriol; when pointed waves rise, sprinkle dog's blood. Liu Jianshu says: When the sea helmsmen know a dragon is rising, they merely thunder gongs and drums; they know typhoons come after the second month and before the fourth, so they set sail after the fourth month. Hong Chu's Record of Strange Seas also speaks of driving off dragons with gongs, drums, bows and arrows and shouting. Fish and dragons fear iron, which is pungent and harms their eyes, so iron oxen and iron centipedes are cast for bridges and dikes. Zhang Qiu of Wugong, in controlling floods, threaded cauldrons on iron pillars and blocked the source of the water, which then became level land. Nanchang is sandy ground, so it was filled with iron trees and iron chains.

《鳥獸類下·龍骨 Dragon Bones》

1
○華陽國志：中江縣東二里三嵎山，六屈三起，出龍骨，民掘取之。馬總唐年補錄曰：咸通末，孔威刺舒州，進龍骨一具，云桐城縣善政鄉有胡舉者言：青龍鬥死於庭中，時四月，尚有繭箔在庭，忽雲雷暴起，雲中聞擊聲，血如雨，洒繭箔上，血不汗箔，漸旋結聚，可拾置掌上，須臾令人冷痛入骨。龍既死，剖之，喉有大瘡，凡長十餘丈，身尾相半，尾本褊薄，鱗鬣皆魚，惟有鬚長二丈，其足有赤膜翳之。宇文懋昭金志言冷水死二龍，熙州野濼見龍三日，爪托一嬰兒，阿保機見黑龍射之。景煥曰：孟蜀時什邡井中火龍出。是龍亦常見之物，無怪古有豢龍氏。其吉弔脂、紫稍花，則龍之精也，言從海漂木上取之，龍拔大木沈蟠故也。南野閒居錄言丁謂有龍精石。贊寧曰：龍漦得煙煤不散。劉昌曰：成化中得龍卵，其堅如石。按外紀孛露鳥名厄馬，卵可作杯，今番舶所市龍卵，即此物也。
○ The Records of Huayang: Mount Sanyu, two li east of Zhongjiang county, with six bends and three rises, produces dragon bones, which the people dig up. Ma Zong's Supplementary Records of the Tang Years says: At the end of Xiantong, Kong Wei as prefect of Shuzhou presented a set of dragon bones, saying that a certain Hu Ju of Shanzheng township in Tongcheng county said a green dragon died fighting in his courtyard; it was the fourth month and silkworm trays were still in the courtyard; suddenly clouds and thunder burst forth, sounds of striking were heard in the clouds, and blood fell like rain on the silkworm trays, but did not stain them, gradually gathering into lumps that could be picked up and put on the palm, and after a moment made one cold and painful to the bone. When the dragon was dead and cut open, there was a large wound in its throat; it was over ten zhang long in all, half body and half tail, the base of the tail flat and thin, the scales and fins all fish-like, except for whiskers two zhang long, and its feet were covered with red membranes. Yuwen Maozhao's Record of the Jin speaks of two dragons dying at Lengshui, a dragon seen for three days on a marsh at Xizhou holding a baby in its claws, and Abaoji seeing a black dragon and shooting it. Jing Huan says: In the time of the Meng [house of] Shu a fire dragon came out of a well at Shifang. So the dragon too is a commonly seen creature, and no wonder antiquity had the Dragon-Rearer clan. The jidiao fat and zishaohua are the essence of dragons, said to be taken from driftwood in the sea, because dragons pull up great trees and sink coiling around them. The Records of Idle Living in the Southern Wilds say Ding Wei had a dragon-essence stone. Zanning says: Dragon slobber does not disperse when mixed with soot. Liu Chang says: In the Chenghua period a dragon's egg was found, as hard as stone. I note that in the Record of the Outer Regions, Peru has a bird called the ema whose eggs can be made into cups; the dragon's eggs that foreign ships now sell are this.

《鳥獸類下·蛟筍 Jiao Shoots》

1
○雉與斑蛇交，入土為蛟蜃，閩人謂之蛟筍，望地氣而知之，掘而食之，猶雷州人掘雷子而食之也。雷子即蛟筍類，頭似烏咮，得火土之氣而生。
○ Pheasants mate with spotted snakes and enter the earth to become jiao and shen; the people of Min call them jiao shoots, know them by watching the earth's vapours, and dig and eat them, just as the people of Leizhou dig and eat thunder-seeds. Thunder-seeds are of the jiao-shoot kind, with heads like crows' beaks, produced from the qi of fire and earth.

《鳥獸類下·海族把勒亞 The Balaena of the Sea Tribe》

1
○此魚長數十丈，首有二大孔，噴水上出，遇舶則昂首注水舶中，亟以盛酒鉅木罌投之，連吞數罌，則俯首而逝。淺處得之，熬油可數千斤。又有劍魚，觜長丈許，有齬刻如鋸，能與把勒亞魚戰而勝。又有二手二足之海魔，舶最畏之。其大如島者，所謂鰲身映天黑也。
○ This fish is several dozen zhang long, with two large holes in its head through which it spouts water upward; when it meets a ship it raises its head and pours water into the ship, and one must quickly throw in huge wooden casks full of wine; after swallowing several casks it lowers its head and goes away. When caught in the shallows, it can be boiled for several thousand jin of oil. There is also the swordfish, whose beak is about a zhang long, with notches like a saw, which can fight the balaena and win. There is also the sea demon with two hands and two feet, which ships fear most. Those as large as islands are what is meant by "the ao's body darkening the sky."

《鳥獸類下·斯得白 The Sidebai》

1
○長二十五丈，性最良善，能保護人，或漁人為惡魚所困，此魚輒為漁人解圍。又有仁魚，嘗負小兒登岸。
○ It is twenty-five zhang long, most good-natured, and can protect people; when fishermen are trapped by evil fish, this fish rescues them. There is also the benevolent fish, which has carried children ashore.

《鳥獸類下·薄里波 The Polypus》

1
○色能隨物而變，附土如土色，附石如石色。
○ Its colour changes with what it touches: on earth it is earth-coloured, on stone stone-coloured.

《鳥獸類下·落斯馬 The Rosmarus》

1
○長四丈許，足短，居海底，罕出水面，皮甚堅，用力刺之不可入，額有二角如鉤，寐時則角掛石，盡一日不醒。
○ About four zhang long, with short feet, it lives on the sea bottom and seldom comes to the surface; its hide is very hard and cannot be pierced however hard one stabs; on its forehead are two horns like hooks, and when it sleeps it hooks its horns on a rock and does not wake for a whole day.

《鳥獸類下·飛魚白角魚 Flying Fish and Albacore》

1
○飛魚尺許，能掠水面而飛。白角兒魚善窺飛魚之影，伺其所向，先至其所，開口待啖，恒相追數十里。舟人以雞羽或白練飄揚水面，上著利鉤，而白角兒吞之，便為人獲。餘賡之師曰：雞魚有肉翅，能飛。
○ Flying fish are about a chi long and can skim the water surface in flight. The albacore watches for the shadow of the flying fish, guesses where it is going, gets there first and waits open-mouthed to eat it, often chasing it for dozens of li. Boatmen flutter chicken feathers or white silk over the water with sharp hooks attached, and the albacore swallow them and are caught. My teacher Yu Gengzhi says: The chicken fish has fleshy wings and can fly.

《鳥獸類下·海馬 The Walrus》

1
○其牙堅白而瑩淨，文理細如絲髮，可為念珠等物。又有海女，亦魚也，骨心可為念珠。陶貞白曰：海馬，蝦類，催產壯陽。此又一種。
○ Its teeth are hard, white and lustrous, with grain as fine as silk threads, and can be made into rosary beads and the like. There is also the sea woman, also a fish, whose bone core can be made into rosary beads. Tao Zhenbai says: The sea horse is of the shrimp kind, and hastens childbirth and strengthens the yang. This is another kind.

《鳥獸類下·蠐螬 Grubs》

1
○蠐螬以背行，快於足，好向東。
○ Grubs move on their backs faster than on their feet, and like to face east.

《鳥獸類下·蜘蛛 Spiders》

1
○蜘蛛置甕，食以膏百日，燃以塗布，則雨不濡。花者最毒，八足止如四足，其絲最毒，能爛物。相傳南太學諸堂無蜘蛛。
○ Put spiders in a jar and feed them on fat for a hundred days; burnt and smeared on cloth, they keep it from getting wet in the rain. Spotted ones are most poisonous, with eight legs that look like four; their silk is most poisonous and can rot things. It is said that the halls of the Southern Imperial University have no spiders.

《鳥獸類下·蛤蚧 Gecko》

1
○蛤蚧為人得，即自嚙其尾。以月首上寅可檢，以是日交也。年老者鳴十餘聲。丹養守宮即此。中德曰：塗臍無子，則冷可知。
○ When a gecko is caught, it bites its own tail. It can be found on the first yin day of the month, because it mates on that day. Old ones call more than ten times. The shougong raised on cinnabar is this. Zhongde says: Smeared on the navel it causes childlessness, so its coldness can be known.

《鳥獸類下·蜳禺 Dunyu》

1
○搜神之蜳禺，即青蚨也，又名蠈蠋。異物志云：如蟬而長，子如蠶，得其子則母飛來就之。贊寧曰：狀如蟆，色青，蹼然，善登木，南人月夜捕之，以牝牡血塗錢，用牝留牡，則錢自歸，若牡就索，則不能歸矣。李恂則言雌雄不捨，能秘精。劉恂言龐降在欖木上，如蟬，可為媚藥。姚有僕在南海訉濠鏡，云不能如淮南註，但塗物相就耳。
○ The dunyu of the Soushen [ji] is the qingfu, also called zeizhu. The Record of Strange Things says: It is like a cicada but longer, its young like silkworms; if you get its young, the mother flies to join them. Zanning says: It is shaped like a toad, green, with webbed feet, good at climbing trees; southerners catch them on moonlit nights and smear coins with the blood of female and male; if one uses the female and keeps the male, the coins return by themselves, but if the male goes looking, they cannot return. Li Xun says that male and female do not part and can retain the essence. Liu Xun says the pangjiang lives on olive trees, like a cicada, and can be used as a love charm. Yao Youpu, enquiring at Haojing in the South Sea, was told that it cannot do what the commentary on the Huainanzi says, but things smeared with it merely come together.

《鳥獸類下·馬蝗 Leeches》

1
○馬蝗燒之成灰，碗覆於地，過露一夜，則灰中蠕蠕矣。傷寒用水蛭逐水。誤吞則渴，可以盂水引之，或服土而下之。有鐵線蝗，其觜方者尤狠。對口癰，取以向癰，彼即吸取癰血，飽則自墮，而癰亦愈。凡刺人血，不可拔，惟重力拍之，則彼自退墜耳。
○ Burn leeches to ash, cover it on the ground with a bowl and leave it under the dew for a night, and the ash wriggles. For cold damage, leeches are used to expel water. If one swallows one by mistake one becomes thirsty; it can be drawn out with a basin of water, or purged by taking earth. There are iron-wire leeches, and those with square mouths are especially vicious. For carbuncles opposite the mouth, apply one to the carbuncle and it sucks out the blood, falling off by itself when full, and the carbuncle heals. When one has stuck into someone's blood it must not be pulled off; only slap it hard and it lets go and falls.

《鳥獸類下·蛇 Snakes》

1
○北方介與蛇俱。蟠必向上。有草、木、水、土四種，有紅者、金翠者。劉郁云：訖立兒地蛇皆四跗，口吐紫艷。青蛙蛇最毒，怒時毒在頭尾。桑柴燒蛇，則見其足。四足蛇則木僕苟印，千歲蝮也。淮南曰：神蛇自斷其身而自相續。隋煬遣人嶺南求得此蛇，長三尺，色錦似金，熟視微黑，解食肉而不毒人，觸之怒則自斷，若刀截，怒定相就如故。蛇死目閉，蘄蛇目開。生舒州界，一開一閉，今在興國，尾如指甲。
○ In the north the shelled creature goes with the snake. When coiling they always face upward. There are four kinds, grass, tree, water and earth, and red ones and gold-green ones. Liu Yu says: In the land of Kirghiz snakes all have four feet and spit purple flames from their mouths. The green frog snake is most poisonous, and when angry its poison is in its head and tail. If a snake is burnt with mulberry wood, its feet appear. The four-footed snake is the mupugouyin, a thousand-year viper. The Huainanzi says: The divine snake cuts its own body and rejoins itself. Emperor Yang of the Sui sent men to Lingnan, who obtained this snake, three chi long, brocade-coloured like gold, slightly black on close inspection; it could eat meat and was not poisonous to people; when touched and angered it broke itself as if cut with a knife, and when its anger subsided rejoined as before. When snakes die their eyes close, but the Qi snake's eyes stay open. Those born in the Shuzhou region have one eye open and one closed; they are now at Xingguo, with tails like fingernails.

《鳥獸類下·蚺蛇 Pythons》

1
○顧岕曰：環黎產山馬與蚺蛇，山馬似鹿而蚺蛇吞之，馬角甚大，而蛇毒一呵，角自解脫。人遇之者，執指大蛇皮以水一揮，即止。段公路曰：蚺蛇牙長六七寸，辟不祥，一枚直牛數頭。蚺長十餘丈。柯古曰：以婦衣投之，則蟠而不起。劉恂曰：普安州有養蚺蛇戶，嘗以木柺翻視之，取膽而釋蛇令去。
○ Gu Jie says: Around the Li lands there are mountain horses and pythons; the mountain horse is like a deer, and the python swallows it; the horse's antlers are very large, but with one breath of the snake's poison the antlers come loose by themselves. People who meet them hold a piece of snakeskin the size of a finger and wave it with water, and it stops. Duan Gonglu says: The python's fangs are six or seven inches long and ward off ill luck, one being worth several oxen. Pythons are over ten zhang long. Kegu says: If a woman's clothes are thrown to it, it coils and does not rise. Liu Xun says: In Pu'an prefecture there are python-keeping households, who turn them over with wooden sticks, take the gall and release the snake.

《鳥獸類下·制蛇 Controlling Snakes》

1
○雄精辟蛇，唾蛇口則伏，唾蛇身亦爛。蛇畏鵝鶴聲，惡燒羊角、姜黃氣。多栽海芋、白芷、急性子，蛇亦不近。其蛻催生，以塞耳治瘧。物性志曰：蛇甚飽則蛻，冬含土蟄，春吐為蛇黃。惟烏蛇不齧物。
○ Realgar wards off snakes; spat into a snake's mouth it makes it lie still, and spat on its body it rots it. Snakes fear the sound of geese and cranes and hate the smell of burnt sheep's horn and turmeric. If much giant taro, angelica and balsam are planted, snakes will not come near. Their sloughs hasten childbirth, and plugged in the ears treat malaria. The Record of the Natures of Things says: When a snake is very full it sloughs; in winter it hibernates holding earth in its mouth, and in spring spits it out as snake yellow. Only the black snake does not bite.

《鳥獸類下·蠬蜂 The Long Bee》

1
○蠬蜂生南海陽春，附橄欖樹，雖有首足，與木葉無異，鳴則自呼，取者候樹葉凋而得之。志曰：土人藏於笥篋中，以聲之清濁卜禍福，或云遇蠱毒必鳴。今陽春縣屬肇慶府。
○ The long bee lives at Yangchun by the South Sea, clinging to olive trees; though it has a head and feet it is no different from a tree leaf; when it calls it calls its own name, and those who collect it wait until the leaves fall to find it. The gazetteer says: The locals keep them in boxes and baskets and divine fortune and misfortune by the clearness or muddiness of their sound; some say they always call when they meet gu poison. Yangchun county now belongs to Zhaoqing prefecture.

《鳥獸類下·蚌蛤 Clams》

1
○聞雷則（疒秋），見月則吐。西施舌、蛤蜊、江珧柱，以胡粉塗唇，安月下，則肥。泥澱種蟶者，當望時取之。糟吐土蛭之油，亦忌晦日開埕。服丹石者忌食蛤蜊。林見素曰：蟶小者名空豸，似蟶，殼薄，實非一種。
○ On hearing thunder they [close up], and seeing the moon they open. Xishi's tongue, clams and pen-shell adductors, with lead white smeared on their lips and placed under the moon, grow fat. Those who cultivate razor clams in silted mud harvest them at the full moon. The oil of lees-spat earth leeches likewise avoids opening the jars on the last day of the month. Those taking cinnabar and minerals must not eat clams. Lin Jiansu says: The small razor clam is called kongzhi; it resembles the razor clam but has a thin shell and is really not the same kind.

《鳥獸類下·蚶田 Ark-Clam Fields》

1
○江盈科曰：四明蚶田，土人磨蚶末，調糞桶鹼成水，洒田中，一點為一蚶，期至收之，如收穀。嶺南有乳田，用米粉洒田中，久之粉皆成如蠶蛹，及期收之，搗以為粉。然則西域種羊，皆此類乎？張宅人言漳泉蟶蚶類皆不能行，伸出飲水而已，自生自長，即謂之種。
○ Jiang Yingke says: In the ark-clam fields of Siming the locals grind ark clams to powder, mix it with lye from dung buckets into a liquid and sprinkle it on the fields, each drop becoming a clam, and in due time harvest them like grain. Lingnan has milk fields, where rice flour is sprinkled on the fields, and after a time the flour all turns into things like silkworm pupae, which are harvested in due time and pounded into flour. Then are the planted sheep of the Western Regions of this kind? Zhang Zhairen says that the razor clams and ark clams of Zhang and Quan cannot move, but only stretch out to drink water, growing by themselves, and this is called planting.

《鳥獸類下·蠶法 Methods for Silkworms》

1
○開簇時近上向陽者佳。雄繭尖細緊小，雌者圓幔厚大。日數足，蛾自出而配之，用厚紙收其子為種。午日以菖文操水浴種，鄭玄曰蠶為龍精，月及大火則浴其種，是也。臘八日以雪水浴之。除夕以馬齒莧、桃符木查水煎，元日曉浴之，則盛。生蛾時宜火煖其室，以蓐紙細切稚桑葉布之，旦夕飼，多頓者先老，計二十五日老，一簿得絲二十五兩，日多絲減，若月餘則十餘兩耳。二眠時宜溫，寒必加火，此後晴明宜通風日。大眠後天氣熱，室宜清涼，亦勿使濕氣上蒸。凡飼葉宜薄散，經雨露濕葉，須風戾之以飼。十分眠乃住食，十分起齊乃投食。向黃時見黃光，即住分抬之時，去其燠沙。若值熏，即多黑死。食冷露葉，必成白殭。食舊幹葉，則腹結，頭大尾瘠。倉卒開門，暗風中者，必多紅殭。蠅叮蠶，則生肚蟲。凡初飼，一晝夜可四十九頓，或三十六頓，次日三十頓。頭眠飼法，一晝夜可六頓，次日漸加葉。停眠時一晝夜可四頓。大眠齊起，一晝夜可三頓，次日加葉至七八頓。午後天晴，取預磨菉豆粉與切下葉和勻，既可省葉，又解蠶毒，仍得絲多，易繰，堅韌有色。其柘葉蠶，先繭以冷水繰之，絲作琴瑟，清響。埤雅曰：蠶與馬同氣，故周禮禁原蠶。老上薄，不可驚，驚則不成繭。偶入男子履，成繭；入女則否矣。蠶生子曰駒，與母同老，無先後。蠶種畏蒸炷，杉木灰層層收之，佳。蠶聞鼓聲則伏而不起，亦畏雷也。蠶三化，先孕後交，不交者亦子，後生而無眉目，易傷，收亦薄。上繭時以物置其旁，即象其物。交州記：桑蠶一年八熟，三都賦所謂八蠶之綿。吳下養蠶畏見生人，親戚皆不來往，謂之歇蠶，而延平養家不忌也。暄曰：本處養者一切無禁，而蠶亦盛熟。

1
○蜾裸蜂銜螟蛉子，自陶貞白謂銜以啖其子，而蘇恭、寇宗奭、李東壁俱信之。今按：不然。一庵云：先螫殺之，而後呼活之。鄰老云：先以泥作窠，銜蟲置中，以泥封之，乃從其外以翼扇之，而嗚嗚然，久之破窠而出，即變同類。一蠮螉嘗造四五窠，人驚之，彼即不來，此窠窠亦槁落。
○ The potter wasp carries off mingling larvae; from Tao Zhenbai on it was said that it carries them to feed its own young, and Su Gong, Kou Zongshi and Li Dongbi all believed it. An old neighbour says: It first makes a nest of mud, carries the larva into it, seals it with mud, then fans it with its wings from outside, humming, and after a long time [the larva] breaks out of the nest, transformed into the same kind. 《鳥獸類下·養蜂 Keeping Bees》

1
○蜂畏蒲蟲，觸其粉即死，故以鱉甲鑿數戶出入之。箱宜數層，向南壁間，煖也。喜順惡逆，以左置右則盛。諸箱立架皆順而稍下，不得過敵祖房。凡蜂分，以上蓋塗蜜或酒糟收之。視其蜜脾，以夜熏紙煙，則上，乃切而取之。臨冬泥墐，衣以綿著。視其食少，不及至春采花，則以子雞退毛去內髒，懸於窶中飼之。其所切者，以布絹泲潔，煎去其沫，為上蜜。其滓則易水煎，浮水上者取而壓幹，復入釜煎，水竭入滑器，脫下即黃蠟也。其所滌水濾潔，置日中曬，亦成醯。王元之曰：王臺子多則分房不盛，山氓以棘刺關於王台，留其一子。中通曰：燒紅箸插入蜜中，箸出煙者，雜餳也；黏者，雜粟粥也；白蜜成塊為上。中履曰：割蜜者以薄荷細嚼塗手面，自不螫人。
○ Bees fear the cattail insect, and die if they touch its powder; so the boxes are made with several entrances chiselled in turtle shell for going in and out. The boxes should be in several tiers, facing south between walls, for warmth. They like what accords and hate what is contrary; with the left placed to the right, they flourish. What is cut is strained clean through cloth or silk and boiled to remove the froth, which makes the best honey. The dregs are boiled again in fresh water; what floats on the water is taken and pressed dry, then boiled again in a cauldron, and when the water is gone, put into a slippery vessel, and what comes out is yellow wax. Zhonglü says: Those who cut honey chew mint finely and smear it on their hands and faces, and are not stung.

1

These are things people do not know.

1

《鳥獸類下·制蟻法 Methods of Controlling Ants》

1
○蟻有數種，能螫人。廚案四足以水桶浸之，剛土畫路，彼亦不過，炭灰、石灰尤驗。蜜煎鋪中蘸肥皂汁拭蜜器，螻蟻不復來。廣東蟻入箱，須置川山甲。東山紀曰：蟻有重四十斤者。
○ There are several kinds of ants, which can sting people. Stand the four legs of kitchen tables in water buckets; or draw a line with hard earth, and they will not cross it; charcoal ash and lime work even better. In preserved-fruit shops, if honey vessels are wiped with soap water, ants do not come again. In Guangdong, when ants get into boxes, pangolin scales must be put in. The Dongshan Record says: There are ants weighing forty jin.

《鳥獸類下·除白蟻法 Methods of Removing Termites》

1
○青梔子實曬黃，能消白蟻為水。濕活樹去皮，頂鑿竅，注桐油，堅置一二日，水盡去，以為梁柱，蟻不生。或用青礬煮柱本，惟中柱不可煮，煮即井水黑。馬教思曰：血忌日五更斫松柱，無白蟻，或斧敲云：今日血忌。蟻自去。中德曰：養竹雞柱下，白蟻畏其聲。中通曰：白蟻必銜水上柱，乃能食木，松易受水，引泥作路，杉木受水易乾，故蟻不上也。一種蜂鑽杉木，須以煙熏之。
○ Green gardenia fruits dried yellow can dissolve termites into water. Strip the bark of living trees, chisel a hole in the top and pour in tung oil, leave them standing for a day or two until the water has all gone, and used as beams and columns they get no termites. Or boil the base of the columns with green vitriol; only the central column must not be boiled, for if boiled the well water turns black. Ma Jiaosi says: If pine columns are cut at the fifth watch on a blood-avoidance day there are no termites; or strike them with an axe saying: "Today is a blood-avoidance day." The ants then leave by themselves. Zhongde says: Keep bamboo partridges under the columns, for termites fear their call. Zhongtong says: Termites must carry water up the columns before they can eat the wood; pine absorbs water easily, and they draw mud along to make a path, while fir absorbs water and dries easily, so the ants do not climb it. A kind of wasp bores into fir wood, and must be smoked out.

《鳥獸類下·辨鳥獸雌雄 Distinguishing Male and Female in Birds and Beasts》

1
○飛之屬陽，故雄鷙於雌；走之屬陰，故牝猛於牡。龍角浪凹峭，目深，鼻豁，鬐鱗密，上壯下殺，朱火蓽蓽者，雄；角靡浪平，鼻直，鬐少，目圓，鱗薄，尾壯於腹者，雌；見乘異記。蝟毛順者雄，逆者雌。啄木羽斑者雄，褐者雌。樗雞五色具者雄，青黑質白斑者雌。蜥蜴亦五色具者雄，不備者雌。蜻蛉綠色者雄，腰間一遭碧色者雌。鼠糞頭尖者雄，圓頭者雌。雀糞尖者雄，圓者雌。右翼掩左者雄，左翼掩右者雌。見餘冬敘錄、集論諸書。俞琰曰：燒鳥毛納水，沈者雄，浮者雌。屠本畯曰：蝦蟆置地上，東行者雄。鵓鳩雄鳴晴，雌鳴陰。大腰無雄，龜鱉類也；細腰無雌，蜾裸蜂類也；殊不盡然。舊曰鱉無雌，而以想生，其實尾短者雌，雌者腹藏卵，能生產，而無腎，雄者腹藏腎而不卵。凡八竅卵生者，龜鱉類咸卵生影伏。合溪曰：野人有目見龜自尾接者。許慎云龜與蛇交，其一端耳。韋絢曰：鶴、鸕鶿皆胎生。此舊語也，按之不然，或間有之。蠣左顧者雄，右者雌，其實蟶、蚶、仙人掌、銅釘之屬，皆以濕熱化生，無雌雄之交也。
○ Fliers belong to yang, so the male is fiercer than the female; runners belong to yin, so the female is fiercer than the male. A dragon whose horns are wavy and steep, eyes deep, nose wide, fins and scales dense, strong above and tapering below, with red fire blazing, is male; one whose horns are flat with even waves, nose straight, fins few, eyes round, scales thin, and tail stronger than the belly, is female; see the Chengyi ji. Hedgehogs with smooth spines are male, those with bristling spines female. Woodpeckers with spotted feathers are male, brown ones female. Lanternflies with all five colours are male, those blue-black with white spots female. Lizards too with all five colours are male, and those without female. Dragonflies that are green are male, and those with a blue band round the waist female. Rat droppings pointed at the end are male, round-ended female. Sparrow droppings pointed are male, round female. Those whose right wing covers the left are male, and those whose left covers the right female. See the Yudong xulu, Jilun and other books. Yu Yan says: Burn bird feathers and put them in water; those that sink are male, those that float female. Tu Benjun says: Put toads on the ground, and those going east are male. The male turtledove calls for fine weather, the female for cloudy. "Large waists have no males" — tortoises and turtles; "slender waists have no females" — potter wasps; this is not entirely so. It used to be said that turtles have no females and are born of thought, but in fact those with short tails are female, the female holding eggs in her belly and able to give birth but having no testes, while the male holds testes in his belly and no eggs. All eight-orificed oviparous creatures, the tortoise and turtle kind, are all oviparous and brood by shadow. Hexi says: Country people have seen tortoises mating tail to tail. Xu Shen's saying that tortoises mate with snakes is only one aspect. Wei Xuan says: Cranes and cormorants are both viviparous. This is an old saying, and on examination it is not so, or only occasionally. Oysters facing left are male and right female, but in fact razor clams, ark clams, goose barnacles, copper nails and the like are all produced by transformation in damp heat, without mating of male and female.

《鳥獸類下·互相制 Mutual Control》

1
○蝟見虎能跳入虎耳，而制於乾鵲。淮南子曰：鵲矢中蝟，即反而受啄，火勝金也。蘇恭曰：虎耳不受雞卵，蝟何能跳？遂不信制虎之說。其實制虎者性，跳入耳者形容之詞也。或言蝟入虎口，刺破其陽而出。中通曰：虎畏蝟之刺而不食耳，豪豬有刺，虎亦無如之何。文曜鉤曰：土勝水，故守宮食蠆，蝍蛆搏蛇；金伐木，故鷹擊雉。桓譚新論曰：桂害獺，杏核殺豬，粉鰍畏椒，蜈蚣畏油。王起云：玄龜食蟒，飛鼠斷猿，狼虱嚙鶴，青要食虎，皆以小制大，在氣不在形也。石蜦似癩蝦蟆，大者徑七八寸，其色近黑，能食蛇，其背骨能殺人，蛇浮水則首仰水上，石蜦仰腹誘蛇，蛇來吞之，則前兩足抱蛇入水，浸死而食之。刺蝟制金蠶，托胎制蜈蚣。鬱林守言有訟金蠶蠱者，土傑獻謀，取兩刺蝟捕獲之。蜈蚣、蠍畏蛉窮，蚰蜒也，以涎繞蠍規之，則不敢動，蛇亦畏之。農家曰：夏初螺出殼產子田間，雨漂殼去，不能自還，則化為蚰蜒，後又化為蝸牛，好升高而焦死，東坡所歎黏壁枯也。蠓蚋因陰濕而生，見陽而死。濕生物多惡香散，故麝化螺，蔥化蚓。蝍蛆，大蛇每自口入，食蛇腹。南地多蛇毒，人行筒置蝍蛆以防之。又能嗅龍腥，深山大柘生之，天將雨，爭就木最高處，蓋聞龍腥耳，雷雨龍亦碎樹。
○ When a hedgehog sees a tiger it can jump into the tiger's ear, yet it is controlled by the magpie. The Huainanzi says: When magpie droppings hit a hedgehog, it turns over and is pecked; this is fire overcoming metal. Su Gong says: A tiger's ear cannot take even a hen's egg, so how could a hedgehog jump in? So he did not believe the account of controlling tigers. In fact, controlling the tiger is in its nature, and jumping into the ear is a figure of speech. Some say the hedgehog enters the tiger's mouth, pierces its genitals and comes out. Zhongtong says: The tiger fears the hedgehog's spines and does not eat it, that is all; the porcupine has spines, and the tiger can do nothing to it. The Wenyao gou says: Earth overcomes water, so geckos eat scorpions and centipedes fight snakes; metal fells wood, so hawks strike pheasants. Huan Tan's New Discourses say: Cassia harms otters, apricot stones kill pigs, loaches fear pepper, and centipedes fear oil. Wang Qi says: The black tortoise eats pythons, the flying squirrel cuts off gibbons, the wolf louse bites cranes, and the qingyao eats tigers — all the small controlling the large, the matter being in qi, not in form. The shilun resembles a warty toad, the large ones seven or eight inches across, nearly black in colour; it can eat snakes, and its back bone can kill people; when a snake swims with its head raised above water, the shilun turns belly up to lure it, and when the snake comes to swallow it, it clasps the snake with its two front legs, takes it into the water, drowns it and eats it. The hedgehog controls the golden silkworm, and the tuotai controls centipedes. The prefect of Yulin said that when there was a lawsuit about golden-silkworm gu, Tu Jie offered a plan to catch it with two hedgehogs. Centipedes and scorpions fear the lingqiong, which is the millipede; it encircles a scorpion with its slime, and the scorpion dares not move; snakes too fear it. The farmers say: In early summer snails come out of their shells and lay eggs in the fields; if rain washes the shells away and they cannot return, they turn into millipedes, and later into snails, which love to climb high and die parched — the "dry on the wall" that Dongpo lamented. Midges and gnats are born from yin damp and die on seeing yang. Things born of damp mostly hate the dispersal of fragrance, so musk turns into snails and onions into earthworms. As for the centipede, it often enters a great snake through the mouth and eats its belly. In the south there are many poisonous snakes, and travellers carry centipedes in tubes to guard against them. It can also smell the stench of dragons; it grows in great cudrania trees deep in the mountains, and when it is about to rain they rush to the highest part of the tree, because they smell the dragon's stench; in thunder and rain the dragon also smashes the tree.

《鳥獸類下·鳥獸通理 General Principles of Birds and Beasts》

1
○雀四時有子，鴿每月有子。春鳥雙，秋鳥群。燕背飛向棲，鷹向飛背棲。黃鶯聲三十二囀，百舌聲十二囀。短腳多伏，長腳多立。鶯巢於樹，鸂鶒於荷，俱接葉，老杜所謂接葉暗巢鶯也。段氏曰：鶻生三子，一為鴟。禽經曰：鸛生三子，一為鶴。瀛海記曰：遼有鷹背狗，乃鷹產三卵，一鷹一雕一狗也。造化權輿曰：夏雀生鶉，楚鳩生鶚。野雞屬陰，先鳴而後鼓翼；家雞屬陽，先鼓翼而後鳴。家禽卵多黃，野禽卵多白，煮之，家禽皆堅，野黃堅白不堅。禽經曰：鵝溯月而伏隨日，鴨複隨月。斑鳩哺子，朝從上下，暮從下上。李衛公言鵝驚鬼，鵁鶄厭火，孔雀辟惡，啄木愈齲。繁露曰：醞去煙，鴟羽去瞇，頸金取火。子規啼必北向，鷓鴣飛必南翥。雞窠邊勿挨磨，鵝窠邊勿絞索。燕銜避戊已。欲燕來者，桐刻雌雄狀投井。雷效云：海竭江枯，投遊波燕名而立泛。智按：龍聞燒燕即動，故可致水，漁者以燕肉和曲則得魚，此即龍嗜燕之一例也。燕本水種，故仍化蛤，可以互證。喜鵲作數，以枝刺下，長狸鼠也。八鳩巢竹林，亦以狸不能上竹。鵲腦骨媚，以其性煖也。鵲有隱巢木，今不驗，桓玄以愚顧愷之耳。造化權輿曰：乾陽為馬，坤陰為牛。馬病則臥，陰勝也；牛病則立，陽勝也。馬起先前足，臥先後足，從陽也；牛起先後足，臥先前足，從陰也。牛羊居丑未之位。牛色蒼，多近春生氣，故聞死則觳觫；羊色白，多近秋殺氣，故聞死不懼。凡草木經牛啖之必重茂，經羊啖之必悴槁，諺曰牛食如澆，羊食如燒，信夫。牛羊下來，注埤雅謂羊性畏露，晚出而早歸，常先於牛。豺祭獸圓，獺祭魚方。魚祭地，獸祭天。獺畏雉尾，狐畏犀角。以驢糞餌狗，當驢馬鳴，狗逐之叫則不止。凡馬牛羊豕遇初六、十六、二十六日生者多夭。虎豹畏角煙氣，入山者燒角，則虎豹自遠。李石云：滇中多豹少虎，以豹食虎也。鱟血碧，鲗血黑，鱉膽辣，鱧膽甘。鱷首縣之齒生，黿肉懸之伸縮。靈蛇自斷自續，蜥蜴斷尾猶動。蛟觸駝煙死。螺螄畏雪落，蟹怕重霧，（魚恆）鰣魚出水僵，海魚見淡水即死。手摩鸚鵡背則喑，月翦鴝鵒舌而語。江北少蜈蚣，關北無蟬。蟬以腹鳴，蟾蜍以脰鳴，蟻以須視。毛蟲如蠶，將繭則涎成一團，落毛其上，使蟲不能入，然後立定而代。芒種螳螂齊出，重九蚊蛛生。花蜘蛛申日能越。陸游曰：馬鞭擊貓，筇竹杖擊狗，皆折。姅變者坐虎皮，則毛壞。貂帽見貓踏則毛結，貉皮見燕則毛落。斑鳩食桑椹，貓食薄荷，虎食狗，雞食蜈蚣，蛇食茱萸，鹿得茶飲，駱駝食柳，虎倀食烏梅，狸精食羊肝，狐鼠之妖食人爪髮，皆醉。雞可求種於灶，貓可求種於牛。曲羽禽之伏，隨日光而轉卵；介蟲之伏，隨日光而影加。蛤、蟹、螺、龜與月盛衰，坎為月為水，離為之偶，故離為龍物。蝙蝠食蚊，其晴不化，溲出為夜明砂，醫目障，取蚊向日視之，其晴正綠。鷹眼碧，蜻蜓眼如碧珠，惟鴿眼有五色。山牛有四眼，海魚有四眼者，猶之六眼龜，乃二眼真，餘則似眼耳。牛有耳而聽以鼻，龜有鼻而息以耳，龍聽以角，謂之聾蟲。廣多巨蛇，北地多貂鼠。貓犬持齋，知朔望。蜂分必黃道吉日。鹿清明後不食苖。狼黍，貓時不殺物。蜮四月朔上弩，八月朔下弩。
○ Sparrows have young in all four seasons, and pigeons every month. Spring birds are in pairs, autumn birds in flocks. Swallows fly facing away and roost facing each other; hawks fly facing each other and roost facing away. The oriole has thirty-two warbles, the blackbird twelve. Short-legged birds mostly crouch, long-legged mostly stand. Orioles nest in trees and mandarin ducks on lotus, both among joined leaves; this is what Old Du meant by "joined leaves darkly nest the oriole." Master Duan says: The falcon lays three eggs, one of which becomes an owl. The Classic of Birds says: The stork lays three eggs, one of which becomes a crane. The Record of the Seas says: Liao has the eagle-backed dog; the eagle lays three eggs, one an eagle, one a vulture and one a dog. The Zaohua quanyu says: Summer sparrows produce quails, and Chu doves produce ospreys. Pheasants belong to yin and crow before flapping their wings; domestic fowl belong to yang and flap their wings before crowing. The eggs of domestic fowl are mostly yolk, those of wild fowl mostly white; boiled, those of domestic fowl are all firm, while in wild ones the yolk is firm and the white not. The Classic of Birds says: Geese brood against the moon following the sun, and ducks again follow the moon. Turtledoves feed their young from top to bottom in the morning and from bottom to top in the evening. Lord Li Wei says geese startle ghosts, the jiaojing wards off fire, peacocks ward off evil, and woodpeckers cure tooth decay. The Luxuriant Dew says: Brewing removes smoke, owl feathers remove dust from the eyes, and neck-gold takes fire. The cuckoo always calls facing north, and the partridge always flies south. Do not grind beside a hen's nest, nor twist rope beside a goose's nest. Swallows carrying [mud] avoid wu and ji days. Those who want swallows to come carve male and female shapes of tong wood and throw them into the well. Lei Xiao says: When the sea dries and the rivers wither, throw in youbo a name for the swallow and the water rises at once. I, Zhi, note: Dragons stir when they smell burnt swallow, so it can bring water; fishermen who mix swallow meat with ferment get fish; this is an instance of the dragon's fondness for swallows. Swallows are originally of a water kind, so they still turn into clams; this can serve as mutual proof. Magpies build several nests, with branches stuck pointing down, to keep off wildcats and rats. The bajiu nests in bamboo groves, also because wildcats cannot climb bamboo. The magpie's brain bone is a love charm because its nature is warm. That magpies have trees that hide their nests is not now borne out; it was how Huan Xuan fooled Gu Kaizhi. The Zaohua quanyu says: Qian, yang, is the horse; kun, yin, is the ox. When a horse is ill it lies down, because yin prevails; when an ox is ill it stands, because yang prevails. The horse rises forefeet first and lies down hind feet first, following yang; the ox rises hind feet first and lies down forefeet first, following yin. Oxen and sheep occupy the positions of chou and wei. The ox is dark in colour, mostly close to the life-giving qi of spring, so it trembles on hearing of death; the sheep is white, mostly close to the killing qi of autumn, so it is unafraid on hearing of death. All plants grazed by oxen flourish again, while those grazed by sheep wither; the proverb says, "The ox eats like watering, the sheep eats like burning" — truly. "Oxen and sheep come down": the commentary of the Piya says that sheep by nature fear dew, so they go out late and return early, always before the oxen. The jackal sets out its offering of beasts in a circle, the otter its offering of fish in a square. Fish offer to the earth, beasts to heaven. Otters fear pheasant tails, and foxes fear rhinoceros horn. If dogs are fed donkey dung, they chase donkeys and horses when they bray and do not stop barking. Horses, oxen, sheep and pigs born on the sixth, sixteenth or twenty-sixth day mostly die young. Tigers and leopards fear the smoke of horn, so those entering the mountains burn horn and tigers and leopards keep away. Li Shi says: Dian has many leopards and few tigers, because leopards eat tigers. Horseshoe crab blood is blue, cuttlefish blood black, turtle gall pungent and snakehead gall sweet. A crocodile's head when hung up grows teeth, and turtle flesh when hung up extends and contracts. The divine snake breaks and rejoins itself, and the lizard's severed tail still moves. The jiao dies when it meets camel smoke. Snails fear falling snow, crabs fear heavy fog, the shad reeves shad stiffens out of water, and sea fish die on meeting fresh water. Stroking a parrot's back makes it mute, and trimming a myna's tongue in [the fifth] month makes it talk. North of the Yangzi there are few centipedes, and north of the passes no cicadas. Cicadas sing with their bellies, toads with their throats, and ants see with their antennae. Hairy caterpillars like silkworms, when about to make cocoons, form their slime into a ball and shed their hair on it so that insects cannot get in, and then stand still and transform. At Grain in Ear mantises all come out, and on the ninth of the ninth month mosquitoes and spiders are born. Flower spiders can leap on shen days. Lu You says: A horsewhip striking a cat and a qiong bamboo staff striking a dog both break. If a menstruating woman sits on a tiger skin, its hair is spoiled. A sable hat trodden on by a cat has its fur knotted, and a raccoon-dog skin seen by a swallow loses its fur. Turtledoves that eat mulberries, cats that eat mint, tigers that eat dogs, chickens that eat centipedes, snakes that eat evodia, deer that drink tea, camels that eat willow, tiger-ghosts that eat smoked plums, fox spirits that eat sheep's liver, and fox and rat goblins that eat human nails and hair all become drunk. Chickens can seek mating from the stove, and cats from the ox. When curved-feathered birds brood, they turn the eggs following the sunlight; when shelled creatures brood, they add shadow following the sunlight. Clams, crabs, snails and tortoises wax and wane with the moon; kan is the moon and water, and li is its counterpart, so li is the dragon creature. Bats eat mosquitoes, whose eyes are not digested and are excreted as bat droppings, which cure eye cataracts; take the mosquitoes and look at them against the sun, and their eyes are pure green. Hawks' eyes are blue-green, dragonflies' eyes like jasper beads, and only pigeons' eyes have five colours. Mountain oxen have four eyes, and sea fish with four eyes, like the six-eyed tortoise, have only two true eyes, the rest merely resembling eyes. Oxen have ears but hear with their noses, tortoises have noses but breathe through their ears, and dragons hear with their horns and are called deaf creatures. Guang has many great snakes, and the north many sables. Cats and dogs keep fasts and know the new and full moons. Bees always swarm on yellow-path auspicious days. Deer do not eat shoots after Qingming. Wolves [at] millet [time] and cats at certain hours do not kill. The yu sets its crossbow on the first of the fourth month and puts it down on the first of the eighth month.

《鳥獸類下·象鼠魚膽常移 The Galls of Elephants, Rats and Fish Often Move》

1
○象膽隨四時在足：春在前左膞，夏在前右膞，秋在後左膞，冬在後右膞。徐鉉以言象膽見稱於宋祖，而張萱所見象房膽在腹，則亦有時在腹，古未推明也。熊膽春在首，夏在腹，秋左足，冬右足。蚺蛇膽隨月轉，上旬近頭，中旬近心，下旬近尾。魚膽春夏近下，秋冬近上。鼠膽在肝，活取則有，見餘冬序錄。按：鼠膽最難取，將死則膽已失矣。虛舟子曰：鼠膽隨人神在。
○ The elephant's gall is in its legs according to the four seasons: in spring in the left foreleg, in summer in the right foreleg, in autumn in the left hind leg, and in winter in the right hind leg. Xu Xuan was praised by the Song founder for speaking of the elephant's gall, yet what Zhang Xuan saw in the elephant house had the gall in the belly, so it is sometimes in the belly, which the ancients did not make clear. The bear's gall is in the head in spring, the belly in summer, the left leg in autumn and the right leg in winter. The python's gall turns with the month, near the head in the first ten days, near the heart in the middle ten, and near the tail in the last ten. A fish's gall is low in spring and summer and high in autumn and winter. A rat's gall is in the liver, and is found when taken alive; see the Yudong xulu. Note: A rat's gall is most difficult to take, for when it is about to die the gall is already lost. Xuzhouzi says: The rat's gall follows the location of the human spirit.
