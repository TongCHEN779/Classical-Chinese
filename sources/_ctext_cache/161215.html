《天類 Heaven》

《天類·象數理氣征幾論 On Images, Numbers, Principle, Qi and the Evidence of the Incipient》

1
○為物不二之至理，隱不可見，質皆氣也，徵其端幾，不離象數。彼掃器言道、離費窮隱者，偏權也。日月星辰，天縣象數如此；官肢經絡，天之表人身也如此；圖書卦策，聖人之冒准約幾如此。無非物也，無非心也，猶二之乎？自黃帝明運氣，唐虞在璣衡，孔子學易以扐閏，衍天地之五，歷數律度，是所首重，儒者多半弗問，故秩序變化之原不能灼然，何怪乎舉禮節樂律而弁髦之，舉倫物舊章而放棄之，謂為聖人之所增設乎哉？核實難，逃虛易，洸洋之流實不能知其故，故吹影鏤空以為恢奇；其言象數者類流小術，支離附會，未核其真，又宜其生厭也。于是乎兩間之真象數舉皆茫然矣。胡康侯曰：「象數者，天理也，非人之所能為也。」天示其度，地產其狀，物獻其則，身具其符，心自冥應，但未嘗求其故耳。學者靜正矣，不合俯仰遠近而互觀之，又何所徵哉？智故隨聞而紀之，自天象始。
○ The ultimate principle by which things are not two is hidden and cannot be seen; its substance is all qi, and to find evidence of its first incipient signs one cannot depart from images and numbers. Those who sweep away concrete things to speak of the Way, and who leave the manifest to probe the hidden, are using a one-sided expedient. The sun, moon, stars and planets are images and numbers hung up by Heaven in this way; the organs, limbs, channels and vessels, the outward expression of Heaven in the human body, are like this; the River Chart and Luo Writing, the trigrams and milfoil stalks, by which the sages covered and set the standard for the incipient, are like this. None of it is not things, and none of it is not mind — how could they be two? Since the Yellow Emperor made clear the circulation of qi, Yao and Shun attended to the armillary sphere, and Confucius studied the Changes, using the remainder stalks for the intercalary month and extending the five numbers of Heaven and Earth, calendrical computation, pitch-pipes and measures have been given first importance; yet most Confucians do not ask about them, and so the source of order and transformation cannot be made clear — is it any wonder, then, that they treat ritual measure and musical pitch as cast-off caps, discard the old institutions of human relations and things, and call them additions made by the sages? Verifying facts is hard and escaping into emptiness easy, and those who drift in vague talk truly cannot know the reasons, so they blow at shadows and carve the void to make a show of the marvellous; while those who speak of images and numbers mostly drift into petty arts, fragmentary and forced, without verifying the truth, so it is no wonder that people tire of them. And so the true images and numbers between Heaven and Earth have all become obscure. Hu Kanghou said: "Images and numbers are the principle of Heaven; they are not something man can make." Heaven displays its degrees, Earth produces its forms, things present their patterns, the body possesses its tallies, and the mind silently responds; it is only that people have never sought the reasons. Scholars may be calm and upright, but if they do not look up and down, far and near, and observe these together, what evidence can they find? So I, Zhi, have recorded things as I heard them, beginning with the heavenly phenomena.

《天類·天象原理 The Principles of the Heavenly Phenomena》

1
○良孺熊公曰：傳天文者多祖裨灶、甘公、唐昧、尹皋、石申之遺，課驗凌雜米鹽。史遷世掌天官，所云「河鼓不欲曲，心星不欲直，老人見治安，不見兵起」，班固沿之，「勾信維散，龜鱉不居漢中」諸語，皆幾以經星為可移矣。夫氣為真象，事為真數，合人于天，而真理不燦然於吾前乎？天地之象至定，不定者，氣蒙之也；天地之數至定，不定者，事亂之也。達者始終古今，深觀時變，仰察蒙氣，俯識亂事，而權衡其理，則天官備矣。或問：「天垂象，見吉凶，何也？」曰：譬諸人身，脾氣病則貌黃，肝病則青，腎病則黑，喜慶惠迪，額潤顙明。華嚴曰：「一國人感惡緣，則彼當土眾生睹諸一切不祥，或見暈玦彗孛，但此國見，彼國眾生本所不見。」蓋氣由地起，吉氣上蒸，為龍雲矞彩之類，人在氣中生養，自有聖賢豪傑挺生；有凶氣上蒸，為霾魃欃槍之類，人在氣中生養，自有饑饉兵戈橫出。故望氣者止宜于當土辨禍福，入國邑而候息耗，不宜于普天率土、百年易世一概牽合。若日星之具體，本自如也，因此地氣有吉凶，則此地人眼從氣中窺，便分祥異。故暈背風霾晴雨之候，百里有不可同觀者。惟彗孛之氣沖入晶宇，所至最高，天下仰見，然比之于七曜之度，不啻下甚，即千里而量測之，差數睹矣。或曰：「地氣一也，何為此方吉，彼方凶，此時吉，彼時凶？」曰：是則數為之也，實胚胎于人事也。如堯舜醞釀宇宙太和元氣，地平天成；及桀紂而以塗炭生民為事，其數應窮，便致湯武放伐。斯固事數相根，而氣操其關龠者也。不獨地氣，天氣亦然。如中國處于赤道北二十度起，至四十度止，日俱在南，既不受其亢燥，距日亦不甚遠，又復資其溫煖，稟氣中和，所以車書禮樂、聖賢豪傑為四裔朝宗。若過南逼日太暑，只應生海外諸蠻人；過北遠日太寒，只應生塞外沙漠人。若西方人所處北極出地與中國同緯度者，其人亦無不喜讀書、知歷理；不同緯度，便為回回諸國，忿鷙好殺。此又一端也。質文之運，三代循環，興廢有定數，皆自人事釀成。當興之時，天地如律回陽，其氣條達，如鏡重磨，其象宣朗；當廢之時，天地如律重陰，其氣鬱閉，鏡蒙塵垢，其象湮暗。此定理也。占候祁禳，原為小數，而警予責已，仰思咎謝，俯答明譴，堯舜湯文以來，自有欽若昭事、無敢戲豫之道法在焉，可忽乎哉？鹿湖潛老夫曰：執氣質而測之，則但顯各各不相知，而各各互相應之通幾猶晦也。夫聲氣風力，實傳心光，受命如向，神不可測，而當前物則，天度同符，格之踐之，引觸酬酢，信其不二，享其不惑，此則有所以為物、所以為心、所以為天者，豈徒委之氣質而已乎？萬歷已未，餘在長溪，親炙壇石先生，喜其精論，故識所折衷如此。
○ Master Xiong Liangru said: Those who transmit astronomy mostly take as their ancestors the legacies of Pi Zao, Master Gan, Tang Mei, Yin Gao and Shi Shen, whose verification of portents is a jumble of trivia like rice and salt. Sima Qian, whose family held the office of astronomer for generations, said that "the Drum of the River should not be bent, the Heart Star should not be straight, and when Canopus appears there is order and peace, when it does not appear war arises," and Ban Gu followed him, with such statements as "the Hook and Extension stars disperse" and "the Turtle and Soft-shelled Turtle do not stay in the Han," all of which come close to treating the fixed stars as movable. Now, if qi is the true image and events the true number, and man is united with Heaven, will not the true principle shine clearly before us? The images of Heaven and Earth are perfectly fixed, and where they seem unfixed it is because qi obscures them; the numbers of Heaven and Earth are perfectly fixed, and where they seem unfixed it is because events disorder them. If the man of understanding traces past and present from beginning to end, observes the changes of the times deeply, looks up to examine the obscuring qi and down to recognize the disordering events, and weighs their principle, then the astronomer's office is complete. Someone asked: "Heaven hangs out images and shows good and ill fortune — how is this?" I reply: compare it to the human body — when the qi of the spleen is diseased the face turns yellow, when the liver is diseased it turns green, when the kidneys are diseased it turns black, and with joy and good fortune and following the right path the forehead is moist and the brow bright. The Avatamsaka says: "When the people of one country are touched by evil karma, the beings of that land see all kinds of inauspicious things, perhaps haloes, broken rings, comets and bushy stars, but only this country sees them, and the beings of other countries do not see them at all." For qi rises from the earth; when auspicious qi steams upward it becomes dragon clouds, auspicious coloured vapours and the like, and men born and nurtured in that qi naturally bring forth sages, worthies, heroes and outstanding men; when baleful qi steams upward it becomes dust-haze, drought demons, the Chanqiang comet and the like, and men born and nurtured in that qi naturally bring forth famine and war. Therefore those who observe qi should only distinguish fortune and misfortune in their own land, and divine prosperity and decline on entering a state or city; they should not lump together all under Heaven or the changes of a hundred years. As for the bodies of the sun and stars, they are always as they are; but because the earthly qi of a place is auspicious or baleful, the eyes of the people of that place, peering through that qi, distinguish auspicious and strange signs. Thus the signs of haloes and back-arcs, wind and haze, clear skies and rain may not be seen alike within a hundred li. Only the qi of comets and bushy stars rushes into the crystalline sphere and reaches the greatest height, so that all under Heaven look up and see it; yet compared with the degrees of the seven luminaries it is far lower, and if measured from a thousand li apart the difference becomes visible. Someone said: "The earth's qi is one; why is it auspicious in this place and baleful in that, auspicious at this time and baleful at that?" I reply: this is due to number, and is really engendered by human affairs. Thus Yao and Shun fostered the supreme harmony and primordial qi of the universe, and the earth was levelled and Heaven completed; but when it came to Jie and Zhou, who made it their business to grind the people into mud and ashes, their number was bound to be exhausted, which brought about the expulsion and punitive campaigns of Tang and Wu. Thus events and number are rooted in each other, and qi holds the key between them. Not only the qi of the earth, but the qi of Heaven too is like this. China, for example, lies from twenty degrees north of the equator to forty degrees; the sun is always to the south, so it neither suffers its scorching dryness nor is it too far from the sun, and it benefits from its warmth, receiving balanced and harmonious qi; that is why, with its common writing and carriage axles, its rites and music, its sages, worthies and heroes, it is the court to which the peoples of the four borders come. Further south, close to the sun, it is too hot and fit only to produce the barbarians beyond the seas; further north, far from the sun, it is too cold and fit only to produce the people of the deserts beyond the passes. Of the Westerners, those who live where the elevation of the North Pole is at the same latitude as China all love reading and know the principles of calendrical science; at other latitudes are the Muslim countries, wrathful, fierce and fond of killing. This is another aspect of it. The cycles of substance and refinement turn through the Three Dynasties, and rise and fall have fixed numbers, all brought about by human affairs. At times of rising, Heaven and Earth are like the pitch-pipes returning to yang: their qi flows freely, and like a mirror repolished their images are bright; at times of decline, Heaven and Earth are like the pitch-pipes in deep yin: their qi is pent up and blocked, and like a mirror covered in dust their images are dim. This is a fixed principle. Prognostication and prayers to avert disaster were originally petty arts, but in taking warning and blaming oneself, looking up to reflect on one's faults and looking down to answer clear reproofs, there has been since Yao, Shun, Tang and Wen a way and method of reverently complying and brightly serving, never daring to be frivolous — how can it be neglected? The Old Man of Qian of Deer Lake said: If one clings to qi and substance to measure it, then all that shows is that each thing is unaware of the others, while the comprehension of the incipient by which each responds to the others remains obscure. Now sound, qi, wind and force truly transmit the light of the mind, receiving the mandate like an echo; the spirit cannot be fathomed, yet the patterns of the things before us tally with the degrees of Heaven; investigating and treading them, touching and responding to them, one trusts that they are not two and enjoys freedom from doubt — here there is that by which things are things, the mind is mind and Heaven is Heaven; how could it be left merely to qi and substance? In the jiwei year of Wanli I was at Changxi and studied in person with Master Tanshi; delighting in his refined discourses, I recorded what I took from them in this way.

《天類·氣論 On Qi》

1
○潛老夫曰：蛻形知氣，蛻氣知神，蛻神歸空，蛻空見理，蛻理還物，如是則物物已耳，又何蛻乎？世惟執形以為見，而氣則微矣。然冬呵出口，其氣如煙；人立日中，頭上蒸歊，影騰在地；考鐘伐鼓，窗欞之紙皆動：則氣之為質固可見也。充一切虛，貫一切實，更何疑焉？聖人合虛實神形而表其氣中之理，西干止會通于惟心，彼離氣執理與掃物尊心，皆病也。理以心知，知與理來，因物則而後交格以顯，豈能離氣之質耶？此伏羲所以合俯仰遠近而通類也。愚者曰：一切物皆氣所為也，空皆氣所實也。物有則，空亦有則，以費知隱，絲毫不爽，其則也，理之可徵者也，而神在其中矣。神而明之，知而無知，然豈兩截耶？知即無知，故不為一切所惑，乃享其神，是曰大定。揭暄曰：所以為氣，吾師言之矣。氣既包虛實而為體，原不礙萬物之鼓其中而依附以為用也。凡諸有形色、有聲聞，莫不賅而存之，天地之間豈有絲毫空隙哉？嬰瓶挈水，閉其一孔，水即不入，氣塞中也；倒而懸之，水亦不出，氣未入也。萬斛之石不能壓一氣球，必氣出盡而後合。千人挽舟，中流莫進，風吹之則飄飄然。大可移石飛山、湧海震地，小則吹箭吸流、轉輪推腹，以至鳥飛魚躍、牛耕馬走、肢體運動，何往而非氣之所為也耶？可見氣之為氣，屬無形而有形，若無力而有力。朝霞之牖戶，雨後之山林，其色倍麗，是潤物之形而為之籠罩者，氣也。水搖而星擊，目眚而燈花，氣動而日摩，其影非一，是幻物而使之多者，氣也。日月之初出也，玻盒之苑邃也，水深而錢浮也，物像像物，其形倍真也，是使物大而遠近而顯者，氣也。氣之統體若一大鏡，萬物之麗于地者悉映于天，故山市海市、暗室一隙，皆得轉吸而見之。大鏡之中不礙其分為各小鏡也，不礙小鏡之即大鏡也。萬其水即萬其月，萬其目即萬其影，住去畢具，無有分別。此其可見者。其不可見者，則以音傳，如風聲、水聲、人聲、鳥聲、諸樂作聲，一時異響雜奏，歷歷倫類，不相蒙掩，即重垣昏夜，間十方一耳，可使達也。至于虹霓雲霞出諸色，寒暑燥濕異諸性，熏香鍐臭，或緣于物，或畜于積，聚散動靜，任其自然，以為玄通也。玄通至矣，是豈黭淺惉懘者所得而窺之也哉？又豈洸洋執冒者所得而滅之也哉？中通曰：氣無陰陽，而充乎物則有陰陽，然亦相反而不相離也。火中之氣反為陰氣，故燃油則光生，而枯木無焰；水中之氣反為陽氣，故沸湯則氣上，而寒泉不升。且陽氣遇陰始見，故口中熱氣夏日隱而冬日顯也；陰氣遇陽始見，故嵓中冷氣冬日藏而夏日出也。
○ The Old Man of Qian said: Shed form and you know qi; shed qi and you know spirit; shed spirit and you return to emptiness; shed emptiness and you see principle; shed principle and you return to things — and so it is just things after all; what then is there to shed? The world clings only to form as what can be seen, and qi is subtle. Yet in winter breath blown from the mouth is like smoke; when a person stands in the sun, vapour rises from his head and its shadow rises on the ground; when bells are struck and drums beaten, the paper in the window lattices all moves: so the substance of qi can certainly be seen. It fills all emptiness and pervades all solidity — what more doubt can there be? The sages united the empty and the solid, spirit and form, and expressed the principle within qi, whereas the Western Indians [the Buddhists] only comprehend things through "mind alone"; departing from qi to cling to principle, and sweeping away things to honour the mind, are both faults. Principle is known by the mind, knowledge comes together with principle, and it is manifested only through the patterns of things and their mutual investigation — how could it depart from the substance of qi? This is why Fu Xi looked up and down, far and near, to comprehend the categories. The Foolish One says: All things are made by qi, and emptiness is all filled by qi. Things have patterns and emptiness too has patterns; from the manifest one knows the hidden without the slightest error — this is the pattern, the verifiable part of principle, and spirit is within it. To make it spiritual and bright, to know and yet not know — but how could these be two separate halves? Knowing is not-knowing, and so one is not deluded by anything and enjoys one's spirit; this is called the great stability. Jie Xuan says: Why it is qi my teacher has already explained. Since qi embraces the empty and the solid as its substance, it does not hinder the myriad things from stirring within it and depending on it for their functioning. All that has form and colour, sound and audibility, is contained and preserved in it — how could there be the slightest gap between Heaven and Earth? If one lifts a vase of water and closes one of its holes, water will not enter, because the qi blocks the inside; hang it upside down and the water will not come out either, because the qi has not entered. A stone of ten thousand bushels cannot crush a ball of air; only when the air has all come out does it close up. A thousand men hauling a boat cannot advance in midstream, but when the wind blows on it, it glides along. On a large scale it can shift rocks and make mountains fly, raise the sea and shake the earth; on a small scale it blows darts, sucks up liquids, turns wheels and pushes the belly, down to birds flying and fish leaping, oxen ploughing and horses running, and the movement of limbs — where is it not the work of qi? One can see that qi, as qi, is formless and yet has form, seems powerless and yet has power. At windows and doors in the morning glow, and in the hills and woods after rain, colours are twice as beautiful: what moistens the forms of things and envelops them is qi. When water is stirred, the stars dance; when the eyes are clouded, lamps flower; when qi moves, the sun rubs; the images are more than one: what makes things illusory and multiplies them is qi. The sun and moon when they first rise, the deep recesses in a glass box, the coin floating up in deep water, things imaging and images of things, their forms twice as real: what makes things large, brings the far near and makes them visible is qi. The whole body of qi is like one great mirror: all the things that rest on the earth are reflected in the sky, so that mountain mirages and sea mirages, and a single chink in a dark room, can all transmit and draw in images to be seen. Within the great mirror nothing prevents its being divided into separate small mirrors, and nothing prevents the small mirrors being the great mirror. Ten thousand waters are ten thousand moons, ten thousand eyes are ten thousand images; staying and going are all present without distinction. These are the things that can be seen. What cannot be seen is transmitted by sound: the sound of wind, of water, of human voices, of birds, of all kinds of music, different sounds played together at once, each distinct in kind and not obscuring one another, which even through double walls and at dead of night, from the ten directions to a single ear, can be made to arrive. As for rainbows, clouds and glows producing colours, cold, heat, dryness and damp giving different natures, fragrances wafting and stenches pervading, some arising from things and some stored up in accumulation, gathering and dispersing, moving and resting as they naturally do — this is the mysterious pervasion. The mysterious pervasion is perfect: how could those who are dim, shallow and stagnant glimpse it? And how could those who drift in vague talk and cling to generalities abolish it? Zhongtong says: Qi has no yin and yang, but when it fills things it has yin and yang, which are opposed yet inseparable. The qi within fire is on the contrary yin qi, so burning oil produces light while dry wood has no flame; the qi within water is on the contrary yang qi, so boiling water sends up vapour while a cold spring does not rise. Moreover, yang qi becomes visible only on meeting yin, so the hot breath of the mouth is hidden in summer and visible in winter; yin qi becomes visible only on meeting yang, so the cold air in rock caves is hidden in winter and comes out in summer.

《天類·氣映差 Distortion by Reflection in Qi》

1
○魏朴亦言之矣：空中皆氣，江海水浮，射之，其專綴之算影皆不直也。置錢于碗，遠立者視之不見，注水溢碗，錢浮于水面矣。此猶日未出而水光浮、日初出而不熱之理也。澉川董漢陽穀謂：京口至瓜洲，江面一千三百餘櫓，一櫓之力不過三尺，則四百丈也；中有金山，廣百五十丈，南北水面共二百五十丈，可以八十餘艘作浮橋，每舟二丈，中虛一丈，加版通行，當名朝宗萬歲橋。昔宋取南唐，繫浮橋于采石，雖暫，亦知其可為矣。寰宇志：京口江闊四十里。蔡寬夫曰：瓜洲在江中也。今闊十七里。憶崇禎乙亥測量，言二千丈餘者，以目光為水光奪，以水嘗飽視其中為極，而所餘猶半也。空中久視，亦為日光所搖，非固精凝定者，測量空遠，豈能準哉？暄曰：氣能使物大，氣能使物顯，氣能使物近。江中見逆風使檣者，隔岸望之，舟已觸岸，而久不回帆，乃知所望之岸非真岸也。又江中有洲，此岸望之，則洲近彼岸，距十分之一；彼岸望之，則近此岸，距十分之一。是知十與一乃中數也，故立清蒙差算法。然地上浮游之氣，日少而夜多，豎少而橫多，千差則蒙中有清，清中有蒙，蒙差少而清差多，要在算者之自得于心也。中通曰：測遠須重表，凡數測而後算，始能無差。又須彼岸有樹或屋或物可作准的，方能測度；不然，則一線江岸，左右無定，宜其不準也。日初出大而不熱者，地氣橫映故大，氣厚隔遠故不熱也；日午熱而不大者，地上氣淺故不大，氣淺易透故熱也。又日初出，光切地圜之界，九輕故不熱；日午，光直射地平，力重故熱。日初出，人目力橫視遠故大；日午，人目力上視短故小。
○ Wei Pu also spoke of this: the sky is all qi, and as the waters of rivers and seas float it, when measurements are taken across them, the computed images strung together are all crooked. Put a coin in a bowl and someone standing at a distance cannot see it; pour in water to fill the bowl, and the coin floats up to the surface of the water. This is the same principle by which, before the sun rises, its light floats on the water, and when it first rises it is not hot. Dong Hanyang (Gu) of Ganchuan said: from Jingkou to Guazhou the river is over thirteen hundred oar-strokes wide, and one oar-stroke goes no more than three chi, making four hundred zhang; in the middle is Jinshan, a hundred and fifty zhang wide, so the water to north and south together is two hundred and fifty zhang, and a pontoon bridge could be made of some eighty boats, each two zhang wide with a space of one zhang between, boarded over for passage, which should be named the Bridge of the Rivers Paying Court for Ten Thousand Years. Long ago, when the Song took the Southern Tang, they moored a pontoon bridge at Caishi, and though it was temporary, it shows that it can be done. The Gazetteer of the World says the river at Jingkou is forty li wide. Cai Kuanfu said: Guazhou is in the middle of the river. Now it is seventeen li wide. I recall that in the yihai year of Chongzhen a measurement gave more than two thousand zhang, because the light of the eye was overpowered by the light of the water, and since the eye, saturated with water, took the middle as the limit, there was still half left over. Looking long into the sky, too, the eye is shaken by the sun's light, and unless one is thoroughly concentrated and steady, how can one measure empty distances accurately? Xuan says: Qi can make things large, qi can make things visible, qi can make things near. When one sees someone on the river tacking against the wind, viewed from the other bank the boat seems already to touch the shore, yet for a long time it does not turn its sail; then one knows that the shore one sees is not the true shore. Again, if there is an islet in the river, seen from this bank the islet appears close to the other bank, at one-tenth of the distance; seen from the other bank it appears close to this bank, at one-tenth of the distance. From this we know that ten and one are the mean values, and so the method of calculating the distortion of clear and turbid air was established. But the floating qi above the earth is less by day and more by night, less vertically and more horizontally; with endless variation there is turbidity in the clear and clarity in the turbid, the turbid distortion small and the clear distortion large, and the essential thing is for the calculator to grasp it in his own mind. Zhongtong says: to measure distances one must use double gnomons, and only by measuring several times and then calculating can one avoid error. There must also be a tree or a house or some object on the far bank to serve as a mark before one can measure; otherwise the river bank is a single line with no fixed left or right, and it is no wonder that the result is inaccurate. The sun is large but not hot when it first rises because the earth's qi reflects it horizontally, hence large, and the qi is thick and the distance far, hence not hot; it is hot but not large at noon because the qi above the earth is shallow, hence not large, and shallow qi is easily penetrated, hence hot. Again, when the sun first rises its light grazes the boundary of the round earth, nine-tenths light, hence not hot; at noon its light shines straight down on the earth's surface with heavy force, hence hot. When the sun first rises the eye looks horizontally and far, hence it appears large; at noon the eye looks upward and short, hence it appears small.

《天類·光論 On Light》

1
○文饒曰：兩間變狀，皆氣光之所為。潛草曰：兩間之光，皆太陽之火也。黃帝經曰：天明則日月不明。蓋天凝其陽精為日，而月與星用之，萬物皆用之。說文：光從火在人上。人正用天之火種，而心正傳天之神光者也。以體質言之，火用為燈而發光；以理言之，未有燈火之前，光之理已具矣。愚者曰：光理貫明暗，猶陽之統陰陽也。火無體，而因物見光以為體，猶心無體，而因事見理以征幾也。晦夜昏黑，地雖遮日，空自有光；人臥暗室，忽然開目，目自有光，何訝虎梟貓鼠之夜視耶？氣凝為形，發為光聲，猶有未凝形之空氣與之摩蕩噓吸，故形之用止于其分，而光聲之用常溢于其餘，氣無空隙，互相轉應也。暄曰：氣本有光，借日火而發，以氣為體，非以日火為體也。故日火所不及處，虛窗空中皆有之，則餘映也。然映日之光黃，映火之光赤，固為從類；而月星光白，野磷光青，暗室光黑，則輾轉互映而遞減者也。水鏡玻璃能照物而不發光，木葉塔影能發光而不照物。目之神光具各種異色，從暗搖之而見，閉而搖之而亦見。可見無物不含光性，以氣為體，不專日與火也，日火皆氣也。中通曰：光小者不隔，而影有窮時，故徑寸之火，影不百丈；光大者雖隔，而影不敵光，故太陽之照，光能含地。
○ Wenrao said: The changing appearances between Heaven and Earth are all the work of qi and light. The Qian Drafts say: The light between Heaven and Earth is all the fire of the Great Yang. The Yellow Emperor's Classic says: When Heaven is bright, the sun and moon are not bright. For Heaven condensed its yang essence into the sun, and the moon and stars make use of it, as do all things. The Shuowen says: "Light" is composed of "fire" above "man." Man properly uses Heaven's seed of fire, and his mind properly transmits Heaven's spiritual light. Speaking of substance, fire is used in lamps and gives out light; speaking of principle, before there were lamps and fire the principle of light was already present. The Foolish One says: The principle of light runs through brightness and darkness, just as yang governs both yin and yang. Fire has no body, but takes as its body the light seen through things, just as the mind has no body but sees principle through events to find evidence of the incipient. In the dark of night, though the earth blocks the sun, the air still has light of its own; when a man lying in a dark room suddenly opens his eyes, his eyes have light of their own — why marvel that tigers, owls, cats and rats can see at night? Qi condenses into form and issues as light and sound, yet there is also the air of qi not yet condensed into form that rubs, stirs, breathes out and draws in with them; therefore the function of form stops at its own share, whereas the function of light and sound always overflows beyond it, for qi has no gaps and they transmit and respond to each other. Xuan says: Qi itself has light, which is brought out by the sun and fire; it takes qi as its body, not the sun or fire. So in places the sun and fire do not reach, in empty windows and in the air there is still light, which is residual reflection. Yet light reflected from the sun is yellow and light reflected from fire red, which follows their kind; while the light of the moon and stars is white, that of will-o'-the-wisps green and that of a dark room black, which are reflections passed on and on, diminishing by degrees. Water, mirrors and glass can reflect things but do not give out light; tree leaves and pagoda shadows can give out light but do not reflect things. The spiritual light of the eye contains all kinds of different colours, which can be seen by rubbing the eyes in the dark, and also seen by rubbing them with the eyes closed. It can be seen that there is nothing that does not contain the nature of light, which takes qi as its body and does not belong solely to the sun and fire; the sun and fire are both qi. Zhongtong says: When the light is small there is no obstruction, but its reach has a limit, so the light of an inch-wide flame does not reach a hundred zhang; when the light is large, even if obstructed, the shadow cannot match the light, so the shining of the sun is such that its light can envelop the earth.

《天類·轉光 Redirecting Light》

1
○日射地上之水，或置鏡及放光石使火照之，則光入于屋梁。今術家使人見光之法，亦暗懸一鏡于衣襟或袖口，列燈燭香煙于地，引人拜祝，燭照鏡光，搖鏡則光見于壁。或懸貓精與大金剛石，則能成五色光。萬歷戊午，老父在蜀，為閔夢得公讞一魔術，知其轉光梁上，射入暗室之鏡，使男女自照，鏡中見其前身，以惑人云。
○ When the sun shines on water on the ground, or when a mirror or a light-emitting stone is set out and fire made to shine on it, the light enters the roof beams. Today the method by which conjurors make people see light is likewise to hang a mirror secretly in the lapel or the cuff, set out lamps, candles and incense on the ground, and lead people to bow and pray; the candles shine on the mirror, and when the mirror is moved the light appears on the wall. Or they hang a cat's-eye and a large diamond, which can produce light of five colours. In the wuwu year of Wanli, when my late father was in Shu, he tried a case of sorcery for Lord Min Mengde and found that they redirected light onto the beam and shone it into a mirror in a dark room, making men and women look at themselves and see their former lives in the mirror, so as to delude people.

《天類·聲論 On Sound》

1
○目以水照，故物攝其中而見；耳有三門，故氣貫而留之乃聞。目與鼻口，皆當面直受之者也；耳乃旁貫中通者也。氣自有聲，空自生聲，惟耳攝而通之，惟心靜而知之。天以雷風為聲，地以竅穴為聲，皆陰陽之氣相摩蕩而不已者也，而人可知矣。暄曰：氣本有聲，故物擊物，氣擊氣，物擊氣，氣擊物，皆成聲；不相擊而氣自飛，亦有聲，特微耳。聲之始也，肺促其氣于喉，喉分于舌唇；鼓其氣于管，管出于孔。銃之爆于空也，雷之震于雲也，空谷之傳呼，眢並之遙語也，天鼓星裂，斧鳴戶響，地下樂作也，氣擊氣也。鐘之搖，弦之馳，杖揮空，蟬振翼，蚊聚蠅飛，蟋蟀切股，箭哮哨聒，石崖浪激，松下濤生，破欞吹息，委巷屧響，無之而非聲，則無之而非與物相擊，亦無之而非氣也。入于地穴，藏于甕中，倚于磬側，靜而聽之，皆有所聞；即萬籟俱寂，掩耳于深夜間，不使有聞，亦若有聞。蓋氣以自飛不定而復相遇也。中德曰：氣貫聲作，心亦有聲；默誦暗記，心亦有字。乃知想從心相，意從心音。中通曰：氣之成聲有二端：一相擊而成，于暄言之詳矣；一穿竅而成。然穿竅而不能成聲者，更有二端：或止獨窮，氣不能出入；或兩竅相穿，旁竅走氣，氣不能留。凡此，則氣不回環于其間，皆不成聲。故耳鼻之不如喉者，喉因上有鼻孔，內有氣管故耳。使氣管有走氣之處，而不專出入于會厭之關，則啞矣。不見簫管有損則不響乎？耳之聞聲者，聲入于左，必穿于則，聽在左，近不能辨，遠則覺也。今之治耳閉者，右耳閉，則右聲入于右，必穿于左，故左耳受聲，則聽在右；右耳受聲，以蔥貫左耳；左耳閉，則以蔥貫右耳。理必交互，可徵也。
○ The eye reflects by means of water, so things are drawn into it and seen; the ear has three gates, so qi passes through and is retained, and then there is hearing. The eyes, nose and mouth all receive directly, facing forward; the ear passes things through from the side and communicates with the inside. Qi has sound of itself, and emptiness produces sound of itself; only the ear draws it in and transmits it, and only the mind, being still, knows it. Heaven makes sound with thunder and wind, and Earth with hollows and holes; all are the qi of yin and yang rubbing and stirring against each other without cease, and so man can know it. Xuan says: Qi itself has sound, so when thing strikes thing, qi strikes qi, thing strikes qi or qi strikes thing, all produce sound; when nothing strikes and qi flies of itself there is also sound, only faint. As for the origin of the voice, the lungs press the qi into the throat, and the throat divides it at the tongue and lips; as for instruments, the qi is blown into the tube and comes out at the holes. A gun exploding in the air, thunder shaking in the clouds, a shout carried through an empty valley, distant voices in a dry well, the heavenly drum and bursting stars, an axe ringing and a door creaking, music sounding underground — these are qi striking qi. The swinging of a bell, the twanging of a string, a staff swung through the air, a cicada vibrating its wings, gathered mosquitoes and flying flies, a cricket rubbing its legs, the whistle of arrows and the chatter of pipes, waves dashing on cliffs, the surge in the pines, breath blowing through a broken lattice, clogs clattering in a lane — there is nothing that is not sound, so there is nothing that does not strike against things, and nothing that is not qi. Go into a cave in the ground, hide inside a jar, lean beside a chime-stone, and listen quietly, and you always hear something; even when all is silent, if you cover your ears in the depths of night so as to hear nothing, it still seems as if you hear something. For qi flies about of itself without rest and meets itself again. Zhongde says: When qi passes through and sound arises, the mind too has sound; when one recites silently and memorizes, the mind too has characters. So we know that "thought" (xiang) comes from the "image in the mind" (xin xiang), and "intent" (yi) from the "sound in the mind" (xin yin). Zhongtong says: There are two ways in which qi produces sound: one is by striking, which Xuan has explained in detail; the other is by passing through holes. But there are two further cases where passing through holes cannot produce sound: either there is only one dead-end hole, so the qi cannot go in and out; or two holes pierce each other and the side hole lets the qi escape, so it cannot be retained. In all these cases the qi does not circulate within, and so no sound is produced. The reason the ears and nose are not like the throat is that the throat has the nostrils above and the windpipe inside. If the windpipe had a place where qi escaped, and qi did not go in and out solely through the gate of the epiglottis, one would be mute. Do we not see that when a flute or pipe is damaged it gives no sound? When the ear hears sound, sound entering the left must pass through to the [right], and the hearing is in the left; near at hand one cannot tell, but at a distance one notices it. In treating blocked ears today, if the right ear is blocked, then sound on the right enters the right and must pass through to the left, so when the left ear receives the sound the hearing is in the right; when the right ear receives the sound, a scallion is passed into the left ear; and when the left ear is blocked, a scallion is passed into the right ear. The principle must be crosswise; this can be verified.

《天類·聲異 Strange Sounds》

1
○遁齋閒覽言：歐公過高唐驛，聞空中人畜聲，父老云：「二十年曾晝過此，謂之海市。」酉陽雜俎言：掘井，聞下車馬人物喧哄聲。又公敘弟宿福清紫微院，夜聞讙呼聲，皆是浙音，人謂之鬼市。曹能始名勝志云：蜀中江縣寧國寺有響壁，若人處者按手而應，則絲竹管絃聲達于外。太姥有空谷傳聲處，每呼一名，凡七聲和之。老父以問壇石熊公，公曰：「峽石七曲也。」人在雪洞，其聲即有餘響；若作夾牆，連開小牖，則一聲亦有數聲之應；層樓檻內門窗紙上大小破隙，則風來作絲竹之音；若高山日暮，聞城市之喧聲，以日氣斂而人靜聽也。愚嘗江上晡出三山峽，即聞魯港、鳩茲之人聲，風順夜靜，則山頭聞百里，不為奇矣。暄曰：荒谷傳聲，甕裏藏聲，兩者一理也。凡地遠者，聲下則聽上，中隔則聲左而聽右，風順則聲近而聽遠，空中有聲，所謂傳也。泰西有益耳遠聽法，與遠鏡同功。中德曰：狗夜聽最遠，以得地氣也。逐虎者以邊鼓覆地雷之，其聲入地穴而愈震也。中通曰：地中有穴，地上之聲悉藏之。成南塘以大甕覆人，聽鑿地道，或以竹筒貫地穴而耳之。
○ The Leisurely Reading of the Dun Studio says that when Lord Ouyang passed the Gaotang post station he heard the sounds of people and animals in the air, and the elders said: "Twenty years ago it passed by here once in daylight; it is called a sea-market mirage." The Miscellany of Youyang says that when a well was dug, the clamour of carriages, horses and people was heard below. Further, when Lord [Ouyang]'s younger brother Xu stayed at the Ziwei Cloister in Fuqing, he heard shouting at night, all in the accent of Zhe, and people called it a ghost market. Cao Nengshi's Gazetteer of Famous Sites says that at the Ningguo Monastery in Jiang County in Shu there is an echoing wall, which responds when someone like a resident presses it with his hand, so that the sound of strings and pipes carries outside. At Mount Tailao there is a place where an empty valley carries sound: each time one calls a name, seven voices answer it. My late father asked Lord Xiong Tanshi about this, and he said: "The gorge rock has seven bends." When a person is in a snow cave his voice has lingering echoes; if a double wall is built with a row of small windows, a single sound will be answered by several; when there are large and small holes in the paper of the doors and windows inside the balustrades of a multi-storey building, the wind coming through makes the sound of strings and pipes; and if on a high mountain at dusk one hears the din of a city, it is because the sun's qi has drawn in and one listens quietly. Once, going out of the Sanshan Gorge on the river in the late afternoon, I heard the voices of people at Lugang and Jiuzi; when the wind is favourable and the night still, hearing a hundred li from a mountaintop is nothing remarkable. Xuan says: A desolate valley transmitting sound and a jar storing sound are one principle. Wherever the ground is distant, if the sound is low it is heard high; if there is an obstruction in between, the sound is on the left but heard on the right; if the wind is favourable, the sound is near but heard far; there is sound in the air — that is what is called transmission. In the Far West there is a method of aiding the ear to hear at a distance, which works like the telescope. Zhongde says: dogs hear farthest at night, because they get the qi of the earth. Tiger-hunters lay a side drum face-down on the ground and beat it like thunder, and the sound goes into the holes in the ground and shakes all the more. Zhongtong says: when there are hollows in the ground, sounds above the ground are all stored in them. Qi Nantang put men under large jars to listen for the digging of tunnels, or passed bamboo tubes into holes in the ground and put his ear to them.

○私鑄者匿于湖中，人猶聞其鋸銼之聲，乃以甕為甃，累而牆之，其口向內，則外過者不聞其聲，何也？聲為甕所取也。暄曰：廣孝曾用此法造器械。又燒空瓦枕，就地枕之，可間數十里外軍馬聲。
○ Illicit coiners hid out in the middle of a lake, but people could still hear the sound of their sawing and filing; so they lined a pit with jars, piling them up into a wall with their mouths facing inward, and then passers-by outside could not hear the sound — why? Because the sound was taken up by the jars. Xuan says: Guangxiao once used this method to make weapons. Also, if a hollow tile pillow is fired and laid on the ground as a pillow, one can hear the sound of troops and horses several dozen li away.

《天類·同聲相應之徵 Evidence that Like Sounds Respond to Each Other》

1
○夢溪筆談曰：友有琵琶，以管色奏雙調，則琵琶有聲應之，以為異物，殊不知乃常理。二十八調，但有聲同者即應；若編二十八調而不應，則是逸調也。古法一律七音，共八十四調，更細分之，逸調至多，偶見其應，便以為奇耳。智按：洛鐘西應，即此理也。今和琴瑟者分門內外，外彈仙翁，則內弦亦動。如定三弦子為梅花調，以小紙每絃帖之，旁吹笛中梅花調一字，此絃之紙亦動。曹師夔鑢磬不應鐘，猶之茂先知銅山崩也。聲音之和，足感異類，豈誣也哉？中通曰：通在高座殿上，以足頓地而鼓有聲，拍掌則鼓不應，因悟曰：亦各從其類也。
○ The Brush Talks from Dream Brook says: a friend had a pipa, and when the shuangdiao mode was played on a wind instrument, the pipa sounded in response, and he took it for something strange, not knowing that this is the normal principle. Among the twenty-eight modes, whatever has the same sound responds; if one plays all twenty-eight modes and it does not respond, it is a lost mode. In the ancient method each pitch had seven notes, eighty-four modes in all, and divided more finely the lost modes are very many; people happen to see a response and think it marvellous. I, Zhi, note: the bell of Luo responding to the west is this very principle. Today those who tune the qin and se distinguish inner and outer: when the "Immortal Old Man" is played on the outer, the inner strings also move. 《天類·五音七調 The Five Notes and Seven Modes》

1
○愚者嘗云：周禮享廟不用商樂，燕樂二十八調無征，有疑者否？段安昌以平上去入紀之，末有上平聲調，則為徵聲紀之，曰商角同用，而宮逐羽音，不可推耶？宮、商、角、變、徵、羽、閏，乃旋宮之七也。遼史四旦各有七調，今時簫笛七調，其此遺乎？沈存中、蔡元定所言合字、四字、一字、上字、工字、尺字、凡字、五字，各有高字，約為十五聲，其概也，詳見通雅。中通曰：七調者，淒涼調、凡字調、閉工調、正宮調、乙字調、梅花調、頂調也。十三調者，黃鐘調、正宮調、大石調、小石調、仙呂調、中呂調、南呂調、雙調、越調、商調、商角調、般涉調、子母調也。以高下分調者，直調也；以曲名分調者，橫調也；而不離乎五音。故簫笛南曲隔五必合，如合、四、上、尺、工、六、五，而合與六同孔，四與五同孔也；用乙字、凡字，則成北調矣。然調中之高下亦有直有橫：如合與六、四與五本一孔，而因氣之緩急分高下者，此橫高下也；正宮之四即乙調之合，乙調之四即梅花之合，本一字而因孔之升降分高下者，此直高下也。正如琴之十三徽為橫，七弦為直耳。至于曲名分調，有階級升降循次而轉者，有遠近升降隔三而轉者，有高字多而低字少者，有低字多而高字少者，有急者，有緩者，此雖橫調，亦未嘗不因高下而分也。始知聲音之理，無出于清濁高下升降緩急之外者，同符河洛，音本天然，不過隨時安名字耳，又何疑乎今樂非古樂哉？如以本黃鐘旋十二律而言，宋燕樂以夾鐘清聲起旋，故宮角互稱；今之十三調，乃二十八調之遺，而名字又改矣。
○ The Foolish One once said: the Rites of Zhou do not use shang music in temple sacrifices, and the twenty-eight modes of banquet music have no zhi — has anyone doubted this? Duan Anchang recorded them by the level, rising, departing and entering tones, and at the end there is a mode of the upper level tone, which records the zhi note; he said that shang and jiao are used together and gong follows the yu notes — can this not be inferred? Gong, shang, jiao, bian, zhi, yu and run are the seven of the rotating tonic. The History of the Liao has seven modes for each of the four dan; are the seven modes of today's xiao and flute the survival of these? What Shen Cunzhong and Cai Yuanding said about the notation characters he, si, yi, shang, gong, che, fan and wu, each with its high form, making about fifteen notes, is the general outline; see the Comprehensive Elegance for details. Zhongtong says: The seven modes are the qiliang mode, the fan mode, the bigong mode, the zhenggong mode, the yi mode, the meihua mode and the ding mode. The thirteen modes are the huangzhong, zhenggong, dashi, xiaoshi, xianlü, zhonglü, nanlü, shuang, yue, shang, shangjiao, banshe and zimu modes. Dividing modes by high and low gives the vertical modes; dividing modes by the names of tunes gives the horizontal modes; and neither departs from the five notes. Thus in southern tunes on the xiao and flute, notes five apart must coincide: of he, si, shang, che, gong, liu and wu, he and liu share a hole and si and wu share a hole; if yi and fan are used, they become northern modes. But within a mode high and low are also either vertical or horizontal: where he and liu, si and wu are originally one hole but are distinguished as high and low by the slackness or urgency of the breath, this is horizontal high and low; where the si of zhenggong is the he of the yi mode and the si of the yi mode is the he of meihua, the same character being distinguished as high and low by the rising and falling of the holes, this is vertical high and low. It is just as on the qin the thirteen studs are horizontal and the seven strings vertical. As for dividing modes by tune names, some ascend and descend step by step in turn, some ascend and descend at a distance, turning every third, some have more high notes than low, some more low notes than high, some are fast and some slow; although these are horizontal modes, they too are always distinguished by high and low. Then one realizes that the principle of sound does not go beyond clear and muddy, high and low, rising and falling, fast and slow, tallying with the River and Luo diagrams; sound is originally natural, and names are merely assigned according to the times — why then doubt that today's music is not ancient music? If one speaks of rotating the twelve pitches from huangzhong as the base, the Song banquet music began its rotation from the clear note of jiazhong, so gong and jiao were called by each other's names; today's thirteen modes are the survival of the twenty-eight modes, and their names have changed again.

《天類·樂節 Marking the Measures of Music》

1
○愚者嘗因版魚而悟柷敔。舊註泥合止而限三聲，其訛且數千年矣。房庶曰：「木，柷敔也。」而貫之為版，非明征耶？泠州鳩曰：「木音直。」正謂可以節樂。均鐘木亦以絃可高下移就，審中節也。鼓雖隱雷，其槌合節，或露版以相錯。琴有入慢，正謂過曲轉慢，猶曲中之賺也。但任冷衍，則短長走版之清曲耳。古用瑟笙，蓋笙呼吸皆得聲，瑟惟指點，不用吟猱，最易學也。瑟實以配琴，笙實以和管篪，豈有不合止於版眼而可聽者哉？
○ The Foolish One once realized the meaning of the zhu and yu from the wooden fish clapper. The old commentaries stuck to their being used to start and stop the music and limited them to three strokes, an error that has lasted several thousand years. Fang Shu said: "Wood means the zhu and yu." And its being strung together into clappers — is that not clear evidence? Lingzhou Jiu said: "The sound of wood is straight." That is precisely to say that it can mark the measures of music. The wood of the pitch-tuner is also moved with the strings to make them higher or lower, to determine the right measure. Although the drum is a muffled thunder, its sticks keep to the measure, or a clapper is shown to alternate with it. The qin has "entering slow," which means slowing down on passing to another tune, like the zhuan in a song. If one just lets it run on coldly, it becomes a pure song that runs off the beat with lengths uneven. In antiquity the se and sheng were used, because the sheng sounds on both breathing in and out, and the se is merely plucked with the fingers without vibrato or slides, so they are the easiest to learn. The se in fact accompanies the qin and the sheng in fact harmonizes with the pipes and flutes — how could anything that does not fit the beats of the clapper be worth hearing?

1
○程子謂中聲，蓋謂商與角之閒、角與征之間也。椒山謂中聲，謂定一中聲，而高下轉旋十五字更不外犯，乃得何調也。與齋謂中聲，謂絃雖在彈，聲實先起于七徽，而後上下從之也。愚者曰：皆有其故。然必從臨岳至龍齦，四五徽之仙翁，猶九十徽之仙翁也，而急緩從上而下有分矣。辟天必東升西旋，命門在中，而必左升右降為順也。

When Jiaoshan spoke of the central sound, he meant fixing one central sound such that the fifteen characters turning high and low no longer stray outside it, and so one can tell which mode it is. When Yuzhai spoke of the central sound, he meant that although the strings are being plucked, the sound really arises first at the seventh stud, and then the higher and lower follow it. The Foolish One says: each has its reason. 1

○ Master Gao said: Of the notes that can be marked going from low to high, on bamboo instruments one gets seventeen or eighteen characters and stops, while on stringed instruments one can get thirty or forty. Mother and child notes answer each other and can be substituted, yin and yang encircle each other, and the principle is extremely subtle. Stranger still, going up step by step, only when one step is left out does a mode form; and northern tunes, where the southern ones leave out a step, may add one step and also form a mode. This is natural and cannot be forced. It is like a staircase, where one must skip a step to fit. To sum up, all speech is likewise. Liu Yongqian said: the qin has twenty-four positions in all, and if one stud is not used there are a hundred and sixty-eight notes and forty-nine harmonics, and with the open strings and the eight sounds of plucking and brushing there are two hundred and twenty-five notes in all; removing those that coincide, forty-eight notes are complete. The eighteen characters of the flute can also be divided into forty-eight. As for the modes, they are spoken of by turning from he. Zhongtong says: Today's seven modes, from the lowest to the highest, comprise nineteen characters in all, but those above high gong are mostly not used, so the lowest is the fan mode, which uses only thirteen characters: he, si, yi, shang, che, gong, fan, liu, wu, yi, high shang, high che and high gong. Rising gradually, each mode higher drops one character at the bottom and adds one at the top. The six holes of the xiao and flute make six modes, and the last hole combined with the middle hole in front makes one more, so there are seven modes. Southern tunes close the holes for yi and fan; if they are used, they correspond to northern tunes.

1
○五六十二，禮運表之，和而月生，貴十二之旁羅也。參兩會于六而兩之，又四破之，各三也。十二辟卦配之。一陽一陰交而生二，陰律娶妻，呂生子，據黃蕤交而生林也，後此旋例矣。連起止則隔八也。十二餘四，猶用九餘三，度成一例，即寓一幾，何相廢邪？馬、班、京、蔡例參九也，李、瞿、信都芳例寒暑也。呂覽含少三寸九分，但以辟卦陽九陰六而數之，殆天符乎？黃鐘復，五陰為三十，一陽為九，非三寸九分乎？蕤賓姤，五陽為四十五，加一陰為六，非五寸一分乎？對貫九寸矣。大呂臨，四陰二十四，二陽十八，為四寸二分；對林鐘遁，四陽三十六，二陰十二，為四寸八分：亦九寸也。太簇泰，對夷則否，皆三陰十八，三陽二十七，為四寸五分，亦九寸也。夾鐘大壯，二陰十二，四陽三十六，為四寸八分；南呂觀，二陽十八，四陰二十四，為四寸二分：亦九寸也。姑洗夬，一陰六，五陽四十五，為五寸一分；無射剝，一陽九，五陰三十，為三寸九分：亦九寸也。中呂干五十四，應鐘坤三十六，亦九寸也。愚者曰：此恒配之端幾，如先天圖也；用之簫管，自有盈虛，如後天圖。中通曰：近見喻春山專此說候氣，如信都芳埋扇可推，然京、蔡之例亦不可廢，用必以簫管為徵。

One yang and one yin intersect and produce two; the yin pitch takes a wife and the lü bears a child — according to this, huang and rui intersect and produce lin, and after this they rotate by rule. 《天類·四行五行說 On the Four Elements and the Five Phases》

1
○問：「中國言五行，泰西言四行，將何決耶？」愚者曰：豈惟異域，邵子嘗言水火土石而略金木矣。地藏水火，分柔土、剛土，為土石也。朱隱老曰：四為體，五為用，金石同體，言金而石隱矣。周子尊水火在上，次表中土，下乃列金木焉。金木者，從土中生出者也。今所據者，地之五材也，金為土骨，木為土皮是也。水為潤氣，火為燥氣，木為生氣，金為殺氣，以其為堅氣也，土為衝和之氣，是曰五行。黃帝曰：六合之內，不離于五。既言五運，又分六氣，不參差乎？播五行于四時，非用四乎？易曰：一陰一陽之謂道。非用二乎？謂是水火二行可也，謂是虛氣實形二者可也。虛固是氣，實形亦氣所凝成者，即是一氣而兩行交濟耳，又況所以為氣而宰其中者乎？神不可知，且置勿論，但以氣言：氣凝為形，蘊發為光，竅激為聲，皆氣也，而未凝未發未激之氣尚多，故概舉氣形光聲為四幾焉。楞嚴七大，地水火風空見識也。地水火風之四大，猶之水火土氣也。有四實則有四空，實皆空所為也，而猶有容餘之空，故表空焉。皆因人目之見而顯，見本于識而藏于識，故表見識焉。心藏神而主性，腎藏精而主命，以見識表之，亦可悟五髒六腑之實是二行矣。若欲會通，正當合二求一，而後知一在二中，五謂之五六無不可者。且請學易。
○ Someone asked: "China speaks of five phases and the Far West of four elements; how shall we decide between them?" The Foolish One says: Not only foreign lands — Master Shao spoke of water, fire, earth and stone and left out metal and wood. The earth stores water and fire, and is divided into soft earth and hard earth, which are earth and stone. Zhu Yinlao said: Four is the substance and five the function; metal and stone are of one substance, and when metal is mentioned stone is implicit. Master Zhou honoured water and fire as above, next set out earth in the centre, and below listed metal and wood. Metal and wood are things that grow out of the earth. What we now rely on are the five materials of the earth: metal is the bones of the earth and wood is the skin of the earth. Water is moistening qi, fire is drying qi, wood is life-giving qi, metal is killing qi — because it is hard qi — and earth is the qi of harmonious blending; these are called the five phases. The Yellow Emperor said: Within the six directions nothing departs from five. Yet having spoken of the five circulations, he further divided six qi — is this not inconsistent? Distributing the five phases among the four seasons — is this not using four? The Changes says: One yin and one yang are called the Way. Is this not using two? One may call them the two phases of water and fire, or one may call them the two of empty qi and solid form. The empty is certainly qi, and solid form too is formed by the condensation of qi; it is just one qi whose two phases interact — and how much more so that which makes qi and governs within it? Spirit cannot be known, so let us set it aside and speak only of qi: qi condenses into form, issues from within as light, and is stirred in hollows into sound — all are qi, and there is still much qi not yet condensed, issued or stirred; so in general I take qi, form, light and sound as the four incipiences. The seven great elements of the Surangama are earth, water, fire, wind, space, seeing and consciousness. The four great elements of earth, water, fire and wind are like water, fire, earth and qi. Where there are four solids there are four voids; the solids are all made by the void, yet there is still a void left over that contains them, so space is set out. Xuan says: The sun can produce the light of its own body, which is what is said to be as large as half a degree; it can produce the light of direct illumination, where the sun's rays strike, near the body and hot; and it can produce the light of mutual reflection, where the sun's rays do not reach, as under the eaves of houses and within dense clouds, and in the morning glows and evening haze reflecting back and forth. What makes the sun's light is fire. The qi of fire always disperses; Heaven being a round body, it cannot disperse, so it must turn following Heaven to converge at the opposite pole, and in the middle it also turns embracing the earth to converge at the front, like water flowing round a pillar-rock and joining behind it. I once watched at sunset how its rays shot through the qi from west to east, embracing the earth like a ring-bridge, and then knew that its rays do not travel straight but can bend to follow things, and cannot be taken by straight lines. The earth's diameter is 28,636 li 36 zhang; the sun, calculated at half a degree, has a diameter of 138,248½ li, more than four times larger than the earth, so light is fat and shadow thin as a matter of course. When the light is smaller than the object, the light is still fat and still cannot be taken by straight lines. All light through cracks contains the whole body of the sun, so light from a small hole falling on the ground in a square place always gives a round shape. Some say that when the sun is half covered in the sky, the images of all the holes are likewise half covered. If one reflects the leak in a basin of water, or fits a telescope into the leak and draws on paper to observe a solar eclipse, the minutes and seconds of the eclipse all take shape — from which one may know that light envelops everything however large and shows everything however small, and overflows the measure of things. Calculated by the volume of spheres, it is a hundred and sixty times the earth, yet its diameter is less than two hundred thousand li, so the theory of half a degree can be free of doubt. Some say that a telescope enlarges objects within a hundred-odd paces several times over; the crystalline heaven is over sixteen million li away and reflects layer upon layer with the qi that fills the sky, so half a degree is still a distant reflection — how do we know that its true body is not even smaller than the earth's diameter? Zhongtong says: The stars and the moon are all water in substance, and water can draw things in, so the stars and moon draw in the sun within their bodies and produce light. The Far West says that a degree of the sun's heaven is two hundred thousand li or several tens of thousands of li, and that the body of the sun is ten-odd degrees or half a degree: they have never settled it. I hold that Heaven can be measured in degrees but not in li; measuring it in degrees, the size of a degree need not be discussed. Dividing Heaven into degrees, the theory of half a degree is right; but knowing that it occupies half a degree of the sun's own heaven, one still cannot compare it with the earth. I once measured it by the ratio at which shadows vanish: with the earth's diameter thirty thousand li, its shadow should vanish at twelve million li, and the surface of the earth is over 13,510,000 li from the edge of the sun, so the earth's shadow does not even reach the sun's heaven — how much less the heavens of the stars?

《歷類·左右一旋說 On the Single Rotation, Left or Right》

1
○問：「左旋右旋何決耶？」愚者曰：仲默註尚書，紫陽註十月之交，說之皆可者也。高皇舉一星以視月，月漸遠于星，然同行而一疾一徐，謂徐者右行，疾者左行，此亦說之可合而不遂決者也。天左旋，日月五星右旋，自人北面言之也。日月星從西向東，其周天各有遲疾；宗動天從東向西，一日一周。或曰歷家取捷，是亦一說也。西法分為九層，各為一種行法，乃可立算。間嘗窮之：黃帝運氣，左升右降，自人南面而言也。東向西行，其天之習氣乎？其定理乎？日月星在一氣之中，各有熟路，丸滾于槽，槽西行急，則槽中之圜物自然東轉，而實西旋順天，但積差之度見于星宿，似乎不與天同而每退焉。其分遲速者，近地者疾，遠地者遲也。帶動之說，固無疑矣。暄曰：天從東而西，政亦稍有不及，較靜天從東而西，日夜不停，特以政較動天，則並為左旋，安得有右轉乎？
○ Someone asked: "How shall we decide between leftward and rightward rotation?" The Foolish One says: Cai Zhongmo's commentary on the Documents and Ziyang's commentary on "At the Conjunction of the Tenth Month" both give acceptable explanations. The Gao Emperor held up a star to watch the moon, and the moon gradually moved away from the star; they move together, one fast and one slow, and he said the slow one moves right and the fast one moves left — this too is an explanation that can be made to fit but does not settle the matter. Heaven turns leftward and the sun, moon and five planets rightward, speaking from the standpoint of a person facing north. The sun, moon and stars go from west to east, each with its own speed round Heaven; the primum mobile goes from east to west, one circuit a day. Some say this is a shortcut taken by the calendar-makers; that too is one view. The Western method divides it into nine layers, each with its own kind of motion, and only so can calculations be made. I have tried to investigate it: the Yellow Emperor's circulation of qi, rising on the left and descending on the right, speaks from the standpoint of a person facing south. Moving from east to west — is it the habitual qi of Heaven? Or is it a fixed principle? The sun, moon and stars are within the one qi, each with its familiar path, like balls rolling in a trough: when the trough moves west quickly, the round things in it naturally turn east, yet really they turn west following Heaven; only the accumulated difference in degrees appears among the stars and lodges, as if they did not move with Heaven but always fell back. As for the division into slow and fast, those near the earth are fast and those far from the earth slow. The theory of being carried along is certainly beyond doubt. Xuan says: Heaven goes from east to west, and the governors too fall slightly short; compared with the still heaven they go from east to west, never stopping day or night; it is only by comparing the governors with the moving heaven [that they seem to lag], so all turn leftward — how could there be a rightward rotation?

《歷類·兩種定極 Two Kinds of Fixed Poles》

1
○赤道之拱架三輪，是靜天之極也；黃道之拱架三輪，是定日月星經緯度之極也。半周天為一百八十二度半，而大統歷日自春分至秋分有空度，恒多八日；秋分至春分有隔度，恒少八日。足知日天之心與宗動左旋天之心不對，每過北八度。此義歷士鮮有明其解者，故須兩種定極以算之。
○ The three circles framed on the equator are the poles of the still heaven; the three circles framed on the ecliptic are the poles fixing the longitude and latitude of the sun, moon and stars. Half the circuit of Heaven is a hundred and eighty-two and a half degrees, but in the Datong calendar, from the spring equinox to the autumn equinox the sun has empty degrees and always takes eight days more, and from the autumn equinox to the spring equinox it has skipped degrees and always takes eight days less. This is enough to show that the centre of the sun's heaven is not aligned with the centre of the leftward-turning primum mobile, but is always eight degrees to the north. Few calendar-makers have understood the explanation of this, so two kinds of fixed poles are needed to calculate it.

《歷類·日月行度 The Motions of the Sun and Moon》

1
○日一日行三百六十五度全數，恒星天過一度；月一日行百五十三度，遲周天十二度，恒星天過十三度。月二十九日六時三刻合于日而同度，為朔；日照月側，為弦；十四日九時餘，日月對望而見全圜，曰望；日光在月上，其魂不見，為晦。日與天會，一年而多五日三時，曰氣盈；月與日會，一年而少五日七時三刻，曰朔虛。其月行疾、日行遲者，以歷算自東行西紀度也。月九行者，四方出入黃道者八，與同黃道者為九也。五星行度，詳老父歷書約。
○ In one day the sun travels the full three hundred and sixty-five degrees, while the heaven of the fixed stars goes one degree further; in one day the moon travels a hundred and fifty-three degrees, lagging the circuit of Heaven by twelve degrees, while the heaven of the fixed stars goes thirteen degrees further. In twenty-nine days six hours and three ke the moon joins the sun at the same degree, which is the new moon; when the sun shines on the side of the moon, it is the quarter; after fourteen days nine hours or so, the sun and moon face each other and the full disc is seen, which is called the full moon; when the sunlight is above the moon and its dark part is not seen, it is the last day. The sun meets Heaven in a year with five days and three hours over, called the surplus of the qi; the moon meets the sun in a year with five days seven hours and three ke short, called the deficiency of the new moons. That the moon moves fast and the sun slow is because calendrical calculation records the degrees as moving from east to west. The moon's nine paths are the eight going in and out of the ecliptic in the four directions, and with the one that coincides with the ecliptic they make nine. For the motions of the five planets, see my late father's Summary of the Calendar Treatises.

《歷類·宿天 The Heaven of the Lodges》

1
○二十八宿為恒星天，向以為與老天帖定，今因歲纏冬至之經星漸差，而乃知其自移也。其曰四分度萬分之一當二千五百分，今作二千四百二十五分，以七十五分與天者，郭守敬之法也。小餘日加二十八分者，黃石齋先生之法也。其曰星一日行三百六十七度，過周天一度，謂之日行一度者，指順天旋而算也。
○ The twenty-eight lodges are the heaven of the fixed stars, formerly thought to be fixed fast to the old heaven; but now, because the fixed stars at the winter solstice of the year's course have gradually shifted, we know that they move of themselves. The saying that the quarter-degree is ten-thousandths making 2,500 fen, now written as 2,425 fen with seventy-five fen given to Heaven, is the method of Guo Shoujing. Adding twenty-eight fen daily to the small remainder is the method of Master Huang Shizhai. The saying that the stars travel three hundred and sixty-seven degrees in a day, exceeding the circuit of Heaven by one degree, and calling it a movement of one degree a day, refers to calculation following Heaven's rotation.

《歷類·日月食 Eclipses of the Sun and Moon》

1
○蔡邕言側匿，許慎言朓朒，解以朔晦月見，此歷差耳。月質以日映為光，合朔日食，月質掩日也；望有月食，地毬之影隔日也。凡見月之處，見食皆同；而日食則異，其不當頂而斜迤者者，不見日食也，故分秒各別。此質測也。古人以占君相致儆者，類應之心幾也。中通曰：平行、相距、實引、交周、分時，各有算法。但日距限如在百度外，則日不食；月距黃緯小于並徑則食，若等者相切，大者不相涉，則月不食。
○ Cai Yong spoke of cenie and Xu Shen of tiao and nü, explaining them as the moon appearing at the new moon and the last day — this is only a calendrical error. The substance of the moon shines by the sun's reflection; at conjunction there is a solar eclipse when the moon's substance covers the sun; at full moon there is a lunar eclipse when the shadow of the earth's globe cuts off the sun. Wherever the moon can be seen, its eclipse is seen alike; but solar eclipses differ, and those for whom the sun is not overhead but slanting do not see the solar eclipse, so the minutes and seconds differ in each place. This is material measurement. That the ancients used them to divine about rulers and ministers and to give warning is the incipience of the mind responding to its kind. Zhongtong says: The mean motion, the distance, the true anomaly, the nodal cycle and the division of time each have their method of calculation. But if the sun's distance from the limit is beyond a hundred degrees, the sun is not eclipsed; if the moon's distance in ecliptic latitude is less than the sum of the radii it is eclipsed, if equal they touch, and if greater they do not meet, so the moon is not eclipsed.

《歷類·五星遲留伏逆 The Slowing, Stationing, Hiding and Retrograde Motion of the Five Planets》

1
○土周二十八歲，木周十二，火約二歲，各于大輪中跳為小輪，人在地視其上下則留，迤行則遲，避日則伏，如水車之旋，下西上東則逆也。金水繞日為小輪，乃日之餘體，隨日而轉耳。舊法詳歷書，積日為算耳，即泰西亦未推明其故也。暄曰：金水繞日為輪，確矣。水輪近日，故速于金。至火、木、土，又皆遇日對衝則遲留，以至于逆，近日則伏也。中通曰：兩閒惟日為陽，而月與五星皆陰，皆用日之光，而遇日正衝則避，及其遠則復來就之，辟如物滾槽中，槽西行急，則物反東轉，且有激而跳為小輪者。此喻奇哉！
○ Saturn's circuit takes twenty-eight years, Jupiter's twelve and Mars's about two; each leaps into an epicycle within its great circle, and to a person on earth viewing them, going up and down they are stationary, moving slantwise they are slow, avoiding the sun they are hidden, and like the turning of a waterwheel, going west below and east above they are retrograde. Venus and Mercury circle the sun in epicycles, being the residual body of the sun and turning with it. The old methods are detailed in the calendar treatises and are only calculations by accumulated days; even the Far West has not yet worked out the reason. Xuan says: That Venus and Mercury circle the sun as epicycles is certain. Mercury's epicycle is nearer the sun, so it is faster than Venus. As for Mars, Jupiter and Saturn, they all slow and station when they come into opposition with the sun, even going retrograde, and are hidden when near the sun. Zhongtong says: Between Heaven and Earth only the sun is yang, and the moon and five planets are all yin, all using the sun's light; when they meet the sun in direct opposition they avoid it, and when far away they come back towards it, like objects rolling in a trough: when the trough moves quickly west, the objects turn east instead, and some are jolted into leaping in epicycles. What a marvellous comparison!

《歷類·四餘 The Four Remainders》

1
○月自南溯北交黃道曰羅，其對即計，所謂龍頭、龍尾，內道口、外道口也。月行最遲之度曰孛。朔不及中成閏，而閏生氣，凡二十八年周，是歷行之算節也。宿曜經出于佛藏，而世以占命，則物物可以端幾配應言之耳。術家執之，世士不解，豈悟極數之深幾乎？
○ Where the moon crosses the ecliptic going from south to north is called Luo, and its opposite is Ji: these are the so-called Dragon's Head and Dragon's Tail, the inner path-mouth and the outer path-mouth. The degree where the moon moves slowest is called Bei. When a new moon falls short of the mid-qi there is an intercalation, and the intercalation produces a qi; the full cycle is twenty-eight years — this is the computational node of the calendar's course. The Sutra of the Lodges and Luminaries comes from the Buddhist canon, and the world uses it to tell fortunes; so things can be matched and made to correspond by their incipient beginnings. The diviners cling to it and scholars do not understand it — how could they grasp the deep incipience of exhausting number?

《歷類·出沒異 Differences in Rising and Setting》

1
○每日繞地一周，天下國土非同時出入也，東方先見，西方後見。東西相去七千五百里，則差一時；相去四萬五千里，則東方為午時，西方為子時。普天下時時曉，時時午，時時晡，時時黃昏，時時夜半，知之乎？
○ The sun goes round the earth once a day, and the countries of the world do not see it rise and set at the same time: the east sees it first and the west later. For places seven thousand five hundred li apart east and west, there is a difference of one double-hour; for places forty-five thousand li apart, when it is the wu hour in the east it is the zi hour in the west. Over all the world it is at every moment dawn, at every moment noon, at every moment late afternoon, at every moment dusk and at every moment midnight — do you know this?

《歷類·晝夜異 Differences in Day and Night》

1
○晝夜長短，由于日之出入；日行虛縮，由于南北極之出地高下。中國處赤道北，夏至近北，則晝長夜短；冬至近南，則晝短夜長；處赤道南者反是。北極之下，半年晝，半年夜，周髀已言之矣。
○ The length of day and night depends on the rising and setting of the sun; the sun's course being longer or shorter depends on the elevation of the North and South Poles above the earth. China lies north of the equator; at the summer solstice the sun is near the north, so the days are long and the nights short; at the winter solstice it is near the south, so the days are short and the nights long; for those south of the equator it is the reverse. Below the North Pole there are six months of day and six months of night, as the Zhoubi has already said.

《歷類·節氣異 Differences in the Solar Terms》

1
○日行赤道北，為此夏至，則為彼冬至；日行赤道南，為彼夏至，則為此冬至。此言瓜蒂、瓜臍之異也。若東西則出沒發斂以漸而異，半其九萬里，即差六時，彼為日交，則此為夜交，彼晨交，則此屬暮矣。
○ When the sun travels north of the equator, it is summer solstice here and winter solstice there; when the sun travels south of the equator, it is summer solstice there and winter solstice here. This is the difference between the stalk and blossom-end of the melon. East and west, rising, setting, spreading and contracting differ gradually; at half of ninety thousand li there is a difference of six double-hours, so that when there it is the turn of day, here it is the turn of night, and when there it is the turn of morning, here it is evening.

《歷類·歷元 The Calendrical Epoch》

1
○舊說日月五星行四千六百一十七年，夜半冬至，其日月如合璧，五星如連珠，為歷元。三統歷二千三百六十三萬九千四十，為復于太極。大衍歷兼章微為日法，半之為天地出符數，三之為四千五百六十，當七精還初之會，又三三之為統，其概也。太乙積年至崇禎癸未一千零一十五萬五千五百六十，皆立法臆定者也。中通曰：歷家大小餘、歲差，皆不盡分數，有必不能齊者，少則不覺，積久差多，故歷未有數百年不改者。然改歷元，不必改歷法；即改日法，亦不改所以推測之法也。隨算隨測，除餘補縮，則時時可立歷元，而大歷元自在也。
○ The old theory was that after the sun, moon and five planets have travelled for 4,617 years, at midnight at the winter solstice the sun and moon are like joined jade discs and the five planets like strung pearls, and this is the calendrical epoch. In the Santong calendar, 23,639,040 [years] is the return to the Supreme Ultimate. The Dayan calendar combined the zhang and wei as the day unit; halved, it is the number of Heaven and Earth issuing their tallies; tripled, it is 4,560, matching the conjunction at which the seven luminaries return to their beginning; and three times three gives the tong — that is the outline. The accumulated years of the Taiyi system up to the guiwei year of Chongzhen are 10,155,560 — all fixed by conjecture in setting up the method. Zhongtong says: The large and small remainders and precession of the calendar-makers are all fractions that do not divide evenly, and some can never be made uniform; when small they are not noticed, but accumulated over a long time the differences grow, and so no calendar has gone for several hundred years without revision. But changing the calendrical epoch need not change the method of the calendar; and even if the day unit is changed, the method of reckoning and measuring is not changed. Calculating and measuring as one goes, removing the surplus and making up the shortfall, an epoch can be set at any time, while the great epoch remains of itself.

《歷類·開闢紀年 The Chronology from the Opening of the World》

1
○問：「開闢紀年有據乎？」曰：伏羲至舜一千二百三十年，夏四百五十八年，商六百四十年，周八百七十三年，秦四十九年，漢四百六十九年，魏一年，晉一百五十五年，宋、齊、梁、陳、隋一百九十八年，唐二百八十九年，後五代五十三年，宋三百二十年，元八十八年，明至崇禎戊辰二百六十一年。泰西曰開闢至伏羲元年甲辰一千七百四十年，彼以一樹證之，安知此樹何年生乎？佛言成住壞空，相輪迥而已，八十轆轤，未核也。邵子始以年月日時征出元會運世，以十二與三十相乘，則因關子明、僧一行而觸合之。鄧綺曰：以方圜積數至坤十之，亦十二萬九千六百之元數也；以通期自乘，亦元數也。是數之天符也。必以西言為征，則一會之說耳。
○ Someone asked: "Is there evidence for the chronology from the opening of the world?" I reply: from Fu Xi to Shun was 1,230 years, the Xia 458 years, the Shang 640 years, the Zhou 873 years, the Qin 49 years, the Han 469 years, the Wei 1 year, the Jin 155 years, the Song, Qi, Liang, Chen and Sui 198 years, the Tang 289 years, the Later Five Dynasties 53 years, the Song 320 years, the Yuan 88 years, and the Ming to the wuchen year of Chongzhen 261 years. The Far West says that from the opening of the world to the jiachen year, the first year of Fu Xi, was 1,740 years, and they prove it by a tree — but how do we know in what year that tree was born? The Buddha speaks of formation, abiding, decay and emptiness, wheels revolving in succession, nothing more; the eighty pulleys have not been verified. Master Shao first derived the cycles, epochs, revolutions and generations from years, months, days and hours, multiplying twelve and thirty, and so combined them with what Guan Ziming and the monk Yixing had touched on. Deng Qi said: Accumulating the numbers of square and round up to Kun and multiplying by ten likewise gives the cycle number of 129,600; and squaring the number of the full period likewise gives the cycle number. This is the heavenly tally of number. If one must take the Western account as proof, it is only the theory of one epoch.

《歷類·節度定紀 Fixing the Record of the Nodes and Degrees》

1
○大統歷、萬年歷每歲平分十五日餘為一節，新法春分後多二日，秋分後少二日，其二至則不殊，以日行嬴縮也。崇禎歷書春分後宿度有空度，秋分後宿度有共度，何耶？格致草曰：蓋歲實歷三百六十五日零三時，赤道正絡天腰，以子午為極；黃道斜絡，以亥已為極。黃赤二道之交為春秋分，南北二陸之盡為冬夏至。獨日天心北過宗動天心與地心四度，往來共過八度，則春分至秋分合一百九十日七時四刻，當以十五日零十時五刻為一節氣；秋分至春分合一百七十四日七時四刻，當以十四日零六時五刻為一節氣。偶閱萬年歷：丁亥五月二十日庚申巳正一刻夏至，十一月二十七日癸亥丑初一刻冬至；戊子五月初一日乙丑申正一刻夏至，十一月初八日戊辰辰初初刻冬至。用前法乘除為定本算之，每時八刻，以初一、初二、初三、初四、正一、正二、正三、正四八刻為准，每日九十六刻，非百刻也。今日纏箕三為冬至，儻從戊子冬至戊辰日辰初初刻，用前法乘除布算以推其後，雖百世可知也。西歷雖云密近，但圖象止四分九十度為三百六十度，少五度四分度之一，每日列宿度或空或共，即二十八宿刊度亦少五度四分度之一，何以齊年？今應照度加分，方無盈歉。如箕九度六十五分，斗二十三度八十分，牛七度十分，女十一度十五分，虛九度十五分，危十六度二十分，室十八度二十五分，璧九度十五分，奎十七度三十分，婁十二度二十分，胃十五度二十分，昴十一度十五分，畢十六度七十五分，觜五分，參十一度十五分，井三十一度三十五分，鬼二度，柳十三度十五分，星六度五分，張十七度二十五分，翼二十度三十分，軫十八度二十五分，角十二度二十分，亢九度六十五分，氐十六度七十五分，房五度六十分，心六度五分，尾十八度四十分：以上三百六十五度二十五分。蓋度率百分，平分黃赤之交，應春秋分前後各一百八十二日七時四刻，然日天心過北，則春分至秋分合一百九十日七時四刻，每十日應縮四十三分六釐大，共縮八度二十五分；秋分至春分合一百七十四日七時四刻，每十日應伸四十七分四釐少，共伸八度二十五分。必每十日明書差數，而後周歲之內列宿無十六度五十分之愆忒。彼一日一度與春分後空度、秋分後其度，爽然自失矣。近惟海寧朱康流、欈李陳肅庵皆事黃石齋先生，知歷法；山東薛儀甫究此廿年；六合湯聖弘好讀書，知授時歷，與黃俞邰善；建陽游子六因良孺熊公而推之，聞其褐塞研極不厭，終當一決。暄曰：日行于天，原無伸縮，惟以地應天，則中國偏居赤道北，北陸近其頂，故春分以後日高天闊，必多行數日，節氣始周其半；秋分後天稍仄，少行數日，節氣亦得其半。非天有伸縮，以地均應之偏也。在他處又不然。
○ The Datong and Wannian calendars divide each year evenly into nodes of fifteen-odd days; the new method has two more days after the spring equinox and two fewer after the autumn equinox, while the two solstices are no different, because of the sun's speeding and slowing. In the Chongzhen Calendar Treatises there are empty degrees among the lodges after the spring equinox and shared degrees after the autumn equinox — why? The Draft on the Investigation of Things says: The tropical year is 365 days and 3 hours; the equator girdles the waist of Heaven squarely, with zi and wu as its poles; the ecliptic girdles it slantwise, with hai and si as its poles. The intersections of the ecliptic and equator are the spring and autumn equinoxes, and the extremes of the southern and northern paths are the winter and summer solstices. Only the centre of the sun's heaven lies four degrees north of the centre of the primum mobile and the centre of the earth, eight degrees in all going and returning, so that from the spring equinox to the autumn equinox is 190 days 7 hours 4 ke, and each solar term should be 15 days 10 hours 5 ke; from the autumn equinox to the spring equinox is 174 days 7 hours 4 ke, and each solar term should be 14 days 6 hours 5 ke. I happened to look at the Wannian calendar: in the dinghai year, the summer solstice was on the twentieth day of the fifth month, gengshen, at the first ke of the middle of si, and the winter solstice on the twenty-seventh day of the eleventh month, guihai, at the first ke of the beginning of chou; in the wuzi year, the summer solstice was on the first day of the fifth month, yichou, at the first ke of the middle of shen, and the winter solstice on the eighth day of the eleventh month, wuchen, at the zero ke of the beginning of chen. Calculating by the foregoing method of multiplication and division as the fixed basis, each double-hour has eight ke, taking the eight ke of beginning one, two, three and four and middle one, two, three and four as the standard, so that each day has ninety-six ke, not a hundred. Today the sun's position at Winnowing Basket 3° is the winter solstice; if one takes the winter solstice of the wuzi year, on the wuchen day at the zero ke of the beginning of chen, and lays out the calculation by the foregoing method of multiplication and division to project forward, even a hundred generations can be known. Although the Western calendar is said to be close, its diagrams divide ninety degrees into four to make three hundred and sixty degrees, five and a quarter degrees short; each day the degrees of the lodges are either empty or shared, and even the printed degrees of the twenty-eight lodges are five and a quarter degrees short — how can the year be made even? Now one ought to add fractions according to the degrees, and only then will there be no excess or deficiency. Thus: Winnowing Basket 9° 65′, Dipper 23° 80′, Ox 7° 10′, Girl 11° 15′, Void 9° 15′, Rooftop 16° 20′, Encampment 18° 25′, Wall 9° 15′, Legs 17° 30′, Bond 12° 20′, Stomach 15° 20′, Pleiades 11° 15′, Net 16° 75′, Turtle 5′, Three Stars 11° 15′, Well 31° 35′, Ghost 2°, Willow 13° 15′, Star 6° 5′, Extended Net 17° 25′, Wings 20° 30′, Chariot 18° 25′, Horn 12° 20′, Neck 9° 65′, Root 16° 75′, Room 5° 60′, Heart 6° 5′, Tail 18° 40′: in all, the above make 365° 25′. For with a hundred fen to the degree, dividing evenly the intersections of the ecliptic and equator, there should be 182 days 7 hours 4 ke before and after the spring and autumn equinoxes; but since the centre of the sun's heaven lies to the north, from the spring equinox to the autumn equinox is 190 days 7 hours 4 ke, and every ten days there should be a contraction of a little over 43.6 fen, a total contraction of 8° 25′; from the autumn equinox to the spring equinox is 174 days 7 hours 4 ke, and every ten days there should be an extension of a little under 47.4 fen, a total extension of 8° 25′. Only by writing out the difference clearly every ten days will the lodges be free of an error of 16° 50′ within the year. Then their "one degree a day" and the empty degrees after the spring equinox and shared degrees after the autumn equinox will be shown up as mistaken. Recently only Zhu Kangliu of Haining and Chen Su'an of Zuili, who both served Master Huang Shizhai, know calendrical method; Xue Yifu of Shandong has studied this for twenty years; Tang Shenghong of Liuhe loves reading, knows the Shoushi calendar and is on good terms with Huang Yutai; and You Ziliu of Jianyang has worked it out following Master Xiong Liangru — I hear he researches it exhaustively in his coarse robe without tiring, and in the end it will be settled. Xuan says: The sun's course in Heaven has in itself no extension or contraction; it is only that when the earth is made to correspond to Heaven, China lies off-centre north of the equator, with the northern path near its zenith, so after the spring equinox the sun is high and the sky broad, and it must travel several more days before the solar terms complete their half; after the autumn equinox the sky is somewhat narrower, it travels several fewer days, and the solar terms likewise complete their half. It is not that Heaven extends or contracts, but the bias of making the earth correspond evenly to it. Elsewhere it is different again.

《歷類·南極諸星圖 Chart of the Stars around the South Pole》

1
○昔滿刺加國處赤道下，南北二極此地皆可測，春秋分日正麗天頂，冬夏至日距頂各二十三度有奇。彼人目境常見晝夜平，亦常見南北極麗天諸星，無一隱者。從閩廣南行六千里即其地，猶是中國閫閾，非可云六合之外存而不論也。故圖之缺西南二宮者，以各星距南極稍遠，西南二宮偶無星麗耳。若最後圜圖，老人星在鶉首，則南宮矣。其海石、金魚、飛鳥、小斗、附臼諸曜於南極甚近，近極星之度最細密，故十二宮皆有所麗，不得不為圜圖。智按：唐志有行海中見南極老人星下大星無數，朱子亦嘗引此矣。今隘者且疑海石、飛鳥、金魚、附臼為怪，何其不達耶？智又按：漢書藝文志載海中星占一卷，得無即此等星耶？當時或姑置之而不傳，以疑未決耳。
○ In the past, the country of Malacca lies under the equator, and from that place both the north and south poles can be observed; at the spring and autumn equinoxes the sun is right at the zenith, and at the winter and summer solstices it is a little over twenty-three degrees from the zenith. The people there regularly see day and night equal, and also regularly see all the stars that hang near the north and south poles, none of them hidden. It is reached by going six thousand li south from Fujian and Guangdong, and is still at the threshold of China; one cannot say of it that it lies "beyond the six directions, to be acknowledged but not discussed." So the chart lacks the western and southern palaces because the stars there are rather far from the south pole, and there happen to be no stars in the western and southern palaces. In the last round chart, Canopus is in Quail's Head, which is the southern palace. The Sea Rock, Goldfish, Flying Fish, Little Dipper, Mortar and other luminaries are very near the south pole, and the degrees of stars near the pole are the finest and densest, so all twelve palaces have stars in them, and a round chart was unavoidable. I, Zhi, note: the Treatise of the Tang records that those sailing in the sea saw countless large stars below Canopus near the south pole, and Master Zhu once cited this too. Today narrow-minded people even suspect the Sea Rock, Flying Fish, Goldfish and Mortar of being fabrications — how can they be so unenlightened? I, Zhi, further note: the bibliographic treatise of the History of the Han lists one juan of Star Divination at Sea — might it not be about such stars? At the time it was perhaps set aside and not transmitted because the doubts were not resolved.

《歷類·占最高法 The Method of Divination by Apogee》

1
○密綴所言五星行最高以定占。或曰：「萬國豈同此歲乎？」曰：萬國各就其國測之，則各國有各國之最高，猶之日出自遼東至甘肅則差四刻，歐羅于中國則日出差七時有餘矣。南方居赤道下者極熱，太陽南陸北陸一交其國，一歲兩度春秋。隋志云：北有煮羊脾而天明者。今推之，由煮羊脾之國再轉，則有日光不沒之國。智按：周髀言之矣，兩極之下，半年晝，半年夜，細推之，惟夏三月明，冬三月暗，春秋如黃昏。
○ What Mizhui says about divining by the apogee of the five planets. Someone said: "Do all countries share this same year?" I reply: If every country measures it for itself, then each country has its own apogee, just as sunrise differs by four ke from Liaodong to Gansu, and between Europe and China sunrise differs by more than seven double-hours. In the south, those living under the equator are extremely hot, and as the sun's southern and northern paths cross their country, they have two springs and autumns a year. The Treatise of the Sui says that in the north there are those for whom the day dawns in the time it takes to boil a sheep's shoulder blade. Inferring from this now, if one goes further on from the country of boiling the sheep's shoulder blade, there is a country where the sunlight never sets. I, Zhi, note: the Zhoubi speaks of this: below the two poles there are six months of day and six months of night; inferring more finely, only the three months of summer are light and the three months of winter dark, and spring and autumn are like dusk.

《歷類·天地大小皆有環列應機，分野非泥說也 Heaven and Earth, Large and Small, All Have Circular Arrays that Correspond to the Incipient; the Field Allocation Is Not a Rigid Doctrine》

1
○枝山前問曰：下洋兵鄧老向歷諸國，唯地上之物有異耳，天象大小遠近顯晦，雖遠國一切與中國無異。予因此知二十八舍分隸中土九州者謬也。然則一行兩戒何所取耶？泰西破分野矣，然知其一端，而不知又有一端也。潛草曰：環而列之，無不可以類配應幾者。以大地應天可，以中國應天亦可，以一郡應天亦可，以一室亦可。古人因此觸幾，其占分野，原非限定，如甲子干支亦用分野，可推矣。詳見周易時論。
○ Zhishan once asked: The old man Deng, a soldier who had gone down to the oceans, said that he had travelled through many countries and only the things on the ground were different; the size, distance, brightness and dimness of the heavenly phenomena, even in distant countries, were in every respect no different from China. From this I knew that assigning the twenty-eight lodges to the nine provinces of the central land is wrong. Then what is to be taken from Yixing's "two boundaries"? The Far West has refuted the field allocation; but it knows one aspect and not that there is another aspect. The Qian Drafts say: Arranged in a circle, anything can be matched by category and made to correspond to the incipient. It is possible to make the whole earth correspond to Heaven, possible to make China correspond to Heaven, possible to make one commandery correspond to Heaven, and possible with one room. The ancients grasped the incipient through this, and their field-allocation divination was never meant to be fixed; since the sexagenary stems and branches also use field allocation, this can be inferred.
