《風雷雨暘類 Wind, Thunder, Rain and Sunshine》

《風雷雨暘類·風征 Signs of Wind》

1
○陸機要覽：列子雲：立春歸八荒，立秋游乎風穴，風至則草木搖落，謂之離合風。古呼風之術有之，然列子御風，亦是寓言。括地志云：龍兌，音脫，有四風穴，洞之深而通氣者，類能生風，不足奇也。凡虛谷回峽而曲者，吹裂笛直聲則風起，長嘯之理即與此近。余遊雁宕大龍湫壁下，老僧云：「群拍掌呼風則風起。」果然。按風術，墨子云：以丁卯日或乙卯日燒三雉羽，揚其灰，風立至；若立止，則又燒黑狗皮，揚灰，風即起。萬畢術曰：焚雉羽可以致風。狼糞為烽燧，大風不折；江犬屯入火藥，逆風不回。駝知風與猦母事，別見。陸游曰：嶺表有瘴母，起時圜黑，久而漸廣，所謂孟婆颶母也。智按：說文颶當作風日，唐有風日海道，可證。泰西曰：太平海無颶風，滿加剌則無風。凡風之暴烈，乃峰島土竅激氣而發者也。王柏云：抱地旋轉而為風。此論其概耳。暄曰：凡勤即生風，張扇之鼓爐也。人行長巷，衣何以動乎？陰陽相驅，即有動氣。連房密室中，坐二門間，則冷氣襲人。此與拍掌吹笛可以類推。
○ Lu Ji's Essential Survey: the Liezi says that at the Beginning of Spring it returns to the eight wilds and at the Beginning of Autumn it roams in the cave of the winds; when the wind arrives the plants are shaken and fall, and it is called the parting-and-meeting wind. Arts of summoning the wind did exist in antiquity, but Liezi's riding on the wind is also an allegory. The Comprehensive Gazetteer says Longdui (pronounced tuo) has four wind caves; deep caves that let air through can generally produce wind, which is nothing strange. Wherever an empty valley and winding gorge bend, a piercing flute-like straight sound blowing through raises the wind; the principle of the long whistle is close to this. When I travelled to the foot of the cliff of the Great Dragon Falls at Yandang, an old monk said: "If everyone claps and calls for the wind, the wind rises." And so it did. As for wind arts, the Mozi says: on a dingmao or yimao day burn three pheasant feathers and scatter the ash, and the wind will come at once; if it stops at once, burn a black dog's skin and scatter the ash, and the wind will rise. The Ten Thousand Ultimate Arts says: burning pheasant feathers can bring wind. Wolf dung used for beacon fires is not broken by great winds; when the [?] of river dogs is put into gunpowder, it does not turn back against the wind. The camel's knowing the wind and the story of the wind-mother are given elsewhere. Lu You said: In Lingbiao there is the miasma-mother, round and black when it rises, gradually spreading over time; this is the so-called Granny Meng or typhoon-mother. I, Zhi, note: in the Shuowen, ju (typhoon) should be written "wind-sun"; in the Tang there was a "wind-sun sea route," which can be cited as proof. The Far West says: the Pacific Ocean has no typhoons, and at Malacca there is no wind. All violent winds are the qi stirred up and released by peaks, islands and holes in the earth. Wang Bo said: Wind is produced by circling round the earth. This speaks of it only in general. Xuan says: Whatever is active produces wind, like spreading a fan or working bellows. When a person walks down a long lane, why do his clothes move? When yin and yang drive each other, there is moving qi. In a closed room among connected rooms, if one sits between two doors, cold air strikes one. This can be inferred by analogy with clapping hands and blowing flutes.

《風雷雨暘類·風行遠近 How Far the Wind Travels》

1
○大塊噫氣為風，然孰為之噫？王柏曰：陽迫陰而出耳。春風上升，秋風下降，夏風橫行，冬風著土而吼穴，此以四時論行法也。夫春期何嘗不靡草，秋月何嘗不飛箏乎？風之高者，其來遠；風之下者，其來近：此以高下論遠近也。夫豈無一時而高下並至者乎？嗚葉者十里，鳴條者百里，搖大枝者五百里，仆大木者千里，折大木者五千里，此又以大小論遠近也。夫搖枝折木，亦視陽夾陰之甚不甚、發之暴不暴耳。狂候乍起曰風頭，豈無近者急而遠者緩乎？惟以時之久暫計遠近者，為僅得之。暄曰：風一日者五千里，一日夕萬里，二日夕天下半之，三日三夕天下盡風。然風之行亦有遲疾，微風飄忽，豈無日夕止百十里者乎？暄嘗于旴邸午炊，得小北風，逮夜及新豐，則一晚之間僅四五十里耳。中履曰：驗風送帆，已費風之力矣。朱子曰：勁風旋轉不停。以天行之疾言之，風亦有至疾者，何如天行之速耶？彼不疾而速者，又何物耶？氣凝為水而下流，與氣聚而騖之為風，自在差別中判。
○ The breath exhaled by the Great Clod is wind, but who breathes it out? Wang Bo said: It is just yang pressing on yin and coming out. Spring winds rise, autumn winds descend, summer winds move horizontally, and winter winds hug the ground and howl in holes; this discusses their manner of movement by the four seasons. But do spring winds never flatten the grass, and do autumn months never send kites flying? High winds come from far, low winds from near: this discusses distance by height. But are there never times when high and low come together? A wind that makes leaves rustle comes ten li, one that makes twigs whistle a hundred li, one that shakes large branches five hundred li, one that topples large trees a thousand li, and one that breaks large trees five thousand li: this discusses distance by force. But shaking branches and breaking trees also depend on how severely yang is squeezed by yin and how violently it bursts out. When a violent wind suddenly rises it is called the wind's head — are there not near ones that are fierce and far ones that are gentle? Only reckoning distance by the length of time comes close. Xuan says: A wind of one day travels five thousand li, of a day and night ten thousand li, of two days and nights half the world, and of three days and three nights the whole world is wind. But the wind's travel is also slow or fast; a light wind drifting about — are there not some that go only a hundred-odd li in a day and night? Once, cooking lunch at the inn at Xu, I caught a light north wind, and by evening it had reached Xinfeng, only forty or fifty li in an evening. Zhonglü says: Testing the wind by its carrying a sail already uses up the wind's force. Master Zhu said: A strong wind whirls without stopping. Speaking of the speed of Heaven's motion, there are also extremely fast winds, but how can they compare with the speed of Heaven's motion? And what is that which is swift without hastening? Qi condensing into water and flowing down, and qi gathering and rushing as wind — they are distinguished within difference.

《風雷雨暘類·南北風寒溫之異 Differences in the Cold and Warmth of South and North Winds》

1
○問：「千里不同風，吹噫一也，何以南風溫，北風寒？」天學曰：風屬于火，日為君火，地發燥熱，橫被直騖，從日而噓則為南風，君火之氣與風俱舒，故溫；從日而吸則為北風，君火既縮，而又吸動地面飄揚之氣，故寒。試觀人口噓氣則唇溫，吸氣則唇冷，理可類推。中國所處，日常在南，是以有噓吸之異。又問：「東風溫潤，西風高燥，何也？」曰：海氣在東，故溫潤；山氣在西，故高燥。又日以東方為來氣，以西為去氣，斯其所以異也。愚者曰：東生西殺，南溫北寒，此常理耳。暄曰：火死則吸，如閉火于瓶，加于人膚而不墜也。日君火有死時乎？同一氣耳，鼓之則涼，噓之則暖，吹之則寒；同一噓耳，近之則暖，遠之則寒。日在南，故南方為近日，北方為遠日。日在南，故風從南來者恒煖，風從北來者恆寒，非有噓吸之異也。
○ Someone asked: "Within a thousand li the wind is not the same, yet blowing and breathing are one; why is the south wind warm and the north wind cold?" Celestial Studies says: Wind belongs to fire, and the sun is the sovereign fire; the earth gives off dry heat, spreading horizontally and rushing straight; when it is breathed out from the sun it becomes the south wind, and the qi of the sovereign fire spreads together with the wind, so it is warm; when it is drawn in toward the sun it becomes the north wind, and the sovereign fire has contracted and moreover draws in the drifting qi of the earth's surface, so it is cold. Try observing that when a person breathes out the lips are warm, and when he breathes in the lips are cold; the principle can be inferred by analogy. Where China lies, the sun is always to the south, so there is this difference between breathing out and breathing in. He was further asked: "The east wind is warm and moist and the west wind high and dry — why?" He replied: The qi of the sea is in the east, so it is warm and moist; the qi of the mountains is in the west, so it is high and dry. Also, the sun takes the east as the coming qi and the west as the going qi; that is why they differ. The Foolish One says: The east gives life and the west kills, the south is warm and the north cold — this is the normal principle. Xuan says: When fire dies it draws in, as when fire is shut in a bottle and put on a person's skin, it does not fall off. Does the sun, the sovereign fire, ever die? It is the same qi: fanned it is cool, breathed out it is warm, blown it is cold; it is the same breathing out: near it is warm, far it is cold. The sun is in the south, so the south is near the sun and the north far from the sun. Since the sun is in the south, winds from the south are always warm and winds from the north always cold; it is not that there is a difference between breathing out and breathing in.

《風雷雨暘類·雨征 Signs of Rain》

1
○熊公曰：灶突發煙，平遠望之，亭亭直上，晴之飆也；蜿蜒而起，如欲上而不得者，雨征也。蓋雲將成雨，空中氣行皆成濕性，煙為濕礙，不得上升，故至宛曲。將雨礎潤，將雨燈爆，理可同觀。朝日出光黯淡、色蒼白者，雨征也。日出時雲多破漏、日光散射者，雨征也。密雲四布，牛羊齕草如常者不雨；若啖食匆遽，似求速飽，雨征也。蠅蚋蚤虻匆遽咂食，雨征也。蠉娟之屬倉皇飛騖，雨征也。穴處之蟲群出于外，雨征也。朔日至上弦，視月兩角，近日一角稍稍豐滿，雨征也。日暈白主晴，赤主風，色如鉛者，雨征也。潛老夫曰：古人舉之矣。京房易飛候曰：晦朔弦望，雲漢四塞，當雨；雲如水牛、黑群羊、飛鳥，五日雨；北斗獨雲，必雨；如兩人提鼓持桴，為暴雨。諺云：月暈風，日暈雨；東鱟晴，西鱟雨；下垂地曰白日幢；烏洞返塢，主晴光；日生兩耳，斷風截雨；日落胭脂紅，無雨亦有風；日落雲裏走，雨在夜半後；月弓多風；偃瓦自下；河作堰，女作橋，合羅陣，風雨飄；魚鱗不雨亦風顛；冬斑卻是護霜天。皆是實征，非數占之例。暄曰：月生齒，星芒角，滿天星搖動，天晦若低，小星白如欲墜，蟻蛭封，商鴹舞，魚浮水面，池塘綠泑，琴聲不清，鼓音不亮，日酷無風，背心獨熱，皆雨征。蓋雨者濕氣之行，故屬濕之像皆應之，而日酷背熱，則又水生于火、變濕之兆也。有曰：雲如車蓋十餘，乃陽火之氣，必暍而落；氣如煙，或青或黑或黃，亦陽火之氣也；云如魚鱗，名曰老鯉斑者，必暍。午晴候五更忽雨，日必晴；雨候清晨日出，必復雨；惟先雲而日漸出者晴，乃準也。中德曰：月令廣義：正月首十二日，每旦以瓦瓶秤水，重則雨多，輕則雨少。蓋水有潮候，月有變遷，此亦難決也。中通曰：當以冬至後數九秤而決之。中履曰：雲掩北斗日有雨，此以北陰占之。凡所云南耳晴，北耳雨；南閃千年，北閃眼前；雲往東，雨無蹤；雲往西，馬濺泥；雲往南，水漲潭；雲往北，好曬麥：皆以西南東北為斷。然又冬夏不同，南北之地亦不同，如自楚入豫，春東北風亦不雨；江南北先雷風則不雨；桂林每雨必先起風；黔多雨，滇多西南風。其類未可執一，各自有說。
○ Master Xiong said: When smoke rises from the kitchen chimney and, seen from afar on the level, goes straight up, it is a sign of fair weather; when it rises winding as if it wanted to go up but could not, it is a sign of rain. For when clouds are about to turn to rain, the qi moving in the air all becomes damp, and the smoke, obstructed by the damp, cannot rise, and so it winds. Before rain the plinth stones grow damp and the lamp sputters; the principle can be seen as the same. When the morning sun rises with dim light and a pale white colour, it is a sign of rain. When at sunrise the clouds have many breaks and the sunlight scatters, it is a sign of rain. When dense clouds spread on all sides and the cattle and sheep crop the grass as usual, it will not rain; if they eat hurriedly as if seeking to fill up quickly, it is a sign of rain. When flies, gnats, fleas and gadflies bite hurriedly, it is a sign of rain. When wrigglers and the like fly about in a flurry, it is a sign of rain. When burrowing insects come out in swarms, it is a sign of rain. From the new moon to the first quarter, looking at the two horns of the moon, if the horn nearer the sun is a little fuller, it is a sign of rain. A white halo round the sun portends fair weather, a red one wind, and a lead-coloured one is a sign of rain. The Old Man of Qian said: The ancients listed these. Jing Fang's Flying Signs of the Changes says: If at the last day, new moon, quarters or full moon clouds block the Milky Way on all four sides, it will rain; if there are clouds like water buffalo, black flocks of sheep or flying birds, it will rain in five days; if there is a lone cloud in the Northern Dipper, it will certainly rain; if it is like two men holding drums and drumsticks, there will be a rainstorm. Proverbs say: "A halo round the moon, wind; a halo round the sun, rain; a rainbow in the east, fair; a rainbow in the west, rain"; "hanging down to the ground, it is called the white-sun banner"; "if the crows' cave returns to the dock, it portends clear light"; "when the sun grows two ears, wind and rain are cut off"; "when the sun sets rouge-red, if not rain then wind"; "when the sun sets running into clouds, rain comes after midnight"; "a bow-shaped moon, much wind"; "tiles laid flat [clouds] come from below"; "when the river makes a weir and the maiden makes a bridge, and the net-formation gathers, wind and rain blow"; "fish-scale clouds, if not rain then a wild wind"; "mottled skies in winter protect against frost." These are all real signs, not of the order of numerical divination. Xuan says: When the moon grows teeth, the stars have rays and points, the whole sky of stars twinkles, the sky is dark as if low, small stars are white as if about to fall, ants seal their mounds, the shangyang bird dances, fish float on the surface, ponds turn green and scummy, the qin sounds unclear, the drum sounds dull, and the sun is fierce with no wind and one's back alone is hot — these are all signs of rain. For rain is the movement of damp qi, so all signs belonging to damp respond to it; and fierce sun and a hot back are a sign of water being born from fire and turning into damp. Some say: if there are a dozen or more clouds like carriage canopies, it is the qi of yang fire and they will vanish in the heat; qi like smoke, green or black or yellow, is also the qi of yang fire; clouds like fish scales, called "old carp spots," will vanish in the heat. If it is fine at noon and suddenly rains at the fifth watch, the day will certainly be fine; if after a spell of rain the sun comes out in the early morning, it will certainly rain again; only when clouds come first and the sun gradually comes out will it be fine — this is accurate. Zhongde says: The Extended Meaning of the Monthly Ordinances: on the first twelve days of the first month, weigh water in an earthen bottle every morning; if it is heavy there will be much rain, if light little rain. But water has its tidal periods and the moon its changes, so this too is hard to decide. Zhongtong says: One should decide it by weighing on the nine-day periods counted after the winter solstice. Zhonglü says: When clouds cover the Northern Dipper, there will be rain that day — this divines by the northern yin. All the sayings, such as "southern ears, fair; northern ears, rain; lightning in the south, a thousand years; lightning in the north, right before your eyes; clouds going east, rain leaves no trace; clouds going west, horses splash in mud; clouds going south, water fills the pools; clouds going north, good for drying wheat," judge by west, south, east and north. But winter and summer differ, and the lands of south and north also differ: going from Chu into Yu, a northeast wind in spring brings no rain; north and south of the Yangzi, if thunder and wind come first it does not rain; in Guilin every rain is preceded by wind; Qian has much rain and Dian many southwest winds. Such cases cannot be held to one rule; each has its own explanation.

《風雷雨暘類·雨異 Strange Rains》

1
○史載雨粟。博物志：南陽小都雨穀，如麥粟，味苦，大者如米豆，黃赤，味如麥，食之除心氣患。此他處吹來者。其載雨肉如肋，或載雨血，則似之而實非也。楮記室曰：至治元年雨鐵，則雷楔隕石之類耳。天啟癸亥，智隨家君都門，晝晦，雨血，以著衣而色赭也。
○ The histories record rains of grain. The Treatise on Broad Matters: at Xiaodu in Nanyang it rained grain like wheat and millet, bitter in taste; the larger kinds were like rice and beans, yellow and red, tasting like wheat, and eating them cured heart-qi ailments. These were blown here from elsewhere. As for the records of rains of meat like ribs, or of rains of blood, they resemble these things but are not really so. The Chu Jishi says: the rain of iron in the first year of Zhizhi was of the same kind as thunder-wedges and fallen stones. In the guihai year of Tianqi, when I, Zhi, was with my father in the capital, the day grew dark and it rained blood, which stained clothes a reddish-brown.

《風雷雨暘類·祈雨古法 Ancient Methods of Praying for Rain》

1
○董子言土龍祈雨之事，王彥威驗李玘以白蛇醫二甕、青衣小兒擊甕致雨之事。舊說有泥楓人而環呼蜥蜴者，此也。成式曰：蜴與蛇私為親家。智按：異物志曰：魚躍，蜥蜴從草中下，共浮水上而交，蓋與龍通氣也。蛇背傷，蜥蜴輒銜草傅之，或以口所含雹療之，故號蛇醫一說純黃色。潛山下九井，旱，殺犬投其中，遂大雨。暄曰：潭中有龍處，投鐵，雨立降。中通曰：或取即且、燕、蛙膏血菵草而投潭中，必大風雨，溢潭而後已。一曰：龍淵投虎骨，龍即起。
○ Master Dong spoke of praying for rain with earthen dragons, and Wang Yanwei verified Li Qi's bringing rain with two jars of white lizards and boys in blue striking the jars. The old account of making figures of maple-wood people and calling round to the lizards refers to this. [Duan] Chengshi said: Lizards and snakes secretly make in-laws of each other. I, Zhi, note: the Record of Strange Things says that when fish leap, the lizards come down from the grass and float together on the water and mate, presumably because they share qi with dragons. When a snake's back is injured, the lizard brings grass in its mouth to apply to it, or treats it with the hail held in its mouth, and so it is called the "snake-doctor" some say it is pure yellow. At the Nine Wells below Mount Qian, in a drought, a dog is killed and thrown in, and then there is heavy rain. Xuan says: Where a dragon lives in a pool, if iron is thrown in, rain falls at once. Zhongtong says: Some take centipedes, swallows and frogs, smear their fat and blood on lanyard grass and throw it into the pool, and there is always a great storm that overflows the pool before it stops. Another says: If tiger bones are thrown into a dragon's pool, the dragon rises at once.

《風雷雨暘類·雷說 On Thunder》

1
○陽在內而欲出，故聲。朱隱老注邵子書，言八種雷。本草載霹歷楔，治驚失心，殺蟧止洩，置箱辟蛀。陳蘇紹得雷錘，重九斤；宋沈括于震木下得雷楔，似斧而無孔。鬼神幽微，不可究極。愚者曰：此亦通理，因而曰皆心也，皆鬼神也，亦冒通耳。質測家曰：火挾土氣而上，急迫之際，火焚而上附天，土成澤而下。星隕為石，亦非星也。時珍言龍口有火光，皆非火也。燥鬱而氣旋，則為閃光，高峰視其下雷雨如漚發，非明征耶？燒樹及物，陽從直起，觸之則焚，焚則有焦，焦則如墨耳。雷書，劉恂以黳石當之。李肇言雷川多雷，秋則伏蟄，人掘地食之，妄矣，此自雷州近陽土中生物如封之類。暄曰：雷乃太陽之氣升于雲中，為陰氣所束，陽氣屬火，雲氣屬水，以水淬火，與相激爭，故發為震撼，而觸之者碎。以其陽氣，故春生秋滅，北方寒，有無雷之國，南方熱，有無日不雷之境也。或云雷有如烏、如豕、如猴諸異相，能殺人，此乃陽氣所生之物，乘陽而出，故渾身屬火，猶水中生鯨鱷，能殺人，人亦得以制之耳。有時夾于樹中，鋸而後拔；有時入于人室，不能遽出；有時伏地如彘，掘之可食。楊道和鉏折其肱，趙陁將與之相格，雷州民揮刀踣之，血流于地。服正遇之魚，佩羭次之羽，半石之草，皆得以卻之。遺楔如斧，固煙與火土結成者也。觸人作字，則煙暈汗流，黑晰相間者也。曾見三丈之內發無彈之藥銃，中人，人為火氣衝仆，其面黑汗成文，不可推耶？至于附氣靈應，神之格思，不可度思，茲且窮質測耳。
○ Yang within wanting to get out — hence the sound. Zhu Yinlao, annotating Master Shao's book, speaks of eight kinds of thunder. The Materia Medica records thunderbolt-wedges, which cure fright and loss of mind, kill worms and stop diarrhoea, and when put in chests ward off moths. Chen Su Shao obtained a thunder-hammer weighing nine jin; Shen Kuo of the Song found under a tree struck by lightning a thunder-wedge like an axe but without a hole. Ghosts and spirits are dark and subtle and cannot be fathomed to the end. The Foolish One says: This too is a comprehending principle, but to say from it that all is mind, all is ghosts and spirits, is a vague generality. The investigators of matter say: Fire carries the qi of earth upward, and at the moment of urgency the fire burns upward to attach itself to Heaven, while the earth becomes moisture and descends. When a star falls and becomes stone, it is not a star either. [Li] Shizhen says the dragon's mouth has firelight, but none of these are fire. When dryness is pent up and the qi whirls, it becomes flashing light; seen from a high peak, the thunder and rain below look like bubbles bursting — is that not clear proof? In burning trees and things, yang rises straight up and burns what it touches, and what burns is scorched, and what is scorched looks like ink. As for the "thunder writing," Liu Xun identified it with black stone. Li Zhao said that the Lei River has much thunder, which hibernates in autumn, and people dig it up and eat it — nonsense; this is just a creature like the feng that grows in the yang soil near Leizhou. Xuan says: Thunder is the qi of the sun rising into the clouds and being bound by yin qi; yang qi belongs to fire and the qi of clouds to water, and as water quenches fire and they clash with each other, it bursts out as a shock, and whatever it touches is shattered. Because it is yang qi it comes alive in spring and dies in autumn, and in the cold north there are countries without thunder, while in the hot south there are lands where not a day passes without thunder. Some say that thunder has strange forms like a crow, a pig or a monkey, and can kill people; these are creatures born of yang qi that come out riding on yang, so their whole bodies are fire, just as whales and crocodiles born in the water can kill people, yet people can also control them. Sometimes they are caught in a tree and can be pulled out only after sawing; sometimes they get into people's houses and cannot get out quickly; sometimes they lie on the ground like pigs, and can be dug up and eaten. Yang Daohe broke its arm with a hoe; Zhao Tuo fought with it; a man of Leizhou felled it with a sword and its blood flowed on the ground. The fish of Fuzheng, the feathers of the Yuci, the grass of Banshi can all ward it off. The axe-like wedges it leaves are certainly formed from smoke, fire and earth. When it strikes a person and makes characters, it is smoke-haze and flowing sweat, black and white intermingled. I once saw a gunpowder gun fired without a ball within three zhang strike a person, who was knocked down by the fiery qi, and the black sweat on his face formed patterns — cannot one infer from this? As for spirits attached to qi responding numinously, "the coming of the spirits cannot be fathomed"; here we only exhaust material measurement.

《風雷雨暘類·雹 Hail》

1
○史載雹毀瓦，如拳，如雞卵，三出，成獸。徐鉉稽神錄言雹與樓等。嘉靖戊戌，吳城雹開眼；西山雹如斗；天啟甲子，雹如人眼。凡雹治狂熱，正醬味，然無病吞之則疫。元光元年七月雹，董仲舒答鮑敞曰：雹，陰脅陽也。氣上薄為雨，下薄為霧，風其噫也，雲其氣也，雷其相擊之聲，電其光也。二氣初蒸，攢聚相合，風多合速，雨大而疏；風多合遲，雨細而密。寒月則雨凝于上，體因風相襲成雪。寒有高下，陰氣暴上，雨則凝結成雹焉，霰之流也。敞曰：「四月無陰，十月無陽，何以明不孤立耶？」董曰：所資一氣也，猶一鼎水，未加火，純陰也；加火極熱，純陽也。純陽無陰，息火，水寒則更陰矣；純陰無陽，加火，水熱則更陽矣。葶藶死于夏，款冬華於寒，水有溫泉，火有涼焰，陰非無陽，陽非無陰也。曰：「冬雨煖，夏雨涼，何也？」曰：冬氣多寒，陽氣多上躋，故人得其煖，而上蒸成雪矣；夏氣多煖，陰氣自下升，故人得其涼，而上蒸成雨矣。愚者曰：漢初董子早精此理，故載之。今以三際質測，急切成就，抱氣在中，故雹常具竅。
○ The histories record hail that broke tiles, like fists or hens' eggs, some three-pronged, some in the shapes of beasts. Xu Xuan's Records of Investigating the Spirits speaks of hail as big as a tower. In the wuxu year of Jiajing, hail at Wucheng had "eyes"; in the Western Hills hail was as big as a dipper; in the jiazi year of Tianqi hail was like human eyes. Hail cures manic fever and corrects the taste of sauces, but swallowed when one is not ill it causes epidemic disease. When it hailed in the seventh month of the first year of Yuanguang, Dong Zhongshu answered Bao Chang: Hail is yin coercing yang. When qi presses upward it becomes rain and downward it becomes fog; wind is its breath, clouds its qi, thunder the sound of their striking each other, and lightning their light. When the two qi first steam up and gather together, with much wind they combine quickly and the rain is large and sparse; with much wind they combine slowly and the rain is fine and dense. In the cold months the rain congeals above, and its body, buffeted by the wind, becomes snow. Cold varies in height; when yin qi suddenly rises, the rain congeals into hail — a kind of sleet. Chang said: "In the fourth month there is no yin and in the tenth month no yang; how can it be shown that they do not stand alone?" Dong said: What they rely on is one qi, like a cauldron of water: before fire is added it is pure yin; with fire added and extremely hot it is pure yang. Pure yang has no yin, yet when the fire is put out and the water becomes cold it becomes more yin; pure yin has no yang, yet when fire is added and the water becomes hot it becomes more yang. Draba dies in summer and coltsfoot flowers in the cold; water has hot springs and fire has cool flames: yin is not without yang and yang is not without yin. [Chang] said: "Winter rain is warm and summer rain cool — why?" [Dong] said: Winter qi is mostly cold, and yang qi mostly rises, so people feel its warmth, and it steams up and becomes snow; summer qi is mostly warm, and yin qi rises from below, so people feel its coolness, and it steams up and becomes rain. The Foolish One says: Master Dong at the beginning of the Han had already mastered this principle, so I record it. Now, by material measurement of the three zones, it forms in haste and holds qi inside, so hailstones always have holes.

《風雷雨暘類·霜雪 Frost and Snow》

1
○霜前冷者，陰氣在先，露遇陰逼而為霜，故先冷也。雪後寒者，陽氣在先，水偪陽死而成雪，故後寒也。雪花六出者，圜一圍六，同體相依，直輳即離，平輳即合，此亦空中旋氣使然也。露為夜候之常，故空冷而成霜；先煖而起同雲，變北風而成雪；春五出，以煖而稍化耳。
○ It is cold before frost because yin qi comes first, and dew, pressed by yin, becomes frost, so it is cold first. It is cold after snow because yang qi comes first, and water, pressing the yang to death, becomes snow, so it is cold afterward. Snowflakes are six-pointed because one round is surrounded by six, bodies of the same kind leaning on each other, which separate when joined end to end and combine when joined side by side; this too is caused by the whirling qi in the air. Dew is the normal sign of night, so the air is cold and it becomes frost; when first warm and then uniform clouds rise and the wind turns north, it becomes snow; in spring flakes are five-pointed, because they partly melt in the warmth.

1
○以遠鏡細測天漢，皆細星，如郎位、鬼尸之類。靈憲注曰：水精為天河。謂星為水之精也。周易時論曰：河漢同坤艮之維。今按之，周輪金魚、海鳥與井參之間、箕尾之間相接矣。黃帝素問以戊巳望黔天之氣而表五運，蓋其指也，微哉！
○ Examined closely with the telescope, the Milky Way is all small stars, like the Gentlemen of the Palace and the Ghost's Corpse. The commentary to the Numinous Constitution says: The essence of water is the Heavenly River. This means the stars are the essence of water. The Discourses on the Changes of Zhou According to the Times says: The Milky Way shares the corner of kun and gen. Now, examining it, around the circle the Goldfish and Sea Bird join the regions between Well and Three Stars and between Winnowing Basket and Tail. The Yellow Emperor's Basic Questions uses wu and ji to observe the qi of the black sky and to express the five circulations — presumably this is what it points to; subtle indeed!

《風雷雨暘類·凍成花鳥草木之形 Ice Forming the Shapes of Flowers, Birds, Plants and Trees》

1
○嘗見紀載：河凍有魚龍花草形，或在屋瓦上有之，類以為祥異。格致草曰：崇禎壬申臘月，餘樓簷瓦凍為花草獅鳳之形，嘆曰：蜃氣象樓臺，廣野氣成宮闕，是凍亦偶然合之，如雨豆雨麥，偶象豆麥耳。松蟲之毛象松，柑蟲之色象柑，濕熱所蒸，頃刻漫山布嶺，耳目口鼻腸胃畢具，可以通觀。又如落星為石，象狗首，便曰天狗，不知是火吸土氣，或至火際，一經鎔練，如陶土成磚，故初落熱不可摩。又見星石久藏變小，亦緣火初煉土，急切成質，苞合有氣，久則氣消而質自斂。雹竅亦然。雪霜霧露之後，微風飄漾，往往成紋耳。愚曰：光氣與形象互成，既有形象，必有所肖，何足怪乎？暄曰：廣昌劉季尖丁丑春雪凌數日，殿門貼梅花，瓦覆蓮花，野老曰：「豐兆也。」蓋雪涷則土堅蝗絕，而芒種時有雨耳。
○ I have seen it recorded that when rivers freeze there are shapes of fish, dragons, flowers and plants, sometimes also on roof tiles, and people generally take them as auspicious or strange omens. The Draft on the Investigation of Things says: In the twelfth month of the renshen year of Chongzhen, the tiles under the eaves of my tower froze into the shapes of flowers, plants, lions and phoenixes, and I sighed: the vapour of the shen clam images towers and terraces, and the qi of the broad fields forms palaces, so ice too happens to form such shapes by chance, just as when it rains beans or wheat they happen to resemble beans and wheat. The hair of pine caterpillars resembles pine and the colour of mandarin caterpillars resembles mandarins; steamed by damp heat, in a moment they cover the mountains and ridges, with ears, eyes, mouths, noses and guts all complete — one can view it all as one. Again, when a falling star becomes a stone shaped like a dog's head, people call it the Heavenly Dog, not knowing that this is fire drawing in the qi of earth, or reaching the fire zone and being once smelted, like clay made into bricks, which is why it is too hot to touch when it first falls. Also, stones from stars kept for a long time are seen to shrink, because the fire first refined the earth and hastily formed its substance, enclosing qi within, and after a long time the qi dissipates and the substance contracts of itself. The holes in hailstones are the same. After snow, frost, fog and dew, a light breeze drifting over often forms patterns. The Foolish One says: Light and qi form shapes and images mutually; since there are shapes and images, they must resemble something — what is there to wonder at? Xuan says: At Guangchang, in the spring of the dingchou year, Liu Jijian [saw] ice from snow lasting several days form plum blossoms stuck on the hall doors and lotus flowers covering the tiles, and the old countrymen said: "A sign of plenty." For when snow freezes, the soil hardens and locusts are killed, and there is rain at the time of Grain in Ear.

1
○老父為南璽卿時，夜無月而見斜暈如虹。范質公大司馬問其故，老父曰：天地體圜，日光又圜，故凡暈光無不圜者，特斜見于地上，則橋起半規耳。陸農師噀日征之，是也。夜見者，空中別迸火光，如春秋恒星不見之類，其光穿地，則為五色暈矣。范公問其占，曰：以端幾類應而言，為陰勝陽。或曰淫徵，何也？曰：因古有虹為淫氣之說也。方士于東海見虹處掘地，得紅蟲，為媚藥，亦取其應耳。
○ When my late father was Chief Minister of the Seal Office in Nanjing, he saw at night, with no moon, a slanting halo like a rainbow. Fan Zhigong, the Minister of War, asked the reason, and my father said: The body of Heaven and Earth is round and the sun's light is round, so all haloes are round; it is only that, seen slanting above the ground, it rises like a bridge in a half circle. Lu Nongshi's verifying it by spraying water against the sun is right. Seen at night, it is separate firelight bursting out in the air, like the fixed stars not being visible in the Spring and Autumn, and when its light pierces the earth it becomes a five-coloured halo. Lord Fan asked how to divine it, and he said: Speaking by the analogical response of the incipient beginning, it is yin prevailing over yang. Some say it is a sign of licentiousness — why? He said: Because in antiquity there was a theory that the rainbow is licentious qi. Occultists dug where they saw a rainbow on the Eastern Sea, found red worms and made them into love potions — also taking up its correspondence.

《風雷雨暘類·彗 Comets》

1
○滿空皆火，近天極熱，如窯中無光，投物則發光耳。此皆地氣迸上，帶物入此熱際，而火光迸射，其久者氣結厚大，隨輪天轉，去地尚近，非列宿之天也。古為掃舊布新，亦以類應言也。大風雨則解。萬歷戊午，彗射紫微，謂之蚩尤旗。舊說天使、天暉、天雁、天保，祥也；地雁、梁星、醟頭、天狗，妖也。爾雅曰狗約。質測之，皆空中火迸，大則芒彗耳。
○ The whole sky is full of fire, and near Heaven it is extremely hot, like the inside of a kiln without light, where anything thrown in bursts into light. These are all earth-qi bursting upward and carrying things into this hot zone, so that firelight shoots out; the long-lasting ones have thick, large knots of qi that turn with the wheel of Heaven, and they are still near the earth, not in the heaven of the lodges. The ancients took them as sweeping away the old and spreading the new, which is also speaking by analogical correspondence. Great wind and rain dissolve them. In the wuwu year of Wanli a comet shot into the Purple Tenuity, and it was called the Banner of Chiyou. The old theory: Tianshi, Tianhui, Tianyan and Tianbao are auspicious; Diyan, Liangxing, Yongtou and Tiangou are baleful. The Erya speaks of the "dog" binding. Measured materially, all are fire bursting out in the air; the large ones are rays and comets.

1
○日中黑子，蓋恒有之，或見或不見。太白有二黑子，填星有四黑子，旋轉其上。其暈而為黑子者，則光所盪也。其以占言者，亦以人心觸幾，而以時日斷也。左藏一言：於夕陽時見滿天黑子，回首向池中觀之，最明，飛動不一，踰數刻而散。中通曰：金水二星附日為輪，正在日下則為黑子，其大小相鬥，則光暈蕩出者也。滿天黑子，乃陽光忽微，其星現為黑影，入水氣盪，故飛動不定耳。
○ Black spots in the sun are probably always there, sometimes seen and sometimes not. Venus has two black spots and Saturn four, which revolve on them. Those that are haloes becoming black spots are stirred up by the light. Those who divine by them also touch the incipient through the human mind and decide by the time and day. Zuo Cangyi said: at sunset he saw the whole sky full of black spots; turning his head to look into a pond, they were clearest, flying and moving variously, and after several ke they dispersed. Zhongtong says: Venus and Mercury are attached to the sun as epicycles, and when they are right below the sun they are black spots; when large and small fight, it is the halo of light stirred out. A sky full of black spots is when the sunlight suddenly grows faint and the stars appear as black shadows, stirred by entering the qi of water, so they fly and move unsteadily.

1
○炙艾火者，宜用陽燧火珠承日，取太陽真火，其次則鑽槐取火為良。若急卒難備，即用真麻油燈或蠟燭火，以艾莖燒點，炙瘡不痛。金石之火烈于草木之火，是矣。八木者：松火難瘥，柏火傷神多汗，桑火傷肌肉，柘火傷氣脈，棗火傷肉吐血，橘火傷營衛經絡，榆火傷骨失志，竹火傷筋損目也。梁書載：武帝時有沙門從北齊齎赤火來，其火赤于常火而小，云以療疾，貴賤爭取之，炙至七炷，多得其驗。吳興楊道慶虛疾二十年，炙之即瘥，咸稱聖火。外紀云：納波里有火山百洞，入洞皆以療疾。
○ For moxibustion fire one should use a burning-mirror or fire-pearl held to the sun to obtain the true fire of the sun; next best is fire obtained by drilling pagoda-tree wood. If in an emergency these are hard to come by, use a lamp of pure sesame oil or a candle flame to light a mugwort stem, and the moxa will not hurt the sore. The fire of metal and stone is fiercer than the fire of plants — that is right. Of the eight woods: pine fire makes healing difficult, cypress fire harms the spirit and causes much sweating, mulberry fire harms the flesh, cudrania fire harms the qi and vessels, jujube fire harms the flesh and causes vomiting of blood, orange fire harms the nutritive and defensive qi and the channels, elm fire harms the bones and causes loss of will, and bamboo fire harms the sinews and damages the eyes. The History of the Liang records that in the time of Emperor Wu a monk brought red fire from the Northern Qi, redder and smaller than ordinary fire, said to cure illness; high and low vied for it, and moxibustion of up to seven cones was mostly effective. Yang Daoqing of Wuxing had suffered from a depletion illness for twenty years, and was cured at once by moxibustion with it, and all called it the holy fire. The Record of Places Outside says: Naples has a volcano with a hundred caves, and going into the caves always cures illness.

《風雷雨暘類·死性之火 Fire with a Dead Nature》

1
○炭者，火閉氣而死者也，無生性，故葬用之，烰炭灰斷蟻路，可驗也。冬夏二至前，以土炭垂兩端，陰氣至則土重，陽氣至則炭重。收茶于瓶，烰炭置上，濕氣不入。墨膠硯滑，烰炭可以磨之。本草焙炙用烰炭者，取易息而力和也。觀礁之毒，可知火各有性。鏡源曰：麩炭啞肺，而釜底煤能消火。暄曰：釜底煤能收暑熱，猶油餅能食油膩，油膩出于餅，見油膩則收，以其枯也；煤為火之窟宅，見火則收，以其死也。烰炭亦然。彼附子、參、茋過炮，無補有損，亦可知矣。中履曰：煎油鍋有時忽燃，若入滴水與投菜物，則其焰愈張，惟以鍋蓋蓋之則息，亦閉火之理也。
○ Charcoal is fire that has died from being shut off from air; it has no life, so it is used in burials, and the ash of charcoal cuts off the path of ants, which can be verified. Before the winter and summer solstices, hang earth and charcoal at the two ends [of a balance]: when the yin qi arrives the earth is heavier, and when the yang qi arrives the charcoal is heavier. When tea is stored in a bottle with charcoal placed on top, damp does not get in. When ink glue makes an inkstone slippery, charcoal can be used to grind it. The Materia Medica's use of charcoal for drying and roasting takes advantage of its being easily extinguished and gentle in force. Seeing the poison of coal, one can know that fires each have their own nature. The Source of the Mirror says: Bran charcoal makes the lungs mute, and the soot at the bottom of a pot can absorb fire. Xuan says: Burial specialists often put sulphur in coffins, so that the qi is warm, the bones last long, and insects and ants do not breed. A north wall makes a house warm north of the equator; but for those sick with epidemics or heatstroke one should open windows.

《地類·水患說 On Floods》

1
○人以水生，以火死，蓋以水火交而生，以水火濟而養，以水下流、火上炎而死也。天地開時，初有水荒；天地壞時，火急生風而吹壞矣。一元中之一會，其始亦有水厄。泰西言洪水時，亞你墨尼亞為甚，猛雨四旬，地面全沒，止遺諾厄數人，考其時當帝嚳之八年壬辰云。中國洪水在堯時，是一徵也。林孟鳴考堯時水患有十七年，揭靜叔考有一百五十八年，引竹書：堯十九年，命共工治河；六十一年，命崇伯鯀治河；六十九年，黜鯀；七十年，錫虞舜之命；七十五年，司空禹治河；八十六年，司空入覲。是則先用共工治水，次用鯀，皆以堤水無功，而導之成功者，實終于舜攝之已已歲也。舜歷試後，乃使治河，書所謂「納于大麓」，孟子所謂「舉舜而敷治焉」。史記：堯使舜入山林川澤，暴風雷雨，帝行不迷。蘇氏曰：洪水為害，堯使舜入山林，相視原隰。正與尚書、孟氏之言合，而孔叢、桓譚乃改大麓為大錄，張萱以為壇會諸侯，徒爾紛紛，不知舜實治水也。益烈山澤，水氣盛，火氣微，故用烈；刊木便相視，亦用烈。國語：共工從孫曰岳，從禹疏壅導滯。史記殷世家：契長，佐治水有功。然則禹之治水，益佐之，岳與契又佐之矣。國語太子晉云：共工墮高堙卑，以禍天下，皇天弗福，共工用滅；有崇伯鯀稱遂共工之過，堯用殛之羽山。虞書曰：靜言庸違，象恭滔天。言水不治也。愚者曰：古時水患甚久，當營巢時亦有，甚不甚，史書頗簡，今合觀之，了然矣。治水之法，堤遏最下，導之為上。孔子贊禹曰：卑宮室而盡力乎溝洫。溝洫即治水之上策也，何待賈讓畫三說耶？張江陵居正在政府，集門下談治河，刑曹商為正獨無言，公問之，商曰：「河不可治。」江陵默然，以為得，遂遷御史。愚者笑曰：關並謂禹治水時，空平原、東郡以為積瀦，以入東北海，其勢易；馮逡欲濬屯氏，賈讓欲徙冀民：皆因而導之之意也。然有說焉：以安堵之郡邑讓河，今可行乎？人各護其土著，誰肯無事而徙其田宅耶？是與羊謀其羞，神禹所不能行也。縣官歲費數百萬，徒歸中飽，不如己之，聽其決，後乃下哀惻之詔，為之移措安置，當委之河者竟委之，旁分浸澱者旁分之，豈不永遠安瀾乎哉？是在明君哲相不言而默運耳。
○ Man lives by water and dies by fire; for he lives by the meeting of water and fire, is nourished by their mutual assistance, and dies when water flows down and fire blazes up. When Heaven and Earth opened, there was first a flood; when Heaven and Earth are destroyed, fire will urgently produce wind and blow it away. In one epoch within one cycle, at the beginning there is also a disaster of water. The Far West says that at the time of the Flood, Armenia was the worst; there was heavy rain for forty days, the surface of the earth was entirely submerged, and only Noah and a few others were left; they reckon the time to correspond to the renchen year, the eighth year of Di Ku. China's flood was in the time of Yao, which is one corroboration. Lin Mengming reckons that the flood in Yao's time lasted seventeen years, and Jie Jingshu reckons a hundred and fifty-eight years, citing the Bamboo Annals: in Yao's nineteenth year he ordered Gonggong to control the river; in the sixty-first year he ordered Gun, Earl of Chong, to control the river; in the sixty-ninth year he dismissed Gun; in the seventieth year he gave the mandate to Shun of Yu; in the seventy-fifth year the Minister of Works Yu controlled the river; in the eighty-sixth year the Minister of Works came to court. So Gonggong was first employed to control the waters and then Gun, both without success by damming the waters, and the one who succeeded by guiding them actually finished in the yisi year of Shun's regency. After Shun had been tested repeatedly, he was made to control the river — what the Documents calls "he was sent into the great foothills," and what Mencius calls "he raised Shun and had him spread order." The Records of the Historian: Yao sent Shun into the mountains, forests, rivers and marshes, and in violent wind, thunder and rain he did not lose his way. Master Su said: The flood was causing harm, and Yao sent Shun into the mountains and forests to survey the plains and wetlands. This agrees exactly with the words of the Documents and Mencius, yet the Kongcongzi and Huan Tan changed "great foothills" to "great record," and Zhang Xuan took it as an altar-meeting of the lords — all useless confusion, not knowing that Shun actually controlled the waters. Yi set fire to the mountains and marshes because the qi of water was strong and the qi of fire weak, so he used burning; cutting trees to make surveying easy also used burning. The Discourses of the States: Gonggong's grand-nephew, called Yue, followed Yu in clearing obstructions and guiding blockages. The Records of the Historian, "Hereditary House of Yin": Xie grew up and assisted in controlling the waters with merit. So in Yu's control of the waters Yi assisted him, and Yue and Xie further assisted. Prince Jin in the Discourses of the States said: Gonggong pulled down the high and filled up the low to bring disaster on the world; august Heaven did not bless him, and Gonggong was destroyed; Gun, Earl of Chong, continued Gonggong's errors, and Yao banished him to death at Mount Yu. The "Documents of Yu" says: "In quiet he speaks, but in action he goes against it; in appearance respectful, he flouts Heaven." This speaks of the waters not being controlled. The Foolish One says: In ancient times floods went on very long, and when people built nests there were floods too, some worse than others; the histories are rather brief, but taken together it is clear. In methods of controlling water, damming is the worst and guiding the best. Confucius praised Yu: "He made his palace low and spent all his strength on the ditches and channels." Ditches and channels are the best policy for controlling water — why wait for Jia Rang's three plans? When Zhang Juzheng of Jiangling was in government, he gathered his followers to discuss controlling the river; Zhengwei of the Ministry of Justice, Shang, alone said nothing, and when the Duke asked him, Shang said: "The river cannot be controlled." Jiangling was silent, took him to have got it right, and promoted him to censor. The Foolish One laughs: Guan Bing said that when Yu controlled the waters he emptied Pingyuan and Dongjun to serve as retention basins, letting it go into the northeastern sea, so its course was easy; Feng Jun wanted to dredge the Tunshi, and Jia Rang wanted to move the people of Ji — all in the spirit of following and guiding it. But there is a point: would it be feasible now to give up peaceful commanderies and towns to the river? Everyone protects his native place; who would be willing to move his fields and house for no reason? This is plotting with a sheep for its mutton, which even the divine Yu could not carry out. The government spends several million a year, which merely fills pockets in between; better to stop and let it break through, and afterward issue a compassionate edict and resettle the people, abandoning to the river what must be abandoned and dividing off to side channels and deposits what should be divided off — would there not then be lasting calm waters? It lies with an enlightened ruler and wise ministers to manage it silently without words.

《地類·水忽清忽變 Waters Suddenly Clearing and Changing》

1

1

1

Enclosed in the earth and drawn up to a mountaintop — what doubt is there? Xuan says: Hollow things float on fast water; welling water is slower than [falling] water. 《地類·潮汐 Tides》

1
○山海經以潮汐為海鰍之出入，藏經以為神龍之變化，葛洪以為天河激湧，金樓子以為鯨，謬矣。博物志云：潮繫日月，若鼎之沸。元耿伯宣謂：方儀之運，大水承之，氣有升降，地有浮沉，而潮汐生焉。皆臆言之也。至元吳彥壽答起崖書，謂月與水陰類相應。張封演曰：月周天而潮應。古洲曰：朔後三日，明出而潮壯；望後三日，魄見而汐湧。每歲仲春，月落水生而汐微；仲秋，月明水落而潮倍；減于大寒，極陰而凝；弱于大暑，畏陽而縮。陰陽消長，不失其時，故曰潮信，即潮汛也。每月十三、二十七日曰水起，是為大汛，各七日；初五、二十日曰下岸，是為小汛，亦各七日。久晴，雖大汛亦不長。凡潮生則水長，但期至有遠近：鎮江至大海五百里，十八日辰時潮至；浙江至海三百里，則十八日醜時至。潮之大小隨月，月高潮小，月低潮大。瓊館志：江浙欽廉之潮，皆有定候，半月東流，半月西流，潮之大小隨長短星，不繫月之盛衰。明州潮，月加子午則長。朱子曰：餘襄公言潮汐甚詳。王清臣揮麈錄曰：浙海門有赭山、龕山，溢于沙灘，故猛而為濤，非徒狹也。邵子曰：海潮者，地之喘息也，所以應月者，從其類也。徐明叔傳墨卿高麗錄與姚寬耐得翁就日錄，皆主元氣呼吸之說，但為地浮水上則陋矣。朱隱老言：月麗卯酉，則潮應乎東西；月麗子午，則應乎南北。采珠者入海底，間遇潮，則水湧而下虛焉，潮高十丈，下所虛亦十丈，以水則虛，以氣則實，采珠者中其氣輒病。地之喘息，寓息于風，生氣也；寓潮于水，死氣也。泰西曰：月為陰精，與水同物，凡濕潤陰寒，皆月主之。既為同物，勢當相就，如呼吸然。潮長之時，江河以及盆盎，無處不長，長則氣入，水為之輕；潮降氣出，水復故重。今人以瓶盛水，每日權之，輕重不等，亦此故耳。獨小水之處，升降甚微，人所不覺也。水族之物，皆望盈晦縮，故月虛而魚腦減，月滿而蛤蚌實也。草木資潤，無不應月，月滿氣滋，月虛氣燥，故上弦以後、下弦以前，不宜伐竹木，為材易蠹，生氣在中也。張僧鑒潯陽記：雞籠山湧泉如潮，朔望尤大。番禺雜記：早潮下，晚潮上，兩水相合曰沓潮。而潮亦有異者：暖姝由筆曰：廣東海一潮，韶州有雌雄泉兩潮，柳州昔相嶺西麓下有潮井，廣百畝，一日三湧三落。張佳胤記：滇大華山泉曰聖泉，一名海眼，應子午卯酉而潮，曰三潮者，夜不見也。外紀：厄毆白亞海潮一日七次。玄中記：貴州城外有漏汋，一日百盈百涸，應刻漏，或云五十盈五十渴。安寧州有潮泉，一日三溢三蘸。連州水下流有斟泉，一日十溢十渴。尤異者，輿地志：移風縣有湖雞，鳴長旦，清如吹角，每鳴應潮至。虔州濂泉旁數里有泉，日三潮，崇禎後不復至。楊穆西野雜記：錢塘江鄰海口有子午潮，不爽。諺云：潮過夷亭出狀元。宋末過之，果出衛徑。自嘉靖甲午以來，不惟不過夷亭，錢塘江邊或旬日不至，時人謂之凍死潮，宋末潮不至，亦可知矣。愚者曰：人日夜五十營，萬三千五百息，一呼一吸，脈且四至；水當火烹，百沸百止，亦氣之上下也。潮之應月，月行有南北陸，地異，或不同耳。暄曰：月為水精，以月主潮者是也。月之所臨則潮起，為正攝；月之對衝，潮亦起，為反攝。月之氣從前順虛，至對衝而極，極而不能前則聚，聚則盈，故月之所在為一月，月氣所聚又一月也。月與水如磁之吸針、珀之拾芥。月繞地而行，潮亦繞地而行；月行天一周，潮循地一周：是天地內無地不潮，無刻不潮也。然氣不入則水不起，故地之呼吸，尾閭泉源之轉變，亦由是而起矣。星亦與月類，夏日無月之夜，星辰自燦，則地氣升而為露，草尾皆有露珠，亦潮也，而必以月為應者，則月之氣大耳。或云伐黃楊于無星之夜，為枕不裂，取其體本燥也。所異者，地有南北，月有高下，或空際為陽氣所隔，或地中有閼塞而他徙，故有不至。氣行于大處，則一躍而至；行于末處遠處，必連躍而後至，而撇水碗者其至一撇之，凡瓦浮水面，始則大躍，繼則連躍，以行遠而銳盡也。至哉吾宓山師之以脈言潮也！
○ The Classic of Mountains and Seas takes the tides as the coming and going of a sea-whale, the Buddhist canon as the transformations of a divine dragon, Ge Hong as the surging of the Heavenly River, and the Jinlouzi as a whale — all mistaken. The Treatise on Broad Matters says: The tides are tied to the sun and moon, like the boiling of a cauldron. Geng Boxuan of the Yuan said: As the square instrument moves, the great waters support it; qi rises and falls and the earth floats and sinks, and so the tides arise. All these are conjectures. In the Zhiyuan period, Wu Yanshou's letter in reply to Qiya said that the moon and water respond to each other as things of the yin kind. Zhang Fengyan said: The moon goes round Heaven and the tides respond. Guzhou said: Three days after the new moon, the bright part appears and the flood tide is strong; three days after the full moon, the dark part appears and the ebb tide surges. Every year in mid-spring, when the moon sets and the water rises, the ebb is weak; in mid-autumn, when the moon is bright and the water falls, the flood is doubled; it diminishes in the Great Cold, congealing in extreme yin, and weakens in the Great Heat, shrinking in fear of yang. The waxing and waning of yin and yang never miss their time, so they are called "tidal faith," that is, the tidal season. The thirteenth and twenty-seventh of each month are called "water rising," the great spring tides, each lasting seven days; the fifth and twentieth are called "below the bank," the small neap tides, also seven days each. After a long fair spell, even at spring tide the water does not rise. Whenever the tide comes in, the water rises, but the time of arrival depends on distance: Zhenjiang is five hundred li from the sea, and the tide arrives at the chen hour on the eighteenth; the Zhe River is three hundred li from the sea, and it arrives at the chou hour on the eighteenth. The size of the tide follows the moon: when the moon is high the tide is small, when the moon is low the tide is large. The Qiongguan Gazetteer: the tides of Jiang, Zhe, Qin and Lian all have fixed times, flowing east for half a month and west for half a month; their size follows the long and short stars and is not tied to the waxing and waning of the moon. The tide at Mingzhou is long when the moon is at zi and wu. Master Zhu said: Yu Xianggong speaks of the tides in great detail. Wang Qingchen's Record of Waving the Duster says: At the sea-gate of Zhe there are Zhe Mountain and Kan Mountain, and [the tide] overflows onto the sandbanks, so it is fierce and becomes a bore, not merely because it is narrow. Master Shao said: The sea tides are the breathing of the earth; they respond to the moon because they follow their kind. Xu Mingshu's Account of Korea transmitted by Moqing and Yao Kuan's Naideweng's Record of Approaching the Sun both hold the theory of the breathing of primal qi, but their idea that the earth floats on water is crude. Zhu Yinlao says: When the moon is at mao and you, the tide answers east and west; when the moon is at zi and wu, it answers south and north. Pearl-divers who go to the sea bottom sometimes meet the tide, and then the water surges up and below it is empty; when the tide is ten zhang high, the emptiness below is also ten zhang; in terms of water it is empty, but in terms of qi it is full, and pearl-divers who are struck by this qi fall ill. The breathing of the earth lodges its breath in the wind, which is living qi, and lodges its tides in the water, which is dead qi. The Far West says: The moon is the essence of yin and of one kind with water; all that is damp, moist, yin and cold is governed by the moon. Being of one kind, they naturally tend toward each other, as in breathing. When the tide rises, rivers and even basins and jars all rise; when they rise, qi enters and the water becomes lighter; when the tide falls, qi goes out and the water becomes heavy again. When people today put water in a bottle and weigh it each day, it varies in weight for this reason. Only in small bodies of water the rise and fall are very slight and people do not notice. Aquatic creatures all wax at the full moon and shrink at the dark moon, so when the moon is empty fish brains diminish and when the moon is full clams and mussels are plump. Plants depend on moisture and all respond to the moon: when the moon is full their qi is moist, and when the moon is empty their qi is dry; so between the first quarter and the last quarter one should not cut bamboo or timber, since as material it is easily eaten by worms, the living qi being inside. Zhang Sengjian's Record of Xunyang: the spring at Jilong Mountain wells up like a tide, especially at new and full moon. The Miscellaneous Records of Panyu: the morning tide goes down and the evening tide comes up, and when the two waters meet it is called the "overlapping tide." And there are strange tides: the Brush of the Contented Beauty says: in Guangdong the sea has one tide, in Shaozhou there are the male and female springs with two tides, and at the western foot of Xixiang Ridge in Liuzhou there is a tidal well a hundred mu in extent, which rises three times and falls three times a day. Zhang Jiayin records: the spring of Dahua Mountain in Dian is called the Holy Spring, also the Sea's Eye, which rises at zi, wu, mao and you; it is called "three tides" because the one at night is not seen. The Record of Places Outside: the sea at Euboea has seven tides a day. The Record of the Mysterious Centre: outside the city of Guizhou there is a leaking spring that fills and empties a hundred times a day, in step with the water clock; some say it fills fifty times and empties fifty times. In Anning Subprefecture there is a tidal spring that overflows three times and sinks three times a day. Below the Lian River in Lianzhou there is the Zhen spring, which overflows ten times and empties ten times a day. Stranger still, the Records of the Lands says: at Yifeng County there is a lake-fowl that crows at daybreak with a sound clear as a horn, and every time it crows the tide arrives. Several li from the Lian spring in Qianzhou there is a spring with three tides a day, which stopped coming after Chongzhen. Yang Mu's Miscellaneous Notes from the Western Wilds: at the mouth of the Qiantang River near the sea there are zi and wu tides that never fail. A proverb says: When the tide passes Yiting, a top graduate will appear. At the end of the Song it passed there, and indeed Wei Jing came first. Since the jiawu year of Jiajing, not only has it not passed Yiting, but along the Qiantang River it sometimes fails to come for ten days at a time, and people of the time call it the "frozen-dead tide"; the tide's not coming at the end of the Song can likewise be understood. The Foolish One says: A person in a day and night makes fifty circuits and thirteen thousand five hundred breaths, and in one breath out and in the pulse beats four times; water heated by fire boils up and stops a hundred times — this too is the rising and falling of qi. As for the tide's response to the moon, the moon's course has southern and northern paths, and the places differ, so they may not be the same. Xuan says: The moon is the essence of water, and it is right that the moon governs the tides. Where the moon looks down, the tide rises — this is direct attraction; in opposition to the moon the tide also rises — this is reverse attraction. The moon's qi follows the void from the front, reaching its extreme at opposition, and at the extreme, unable to go further, it gathers, and gathering it swells; so where the moon is there is one bulge, and where the moon's qi gathers there is another. The moon and water are like the lodestone drawing a needle and amber picking up mustard seeds. The moon goes round the earth and the tide goes round the earth too; the moon goes round Heaven once and the tide round the earth once: so within Heaven and Earth there is no place without tides and no moment without tides. But if qi does not enter, the water does not rise; so the earth's breathing, and the changes of the sea's outlet and the springs' sources, also arise from this. The stars are of the moon's kind too: on moonless summer nights when the stars shine brightly, the earth's qi rises and becomes dew, and the tips of the grass all have dewdrops — this too is a tide; but the response must be to the moon because the moon's qi is great. Some say that boxwood cut on a starless night and made into pillows does not crack, because its substance is dry to begin with. The differences are that the land has south and north and the moon high and low, and sometimes the air is cut off by yang qi or there is a blockage in the earth that diverts it, so there are times it does not arrive. When qi moves in a large place it arrives in one leap; in narrow or distant places it must leap repeatedly before arriving; and in skimming stones on water it arrives at one skim — a tile floating on the water first leaps high and then leaps repeatedly, going far until its force is spent. How perfect is my teacher Mishan's explaining the tides by the pulse!

《地類·水圜 The Roundness of Water》

1
○金幼孜北征記：永樂征本雅失里，至闊海灣，遠望之，其水中高如山，近處若極卑，白浪隱隱，自高而下，周千餘里，乾雍、臚朐七河注其中，賜名玄冥池。暄曰：地形圜，水附于地者亦當圜，凡江湖以及盆盎之水，無不中高，特人不覺耳。
○ Jin Youzi's Record of the Northern Campaign: when Yongle campaigned against Bunyashiri and reached Kuohai Bay, seen from afar the water rose high in the middle like a mountain, while near at hand it seemed very low, with white waves dimly visible from high to low, over a thousand li in circumference; seven rivers including the Qianyong and Luqu flow into it, and it was named the Pool of the Dark Mystery. Xuan says: The earth is round in shape, so the water attached to the earth must also be round; all water in rivers and lakes and even in basins and jars is higher in the middle; only people do not notice.

《地類·沸珠泉 Boiling-Pearl Springs》

1
○南城上塘寧氏田中有小池一泓，泉自沙中滾出，數百線直上。胡文穆廣言坪下有沸珠泉。愚按：濟南金線泉亦然。爾雅曰：瀵，大出尾下。如濟南趵突泉、河源星宿海，同類，沸珠、金線則其細者也。遼外有濆沙泉。
○ In the field of the Ning family at Shangtang in Nancheng there is a little pool where a spring bubbles up out of the sand in hundreds of threads straight upward. Hu Wenmu (Guang) speaks of a boiling-pearl spring below the flat. The Foolish One notes: the Golden Thread spring of Jinan is the same. The Erya says: Fen is a large outflow from below. Such as the Baotu spring of Jinan and the Starry Sea at the source of the Yellow River are of the same kind; boiling pearls and golden threads are the fine ones. Beyond Liao there is a sand-spouting spring.

《地類·山下出泉 Springs Issuing from Below Mountains》

1
○金生水者，金石同出于土，金礦亦石也。石乃土骨，生水，山脈隆起者，陰行陽長，堅則為石耳。暄曰：山出泉，乃石生水，如人身之骨節必通髓路也。中履曰：南方泉多，如匡廬絕頂皆有泉；北如太行、太華，必山腰以下乃有泉，井則有十丈始得泉者。
○ Metal produces water because metal and stone both come out of the earth, and metal ore is also stone. Stone is the bones of the earth and produces water; where mountain veins rise, yin moves and yang grows, and when hard it becomes stone. Xuan says: Mountains producing springs is stone producing water, as the bones and joints of the body must have channels for the marrow. Zhonglü says: In the south there are many springs, as on the summits of Kuanglu there are springs everywhere; in the north, as on Taihang and Taihua, springs are found only below the mountainside, and there are wells where one finds water only at ten zhang.

1
○江水定清可比惠泉者，以其淡也。梧州冰井水與惠泉同味。梅雨水、雪水味輕。東坡云：天落水炊飯，白米紅，紅米白詳前水論。
○ River water, when settled and clear, can rival the Hui spring because it is bland. The water of the Ice Well at Wuzhou tastes the same as the Hui spring. Plum-rain water and snow water taste light. Dongpo says: When rice is cooked with water fallen from the sky, white rice turns red and red rice white see the earlier discussion of water.

《地類·烹水法 Methods of Boiling Water》

1
○北水重于南水，泉水重于江河水，宜多一沸，則養人。中德曰：凡烹苦泉，數沸澄之，再烹則味甘。水烹沸，以溪中沬為末投之，沸即止。
○ Northern water is heavier than southern water, and spring water heavier than river water; it should be boiled one extra time, and then it is nourishing. Zhongde says: When boiling bitter spring water, boil it several times and let it settle, then boil it again, and it will taste sweet. When water boils up, powder made from the foam of a stream thrown in stops the boiling at once.

《地類·貯水洗水法 Methods of Storing and Washing Water》

1
○盛泉水宜載其小石一二，甕須置陰庭，覆以沙石，使承星露，則英華不散。羅廩曰：梅水後不堪飲，大甕滿貯，乘熱投伏龍肝一塊。收泉水壞，燒炭投之，即如故。楫曰：有洗水法：泉水壞，汲江河水攪而洗之，久澄，泉重自歸下，江河水自浮上，先依原水之高下竅甕，至此洩之，所存水倍甘于初。
○ When storing spring water, one should put in one or two of its small stones; the jar should be placed in a shaded courtyard, covered with sand and stones, so that it receives starlight and dew, and its essence will not disperse. Luo Lin said: After the plum rains water is undrinkable; fill a large jar and, while it is hot, throw in a piece of stove-earth. When stored spring water goes bad, burn charcoal and throw it in, and it will be as before. Ji says: There is a method of washing water: when spring water goes bad, draw river water, stir it in to wash it, and let it settle for a long time; the spring water, being heavy, sinks to the bottom and the river water floats on top; first bore a hole in the jar at the level of the original water, and drain it there, and the water that remains is twice as sweet as at first.

《地類·澄水 Clarifying Water》

1
○贊寧云：阿膠澄濁，不獨補血也。東阿井，濟水所瀵，煎黑衛子皮成，以色綠易碎者為佳。凡水污濁不堪，投少許于中，即清。尋常定水，白礬、赤豆、杏仁、雄黃、石膏皆可。若池水渾濁，以瓶盛糞，扎之以箬，投水即清。又池中置牛骨，則水不涸。
○ Zanning said: Donkey-hide glue clarifies turbidity, besides tonifying the blood. The well of Dong'e, which is spouted by the Ji River, is boiled with black donkey hide to make it, and the green, brittle kind is best. Whenever water is filthy and turbid beyond use, a little of it thrown in makes it clear. For ordinary settling of water, alum, red beans, apricot kernels, realgar and gypsum all serve. If pond water is muddy, put dung in a bottle, tie it up with bamboo leaves and throw it into the water, and it will clear. Also, if ox bones are placed in a pond, the water will not dry up.

《地類·試井 Testing Wells》

1
○凡家井閉氣，秋夏中之殺人，先以雞毛投之，直下無毒，迴舞而下不可犯，當以醋數斗澆之，方可入。凡井水沸溢，不可飲，于三十步內取青石一塊投之，即止。又平明望氣所出，掘之得泉；或以燈縣坎中，亦辨水氣。地中多空隙，有風射，掘者當之病。暄曰：此與潮下氣毒同為一類。今葬家及修築動土犯之，或病或死，亦此故耳，惟常治者乃不畏。
○ When a household well is shut off from air, those who are struck by it in autumn and summer die; first throw in a chicken feather: if it drops straight down there is no poison, but if it swirls down one must not go in; one should pour several dou of vinegar into it before entering. When well water boils up and overflows it is not drinkable; take a piece of bluestone from within thirty paces and throw it in, and it will stop. Also, at dawn look for where vapour comes out and dig there to find a spring; or hang a lamp in the pit to tell the qi of water. In the earth there are many hollows with winds shooting through, and diggers who are struck by them fall ill. Xuan says: This is of the same kind as the poisonous qi under the tides. Today burial specialists and builders who break ground and are struck by it fall ill or die for this reason; only those who do it regularly are not afraid.

《地類·水能駐顏 Waters that Preserve the Complexion》

1
○龍魚河圖曰：北海玄洲有玄澗，水如蜜味，服之長生。亞墨利加女子島旁有島，出異泉，以日未出時汲之，浴面百遭，老者復如童子。綠珠井在廣西博白縣雙角山下，生女汲此水洗之則艷。臨川銅山二泉，一沸一寒。五峰山不者能潤人顏。中通曰：水煖者潤顏，長生洗艷，溢語耳。
○ The River Diagram of Dragons and Fish says: At Xuanzhou in the Northern Sea there is a dark stream whose water tastes like honey, and drinking it gives long life. Beside the Isle of Women in America there is an island with a strange spring; if one draws it before sunrise and washes the face a hundred times, the old become like children again. The Green Pearl Well is below Shuangjiao Mountain in Bobai County in Guangxi, and girls born there who wash in this water become beautiful. Of the two springs of Tongshan in Linchuan, one is boiling and one cold. [The spring] below Wufeng Mountain can moisten the complexion. Zhongtong says: Warm water moistening the complexion is true, but long life and beautifying by washing are exaggerations.

《地類·水益氣已疾 Waters that Augment Qi and Cure Illness》

1

1

○ When water flows slowly it is straight, when fast it twists. The Record of Places Outside: in England there is a sea-pit which at high tide sucks in water and never fills, and when the tide ebbs spouts water as high as a mountain; when it is sucking water, if a person stands beside it and his clothes once touch the water, he is sucked in. At Kunshan in Suzhou there is the Shangyang pool, also a whirlpool, which boats avoid passing.

1

Earth from an east wall, earth from a highway, yellow earth and winter ash differ in their uses for treatment; water of the heavenly river, eastward-flowing water, counterflowing water, mountain springs, well-flower water, roof drips and roadside puddles are utterly different in nature. Some are steamed in the sun, some treated with fire. In flat villages where rapid streams cannot be had, one may use "sweet soft water" tossed a thousand times. The taste of water changes with the twenty-four solar terms; this is a mutual response with the climate, not limited by region.

○馮時可言：滇中裘葛可無備，一雨便秋，夜則擁綿，惟永昌、臨安夏間差熱耳；然無日無風，春尤顛狂，凡風皆西南風，若東南風即媒雨，大約正月半後至四月多風。舊志曰：雨師好黔，風伯好滇。冬日不短，望後至二十夜，月猶圜滿。偽唐張暨紀賈黃中談錄曰：夾河風性寒，故民多傷風；河洛東地醎，水性冷，故食麥而無熱疾；滑臺風寒冷，士民舀附子如芋栗。愚者曰：市上水溫，深山水寒，而深山亦有水性熱者中履曰如杉關壽昌水熱；北方固寒，凡山之陽猶溫；閩越冬溫，而徐渤記元庚寅大雪，荔枝盡死。敦公言：緬甸近海甚，交趾六月正午時熱，夜間極涼，衣服如中國，非所謂披髮而以全幅圍身者也。馬觀瀛涯勝覽曰：葛蘭國諺云：「半年有雨半年晴。」七月小霽，八月望後大晴，三月又雨。周達觀真臘記曰：四時常如五六月，不識霜雪，半年有雨，半年絕無，九月每日下雨，午後方下，猶北方之延山暴也。外紀：印弟亞有加得山，中分南北，其南半自立夏以至秋分無日不雨，反是則片雲不合，酷暑難堪，惟日有涼風解之，其風自已至申從海西來，自亥至寅從陸東來，草木異者不可勝數。海惟太平海淺，亙古今無大風浪。大西洋海極深，從大西洋至大明海，四十五度以南，其風常有定候；至四十五度以北，風則變亂，倏忽更二十四向。小西洋海潮極高大。至大浪山，風浪甚險。滿剌加則無風而倏起里許之波。獨利未亞海近赤道下，常苦無風，天氣酷熱，食物俱壞。諸海無冰，北海則冰而堆成冰山矣。哥阿之地無雨，常晴，至三十六年德墨多則多雨。北海雨少，夏月有雨，濕土而止，則遠日風高也。泥入多國無雨，亦無雲氣，但有泥祿河一年一漲。亞大臘山之頂，終古晴明無風，蓋其地獨高而風微不覺也，雲雨皆在其下耳。
○ Feng Shike said: In Dian one need not prepare furs and summer cloth, for one rain makes it autumn, and at night one wraps up in floss; only Yongchang and Lin'an are somewhat hot in summer; but there is no day without wind, especially wild in spring, and all the winds are southwesterly; if there is a southeast wind it brings rain; roughly from mid-first-month to the fourth month there is much wind. The old gazetteer says: The Master of Rain loves Qian and the Earl of Wind loves Dian. Winter days are not short, and after the full moon until the twentieth night the moon is still round and full. Zhang Ji of the false Tang, recording Jia Huangzhong's Record of Conversations, says: The wind between the rivers is cold in nature, so many people catch colds; the land east of the Yellow River and Luo is salty and the water cold in nature, so people eat wheat without suffering hot illnesses; at Huatai the wind is cold, and officials and people scoop up aconite like taro and chestnuts. The Foolish One says: Water in the market is warm and water in the deep mountains cold, but there are also hot waters in the deep mountains Zhonglü says: such as the hot water of Shouchang at Shanguan; the north is certainly cold, but the sunny side of mountains is still warm; Min and Yue are warm in winter, yet Xu Bo records that in the gengyin year of the Yuan there was heavy snow and the lychees all died. Master Dun said: Burma is very near the sea; in Jiaozhi it is hot at noon in the sixth month and very cool at night, and the clothes are like those of China, not what is called "hair loose and the whole width of cloth wrapped round the body." Ma Guan's Overall Survey of the Ocean's Shores says: A proverb of the country of Quilon says: "Half the year rain, half the year fine." In the seventh month it clears a little, after mid-eighth-month it is quite fine, and in the third month it rains again. Zhou Daguan's Record of Zhenla says: The four seasons are always like the fifth and sixth months, and they do not know frost or snow; for half the year there is rain and for half the year none at all; in the ninth month it rains every day, coming only in the afternoon, like the mountain showers of the north. The Record of Places Outside: in India there are the Ghat mountains dividing south and north; in the southern half it rains every day from the Beginning of Summer to the autumn equinox, and in the other half not a cloud gathers and the heat is unbearable, relieved only by a cool wind each day, which blows from the sea in the west from si to shen and from the land in the east from hai to yin; strange plants are too many to count. Of the seas, only the Pacific is shallow and has never had great storms. The Atlantic is very deep; from the Atlantic to the Great Ming Sea, south of forty-five degrees the winds always have fixed seasons; north of forty-five degrees the winds are chaotic, shifting suddenly through twenty-four directions. The tides of the Little Western Ocean are extremely high. At the Cape of Good Hope the wind and waves are very dangerous. At Malacca there is no wind, yet waves a li long suddenly arise. Only the sea of Africa near the equator often suffers from lack of wind, and the weather is so hot that food all spoils. The seas have no ice, but the northern sea freezes and piles up into icebergs. In the land of Goa there is no rain and it is always fine, and at Timor at thirty-six [degrees] there is much rain. The northern sea has little rain; in summer there is rain that only wets the soil, because it is far from the sun and the wind is high. In the country of Nile there is no rain and no cloud-vapour, only the Nile River rises once a year. The summit of Mount Atlas has always been clear and windless, because the place is uniquely high and the wind is too slight to be felt; clouds and rain are all below it.

《地類·海市山市 Sea Mirages and Mountain Mirages》

1
○泰山之市，因霧而成，或月一見，嘗于霧中見城闕旌旗、絃吹之聲，最為奇。海市或以為蜃氣，非也。張瑤星曰：登州鎮城署後太平樓，其下即海也，樓前對數島，海市之起，必由于此，每春秋之際，天色微陰則見，頃刻變幻。鹿征親見之：島下先湧白氣，狀如奔潮，河亭水榭，應目而具，可百餘間，文窗雕闌，無相類者。又一次，則中島化為蓮座，左島立竿懸幡，右島化為平臺，稍焉三島連為城堞，而幡為赤熾。睢陽袁可立為撫軍時，飲樓上，忽艨艟數十揚幡來，各立介士，甲光曜目，朱旆蔽天，相顧錯愕，急罷酒，料理城守，而船將抵岸，忽然不見，乃知是海市。遁齋閒覽曰：湘潭方廣寺，四月朔日在東壁，則照見維揚官府樓堞民舍，影著壁上。張邦基言：在四明舶局時，王璪粹昭往昌國普陀山禱雨，言洞甚深，但聞水聲，上復有洞穴，日光所射，可見數十步，菩薩現像于此中，餘密禱之，見碧玉闌楯，又現紋如珊瑚，于深處見菩薩像下身，僧言番賈來施而禱，或見淨瓶，或見瓔珞，或見橋梁，或無所睹，粹昭平生倔強，至是頗信。宓山笑曰：又弱一箇焉。林景熙蜃說曰：漢志載海旁蜃氣象樓臺，初未信也。避寇海濱，僮報海中忽湧數山，予登聚遠樓，見奇峰疊巘、城郭臺榭，中有浮圖老子宮，詭異千狀，近晡而滅，筆談所紀，往往類此。噫！秦之阿房，楚之章華，魏之銅雀，陳之臨春、結綺，突兀凌雲者何限，運去代遷，蕩為焦土浮埃，是亦蜃也。暄曰：氣映而物見。霧氣白湧，即水氣上升也。水能照物，故其氣清明上升者亦能照物，氣變幻，則所照之形亦變幻。暄邑近南關內有一小池，一日近晡，見有一騎倒影西岸水中，北馳而過。此所謂空中一大鏡，水漚窗隙則轉映之小鏡也。地上人物，空中無時不有，特氣聚則顯耳，故下論山海都地，悉得見之。中通曰：空氣之攝影，自下而上；池水之攝影，自上而下：故影皆倒。山海之氣橫攝市影，故影皆順。暄曰：倒影者轉攝則順。
○ The markets of Mount Tai form from mist and appear perhaps once a month; people have seen in the mist city towers and banners and heard the sound of strings and pipes, most marvellous. Some take sea mirages to be the breath of the shen clam, which is wrong. Zhang Yaoxing said: Behind the garrison office of Dengzhou is the Taiping Tower, below which is the sea; in front of the tower lie several islands, and sea mirages always arise from them; every spring and autumn, when the sky is slightly overcast, they are seen, changing in an instant. Luzheng saw it in person: first white vapour welled up below the islands like a rushing tide, and pavilions by the river and halls by the water appeared before the eye, some hundred bays of them, with patterned windows and carved railings, no two alike. Another time the central island turned into a lotus seat, a pole with a banner rose on the left island, the right island turned into a terrace, and shortly the three islands joined into city battlements, and the banners became red flames. When Yuan Keli of Suiyang was Grand Coordinator, he was drinking in the tower when suddenly several dozen warships came with banners raised, each manned with armoured soldiers, the gleam of armour dazzling and red flags covering the sky; everyone looked at one another in alarm, the drinking was hastily stopped and the defence of the city arranged; but as the ships were about to reach the shore they suddenly vanished, and then they knew it was a sea mirage. The Leisurely Reading of the Dun Studio says: At the Fangguang Monastery in Xiangtan, when the sun is on the east wall on the first day of the fourth month, it reflects the government buildings, towers, battlements and houses of Yangzhou, whose images appear on the wall. Zhang Bangji said: When he was at the Siming shipping bureau, Wang Zao (Cuizhao) went to Mount Putuo in Changguo to pray for rain and said the cave was very deep and only the sound of water could be heard; above it there was another cave, where the sunlight shone in so that one could see several dozen paces, and the bodhisattva manifested her image within; he prayed secretly and saw jade-green railings, then patterns like coral appeared, and deep inside he saw the lower half of the bodhisattva's image; the monks said that when foreign merchants came to give alms and pray, some saw a pure vase, some a necklace, some a bridge, and some saw nothing; Cuizhao, stubborn all his life, at this came rather to believe. Mishan laughs: Another one weakened! Lin Jingxi's Account of the Shen Clam says: The Treatise of the Han records that the breath of the shen clam beside the sea images towers and terraces, and at first I did not believe it. Taking refuge from bandits by the sea, a servant reported that several mountains had suddenly welled up in the sea; I climbed the Juyuan Tower and saw strange peaks and layered crags, city walls, terraces and pavilions, with pagodas and Daoist temples among them, a thousand weird shapes, which vanished toward late afternoon; what the Brush Talks records is often like this. Alas! The Afang of Qin, the Zhanghua of Chu, the Bronze Bird of Wei, the Linchun and Jieqi of Chen — how many soared towering to the clouds, yet when their fortune passed and the age changed they were swept away into scorched earth and floating dust: these too were mirages. Xuan says: When qi reflects, things are seen. The white welling of mist is water vapour rising. Water can reflect things, so its clear, bright, rising vapour can also reflect things, and as the vapour changes, the shapes reflected change too. Near the South Gate in my town there is a small pond, and one day toward late afternoon I saw the inverted image of a horseman in the water by the west bank, galloping north and past. This is what is meant by the air being one great mirror, while bubbles and window chinks are small mirrors that pass on the reflection. The people and things on the ground are in the air at all times; it is only when the vapour gathers that they become visible; so, as discussed below, mountains, seas, cities and lands can all be seen. Zhongtong says: The air captures images from below upward, and pond water captures images from above downward, so the images are all inverted. The vapour of mountains and seas captures the images of markets horizontally, so the images are all upright. Xuan says: An inverted image captured again becomes upright.

《地類·陽焰水影旱浪 Yang Flames, Water Images and Dry Waves》

1
○燕、趙、齊、魯之郊，春夏間野望曠遠處，如江河白水盪漾，近之則復不見，土人稱為陽焰。蓋真火之氣望日上騰，而為濕潤之水土所鬱留，搖颺重蒸，故遠見其動；莽蒼之色得氣而凝厚，故又見其一片浩然，如江河之流也。地出硝，山出硫，皆屬火化，磽瀉之原，冬寒生鹼，其理亦同。若江南沮淖，則但見蒼莽，不見陽焰，而硝與硫與鹼皆不自此產矣。晉苻堅載記曰：建元十七年，長安有水影，遠觀若水，視地則見人，至則止，亦謂之地鏡。陸友仁曰：宋寶祐六年四月，常州晉陵之黃泥岸亦有此異，相傳呼為旱浪。愚者曰：日中野馬飛星燁然者，陽焰之端也；奇者為水影旱浪。實則凡光生焰，焰自屬陽；凡光似鏡，鏡能吸影；光與光吸，常見他處之影于此處。雲分衢路，日射回薄，其氣平者為陽焰旱浪，其氣厚者為山市海市矣。暄曰：乙酉十月，嘗晨登高山見之，凡四望山腰以下，悉為白浪所浸，莽若江河，久之乃隱。兆兗曰：高山鋪雲海，常也，旱浪乃是白氣。
○ In the countryside of Yan, Zhao, Qi and Lu, in spring and summer, looking out across the fields at far, open places, it is like the white water of a river rippling, which disappears on approach; the local people call it yang flames. For the qi of true fire rises toward the sun and is held back by moist water and soil, swaying and steaming again and again, so from afar one sees it move; the colour of the wilds, receiving the qi, thickens, so one also sees a vast expanse like the flowing of a river. The ground producing saltpetre and the mountains sulphur are all transformations of fire, the sources of barren, draining soil; in the cold of winter it produces alkali, and the principle is the same. South of the Yangzi, in the marshy mud, one sees only the vast greyness and no yang flames, and saltpetre, sulphur and alkali are not produced there. The annals of Fu Jian in the History of the Jin say: In the seventeenth year of Jianyuan there was a water image at Chang'an which from afar looked like water, and looking at the ground one saw people, but on arrival it stopped; it was also called the earth mirror. Lu Youren said: In the fourth month of the sixth year of Baoyou of the Song, the same wonder occurred on the Yellow Mud Bank at Jinling in Changzhou, and it was popularly called the dry wave. The Foolish One says: The "wild horses" in the sun and the shimmering flying stars are the beginning of yang flames; the remarkable ones are water images and dry waves. In reality, all light produces flame, and flame belongs to yang; all light is like a mirror, and mirrors can draw in images; when light draws in light, images from elsewhere are often seen here. Where clouds divide the roads and the sun's rays shine back and forth, if the qi is level it makes yang flames and dry waves, and if the qi is thick it makes mountain mirages and sea mirages. Xuan says: In the tenth month of the yiyou year I once climbed a high mountain at dawn and saw it: everywhere below the mountainsides was submerged in white waves, vast as rivers, which only gradually disappeared. Zhaoyan says: A sea of clouds spread over high mountains is normal; the dry wave is white vapour.

○北史：屈茨川在龜茲國西北大山中，水如膏流出成川，行數里入地，狀如弟糊，甚臭，人齒髮落，服之再生，癘人服之亦愈。水經注：高奴洧水肥可然。夢溪筆談曰：鄜延出石油，即高奴縣脂水也，生于水際沙石，與泉水相雜，惘惘而出，土人以雉尾裛之，可採入□中。張華言：延壽縣南山有溝脂，始黃後黑，謂之石漆。方鎮編年錄謂之地脂。時珍以為石腦油，一曰硫黃油。今雲南、緬甸、廣之南雄皆有之。高麗有火油，臨邛有井油。外紀：寡斯大山、孛露有泉如脂膏，人取燃燈，或作油漆用。凡井油火，以水澆之愈熾，以地灰撲之則滅。中德曰：王烈得石髓以與嵇叔夜，成青石，亦此類乎？信坑水可接骨，繡水可以淬刀，皆水之有脂者。
○ The History of the Northern Dynasties: the Quci River lies in the great mountains northwest of Kucha; its water flows out like grease to form a river, which after several li enters the ground; it is like paste and very smelly; people whose teeth and hair have fallen out drink it and they grow again, and lepers who drink it are also cured. The Commentary on the Water Classic: the fat of the Wei River at Gaonu can be burned. The Brush Talks from Dream Brook says: Fu and Yan produce rock oil, which is the fat water of Gaonu County; it arises from the sand and stones at the water's edge mixed with spring water, oozing out slowly; the local people soak it up with pheasant tails and can collect it into [?]. Zhang Hua said: In the southern mountains of Yanshou County there is ditch-fat, first yellow and then black, called stone lacquer. The Chronological Record of the Garrisons calls it earth fat. [Li] Shizhen took it to be rock-brain oil, also called sulphur oil. Today Yunnan, Burma and Nanxiong in Guang all have it. Korea has fire oil and Linqiong has well oil. The Record of Places Outside: in the great mountains of Cusco in Peru there are springs like grease, which people take to burn in lamps or use as lacquer. Fire from well oil blazes more fiercely when water is poured on it, and is put out by smothering with earth-ash. Zhongde says: Wang Lie obtained stone marrow and gave it to Ji Shuye, and it turned into green stone — is this of the same kind? The water of Xinkeng can set bones, and the water of Xiu can be used to quench knives — these are all waters with fat.

○山海經：道州有煖谷。江浦志：秦少游述天下溫泉十三處。陸友仁言：匡廬、汝水、尉氏、駱谷、驪山、和州之惠濟、渝州之陳氏山居為最。述征記曰：東萊郡出溫泉，恒沸，鳥墜輒爛。安成紀云：宜陽鄉出溫泉，耒陽縣出溫泉。博物志云：不周雲川之水，溫如湯。梁川記云：漢水南有溫泉，周圍數千步，冬夏常沸，可熟雞子，未至二十里，便望見白氣衝天。袁山松宜都山川記曰：銀山縣溫泉，夏纔煖，冬大熱，上常有霧氣，浴病即愈。零陵縣記云：溫泉中有伏石，分流，其陰寒，其陽沸。劉義慶幽明錄曰：艾縣輔山有溫冷二泉，熱煮雞豚，冷若冰雪，流數丈而合。又泛水北合婁山鄤水之溫泉，至冬則煖。瀧水合雲水處有湯泉，投腥即熱，有細赤魚游之，即始興記之溫泉赤魚也。沮陽城大翮、小翮山有溫湯，下足便爛，不得言，言則愈熱。南陽宛縣有紫山湯谷。魯陽縣大和川合溫泉水，湯側有石銘曰皇女湯。武周縣有黃水，合火山水，有火井、火鼠，火井之旁有溫井。魏土地記曰：代桑乾城水北有溫湯療疾。下洛城橋山下有溫泉。溳水逕新城，會溫水，熱可潯雞。魚腹之鹽水與溫泉對注。汝寧商山有溫泉。南康郡志遠公朱沙泉詩曰：「一峰高拂白雲邊，下有朱砂如火燃。已是氣蒸千里煖，如何澗石溜溫泉？」即周景式所云主簿山之溫也，在黃龍山北麓，有二池。張勃吳錄曰：丹陽江乘縣湯泉六處，禽魚立爛，而煮豆穀終日不熟。筆乘亦載之。升庵言：雲南溫泉甚多，而安寧無硫磺氣。後周王有銘曰：白礬上徹，丹砂下沉。可知溫泉以丹砂為上。胡仔漁隱叢話取黃山朱砂泉，春微紅，可茗；驪山是礐泉。郭青螺豫章詩話言：福州之溫泉以溫田膏稻；廬陵、太興、新田二溫泉，分寧最熱；毛竹山泉則溫；臨川銅山二泉，一沸一寒；五峰山下者能潤人顏。今北京西山畫眉溫泉，大小湯凡三，而遵化縣北之溫，即古稱徐無城東之溫，戚繼光甃之。薊馬蘭峪溫泉最甘馨，天寧四溫，此其一耳。和州二溫，一無硫氣，智按即陸友仁所云惠濟泉也。敝鄉溫泉出于石穴。天下處處有之，不能悉記。先儒曰：地中陽氣遇濕而結為硫，雷火亦有硫氣，陽氣也。唐子西取硫置水，水不溫，以此駁之。熊三拔謂別無朱砂、礐、礬之別。金泥閣曰：西國有七十餘湯，各標主治，布那姑山皆硫，不聞泉湯也。蓋陰質陽氣為湯，陽質陰氣為焰為涼。湯泉以貯器還涼，湯泉冷泉各煮沸，必同時。晉長明燈色青不熱，質存氣易，此其徵矣。暄曰：溫泉療冷，與硫同治，其不作硫氣者，有所隔別，如重湯煮物也。然陽遇水而死為硫，水遇陽而死為礬，水火相切，互有所變，故礬可收硫毒。中通曰：陽氣聚而過，或出溫泉，或結硫，或為火井，非硫來溫泉也。凡硫、礐、礦皆陽氣為濕閟于土者，礐砒與銅礦相因，礦則最烈于爛物，惟硫專顯其熱耳。朱砂與溫泉無與。
○ The Classic of Mountains and Seas: Daozhou has a warm valley. The Jiangpu Gazetteer: Qin Shaoyou described thirteen hot springs in the realm. Lu Youren said: Those of Kuanglu, the Ru River, Weishi, Luogu, Mount Li, the Huiji of Hezhou and the Chen family's mountain dwelling in Yuzhou are the best. The Record of the Campaign says: Donglai Commandery produces hot springs that are always boiling, and birds falling in rot at once. The Annals of Ancheng say: Yiyang township produces hot springs, and Leiyang County produces hot springs. The Treatise on Broad Matters says: The waters of the Buzhou cloud-river are as warm as soup. The Record of the Liang River says: South of the Han River there is a hot spring several thousand paces round that boils winter and summer and can cook chicken eggs; twenty li before reaching it one can see white vapour rising to the sky. Yuan Shansong's Record of the Mountains and Rivers of Yidu says: The hot spring of Yinshan County is only warm in summer and very hot in winter, always with mist above it, and bathing cures illness at once. The Record of Lingling County says: In a hot spring there is a hidden stone that divides the flow, its yin side cold and its yang side boiling. Liu Yiqing's Records of the Hidden and Visible Worlds says: On Mount Fu in Ai County there are a hot and a cold spring, the hot one cooking chickens and pigs and the cold one like ice and snow, which join after flowing several zhang. Also the Fan River to the north joins the hot spring of the Man River at Mount Lou, which is warm in winter. Where the Long River meets the Yun River there is a hot spring that heats up when meat is thrown in, in which small red fish swim — the red fish of the hot spring in the Record of Shixing. On the Great Wing and Small Wing Mountains at Juyang city there are hot springs that scald the feet as soon as one steps in; one must not speak, for if one speaks it grows hotter. At Wan County in Nanyang there is the hot valley of Zishan. At the Dahe River in Luyang County, which joins the water of a hot spring, there is a stone inscription beside the spring reading "Imperial Daughter's Hot Spring." At Wuzhou County there is the Yellow River, which joins the water of the Volcano, and there are fire wells and fire rats, and beside the fire wells there are warm wells. The Record of the Lands of Wei says: North of the river at Sanggan city in Dai there is a hot spring that cures illness. There is a hot spring below Mount Qiao at Xialuo city. The Yun River passes Xincheng and meets a warm stream hot enough to scald chickens. The salt water of Yufu flows in opposite a hot spring. At Shangshan in Runing there is a hot spring. The Gazetteer of Nankang Commandery has Master Yuan's poem on the Cinnabar Spring: "One peak brushes high at the edge of the white clouds,
and below it cinnabar burns like fire. Since its vapour already warms a thousand li,
why should the stones of the stream flow with a hot spring?" This is the warm spring of Zhubu Mountain mentioned by Zhou Jingshi, at the north foot of Huanglong Mountain, with two pools. Zhang Bo's Record of Wu says: At Jiangcheng County in Danyang there are six hot springs, in which birds and fish rot at once, yet beans and grain boiled in them all day do not cook. The Brush Ride also records it. Sheng'an said: Yunnan has many hot springs, but those of Anning have no smell of sulphur. A king of the Later Zhou had an inscription: "White alum penetrates above, cinnabar sinks below." So one may know that hot springs with cinnabar are the best. Hu Zai's Collected Talks of the Fisherman Recluse cites the Cinnabar Spring of Mount Huang, slightly red in spring and good for tea, and says that Mount Li's is an arsenolite spring. Guo Qingluo's Remarks on the Poetry of Yuzhang says: The hot springs of Fuzhou warm the fields and enrich the rice; of the two hot springs of Luling, Taixing and Xintian, Fenning's is the hottest; the spring of Maozhu Mountain is warm; of the two springs of Tongshan in Linchuan one boils and one is cold; and the one below Wufeng Mountain can moisten the complexion. Today the Huamei hot spring in the Western Hills of Beijing has three large and small baths, and the hot spring north of Zunhua County is the one anciently called the hot spring east of Xuwu city, which Qi Jiguang lined with bricks. The hot spring of Malanyu in Ji is the sweetest and most fragrant, one of the four hot springs of Tianning. Of the two hot springs of Hezhou, one has no smell of sulphur; I, Zhi, note that it is the Huiji spring mentioned by Lu Youren. The hot spring of my home county comes out of a rock cave. They exist everywhere in the realm and cannot all be recorded. A former Confucian said: The yang qi in the earth meets damp and congeals into sulphur; lightning fire also has the smell of sulphur, because it is yang qi. Tang Zixi put sulphur in water and the water did not become warm, and refuted it with this. Sabatino de Ursis said there is no distinction of cinnabar, arsenolite or alum. Trigault said: In Western countries there are more than seventy hot baths, each labelled with the illnesses it treats; Mount [?] is all sulphur, yet one does not hear of hot springs there. For yin substance with yang qi makes hot springs, and yang substance with yin qi makes flames and coolness. Hot-spring water stored in a vessel turns cool again, and hot-spring and cold-spring water boiled separately will boil at the same time. The ever-burning lamp of the Jin, green in colour and not hot, with substance preserved and qi changed, is proof of this. Xuan says: Hot springs treat cold, as sulphur does; those that do not smell of sulphur are separated by something, like cooking in a double boiler. But yang meeting water and dying becomes sulphur, and water meeting yang and dying becomes alum; water and fire rubbing against each other change each other, so alum can absorb the poison of sulphur. Zhongtong says: When yang qi gathers in excess, it either issues as hot springs, or congeals into sulphur, or becomes fire wells; it is not that sulphur makes hot springs. Sulphur, arsenolite and ores are all yang qi shut in the earth by damp; arsenolite and arsenic go together with copper ore, and ore is the fiercest at rotting things; only sulphur shows its heat exclusively. Cinnabar has nothing to do with hot springs.

《地類·醴泉甘露有數說，本以甘而立名也 There Are Several Theories of Sweet Springs and Sweet Dew; Originally They Were Named for Their Sweetness》

1
○九成宮醴泉銘，魏徵撰，歐陽詢書：開皇時以杖導之，有泉湧出，因名醴泉。彥遠跋曰：漢儒言地出醴泉、天降甘露為瑞，則列子神瀵之類也。爾雅曰：甘露時降，萬物以嘉，謂之醴泉。智按：爾雅作甘露時降，則以時雨為醴泉。禹本紀言昆崙有醴泉，法苑珠林言太山東有醴泉，亦非含文嘉、中候所謂之醴泉也。九成宮之泉，猶山谷之題新喻醴泉耳。王仲任論衡因爾雅醴泉為甘雨，並疑景星非著天之大星，得毋以上文景風為景星乎？山海經：沃搖山民飲甘露。呂覽言三危揭雩之露，拾遺言昆崙甘露如丹。一統志：雅州蒙山有甘露，其凝如脂，其甘如飴，曰日膏、天油、神漿，言瑞應者名之也。度爾格國樹如椽栗，夜露凝為蜜，食之極甘。杜鎬云：甘露非瑞也，草木將枯，精華頓發，謂之雀餳。建康實錄：陳末覆舟山、蔣山松柏林冬日嘗出木醴，後主以為甘露，果瑞耶？王鞏曰：周章仲元宰衡陽，甘露降松竹如珠，取一枝示貢父，貢父曰：「爵餳也，飲之令人疾。」都尉高懿廳槐有甘露，鄭敬曰：「但樹汁耳。」智按：此厭瑞之說也，不礙為藥。本草：甘露服之潤五臟。大食國收露暴成甘露蜜。
○ The "Inscription on the Sweet Spring of the Jiucheng Palace," composed by Wei Zheng and written by Ouyang Xun: in the Kaihuang period, when [the ground] was probed with a staff, a spring gushed out, and so it was named Sweet Spring. Yanyuan's colophon says: The Han Confucians took the earth's producing sweet springs and Heaven's sending down sweet dew as auspicious omens; this is of the kind of the Liezi's divine spring. The Erya says: "When sweet dew falls in season and all things are thereby made good, it is called a sweet spring." I, Zhi, note: the Erya says "sweet dew falls in season," so it takes timely rain as the sweet spring. The Basic Annals of Yu says there is a sweet spring on Kunlun, and the Forest of Pearls in the Garden of the Law says there is a sweet spring east of Mount Tai; these too are not what the Hanwenjia and Zhonghou call sweet springs. The spring of the Jiucheng Palace is like Shangu's inscribing the sweet spring of Xinyu. Wang Zhongren's Balanced Discourses, following the Erya, takes the sweet spring as sweet rain, and even doubts that the "bright star" is a great star manifest in the sky — might it not be that he took the "bright wind" of the preceding text for the bright star? The Classic of Mountains and Seas: the people of Mount Woyao drink sweet dew. The Annals of Lü speak of the dew of Sanwei and Jieyu, and the Record of Gleanings says the sweet dew of Kunlun is like cinnabar. The Unified Gazetteer: on Mount Meng in Yazhou there is sweet dew, congealed like fat and sweet like malt sugar, called day-fat, heavenly oil and divine syrup — names given by those who speak of auspicious responses. In Turkey there are trees like rafter-chestnuts on which the night dew congeals into honey, extremely sweet to eat. Du Gao said: Sweet dew is not auspicious; when plants are about to wither, their essence suddenly comes out, and it is called sparrow-syrup. The True Records of Jiankang: at the end of the Chen, the pine and cypress woods of Fuzhou Mountain and Jiang Mountain once exuded tree-syrup in winter, which the Last Ruler took as sweet dew — was it really auspicious? Wang Gong said: When Zhou Zhang (Zhongyuan) was magistrate of Hengyang, sweet dew fell on the pines and bamboos like pearls; he took a branch and showed it to Gongfu, who said: "It is sparrow-syrup; drinking it makes people ill." When sweet dew appeared on the pagoda tree in the hall of the commandant Gao Yi, Zheng Jing said: "It is only tree sap." I, Zhi, note: these are views that disdain omens, which does not prevent its being a medicine. The Materia Medica: sweet dew taken moistens the five viscera. The Arabs collect dew and dry it in the sun to make sweet-dew honey.

《地類·砂異 Strange Sands》

1
○燉煌城南有沙丘城，太古時常自鳴，一名神沙山，其沙色黃，沙下有泉，曰沙井，人登岸，聞鼓角之聲。劉敬叔則云：涼州沙丘覆師積尸，後有鼓角聲。販玉璞者言其砂可以碾玉。塞外多沙，謂之沙漠，有流沙，沙逐水流。沈存中言：有陷河，一失足，人馬俱陷，望日落起暈則難行。一日沙海，行者驅駝先行，以駝微知其處也。遼陽有沙泉，泉湧出，其沙可磨寶石。度爾格國有沙海，沙乘大風如浪，行旅過之，常為所壓。泰西漏沙，色白，小圜如一，故以之代刻漏。杉關以北，細沙皆方棱而帶金晃之色。金幼孜北征記：封王佗號鳳凰山，有沙城海子，沙中穴即鼢鼠穴，沙有黃如金、白如玉，又有青黑者。全邊記曰：邊牆難修，忽然沙起，則沙與城平，北方十日之內必有一風，名黃沙，大甚者晝晦。北方風高地燥，沙塵易起，不似南方澤多地潤者也。鄧左之曰：章門飛沙，回抱如堤，隔宿坎之，明日復滿，偶有下者，已復故處。雩都固石之砂圖，相傳異之，則異矣。愚者曰：知其故則常矣。歐陽宗衡曰：固石距雩百餘里，溪中有洲，寬敞里許，周匝皆木石山屏之，中皆白砂平鋪，光潤如砌玉，欲見圖者隔宿禱之，其出也，烏跡魚文、卦例飛符、蜃閣海市、枯木寒鴉，無不畢具，錯綜變化，前後見者各出機軸，無有重複，其來無首，其去無尾，幻哉！暄曰：此如河凍瓦凌，恆有魚龍獅鳳及花草之形，皆風力所成者，世有其象，即有其氣，有其氣，即可以緣物而顯。中德曰：全邊略記是先祖賜環職方時所編。
○ South of the city of Dunhuang there is the city of sand dunes, which in high antiquity often sounded of itself; it is also called Mount Shensha; its sand is yellow, and below the sand there is a spring called the Sand Well; people climbing its bank hear the sound of drums and horns. Liu Jingshu, however, says that at the sand dunes of Liangzhou an army was overthrown and its corpses piled up, and afterward there were sounds of drums and horns. Dealers in jade say its sand can be used to grind jade. Beyond the frontier there is much sand, called the desert, and there is quicksand, which moves with water. Shen Cunzhong said: There are sinking rivers, and with one false step man and horse sink together; when a halo rises at sunset it is hard to travel. One kind is called the sand sea, and travellers drive camels ahead, because camels have a faint sense of where it is. In Liaoyang there is a sand spring that gushes out, and its sand can polish precious stones. In Turkey there is a sand sea, where the sand rides great winds like waves, and travellers passing through are often buried by it. The hourglass sand of the Far West is white, small and uniformly round, so it is used in place of the water clock. North of Shanguan the fine sand is all square-edged with a gleaming gold colour. Jin Youzi's Record of the Northern Campaign: [the mountain] where King Tuo was enfeoffed is called Phoenix Mountain, with a sand-city lake, and the holes in the sand are the burrows of mole-rats; the sand is yellow like gold, white like jade, and also green and black. The Record of the Whole Frontier says: The frontier wall is hard to keep in repair, for sand suddenly rises until it is level with the wall; in the north, within every ten days there is always a wind, called the yellow sand, and in very severe ones the day turns dark. In the north the wind is high and the land dry, so sand and dust rise easily, unlike the south, where there are many marshes and the land is moist. Deng Zuozhi said: The flying sand at Zhangmen curves round like a dyke; dig into it in the evening and the next day it is full again; occasionally some is carried down, but it has already returned to its old place. The sand pictures of Gushi in Yudu have been passed on as wonders, and so they are wonderful. The Foolish One says: Once one knows the reason, they are ordinary. Ouyang Zongheng says: Gushi is over a hundred li from Yu; in the stream there is an islet about a li across, screened all round by wooded rock hills, and inside it is all white sand spread flat, glossy as laid jade; those who wish to see the pictures pray the night before, and when they appear — crow tracks and fish patterns, hexagram diagrams and flying talismans, mirage towers and sea markets, withered trees and cold crows — everything is complete, intricately changing, and those who see them at different times each see their own designs, with no repetition; they come without a head and go without a tail — how illusory! Xuan says: This is like frozen rivers and icy tiles, which always have shapes of fish, dragons, lions, phoenixes and flowers, all formed by the force of the wind; since the world has such images, it has such qi, and having such qi, it can become visible by way of things. Zhongde says: The Brief Record of the Whole Frontier was compiled by my great-grandfather when he was restored to the Bureau of Operations.

《地類·立土橫土各有崖文 Upright and Horizontal Soils Each Have Patterns on Their Cliffs》

1
○河北多立土，人鑿洞以居，其崖上有紋者，削之而紋如故也。平賜府候馬驛澮河兩岸石土上皆手跡，或掌或拳，若印，削去之，其中復然。薛文清公以崖層紋為陰陽之摩盪，陸子淵曰：實是水所漾耳。虛舟子曰：大同山中有人骨，在山腰，上下五六十丈皆石，惟中一帶四五丈高皆髑髏脛節，齦齦然，關中山亦有之，豈上古之水所漾耶？何人骨能久留至此耶？如武夷之仙蛻，豈似之而非耶？蓋土石自然有文有色，故各有肖，猶冰凌之成花鳥形也。
○ North of the Yellow River there is much upright soil, and people dig caves to live in; where there are patterns on the cliff, if they are shaved off the patterns remain as before. At the Houma post station in Pingyang Prefecture, the stones and earth on both banks of the Hui River all have handprints, palms or fists, like seals; shaved off, they appear again inside. Xue Wenqing took the layered patterns of cliffs to be the rubbing and stirring of yin and yang; Lu Ziyuan said: "They are really what water has rippled." Master Xuzhou says: In the mountains of Datong there are human bones in the mountainside; above and below for fifty or sixty zhang it is all stone, but a band in the middle four or five zhang high is all skulls and shin bones, jagged and grinning; the mountains of Guanzhong also have them — were they rippled there by the waters of high antiquity? How could human bones remain here so long? Like the immortal's sloughed remains of Wuyi — are they something that resembles them but is not? For earth and stone naturally have patterns and colours, so each resembles something, just as icicles take the shapes of flowers and birds.

《地類·渾河鹵地有宜 Muddy Rivers and Saline Land Have Their Proper Uses》

1
○寧夏漢唐渠利支溝，用板壅泥而水過之，鹵泄板下，故稻比江南。上谷渾河入畿，柳慶亦有渾河。禹力溝洫，正所以收泄也。北塞風勁，紫土童然，惟夏月發生，除松柏外，秋即凋矣，故種植與南異，梁稷穭稻其宜耳。粵近海田淄泥，不用犁耙，五月乘桴播種，稱高秋翦，翦後潮大來，沃草乃燒林以煎鹽。杜粵人記：黃山不浮田泥上水浮土，乘板播種，與葑田異。
○ In Ningxia the Han and Tang canals make good use of branch ditches, damming the silt with boards so that the water passes over it and the salt drains out below the boards, so their rice rivals that of the south. The muddy river of Shanggu enters the capital region, and Liu and Qing also have muddy rivers. Yu's devoting his strength to ditches and channels was precisely for drainage. On the northern frontier the wind is fierce and the purple earth bare; things grow only in the summer months, and apart from pines and cypresses everything withers in autumn; so planting differs from the south, and millet, sorghum, wild grain and rice are what suit it. In Yue the fields near the sea are black mud, and no plough or harrow is used; in the fifth month they sow from rafts, calling it the "high autumn cutting"; after the cutting the tide comes in strongly, the grass is fertilized, and then the woods are burned to boil salt. Du Yueren records: Huangshan does not float; on the mud of the fields, where the water floats the soil, they sow from boards, which is different from floating-reed fields.

《占候類·占幾 Divining the Incipient》

1
○易四道以尚占終，受命如嚮，精攝神變，至誠神明，酬酢佑之，非情識妄想之所能及也。遠公曰：易以感為體。故無體不因乎幾，無可言矣。平素窮盡至理，臨時觸幾而應之，鏡聽筳篿，其為無心之符一也。嚴君平、關子明，蓋深得聖人尚占之情者乎？揚、邵、張、燧本以明其故，而應幾因之。人心自靈，蔽于欲忿，因二以明失得之報，凡民十皆得五，何況通神明、類萬物者，詎定以管、郭驚奇耶？三式卦影，莫不本此，以術造駴有之，故聖人藏諸易而不瀆告也。中履曰：不以通幾混質測，詎以質測廢通幾乎？兩間之象，無非類應配幾也；幽明之感，無非常變順逆也：人事天道，果二乎哉？聖人引觸，不可思議，自非神明，難信及此。
○ The Changes ends its four ways with valuing divination: it receives the mandate like an echo, the essence gathered and the spirit transforming; utmost sincerity and spiritual brightness respond and assist, which feelings, consciousness and deluded thought cannot reach. Master Yuan said: The Changes takes stimulus as its substance. So there is no substance that does not depend on the incipient, and there is nothing more to say. One exhausts ultimate principle in ordinary times and responds by touching the incipient when the moment comes; listening by mirror and divining by bamboo slips are alike tallies without intention. Yan Junping and Guan Ziming — did they not deeply grasp the sages' purpose in valuing divination? Yang, Shao, Zhang and Sui basically explained the reasons, and responding to the incipient followed from it. The human mind is naturally numinous but obscured by desire and anger; through the two it understands the rewards of gain and loss, and ordinary people get five out of ten — how much more one who comprehends spiritual brightness and categorizes the myriad things; why fix on Guan and Guo as marvels? The three methods and the hexagram shadows are all based on this; that some make it frightening by their arts is why the sages stored it in the Changes and did not tell it lightly. Zhonglü says: One should not confuse comprehension of the incipient with material measurement — how could one abolish comprehension of the incipient for material measurement? The images between Heaven and Earth are all categorical responses matching the incipient; the stimuli of the hidden and visible are all constancy and change, compliance and opposition: are human affairs and the Way of Heaven really two? The sages' drawing forth and touching is inconceivable, and only one with spiritual brightness could believe in it to this degree.

《占候類·藏智于物 Hiding Wisdom in Things》

1
○說卦傳類萬物于八卦，悟此幾乎？禮運本天肴地，舉五六十二之相旋，悟此幾乎？以天盤加地盤，而生克制化，隨變應節，易軌三式，皆有經緯宇宙焉。常運，經也；占以人歷其位者，緯也。焦熱冱寒，視歷此者何人耳。愚者嘗言：以推移之宙消貪心，以規矩之宇辨物則，而一萬俱畢矣。去者已去，來者未來，今又逝也，貪執何為？達人樂此，而荒狂又竊之，故必明六合五破之宇，處處皆然，乃知物之則，即天之則，即心之則也。管子曰宙合，謂宙合宇也，灼然宙輪于宇，則宇中有宙，宙中有宇。春夏秋冬之旋輪，即列于五方之旁羅盤，而析幾類應，孰能逃哉？聖人不惡賾動，藏智于物，故圖書象數，舉其端幾，而衍易以前民用，損益盈虛，推行變化，在其中矣，要不離乎統類配應之時位也。人人本具，愚夫取讖，猶且不爽，況齋戒至誠而叩神明靜正之士乎？故曰：至誠如神。中通曰：宙輪宇中，此真破天荒之一決也。心本不自知其心，愈窮愈幻，而心所造之事、心所見之物，森然不紊也，自非格物之比量，豈享物格之現量哉？消心之言，止貴平心，心平乃虛，虛乃明，乃能燭物之理，而不被物惑，亦不為談物者所惑，亦不為掃物者所惑，果然不惑，則因物付物之本空也。卦氣、太宮、洞極、元包、皇極、潛虛、蔡疇以至太乙用十六、象正三推八八，皆衍數之節度，而理自冥應者也。
○ The "Discussion of the Trigrams" categorizes the myriad things under the eight trigrams — does it not grasp this incipience? The "Evolution of Rites" bases itself on Heaven and models itself on Earth, citing the mutual rotation of five, six and twelve — does it not grasp this incipience? Placing the heaven plate on the earth plate, generating, overcoming, controlling and transforming, changing and responding to the nodes — the tracks of the Changes and the three methods all have warp and weft spanning space and time. The constant circulation is the warp; divination of a person's passing through its positions is the weft. Scorching heat or freezing cold depends on who passes through it. The Foolish One once said: use time, which moves on, to dissolve the greedy mind, and space, with its compass and square, to distinguish the patterns of things, and the one and the myriad are complete. What has gone is gone, what is to come has not come, and the present is passing too — what is the use of greedy clinging? The enlightened delight in this, and the wild and reckless steal it; so one must understand the space of the six directions and five divisions as being so everywhere, and then one knows that the pattern of things is the pattern of Heaven and the pattern of the mind. Guanzi spoke of zhouhe, meaning time combining with space; clearly, time revolves within space, so that within space there is time and within time there is space. The revolving wheel of spring, summer, autumn and winter is arranged on the side-net of the five directions, and analysing the incipient by categorical response, who can escape it? The sages do not dislike the complex and moving but hide wisdom in things, so the River Chart and Luo Writing and the images and numbers raise their incipient beginnings, and the Changes was elaborated to anticipate the people's needs; diminishing and increasing, filling and emptying, carrying on and transforming are all within it, essentially not departing from the times and positions of unified categories and matching correspondences. Everyone has this inherently; even fools taking omens are not wrong — how much more scholars who fast with utmost sincerity and knock at spiritual brightness in stillness and uprightness? So it is said: Utmost sincerity is like a spirit. Zhongtong says: Time revolving within space — this is truly a decision that breaks through the wilderness. The mind does not by itself know the mind, and the more one probes the more illusory it becomes, yet the affairs the mind creates and the things the mind sees are dense and orderly; without the inferential knowledge of investigating things, how could one enjoy the direct knowledge of things investigated? Talk of dissolving the mind values only calming the mind; when the mind is calm it is empty, when empty it is bright, and then it can illuminate the principles of things without being deluded by things, nor deluded by those who talk of things, nor deluded by those who sweep things away; if one is truly undeluded, then giving things to things is the original emptiness. The trigram qi, the Taixuan, the Dongji, the Yuanbao, the Huangji, the Qianxu, Cai's Categories, down to the Taiyi's use of sixteen and the Images Rectified's three projections of eight-eights, are all measures of the elaborated numbers, with which principle silently corresponds.

《占候類·雨占 Divining Rain》

1
○黃子發相雨書曰：以戊申日候日欲入時，日上有冠雲，不論大小，四方黑者大雨，青者小雨。北斗中有雲，後五日雨。河中有雲三枚相連，三日大雨。六甲日四方雲皆合者，即雨。躍魚雲遊疾者，雨。諺云：雷多于戊、已、丙、丁日下。趙雲州云：凡遇戊午、已未日，天必變雨，或亢、壁宿直日可免。吳諺：正月建三亥，湖田變成海。陸泳吳下田家志曰：初三月下有橫，初四暗盤，有月交，二十五日有雨，則主久雨。三月三日聽蝦蟆叫，驗湖水之高下。今俗皆唱神以視所止為驗，謂之打水。又諺曰：春雨甲子，乘船入市；夏雨甲子，赤地千里；秋雨甲子，禾頭生耳；冬雨甲子，飛雪千里。又云：戊午元同甲子期，始終七日最稀奇，七日多晴兩月燥，七日多雨兩月泥。甲申雨主米暴貴，春主五谷不收，夏主傷田禾，秋主六畜死，冬主人多病。方言云：甲申猶自可，乙酉怕殺人。又壬子雨則久陰。又云：甲子豐年丙子旱，戊子蝗蟲庚子亂，若逢壬子水滔滔，俱在正月上旬看；不怕正月二十陰，只怕二十一日晴；三月三日晴，桑葉挂銀瓶；六月初三晴，山筱盡枯零；四月十六鳥漉漉，不論高低一齊熟；四月廿八小分龍，是日當雨惡乾風；六月西風吹遍草，八月無風三伏熱；重陽無雨一冬晴，立冬之日尤分明；雷若震在十月裡，來年五月內有水；久晴視戊已，久雨視丙子；夏壬子雨，牛食多腐；雨下六壬頭，低田可便休。愚者曰：此數者與壬子水日占雨者同，豈有一日九州皆同下雨之理？青田以此日望氣占之，氣在天，然去地近，移數十百里即變。其法分五色五行之形，以黎明從高處望之，與日辰相生或相解論理。隔日陰晴，于日出入前察之最驗，大率春夏定驗，秋冬應稍遲。田家五行以多積而驗之，風雨故自有候，其以甲子言者，理不出于盈虛，而觸其端幾耳，所謂驗者，但十得五耳。邵子曰：聖人知九餘三，凡人知三闕九。喧曰：以各方之土地雲物驗雨暘者，十應七八；以各方之時令晦朔驗雨暘者，十應二三；以干支合者，不應也。中通曰：風角詳後漢李尋、郎顗傳，後實本黃帝八風也。介葛盧、公冶長、管輅皆能鳥占，此與干支諸例同一觸幾，惟相人、堪輿、望氣猶屬質測也。黑室辨絲，望氣乃驗，觸幾以誠，而平時窮理，斷非苟然。
○ Huang Zifa's Book of Reading Rain says: On a wushen day watch the sun as it is about to set; if there is a crown of cloud above the sun, whatever its size, if it is black on all sides there will be heavy rain, if green, light rain. If there is cloud in the Northern Dipper, it will rain five days later. If there are three clouds joined in the [Milky] River, there will be heavy rain within three days. If on the six jia days the clouds on all four sides come together, it will rain at once. If clouds like leaping fish move fast, it will rain. A proverb says: Thunder falls mostly on wu, ji, bing and ding days. Zhao Yunzhou says: Whenever a wuwu or jiwei day comes, the weather will turn to rain, unless the Neck or Wall lodge is on duty that day. A Wu proverb: If the first month has three hai days, the lake fields turn into sea. Lu Yong's Record of the Farmers of Wu says: If on the third the moon has a bar beneath it, on the fourth a dark disc, and there is a lunar crossing, and it rains on the twenty-fifth, it portends long rain. On the third day of the third month listen to the frogs croaking to tell the height of the lake water. Today it is the custom to sing to the god and take where it stops as the sign, which is called "beating the water." Another proverb says: Rain on a jiazi day in spring — take a boat to market; in summer — red earth for a thousand li; in autumn — ears grow on the crops; in winter — flying snow for a thousand li. It also says: Wuwu shares the same term as jiazi, from start to end seven days, most rare; seven days mostly fine, two months dry; seven days mostly rain, two months mud. Rain on a jiashen day portends rice rising suddenly in price; in spring it portends failure of the five grains, in summer harm to the crops, in autumn the death of livestock, and in winter much illness among people. A saying goes: "Jiashen is still tolerable; yiyou frightens people to death." Also, rain on a renzi day means long overcast. It also says: Jiazi, a year of plenty; bingzi, drought; wuzi, locusts; gengzi, disorder; if renzi comes, floods of water — look for them all in the first ten days of the first month; do not fear cloud on the twentieth of the first month, fear only a fine twenty-first; fine on the third of the third month, the mulberry leaves hang silver bottles; fine on the third of the sixth month, the mountain bamboo all withers; on the sixteenth of the fourth month birds are wet, high and low all ripen together; the twenty-eighth of the fourth month is the minor dividing of the dragons, and it should rain that day, a dry wind is bad; in the sixth month the west wind blows over all the grass, in the eighth month no wind, the dog days are hot; if there is no rain at the Double Ninth the whole winter is fine, especially clear on the Beginning of Winter; if thunder sounds in the tenth month, there will be floods in the fifth month next year; after a long fair spell watch wu and ji, after long rain watch bingzi; rain on a renzi day in summer, the oxen's fodder mostly rots; when rain falls at the head of the six ren, give up on the low fields. The Foolish One says: These are the same as divining rain by the water of renzi days — how could it rain on the same day in all nine provinces? Qingtian observed the qi on these days to divine; the qi is in the sky, but it is near the earth, and moving tens or hundreds of li it changes. His method divides the shapes of the five colours and five phases, watches them at dawn from a high place, and reasons by their mutually producing or dissolving the day and hour. For whether the next day will be cloudy or fine, observing before sunrise and sunset is the most reliable; generally in spring and summer it is certain, in autumn and winter the response is a little slower. The Five Phases of the Farmer verifies them by much accumulation, and wind and rain naturally have their signs; as for speaking by jiazi, the principle does not go beyond filling and emptying, and only touches their incipient beginnings; what is called verification only gets five out of ten. Master Shao said: The sage knows nine and leaves three; the ordinary man knows three and lacks nine. Xuan says: Verifying rain and sunshine by the land and cloud-signs of each region, seven or eight out of ten respond; verifying them by the seasons and new and full moons of each region, two or three out of ten respond; by combinations of stems and branches, none respond. Zhongtong says: Wind divination is detailed in the biographies of Li Xun and Lang Yi in the History of the Later Han, but is really based on the Yellow Emperor's eight winds. Jie Gelu, Gongye Chang and Guan Lu could all divine by birds, which is the same touching of the incipient as the stem-and-branch methods; only physiognomy, geomancy and observing qi still belong to material measurement. Distinguishing silk in a dark room, observing qi is then verified; touching the incipient with sincerity while exhausting principle in ordinary times is certainly not a casual matter.

《占候類·南北方雨暘之異 Differences in Rain and Sunshine between South and North》

○南方近日之度，火土之氣時為太陽暴照，如人身熱則汗液也。北方遠日，又春夏多風，致火土之氣疏越，故雨恒少。閩中春月恒雨，夏月日出則酷暑，旋復有雨，如滿剌伽國處赤道下，四時皆裸，賴日日有雨以解其蘊隆，可見暘為雨之根也。互詳風土條、雨征條。水則毀，三歲處；木則康，三歲處；火則旱。糴有時斂則決，萬物不過三歲而發矣。天下六歲一穰，六歲一康，凡十二歲一饑。從寅至未，陽也。陰且盡之歲，收五穀以應陽之至也；陽且盡之歲，發糴以收野宅、牛馬、棺木，以應陰之至也：此皆十倍，次五倍。甲貨之戶粢，乙貨黍，丙貨赤豆，丁貨稻，戊貨麥，已貨大豆，庚貨穬，辛貨果，壬癸無貨。由此觀之，其歲候之所自始乎？要亦盈虛消息之概也。潛草也：四立分至，占風方雨候雲氣，此中節之幾乎？量日月影，非矣。雄雷旱，雌雷水，亦以初幾言之。東風生蟲，西風殺蟲。稻自發科揚花後忌東風，若南與西不忌也。夏至後十日內有一日北風，則蟲盡去，然遲稻又惡北風。諺曰：禾畏東風麥畏西，棉花畏北。栽忌東風，釀厭東風，寒則物堅也。芋喜東風，芋毒不擇也，美草則嬌。春西風無雨，東則雨；夏北風乃雨；夏發東風，秋間生蟲。清明喜晴，芒種宜遲雨，八月朔雨角田下熟，九月朔起占來年穀，南風米貴，重九尤關，十月霧曰沬露，相去二百五日水至。冬至囊諸種窖之，五十日取驗多寡，則知來歲所宜，此氣幾也。中元雨曰翹籩。除夕、元旦之秤水，猶冬至土炭也。徵諸物者：梅少實則秫少，杏花多則宜豆，穀雨雨則多魚，上已則占蛙聲，鵜鶘主水，檀葉定候。鳳仙芒種前花，野薇立夏前花，藕夏至前花，筍透林，鯽脊骨曲，皆主水。潁諺曰：黃鷷口禁，喬麥斗。夏中候黃鷷不鳴，則喬麥可廣種也。六月六日雨主蟲。清晨雨，蟲生園畦；上午雨，蟲生田；下午雨，蟲生山。薜荔果裸，劈開滿者年豐。東坡曰：益智候禾。南海產益智，花實皆長穗而分三節，觀其上中下節以候早中晚之豐凶。鄭所南曰：瓊花占歲，猶之師曠以先生占草也。薺先生豐，葶藶苦，水藻惡，蒺藜則旱，艾則病，蓬則流。宓山愚者曰：自杜臺卿廣月令，而察物變、紀種采者，二十四氣咸列程品，要而言之，分至八節舉其概耳，寔以物性之陰陽應地上之寒暑，以太陽緯度為進退也。中通曰：冬春種植，二分移接，清明浸稻種，白露後種麥菜，此八節之準也。如元日浴蠶，連取柏露；二月采黃精、術、百合；醃鴨卵勿過清明；傅肱蟹譜：二月初三，蟛蚏出海；清明後種姜；社日治果木；驚蟄禁蟻；上庚納鯉鱉；上巳造曲母，采何首烏，煉松脂；寒食留飯懸乾；四月採茈胡、稀薟、夏枯、烏頭；烏賊過小滿小，青梅過小滿黃，蠶過小滿無絲，葵過小滿則長；芒種栽梔，插榴杉；端午收竹神水、桃柏葉，練葛，紅花、術，取鵲腦，豕首血和雄黃；夏至取螻蛄夜至者；三伏造醬，餌硫，曝瓜菜種；六月六儲冰；七夕采萍，收角蒿；八月脫果包鶴膝，采大黃；九日占風，制木，種鶯粟，或曰中秋午種乃生；霜後取漆，採桑葉、金櫻，凡藥以秋收者多；十月壅椒，種參耆；十一月種桑，取熊膽；冬至薺生苖，種附子；十二月伐木不蛀，四月七月亦可；臘雪應百二十日之雨，不特凍土殺蝻也，嶺南以霜多亦占來豐；臘收楝汁，插柳椐。此但隨節紀之，亦可前後，地視日度、雨暘最切耳，壬丙定梅，豈足拘乎？
○ The south is near the sun's degrees, and the qi of fire and earth is at times scorched by the sun, like a hot human body that sweats. The north is far from the sun, and moreover there is much wind in spring and summer, which disperses the qi of fire and earth, so there is always little rain. In Min it always rains in spring, and in summer when the sun comes out it is fiercely hot and soon there is rain again; it is like the country of Malacca, which lies under the equator, where people go naked all year and depend on daily rain to relieve the pent-up heat; one can see that sunshine is the root of rain. See also the entries on climate and on signs of rain. When [the year star is in] water there is ruin, lasting three years; in wood there is plenty, lasting three years; in fire there is drought. Grain purchased at the right times and stored is then released; all things come round within three years. In the realm there is a bumper harvest every six years and plenty every six years, and a famine every twelve years. From yin to wei is yang. In years when yin is about to be exhausted, one gathers the five grains in response to the coming of yang; in years when yang is about to be exhausted, one sells grain and buys up land, houses, oxen, horses and coffins in response to the coming of yin: these all yield tenfold, the next fivefold. Jia years [the crop] is millet, yi years broomcorn millet, bing years red beans, ding years rice, wu years wheat, ji years soybeans, geng years barley, xin years fruit, and ren and gui years nothing. Seen from this, is it not where the seasons of the year begin? In essence it is also the general pattern of filling and emptying, waxing and waning. The Qian Drafts: At the four beginnings, the equinoxes and solstices, divining by the direction of the wind and the cloud-qi of the rain — is this not the incipience of keeping the measure? Measuring the shadows of the sun and moon is wrong. Male thunder means drought and female thunder rain — this too speaks of the first incipience. East winds produce insects and west winds kill insects. Rice, after it tillers and flowers, dislikes east winds but does not mind south and west winds. If within ten days after the summer solstice there is one day of north wind, the insects are all gone; but late rice dislikes north winds. A proverb says: Grain fears the east wind, wheat fears the west, and cotton fears the north. Transplanting avoids east winds and brewing dislikes east winds, because cold makes things firm. Taro likes east winds, because taro is poisonous and indiscriminate; fine grasses are delicate. In spring a west wind brings no rain and an east wind rain; in summer a north wind brings rain; if an east wind blows in summer, insects will breed in autumn. At Qingming one wants fine weather; at Grain in Ear rain should be late; rain on the first of the eighth month means the corner fields ripen low; on the first of the ninth month one begins to divine next year's grain, a south wind meaning dear rice, the Double Ninth being especially important; fog in the tenth month is called "foam dew," and two hundred and five days later water will come. At the winter solstice, bag the various seeds and store them in a cellar, and after fifty days take them out to test how much [they weigh], and one will know what suits the coming year: this is the incipience of qi. Rain at the Ghost Festival is called "upturned baskets." Weighing water on New Year's Eve and New Year's Day is like hanging earth and charcoal at the winter solstice. As for verification by things: when plums bear little fruit, glutinous millet will be scarce; when apricot blossoms are many, it suits beans; rain at Grain Rain means many fish; at Shangsi divine by the frogs' croaking; pelicans portend floods; sandalwood leaves fix the season. Balsam flowering before Grain in Ear, wild roses flowering before the Beginning of Summer, lotus flowering before the summer solstice, bamboo shoots coming through the wood, and crucian carp with curved backbones all portend floods. A proverb of Ying says: When the yellow oriole holds its tongue, buckwheat yields a dou. In midsummer, watch whether the yellow oriole sings; if not, buckwheat can be sown widely. Rain on the sixth of the sixth month portends insects. Rain early in the morning, insects breed in the gardens; rain in the morning, insects breed in the fields; rain in the afternoon, insects breed in the hills. When the fruits of the climbing fig are bare, those which are full when split open mean a good harvest. Dongpo said: The yizhi fruit foretells the rice. In the Southern Sea yizhi is produced, whose flowers and fruits all form long spikes divided into three sections; by looking at the upper, middle and lower sections one foretells the harvest of the early, middle and late crops. Zheng Suonan said: Divining the year by the jade-flower is like Shi Kuang's divining by the grass that comes up first. If shepherd's purse comes up first there is plenty; draba, bitterness; water-weed, bad times; caltrop, drought; mugwort, illness; tumbleweed, wandering. The Foolish One of Mishan says: Since Du Taiqing extended the Monthly Ordinances, observing the changes of things and recording sowing and gathering, the twenty-four qi have all been listed with their procedures; in essence, the eight nodes of the equinoxes and solstices give the outline, really making the yin and yang of the nature of things correspond to the cold and heat on earth, advancing and retreating with the sun's latitude. Zhongtong says: Planting in winter and spring, transplanting and grafting at the two equinoxes, soaking rice seed at Qingming, sowing wheat and vegetables after White Dew — these are the standards of the eight nodes. Thus: on New Year's Day bathe the silkworm eggs and take cypress dew; in the second month gather Solomon's seal, atractylodes and lily bulbs; pickle duck eggs not later than Qingming; Fu Gong's Manual of Crabs: on the third of the second month the pengqi crabs come out of the sea; plant ginger after Qingming; tend fruit trees at the she festival; at the Awakening of Insects forbid ants; on the first geng day put in carp and turtles; at Shangsi make the ferment starter, gather fleeceflower root and refine pine resin; at Cold Food keep rice and hang it to dry; in the fourth month gather bupleurum, siegesbeckia, self-heal and aconite; cuttlefish after Lesser Fullness are small, green plums after Lesser Fullness are yellow, silkworms after Lesser Fullness have no silk, and mallows after Lesser Fullness grow tall; at Grain in Ear plant gardenias and set pomegranate and fir cuttings; at the Double Fifth collect the divine water of bamboo and peach and cypress leaves, scour hemp-cloth, [gather] safflower and atractylodes, take magpie brains, and mix pig's-head blood with realgar; at the summer solstice take mole crickets that come at night; in the dog days make sauces, take sulphur, and dry melon and vegetable seeds in the sun; on the sixth of the sixth month store ice; on the Seventh Night gather duckweed and collect jiaohao; in the eighth month remove the fruit bags and gather crane's-knee and rhubarb; on the ninth divine by the wind, work wood and sow poppies, some say sown at noon at Mid-Autumn they will grow; after frost take lacquer and gather mulberry leaves and Cherokee rose, most medicines being gathered in autumn; in the tenth month earth up pepper and sow ginseng and astragalus; in the eleventh month plant mulberries and take bear's gall; at the winter solstice shepherd's purse sprouts, and aconite is planted; in the twelfth month fell trees and they will not be worm-eaten, as also in the fourth and seventh months; snow in the twelfth month answers to rain a hundred and twenty days later, and not only freezes the soil and kills the locust larvae; in Lingnan much frost also foretells a good harvest next year; in the twelfth month collect chinaberry juice and plant willow and ju cuttings. These are merely recorded by the nodes and may be earlier or later; the lands should be judged by the sun's degrees, and rain and sunshine are most pressing — how can fixing the plum rains by ren and bing days be binding?
