《鳥獸類上 Birds and Beasts, Part One》

《鳥獸類上·鵝 Geese》

1
○以一歲再伏者為種，三雌三雄，雄宜少，雌宜老。八月生卵可菢，五六月生卵熱，不堪菢，則拔去其兩翅十二翮以停之。鵝母頭欲小，口齕有小珠，滿五者生卵多，滿三者為次。鵝不食蟲，諺曰鵝齋。
○ Those that brood twice in a year are kept for breeding, three females to three males, the males preferably young and the females old. Eggs laid in the eighth month can be brooded; eggs laid in the fifth and sixth months are too hot to brood, so twelve quills are plucked from her two wings to stop her. The mother goose should have a small head; if the edge of her bill has small beads, she lays many eggs if there are five, and is second-rate if there are three. Geese do not eat insects; the proverb calls it the goose's fast.

《鳥獸類上·鴨 Ducks》

1
○宜六雌一雄。春卵四月出者良。端午不放栖，只乾食，不與水，則日日生卵。禽惟鴨不能菢子。養湖鴨者，砌土池，置千卵，而以粟火溫其外，時至則出，或以稻伏。養者取其卵聽之，而別雌雄，留其雌者。然此小鴨買與人養，多死，彼自有藥解其火毒。
○ Six females to one male is suitable. Spring eggs hatched in the fourth month are good. If at the Duanwu festival they are not let out to roost and are fed only dry food without water, they lay eggs every day. Of fowl, only ducks cannot brood their eggs. Those who raise lake ducks build earthen pits, put in a thousand eggs and warm them from outside with a millet-husk fire, and in due time they hatch; or they are incubated in rice. The raisers take the eggs and listen to them to distinguish male and female, keeping the females. But when these ducklings are sold to others to raise, many die; the raisers have their own drug to neutralize the fire poison.

《鳥獸類上·雞 Chickens》

1
○霜降時尾之者良。母雞下卵時，逐月內雜飼麻子，永不肯菢，常生卵。忌柳柴煙，能損目。有病，灌以清油。傳瘟，磨鐵漿染米與食。水眼，以白礬傅之。養雞無雄，取其卵蘸童便，就釜底染煤而育。凡雞鵝鴨伏卵，慮有擊聲，卵毈，故雷震必以鐵置其巢。埤雅曰：伏卵忌磨，鵝惡絞車聲。凡初生必饑之，過三晝夜乃鷇之，不則內閉。烏骨雞舌黑者，骨黑，則肉黑。泰和低腳者補。外紀：墨是可國有雞大於鵝，吻上有鼻，可伸縮如象。馬拿莫大巴是黑人國，產雞皆黑。老雄雞胸中有物，如道人裸身跪而反接。雞首塗鸕鶿血，即伏不能起。雞生三日即能自食。雞伏二十日乃碎啄而出，出三日乃能行。卵留二三月，即伏不出矣。少夫曰：雞兩眼旁通，可以治耳。愚者少見術家釘雞眼於柱，少頃拔之，無血，雞走如故，蓋雞兩眼止隔一膜也。中德曰：雞子開小竅，去黃白，入露水，糊以油紙，日中曬之，離地三四尺。中履曰：小雞為群雞踏欲死者，急取本盆覆之，以箸敲其底，自活。
○ Those bred around Frost's Descent are good. When a hen is laying, if hemp seed is mixed into her feed throughout the month, she will never want to brood and keeps laying. They avoid willow-wood smoke, which can damage their eyes. If ill, dose them with clear oil. For epidemics, grind iron slurry, dye rice with it and feed it to them. For watery eyes, apply alum. If chickens are kept without a cock, take their eggs, dip them in a boy's urine, colour them with soot from the bottom of the pot, and hatch them. Whenever chickens, geese or ducks are brooding, one fears loud noises that addle the eggs, so in thunder one must put iron in the nest. The Piya says: Brooding eggs avoid the mill, and geese hate the sound of a windlass. All newly hatched chicks must be starved for three days and nights before being fed, otherwise their insides close up. Black-boned chickens with black tongues have black bones and so black flesh. The short-legged ones of Taihe are tonic. The Record of the Outer Regions: The country of Mexico has chickens larger than geese, with a nose on the beak that can extend and retract like an elephant's. Monomotapa is a country of black people, and its chickens are all black. Old cocks have a thing in their breasts like a Daoist kneeling naked with hands tied behind. If a chicken's head is smeared with cormorant blood, it crouches and cannot rise. Chicks can feed themselves three days after hatching. A hen broods twenty days before [the chicks] break the shell by pecking, and three days after hatching they can walk. Eggs kept two or three months will not hatch when brooded. Shaofu says: A chicken's two eyes are connected sideways, and can cure the ears. When young, the Foolish One saw a conjurer nail a chicken's eye to a post and pull it out after a moment without bleeding, and the chicken walked away as before, because the chicken's two eyes are separated only by a membrane. Zhongde says: Make a small hole in an egg, remove the yolk and white, fill it with dew, seal it with oiled paper and dry it in the sun three or four chi above the ground. Zhonglü says: When a chick is trodden by the flock and about to die, quickly cover it with its own basin and tap the bottom with a chopstick, and it revives.

《鳥獸類上·鴿 Pigeons》

1
○穀食飼以硝水，能取食，漚之積其餘，再飼，易伏卵。凡病，取古墻中螺朽殼并續隨子、銀杏搗為丸。狗糞中米，鴿食則死。鴿交獨雌乘雄。歲十二生。白蟻惡鴿與竹雞聲。
○ Fed grain soaked in saltpetre water, they can take food; steep the remainder and feed it again, and they easily brood. For any illness, take rotted snail shells from old walls together with caper spurge and ginkgo, and pound them into pills. If pigeons eat rice from dog dung, they die. In pigeon mating only the female mounts the male. They breed twelve times a year. Termites hate the sound of pigeons and bamboo partridges.

《鳥獸類上·雉 Pheasants》

1
○雉聞雷而雊，常與蛇交，其子在地，遇雪則深入為蛟蜃。爾雅所列，以色分之。其飛高不過丈，故城以雉度。以文，故士用為贄。性毅，故武士飾之。然藏頭而不能藏尾。外紀：既未蠟之雉有重十五六斤者。
○ The pheasant crows on hearing thunder and often mates with snakes; its young on the ground, when it snows, go deep and become jiao and shen. The Erya distinguishes them by colour. It flies no higher than a zhang, so walls are measured in zhi. Because of its patterns, scholars use it as a gift on first meeting. Because of its resolute nature, military men adorn themselves with it. But it hides its head and cannot hide its tail. The Record of the Outer Regions: Java has pheasants weighing fifteen or sixteen jin.

《鳥獸類上·翡翠 Kingfishers》

1
○漢書越王獻武帝翠鳥五十雙，即翡翠也。顧岕曰：翡翠生於黎之茂林，晴霽日始出。愚見廣州七夕以翠艇遊。此鳥隨處有之，廣南多也。
○ The History of the Han says the King of Yue presented fifty pairs of green birds to Emperor Wu; these are kingfishers. Gu Jie says: Kingfishers live in the dense forests of the Li, and come out only on clear days. I saw in Guangzhou on the Seventh Evening people boating in kingfisher boats. This bird is found everywhere, but most in Guangnan.

《鳥獸類上·鶴 Cranes》

1
○鶴聞降真香則降，其糞能化石。有白者、灰色者。夜以一足立而睡。儲泳曰：道士用活雄鳩血書符，殺命助靈以召鶴，雀取紫降真香茶煮出油而焚之，鶴即至。夷門廣牘曰：鶴脛脆易折，折者截青竹三四寸，手劈兩片，掘白頸蚯蚓數條鋪管中，夾而縛定，仍取數條與啖之，候飯頃即如故。足有龜紋者佳。
○ Cranes descend when they smell rosewood incense, and their droppings can dissolve stone. There are white ones and grey ones. At night they sleep standing on one leg. Chu Yong says: Daoists write talismans in the blood of a live male turtledove, killing life to aid their magic, to summon cranes, or take purple rosewood incense boiled in tea until oil comes out and burn it, and cranes come at once. The Yimen Guangdu says: The crane's shank is brittle and breaks easily; for a broken one, cut three or four inches of green bamboo, split it in two by hand, dig several white-necked earthworms and lay them in the tube, clamp and bind it, and give it several more to eat, and within the time of a meal it is as before. Those with tortoise-shell patterns on the feet are good.

《鳥獸類上·孔雀 Peacocks》

1
○雜俎曰：孔雀聞雷而孕。今按：孔雀出南海，群飛，數為偶，雌者尾短，無金碧，雄者生三年有小尾，五年成大尾，三四月後復凋。自喜其尾，雨不能高飛，人往擒之。養久，見人彩衣，聞絃歌，張翅而舞；見人穿釘靴、持雨傘，逐啄之。煮食味似鵝，解百藥毒。有與蛇匹生者。一曰都護。取尾者將刀急斷之，彼若回顧，金翠無彩矣。其首血能鴆人。尾作帚可收冰片，見墨莊漫錄。人拍其尾則舞。
○ The Miscellany says: Peacocks conceive on hearing thunder. I now note: Peacocks come from the South Sea, flying in flocks, pairing in several; the females have short tails without gold and green; the males grow small tails after three years and full tails after five, which wither again after the third or fourth month. They delight in their tails, and in rain cannot fly high, when people go to catch them. Kept long, when they see people in coloured clothes or hear strings and singing, they spread their wings and dance; when they see people wearing nailed boots and carrying umbrellas, they chase and peck them. Boiled and eaten, they taste like goose and neutralize a hundred poisons. Some mate with snakes. They are also called duhu. Those who take the tail must cut it off quickly with a knife, for if it looks back, the gold and green lose their brilliance. The blood of its head can poison people. The tail made into a brush can gather borneol; see the Casual Records of the Mozhuang. If people pat its tail, it dances.

《鳥獸類上·鸛 Storks》

1
○鸛有長水石，巢中養魚。或曰取礬繞卵助躁，術家取之。又能禹步轉石覓蛇。今按：鸛巢無礜，或礜地鸛取之耳。蔣八公曰：鸛仰鳴晴，俯鳴陰，其巢高下驗風。
○ The stork has the long-water stone, and keeps fish in its nest. Some say it takes alum to surround its eggs and aid their heat, and conjurers take it. It can also pace the Yu step and turn stones to find snakes. I now note: There is no yu stone in storks' nests; perhaps storks take it on yu-stone ground. Jiang Bagong says: When storks call looking up it will be fine, and looking down it will be cloudy; the height of its nest tests the wind.

《鳥獸類上·壓油鳥 The Oil-Pressing Bird》

1
○蓋州鳥名壓油，肥則自投番舶壓油，皮骨僅存，投之水即活。
○ In Gaizhou there is a bird called oil-press, which when fat throws itself into the foreign ships to be pressed for oil until only skin and bone are left, and when thrown into water it revives.

《鳥獸類上·雁 Wild Geese》

1
○霜前白雁食稻飽，飛不起，易為人得。李豫亨推篷寤語曰：雁肥而歸，故借蘆以助風力，塞北風高則無事此，故投蘆於雁門關，非避網也。
○ Before frost the white wild geese eat their fill of rice, cannot fly, and are easily caught. Li Yuheng's Awakened Words beneath the Raised Awning says: The geese return fat, so they carry reeds to aid the force of the wind; north of the passes the wind is strong and they have no need of this, so they drop the reeds at the Yanmen Pass; it is not to avoid nets.

《鳥獸類上·燕 Swallows》

1
○燕初銜泥，必四堆，橫一草。胸斑者胡燕，不多呢喃，巢作小口。胸赤者越燕，好鳴，其巢門向上。舊說燕畏煙火，戊日不歸。貞白曰：胡燕作窠長，能容二尺絹者，令人家富。若窠北向，尾屈白者，是數百歲燕，仙經謂肉芝，食之延年。此不可信。周益公見吉江蟄燕。文崑山治橋，土中皆燕。朱翼引郗鑒為兗州，掘野鼠蟄燕食之。
○ When swallows first carry mud, they always make four heaps with a straw across them. Those with spotted breasts are Hu swallows, which do not twitter much and make nests with small openings. Those with red breasts are Yue swallows, which like to sing, and whose nest openings face upward. The old saying is that swallows fear smoke and fire and do not return on wu days. Zhenbai says: If Hu swallows make a nest long enough to hold two chi of silk, the household becomes rich. If the nest faces north and the tail is curled and white, it is a swallow several hundred years old, which the immortals' classics call flesh fungus; eating it prolongs life. This cannot be believed. Lord Zhou Yi saw hibernating swallows at Jijiang. When Wen Kunshan repaired a bridge, the earth was full of swallows. Zhu Yi cites how Xi Jian, at Yanzhou, dug up field mice and hibernating swallows to eat.

《鳥獸類上·燕窩 Edible Birds' Nests》

1
○王麟洲曰：燕窩菜，海燕所築，銜之以飛，倦則置海面浮之，久復銜飛，吹泊山澳，人乃得之。陳懋仁曰：金絲燕育子時，群飛近沙，啄蠶螺食，蠶螺背有兩肋，堅潔而白，食之可補虛損，已勞痢，故燕食之，肉化而肋不化，并津液嘔出，結為小窩，附石上。劉薦叔曰：燕兩生雛者不能還，則冬蟄海邊。有人云海生沙筋，如石花菜之類，親見燕銜作窠，麟洲無功其誤聽與？姚有僕曰：燕窩能止小便數，本草未載。
○ Wang Linzhou says: Bird's-nest vegetable is built by sea swallows, which carry it as they fly, and when tired put it on the sea surface to float, after a while carry it and fly on again; it is blown into mountain coves, and people get it. Chen Maoren says: When golden-thread swallows raise young, they fly in flocks near the sand and peck silkworm snails to eat; silkworm snails have two ribs on their backs, hard, clean and white, which when eaten tonify depletion and cure consumptive dysentery; so when swallows eat them, the flesh is digested but not the ribs, which are vomited up with saliva and form small nests clinging to rocks. Liu Jianshu says: Swallows that raise two broods cannot return, and hibernate by the sea in winter. Someone says that the sea produces sand sinew, of the agar-weed kind, and that he saw swallows carry it to make nests; did Linzhou, lacking the effort, hear it wrongly? Yao Youpu says: Bird's nest can stop frequent urination, which the Materia Medica does not record.

《鳥獸類上·制燕法 Methods of Controlling Swallows》

1
○軒縣艾作人，朱書鳳凰在此，五幡一挂中棟，四挂前後架，則燕不來巢，或以蛇血書。方勺曰：畫上二條，曰驚燕，燕見紙條則飛去。中履曰：燕不窠樁棟，又燒煙即去。
○ Hang mugwort made into a figure with "the phoenix is here" written in vermilion, one of five pennants on the central beam and four on the front and back frames, and swallows will not nest; or write it in snake's blood. Fang Shao says: Draw two strips called "startle swallows," and when swallows see the paper strips they fly away. Zhonglü says: Swallows do not nest on stake-beams; also, burning smoke drives them away.

《鳥獸類上·鷓鴣 Partridge》

1
○鷓鴣獨居一丘林，有他雀來必鬥，人以鷓鴣囮誘斗而取之。鷓鴣一名樢音述，多對啼。南越志云：其名自號杜薄州。一曰鉤輈格傑。樢肉解金毒，中樢毒以姜解之，以樢食半夏也。
○ The partridge lives alone in one hillside forest, and if other birds come it always fights them; people use a partridge as a decoy to lure them into fighting and catch them. The partridge is also called shu pronounced shu, and often calls in pairs. The Record of Southern Yue says: It calls its own name, du-bo-zhou. Another says gou-zhou-ge-jie. Partridge flesh neutralizes metal poisons; for partridge poisoning ginger is the antidote, because the partridge eats pinellia.

《鳥獸類上·盍鴠 Hedan》

1
○十月毛落而寒號，忍凍，冬聚柏實食之，又自食其遺，遺而復食，故其矢為五靈脂，此東壁所未詳者。
○ In the tenth month it moults and cries with cold, enduring the freezing; in winter it gathers cypress seeds to eat, and also eats its own droppings, dropping and eating again, so its droppings are flying-squirrel droppings; this is something Dongbi did not detail.

《鳥獸類上·火老鴉 The Fire Crow》

1
○山啄木鳥頭上有赤尾，野人呼為火老鴉，能食火炭。王元之詩云：淮南啄木大如鴉，頂似仙鶴堆丹砂。或見其銜火向家，則必有災。尋常閒物自有異性，豈必劉郁所言富浪之食火雞而後訝之邪？
○ The mountain woodpecker has red on its head and tail; country people call it fire crow, and it can eat burning charcoal. Wang Yuanzhi's poem says: "The woodpecker of Huainan is as big as a crow,
its crown like a crane's piled with cinnabar." If one is seen carrying fire toward a house, there will surely be disaster. Ordinary common creatures have strange natures of their own; why need one marvel only at the fire-eating bird of Fulang that Liu Yu speaks of?

《鳥獸類上·白鷁 White Egret》

1
○雌雄相視則孕，雄鳴上風則孕。古稱鵁鶄以目交，屬玉，雎鳩類也。然鹿湖人有見鵁鶄交者。
○ Male and female conceive by looking at each other, and also when the male calls upwind. The ancients said the jiaojing mates by the eyes; it is the shuyu, of the osprey kind. But people of Luhu have seen jiaojing mating.

《鳥獸類上·亞既刺鳥 The Eagle》

1
○利未亞有鳥曰亞既利，乃百鳥之王，壽最長，生子令視日，不瞬者乃留之，受人之德亦報人。
○ Libya has a bird called the aquila, the king of all birds and the longest-lived; it makes its young look at the sun and keeps only those that do not blink; it repays people for kindness received.

《鳥獸類上·養鸕鶿法 Method of Raising Cormorants》

1
○鸕鶿六月畏寒，養之者以絮裹之，喂以胡椒，秋冬乃放之入水取魚。其菢子在夏。強者能啄大魚之睛，隨其浮游，而數鸕鶿制之以上。
○ Cormorants fear cold in the sixth month; their keepers wrap them in wadding and feed them pepper, and in autumn and winter release them into the water to catch fish. They brood in summer. The strong ones can peck out the eyes of large fish, following them as they swim, and several cormorants subdue them and bring them up.

《鳥獸類上·能言鳥 Talking Birds》

1
○鸚鵡、秦吉了、倒挂、麼鳳，一類也。鸚小，有五色；吉了大，惡煙；倒挂鳥食香煙而復吐之。能言者，聲似耳。孫勉韻：哵哵，即鸜鵒，俗呼八哥。白露日取而閉甕中，竟夕，屆天中小修共舌，如是者三，妙解人語。晉司空桓豁鎮荊州，有此鳥，聽琵琶，學座客言，多肖，雝鼻者納頭甕中，遂爾酷似。
○ Parrots, the Qin jiliao, the hanging parrot and the momo phoenix are of one kind. Parrots are small and five-coloured; the jiliao is large and hates smoke; the hanging bird eats incense smoke and spits it out again. Those that can speak only resemble the sounds. Sun Mian's rhyme book: baba is the myna, commonly called eight-brother. On the day of White Dew, catch one and shut it in a jar all night, and at midday trim its tongue; do this three times and it understands human speech wonderfully. When Huan Huo, Minister of Works of the Jin, governed Jingzhou, he had such a bird, which listened to the pipa and imitated the speech of guests very closely; for those with nasal voices it put its head into a jar, and then its imitation was perfect.

《鳥獸類上·牛 Cattle》

1
○耕牛眼去角近，眼欲大，眼中有白脈貫瞳，頸稍長大，後腳股闊，並快。毛欲短密，疏長則不耐寒。齒牙欲白，耳角欲近，角欲得細，身欲得粗，尾稍長大，無帶抱喉及臨耳者，並吉。俗呼旋謂之箭，四箭、六箭相對者佳。面短毛赤，登尾稍亂毛轉，並主命夭。凡母牛毛白乳紅，孳子多；乳疏黑，無子，謂之飄沙。其生子時，子臥面相向，吉；相背，子疏。一夜便糞三次，一年生一子；一夜一次，三年生一子。寧戚相牛經、褚榮陽法，皆後作也。酉陽云：毛少骨多有力，溺射箭者，良牛也。眼下旋毛曰淚滴。齒齦有緣，招官災。兩角間有亂毛，妨主。耳後旋毛，招盜賊。牛畏熱，又畏寒，草雜豆飼，肥潤耳濕，冬以牛衣，則無病也。草脹，以醋灌之，硝亦可，或從後探去其毒。若瀉痢，煎敗毒散或解毒湯飲之。吉水瀟瀧之水南水北，其牛不穿鼻而耕，金谿之遠都亦然，土人相傳羅隱授記，則附曾也。牛生有重踶，三日內割其外皮，則後來行不畏石。生四齒時，教以耒耜。視其角根細，則老矣。漳州云：牛為雷擊死者，其皮鞔鼓，海舶用之，見龍雲起則擂之，龍則不近。山西黃牛大於水牛，一牛牽一乘大車。水牛胎十二月而生，黃牛胎十月。馬初生，人以青布拭之，即能行；子牛生，其母自舐之，即能行。牛無空胎，一年一生。
○ In ploughing oxen, the eyes should be near the horns and large, with white veins running through the pupils; a slightly long neck and broad hind thighs are both swift. The hair should be short and dense; if sparse and long it cannot bear the cold. The teeth should be white, the ears near the horns, the horns fine, the body thick and the tail rather long and large; those without a band around the throat or near the ears are all auspicious. Whorls are commonly called arrows, and those with four or six arrows facing each other are good. A short face with red hair, or hair turned awry near the tail, both portend a short life. A cow with white hair and red teats bears many calves; one with sparse black teats is barren, which is called drifting sand. When she calves, if the calf lies facing her, it is auspicious; if facing away, calves are sparse. If she dungs three times in a night she bears a calf every year; if once a night, a calf every three years. Ning Qi's Classic of Judging Cattle and Chu Rongyang's method are later works. Youyang says: Those with little hair, much bone and strength, whose urine shoots like an arrow, are good oxen. A whorl under the eye is called a teardrop. Edges on the gums bring official troubles. Disordered hair between the horns harms the owner. Whorls behind the ears attract thieves. Cattle fear heat and also cold; fed grass mixed with beans they become fat and glossy with moist ears, and covered with ox-blankets in winter they have no illness. For bloating from grass, dose with vinegar, or saltpetre will do, or remove the poison by probing from behind. For diarrhoea, give them a decoction of Antitoxic Powder or Detoxifying Decoction. North and south of the Xiaolong River in Jishui, oxen plough without having their noses pierced, and also in the distant districts of Jinxi; the locals say it was ordained by Luo Yin, which is a fabrication. When a calf is born with double hoofs, cut off the outer skin within three days, and later it will walk without fearing stones. When it grows four teeth, teach it the plough. When the roots of the horns look thin, it is old. In Zhangzhou they say: Drums covered with the hide of oxen killed by lightning are used on ocean ships, and beaten when dragon clouds are seen rising, and the dragon will not come near. The yellow oxen of Shanxi are larger than water buffaloes, one ox pulling a large cart. The water buffalo is born after twelve months' gestation, the yellow ox after ten. When a foal is born, people wipe it with blue cloth and it can walk at once; when a calf is born, its mother licks it and it can walk at once. Cows have no barren pregnancies, and bear once a year.

《鳥獸類上·羊 Sheep》

1
○臘月孳者良。牡羔三日即綸絲結痿其外腎。以草雜糯豆，則易肥。春夏早放早收，收遲遇午熱，汗出，有龍塵入膚，即生疥癩。秋冬遲放，若食早露水草，口生瘡，又鼻生膿，或腹瀉，則表減而墳首也。棧宜高幹，久在泥中則生繭踶，作癩。羊好鹽，鹽啖之則綿毛。四月翦之，不熱；八月再翦之，令長氄過冬。凡疥癩，炒蕎麥子為黑灰，和白礬、硫黃末，油調，先刮癩而擦之，愈。羊夾踶，火燔鐵篦而熬羖羊脂，勻上烙之，勿令入水，次日即愈。羊為火畜，功主青盲，北羊為勝。埤雅曰：羊性前逆。王延德高昌行記：羊尾重三斤。大尾羊即靈羊，細毛薄皮，西番以作紙，杭州以作燈。王麟洲曰：黑羊皮能療杖瘡。閩中盛產羊，獨汀不產羊。羊食仙茅，舉體皆脂。外紀：際刺產異羊，絨細無比，雨中衣之，略不沾濡。
○ Those born in the twelfth month are good. At three days, male lambs have their testicles tied off with silk thread to wither them. Fed grass mixed with glutinous rice and beans they fatten easily. In spring and summer let them out early and bring them in early; if brought in late they meet the noon heat and sweat, and dragon dust enters the skin and they get mange. In autumn and winter let them out late; if they eat grass wet with early dew, they get mouth sores and nasal pus, or diarrhoea, and then their coats thin and their heads swell. The pen should be high and dry; if they stand long in mud they get hoof blisters and mange. Sheep love salt, and fed salt their wool becomes soft. Shear them in the fourth month so they are not hot, and again in the eighth so they grow long down for the winter. For mange, roast buckwheat seeds to black ash, mix with powdered alum and sulphur and oil, first scrape the scabs and rub it on, and they recover. For foot-rot, heat an iron comb in fire, melt ram's fat, spread it evenly on and brand it, do not let it get wet, and the next day it is healed. The sheep is a fire animal, effective chiefly against glaucoma, and northern sheep are best. The Piya says: The sheep by nature is contrary in front. Wang Yande's Record of a Journey to Qocho: sheep tails weigh three jin. The fat-tailed sheep is the ling sheep, with fine wool and thin skin, which the Western foreigners make into paper and Hangzhou into lanterns. Wang Linzhou says: Black sheepskin can heal sores from beatings. Min produces many sheep, but Ting alone produces none. Sheep that eat curculigo become fat all over. The Record of the Outer Regions: Chile produces a strange sheep whose wool is incomparably fine, and clothes of it worn in the rain hardly get wet.

《鳥獸類上·種羊法 Methods of Planting Sheep》

1
○吳萊有西域種羊皮畫褥歌。劉郁出使西域記言以羊臍種，聞雷生，臍驚以木聲，臍乃斷，至秋可食，臍內復有種，名瓏種羊。北戶錄亦言大秦有地生羊，臍與地連，但走馬擊鼓以驚絕之。吳萊淵穎集言以脛骨種。可信物理猶今以火灰伏鴨卵、飯合稗暴掩土而種蟲飼雞也。東山紀言有四角、六角、九尾羊。羚羊即山海經之羬。費信星槎勝覽云：阿丹國羚羊自胸生尾，垂九塊，名九尾羊，即挂樹蹙節之羚羊也。有遺瞿稼軒年伯者，如指大，有十六節，耳就之有聲。
○ Wu Lai has a "Song of the Painted Rug of Planted-Sheep Skin from the Western Regions." Liu Yu's Record of an Embassy to the Western Regions says that sheep are planted from the navel; on hearing thunder they are born, the navel is startled by a wooden noise and breaks, and by autumn they can be eaten; within the navel there is more seed, called the long planted sheep. The Record of the Northern Doors also says that in Da Qin there are earth-born sheep, their navels joined to the earth, which are startled loose only by galloping horses and beating drums. Wu Lai's Yuanying Collection says they are planted from shin bones. That this may be credible by the principles of things is like the present incubating of duck eggs in warm ash, and planting insects to feed chickens by covering rice and barnyard grass with earth. The Dongshan Record speaks of sheep with four horns, six horns and nine tails. The antelope is the qian of the Classic of Mountains and Seas. Fei Xin's Triumphant Visions of the Star Raft says: The antelope of Aden has a tail growing from its chest, hanging in nine pieces, called nine-tailed sheep; it is the antelope that hangs in trees with wrinkled joints. One was given to my senior Qu Jiaxuan, the size of a finger, with sixteen joints, which made a sound when one put one's ear to it.

《鳥獸類上·豕 Pigs》

1
○相傅留長孺相彘法：短項無柔毛者良，一廂有三牙者難留。椎豚不長，搗麻子二升，鹽半之，煮和糠飼之，立肥。豕必豶。牢止宜二三育，多則胎殰矣。熱病，斷其尾尖，刺其耳，血流，啖生萊菔、朴硝，驅浴於水，愈。疥癩，用豆油滓、百草霜擦之，復飼百草霜，愈。遼東白蹢為奇，廣則大抵花白。
○ The method of judging pigs attributed to Liu Changru: those with short necks and no soft hair are good, and those with three teeth on one side are hard to raise. When castrated piglets do not grow, pound two sheng of hemp seed with half as much salt, boil, mix with bran and feed them, and they fatten at once. Pigs must be castrated. A sty should hold only two or three litters; more and the foetuses miscarry. For fever, cut off the tip of the tail and prick the ears to let the blood flow, feed raw radish and mirabilite, and drive them to bathe in water, and they recover. For mange, rub with bean-oil dregs and pot soot, and feed them pot soot too, and they recover. The white-hoofed ones of Liaodong are remarkable, while those of Guang are mostly spotted white.

《鳥獸類上·馬 Horses》

1
○馬援鑄銅馬式、李伯樂馬經、黃直馬法，皆後作也。三十二相訣曰：眼似垂鈴鮮紫色，滿空凸出不驚然。白縷貫瞳行五百，斑如撒豆不同看。面顱倒擊如鐮背，鼻如金盞可藏拳。口又須深牙齒遠，舌如垂劍色如蓮。口無黑靨須長命，唇如垂箱蓋一般。食槽寬闊腮無肉，咽要平分筋要闌。耳如揚葉刀批竹，燕骨高兮髀不堅。入肉分弓彎左右，龍會高兮上古傳。項長如鳳須彎曲，鬃毛茸細要如綿。鬐長膊闊槍風小，臆高胸闊腳前寬。膝要高兮圓似掬，骨細筋粗節要攢。踶要圓實須卓立，身中充闊要平安。肋骨彎弓須緊密，排安肉厚穩金鞍。三峰壓壓須藏骨，臥如猿落重如山。鵝鼻曲直須停穩，尾似流星散不連。膏筋大小須勻壯，下節攢筋緊一錢。羊髭有距如雞距，能急奔走日行千。其旋毛訣曰：頂旋號騰蛇，不利長當知。後旋曰喪門，前欠曰挾屍。黑色耳全白，從來號孝頭。的盧如入口，有福亦須休。背旋鞍貼下，駝屍之說真。眼下毛生旋，遙看是淚痕。簷耳駝鬃項，雖然毛病殊。若然兼抱尾，有實不如無。董逌曰：漢武見汗血馬，血從前膊小孔中出，今畫汗血出於革毛。張戡作番馬，缺耳犁鼻，蓋謂鼻不裂則氣盛衝肺，耳不缺則風搏不聞音也。韓幹考時日面方位，然後定形骨毛色，豈無謂乎？凡馬胎十二月而生，其壽約三十年。畏暑不畏寒。棧中以藖豆雜貫眾秣之。病宜洗澡，不宜日曬。駒生時以干土塵遍染其體而揉捻之，能馴而不啼嚙。以銀簪分界其鬃，則兩開。韓鄂云：係猴則馬不疥。元志曰：每月上寅以鹽啖之，則馬無病。
○ Ma Yuan's cast bronze horse model, the Horse Classic of Li Bole and Huang Zhi's horse method are all later works. The Formula of Thirty-Two Points says: "Eyes like hanging bells, bright purple,
filling the sockets and bulging, yet unstartled. White threads through the pupils — it goes five hundred [li];
spots like scattered beans are not to be looked at. The forehead struck back like the back of a sickle,
the nostrils like golden cups that can hold a fist. The mouth should be deep and the teeth far apart,
the tongue like a hanging sword, coloured like lotus. A mouth without black spots means long life;
lips like the lid of a hanging box. The feeding trough wide, the cheeks without flesh,
the throat evenly divided, the sinews firm. Ears like raised leaves, cut like split bamboo;
swallow bones high, and the thighs not firm. Into the flesh the bow divides, curving left and right;
the dragon-meeting high, as handed down from antiquity. The neck long like a phoenix's, and curved;
the mane soft and fine, like cotton. The withers long, the shoulders broad, the spear-wind small;
the breast high, the chest broad, the forelegs wide apart. The knees should be high and round as if cupped;
bones fine, sinews thick, and joints compact. The hoofs should be round and solid and stand upright;
the body full and broad and steady. The ribs bowed and close together;
the flesh thick under a steady golden saddle. The three peaks pressed down, hiding the bones;
lying like a gibbon dropped, heavy as a mountain. The goose-nose, curved or straight, should be steady;
the tail like a shooting star, spread but not joined. The fat sinews, large and small, even and strong;
the lower joints with sinews bunched tight as a coin. The sheep's whiskers with spurs like a cock's spurs —
it can gallop fast, a thousand [li] a day." The Formula of Whorls says: "A crown whorl is called the soaring snake;
know that it brings no long life. A rear whorl is called the mourning gate,
a front one the corpse-bearer. A black horse with ears all white
has always been called mourning-head. If dilu [marks] enter the mouth,
even with good fortune it must end. A back whorl right under the saddle —
the saying 'carrying a corpse' is true. A whorl of hair below the eye,
seen from afar, is a tear stain. Eaves-ears and a camel-maned neck,
though the faults are different, if also coupled with a clasped tail,
it is worse than having none." Dong You says: Emperor Wu of the Han saw that the blood-sweating horse's blood came out of small holes in the forelegs, yet now paintings show the blood sweat coming out of the hide. Zhang Kan painted foreign horses with notched ears and slit noses, because it is said that if the nose is not slit the qi is too strong and rushes the lungs, and if the ears are not notched the wind beats them and they cannot hear. Han Gan examined the time, day, face and direction before fixing the form, bones and coat colour — was this without meaning? The horse is born after twelve months' gestation and lives about thirty years. It fears heat but not cold. In the stable it is fed hay and beans mixed with cyrtomium. When ill it should be bathed and not exposed to the sun. When a foal is born, rub its whole body with dry earth dust and knead it, and it becomes docile and does not neigh or bite. Part its mane with a silver hairpin, and it falls to both sides. Han E says: If a monkey is tied [in the stable], horses do not get mange. The Yuan Treatise says: If they are fed salt on the first yin day of each month, horses have no illness.

《鳥獸類上·驢 Donkeys》

1
○以鳴聲數多者強。耳似翦，踶似鐘，尾似刷，一連三滾者，有力也。忽里謨斯國有福祿，似驢而花文，即儀禮所謂閭也。騾耐走，不多病，病則難治。生騾同馬法。段成式曰：騾不孳乳。愚者按：厄入多國騾能傳種。說文：騾，驢父馬母；駃騠，馬父驢母；駝貊，驢父牛母；漢匈奴傳所謂奇畜也。凡馬騾皆畏騎者足夾之，當胸箕心之傍捶之，則彼痛颭。養驢馬者令後足低於前足，使其箕心漸大，亦一法也，箕心大者力足，自不踹邊樸路。凡眼泡上有小窩，窩有直筋橫筋，則強。凡生跛而醫愈者，其足之筋必起小珠如豆大，以此為驗。老馬食地黃為駒。食鼠糞，腹脹。食雞糞，生骨眼。飲猿溺，辟瘟。其嚙人者，以殭蠶塗其喙。馬尾後抽轡破者，以蛇蛻燒灰，香油調敷，或干摻之。破脊，以臭溝泥塗之，愈。馬駒生，布灰作圈，駒止圈中，謂之新灰拘駒。驢馬駒隨母，則知月中月末月初生者在母前，月後生者在母後。萬畢曰：螢火裹皮置土中，馬見之鳴叫，不敢行。贊寧說：海馬骨在地，則馬不肯行。吳人呼疾速為飛風。唐制：風印右髆，飛印左髆，止監尚乘局尾側印三花。
○ Those that bray many times are strong. Ears like scissors, hoofs like bells, tail like a brush, and rolling over three times in a row show strength. Hormuz has the fulu, like a donkey but with patterns, which is what the Yili calls lü. Mules endure travel and are seldom ill, but when ill are hard to cure. Breeding mules follows the method for horses. Duan Chengshi says: Mules do not breed. The Foolish One notes: In Egypt mules can breed. The Shuowen: the luo has a donkey father and horse mother; the jueti has a horse father and donkey mother; the tuomo has a donkey father and cow mother; these are the strange animals of the Xiongnu biography of the Han. All horses and mules fear riders gripping them with the feet, and if struck beside the jixin at the chest, they flinch with pain. Those raising donkeys and horses keep the hind feet lower than the forefeet so that the jixin gradually grows larger, which is another method; those with a large jixin have plenty of strength, and naturally do not tread the edges or stumble on the road. If there is a small hollow above the eyelids with vertical or horizontal sinews in it, it is strong. When one born lame has been cured, small beads like beans always rise on the sinews of the foot; this is the test. Old horses that eat rehmannia become [like] colts. Eating rat droppings makes their bellies swell. Eating chicken droppings gives them bone-eye. Drinking monkey urine wards off epidemics. For those that bite people, smear their muzzles with stiff silkworms. For sores behind the tail from the crupper, apply ash of burnt snake slough mixed with sesame oil, or sprinkle it dry. For a broken back, smear it with stinking ditch mud, and it heals. When a foal is born, spread ash to make a ring and the foal stays in the ring; this is called new ash confining the foal. When donkey and horse foals follow their mothers, one knows that those born in the middle, end or beginning of the month go in front of their mothers, and those born after the month behind. The Ten Thousand Infallible Arts say: If fireflies wrapped in skin are put in the ground, horses that see them neigh and dare not go on. Zanning says: If sea-horse bone is on the ground, horses will not go on. The people of Wu call great speed flying wind. The Tang regulations: "wind" was branded on the right shoulder and "flying" on the left, and only the Directorate of the Imperial Stables branded three flowers beside the tail.

《鳥獸類上·犬 Dogs》

1
○焚艾煙熏其鼻，長則捕鼠。食竹茹則啞，食木鱉則死。廬山紀事曰：以苧麻、烏頭和豚肉使狗食之，則喑。雜五行志曰：白犬虎文，畜之至萬石；黑犬白耳，主富貴；黑犬白前兩足，宜子孫；白犬黃頭，黃犬白尾，或白前兩足，皆吉。瑣碎錄曰：黑犬能辟伏屍。舌青斑者識盜賊。無故掘坑者有災。獵犬瘦而長，爾雅所謂長喙獫，短喙猲驕也。大者曰獒，今西洋多有來者。狗生七日乃開眼能行。諺曰：春臭夏蠅秋靈冬蠢。小犬吠不止，滴香油鼻中。沈周雜記云：狗肝色如泥土，臭味亦然，故人夜行土上，則肝氣動，蓋相感也。孟詵曰：狗膽和通草、桂為丸服，令人隱形。魏志華陀以狗膽治劉勛女膝瘡，出蟲若蛇。故人傳曰白赤犬膽為能辟邪，晉元永昌二年訛言蟲病得白犬膽可救是也。楮記室曰：至治元年，玉案山產小赤犬，群吠遍野。暗厄利亞犬猛，一犬可殺一虎，遇獅亦不避也。哥而西加產犬能戰，一犬可當一騎。暄曰：犬以溺記路，回嗅而知之。
○ If their noses are smoked with burning mugwort, when grown they catch rats. Eating bamboo shavings makes them dumb, and eating momordica kills them. The Records of Mount Lu say: If dogs are fed ramie and aconite mixed with pork, they become mute. The Miscellaneous Treatise on the Five Phases says: Keeping a white dog with tiger stripes brings ten thousand shi; a black dog with white ears brings wealth and rank; a black dog with white forefeet benefits descendants; a white dog with a yellow head, a yellow dog with a white tail, or white forefeet, are all auspicious. The Trivial Records say: Black dogs can ward off lurking corpses. Those with blue-spotted tongues recognize thieves. Those that dig pits for no reason portend disaster. Hunting dogs are lean and long; they are what the Erya calls the long-snouted xian and the short-snouted xiejiao. The large ones are called ao, many of which now come from the Western Ocean. Puppies open their eyes and can walk seven days after birth. The proverb says: In spring they stink, in summer they draw flies, in autumn they are clever, in winter dull. When a puppy keeps barking, drip sesame oil into its nose. Shen Zhou's Miscellaneous Records say: A dog's liver is the colour of mud and earth and smells and tastes like it, so when someone walks on earth at night the dog's liver qi stirs, because of mutual response. Meng Shen says: Dog's gall made into pills with akebia and cinnamon makes one invisible. The Record of Wei: Hua Tuo treated a sore on the knee of Liu Xun's daughter with dog's gall, and a worm like a snake came out. So the saying that the gall of white or red dogs can ward off evil, and the rumour in the second year of Yongchang of Emperor Yuan of the Jin that worm disease could be cured with white-dog gall, are this. A cat's palate has ridges, and those with nine ridges can catch rats. Those that bunch their four feet when lifted by the ears are good, for those that stretch are lazy. They also catch frogs and fish. If a cat holds something in its mouth, blow in its ear and it lets go. Those that come from abroad have golden eyes, silver eyes, or one gold and one silver. Eating mint makes them drunk. If one is born alone in a litter, it is abandoned. If three or four in a litter are all male or all female, they should not be kept. Visitors to Xiangshan'ao have a small dog like a monkey that can catch rats; this is the menggong, and the Materia Medica's taking the menggong for a snake is wrong. Cats die if they eat licorice. When nursing, if the kittens are ill and the milk scant, or if seen by someone born in the year of the tiger, they eat their kittens. When a kitten mews without stopping, smear powdered tangerine peel on its nose. Dogs born in autumn are best, and cats born in spring. When a female has no male, seek mating from the cow's-face left store. One says: In a bad year when a female cat cannot find a male, put the cat in a dou measure, pray before the stove and pat it three times on a heap of cow dung, and it conceives. Formerly I did not believe it, but now it proves so. Xuan says: When it is calling in spring, dip it in water three times and it conceives.

《鳥獸類上·象 Elephants》

1

○ Wu Hua's Notes on Things Seen and Heard says: Zheng Wenzhen said that elephants fear smoke, and people fend them off with torches tied to long poles. Their eyes hate flies and gnats, and when there is sunlight they do not come out. In the south they set awls in wooden shoes, cover them with grass and make pitfalls. On the islets of the rivers of the Western Sea there is xitang water, like ointment, black, which only ox horns can hold without leaking; people smear it on trees, and elephants that brush against it cannot get away, and Kunlun slaves smear it on their bodies and ride the elephants home. The Dharma Master Liang Xiang says: In the Western Regions the elephant is called gaja; they mate floating in water facing each other, and the young are born after five years. The flesh of the trunk is very tender; there is something like a claw at its tip, which can pick up needles, its power exceeding a lodestone's. The tusks boiled with salt soften by themselves and can control monsters; this is the "piercing with elm" of the Rites of Zhou. In Yunnan households keep elephants to carry loads. The ivory of Chaozhou is small and red. The elephants of Monomotapa are extremely large, with tusks weighing up to two hundred jin. Tian Shichen says the elephant has three galls, two in its feet and one not in its feet, so the accounts of Xu Xuan and Zhang Xuan can be reconciled.

《鳥獸類上·虎 Tigers》

1
○虎畏尖，不越籬。虎畏日，與梟同。畏火，愛毛也。不棲高林，畏枝上雀糞爛其毛也。離山必一吼。啞魯有飛虎，長尾奮翅。虎來，其氣不可當，蟲鳥皆伏也。虎盜犬彘去，急舂空碓數十聲，有以磚塞灶門者，有以木覆篩掩碓口，虎即齒酸，得犬彘不能食。今野人有怒罵奪虎氣者，虎置銜物而去，蓋虎饑則難犯，飽則易制。行人張蓋而虎不犯，皆蓋虎疑也。又有設襁膠布制虎法。中德曰：屈木而藏械發之，曰襁。膠布，虎踏則躁而困。暄曰：虎聞人呵喝聲，則伏匿不動，守虎待張及深山樵採行旅過者，恒用此法。其食物也，依月食首尾中，餘以柴掩之。
○ The tiger fears points and does not cross hedges. The tiger fears the sun, like the owl. It fears fire because it loves its fur. It does not lodge in tall forests, fearing that sparrow droppings from the branches will rot its fur. On leaving the mountains it always roars once. Yalu has flying tigers with long tails and beating wings. When a tiger comes, its qi is irresistible, and insects and birds all lie low. When a tiger steals a dog or pig, quickly pound an empty mortar several dozen times, or block the stove opening with a brick, or cover the mortar mouth with a wooden sieve, and the tiger's teeth go sore and it cannot eat the dog or pig. Nowadays when country people curse angrily to take away the tiger's qi, the tiger drops what it carries and goes, for a hungry tiger is hard to oppose but a sated one easy to control. Travellers open umbrellas and the tiger does not attack, all because the tiger is suspicious. There is also the method of controlling tigers by setting qiang and glued cloth. Zhongde says: Bending wood with a concealed trigger released is called qiang. With glued cloth, when the tiger steps on it, it becomes agitated and trapped. Xuan says: When a tiger hears people shouting, it hides and does not move; those keeping watch for tigers and woodcutters and travellers in deep mountains always use this method. In eating, it eats according to the moon, the head, tail or middle, and covers the rest with brushwood.

《鳥獸類上·猿狖 Gibbons and You Monkeys》

《鳥獸類上·兔 Hares》

1

《鳥獸類上·制鼠法 Methods of Controlling Rats》

1

On the Seventh Evening cage nine rats and bury them deep under heavy earth. Or stop the holes with lotus stems and burdock seeds, and the rats leave. Daoist books say to stop their holes on yin and chen days with spells — is this suppression magic? Chu Yong says: Wolf dung and black dog skin burnt with drugs can draw rats. He also says: Burn horseshoe crab and rats come. Zhongde says: Take a large male rat, castrate it and let it go, and it bites the rats all over the house to death. Also, keep a squirrel, and it too will not tolerate rats. Zhongtong says: Equal parts of peach heads, toon bark, loofah vine and chinaberry green leaves, dried and powdered, with a qian of arsenic added and burnt, make the rats leave by themselves. Zhonglü says: Powder equal parts of benzoin, crab roe and squirrel and burn it in the quiet of night; if the rats come, they must not be beaten to death, but put in something and buried in the earth, and they will not return.

《鳥獸類上·麒麟異獸王 Qilin and the King of Strange Beasts》

1
○阮霧靈曰：辟邪、天祿，蓋天鹿也。南陽宗資墓前石天祿有鱗，殆即麒麟乎？馬觀曰：阿丹國產麒麟，前足高九尺餘，後足六尺餘，項長頭昂。虛舟子曰：牛感龍而生麟。外紀：印弟亞有獸如牛而大如象，少低，全身皮甲如鯊皮，銃箭不入，其骨肉皮角牙踶糞俱藥也，名為罷達，蓋麟之類乎？罷達喜殺象與馬，居水中可數十日。印弟亞即五印度也。又有獨角獸，能人語，解蛇毒，蓋角端也，或以為白澤。贊寧亦曰：銀州出獨步獸，如獅，身黃，一角，有意氣。麒麟、角端，仁獸也。又有教獸，亦仁獸也，形如狐，赤白色，尾大，有君臣父子之別，見獸必教之，曉號峰上。
○ Ruan Wuling says: The bixie and tianlu are heavenly deer. The stone tianlu in front of Zong Zi's tomb at Nanyang has scales — is it the qilin? Ma Guan says: Aden produces the qilin, its forelegs over nine chi high and its hind legs over six chi, with a long neck and head held high. Xuzhouzi says: Cattle stirred by dragons give birth to qilin. The Record of the Outer Regions: India has a beast like an ox but as large as an elephant, a little lower, its whole body armoured with skin like sharkskin, which guns and arrows cannot pierce; its bones, flesh, skin, horn, teeth, hoofs and dung are all medicines; it is called bada — is it of the qilin kind? The bada likes to kill elephants and horses, and can live in water for several dozen days. India is the Five Indias. There is also the unicorn, which can speak human speech and neutralizes snake venom; it is the jiaoduan, or some take it to be the baize. Zanning also says: Yinzhou produces the solitary-pacing beast, like a lion, yellow-bodied, with one horn, and spirited. The qilin and jiaoduan are benevolent beasts. There is also the teaching beast, also benevolent, shaped like a fox, red and white, with a large tail; it distinguishes ruler and minister, father and son, and whenever it sees beasts it teaches them, calling at dawn on the peaks.

《鳥獸類上·鹿麋 Deer and Elk》

1
○山鹿陽，澤麋陰。惟牡有角。鹿黃而斑，麋色青黑，肉踶下有二竅，為夜目，其角丫少。淮南曰：孕女見麋而子四目也。鹿喜食龜與九種草，又喜食紙。居則環角外向，臥則口朝尾閭，以通督脈。其曰鹿戴玉而角斑，謂其六十年懷璚於角下也，故淡寮稱斑龍焉。存中言骨易長無如鹿角，則茸不如角力矣。其云陽角補陰，陰角補陽，而東坡又言鹿補陽，麋補陰，當何從乎？中通曰：新殺鹿血，新梭布兜之，不染。中履曰：麀鹿濯濯，好浴浮水，故亦食蘋，本草不必笑陸佃也。
○ The mountain deer is yang, the marsh elk yin. Only the males have antlers. The deer is yellow and spotted, the elk blue-black, with two holes under the fleshy hoofs that serve as night eyes, and its antlers have few tines. The Huainanzi says: A pregnant woman who sees an elk bears a child with four eyes. Deer like to eat tortoises and nine kinds of herbs, and also like to eat paper. At rest they form a ring with antlers facing outward, and lying down turn their mouths to the coccyx to open the governing vessel. The saying that deer wear jade and so their antlers are spotted means that at sixty they hold jade under their antlers; so Danliao calls them spotted dragons. Cunzhong says that no bone grows as easily as deer antler, so velvet is not as strong as antler. It is said that yang antlers tonify yin and yin antlers tonify yang, while Dongpo also says that deer tonify yang and elk tonify yin; which should one follow? Zhongtong says: Fresh deer blood caught in new shuttle cloth does not stain it. Zhonglü says: "The does glossy and sleek" — they love to bathe and swim, so they also eat duckweed; the Materia Medica need not laugh at Lu Dian.

《鳥獸類上·茶首 Chashou》

1
○宛委餘編曰：茶首，音蔡茂，神鹿，兩頭，出永昌郡，其矢可敷瘡毒。按博物志作茶苴機，一作蔡餘義，本草作蔡苜機，聲形互訛耳。洱記曰：兩頭者，似也。土人呼為義希，取其糞也。雜俎引為邪希。
○ The Wanwei Yubian says: Chashou, pronounced caimao, is a divine deer with two heads, from Yongchang commandery, whose droppings can be applied to poisonous sores. I note that the Treatise on Curiosities writes chajuji, also caiyuyi, and the Materia Medica caimuji, errors of sound and form. The Record of Er says: The two-headed is a likeness. The locals call it yixi, and take its droppings. The Miscellany cites it as xiexi.

《鳥獸類上·豪豬 Porcupines》

1
○向謂脊有箭，今見田夫守芋而得之，彼觸之即放箭，再觸之，三觸之，而彼氣竭矣，遂圍擊之。芋味麻人，惟彼食之，他獸不食也。虎亦畏其刺。
○ It used to be said they have arrows on their backs; now I have seen farmers guarding taro catch them; when touched they shoot their quills, touched again and a third time, and then their qi is exhausted, and they surround and beat them. Taro numbs people's mouths, and only they eat it; other beasts do not. Even tigers fear their quills.

《鳥獸類上·香狸 Civets》

1
○香狸外腎，腎類也，即蒙化府之香髦，其陰則一，而囊有四子。利未亞有狸似麝，臍後有肉囊，香滿其中，即病，向石上剔出之，始安，香如蘇合油而黑，其貴次於龍涎。
○ The external kidneys of the civet are of the kidney kind; it is the fragrant mao of Menghua prefecture, whose genitals are single, but with four testes in the scrotum. Libya has a civet like the musk deer, with a fleshy sac behind the navel filled with musk; when [the sac is full] it is ill, and rubs it out against a stone before it is at ease; the musk is like storax oil but black, and its value is second only to ambergris.

《鳥獸類上·龍虱 Dragon Lice》

1
○漳州人傳：中秋前三日，海口飛墮龍虱，餘日絕無，食之除面靬，活血。少隨老父福寧見之，後在姚有僕署中，雲自濠鏡來，則他處亦出此，何漳獨異也？蓋是甲蟲，大如指頂，甲下有翅，熏干油潤，去甲翅，啖似火魚之變味。漳之異此，亦猶廣之食河蟲，殆他處不取耳。
○ The people of Zhangzhou say that on the three days before mid-autumn dragon lice fly down at the harbour mouth, and on other days there are none at all; eating them removes facial blemishes and invigorates the blood. When young I accompanied my father to Funing and saw them, and later in Yao Youpu's office was told they came from Haojing, so they are produced elsewhere too; why is Zhang alone thought strange? They are beetles the size of a fingertip, with wings under the wing-cases; smoked dry and moistened with oil, with the wing-cases and wings removed, they taste like the transformed flavour of fire fish. Zhang's peculiarity in this is like Guang's eating river worms; probably other places just do not take them.
