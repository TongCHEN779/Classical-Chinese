《器用類 Implements》

《器用類·箋紙 Letter Paper》

1
○永樂於江西造連七紙、奏本，出鉛山；榜紙，出浙之常山、廬之英山。宣德五年造素馨紙，印有洒金箋、五色金粉、磁青蠟箋。此外薛濤箋，則礬潢雲母粉者；鏡面高麗，則繭紙也。後唐澄心堂紙絕少。松江潭箋，或仿宋藏經箋，漬荊川連，芨褶蠟砑者也。宣德陳清款，白楮皮，厚可揭三四張，聲和而有穰。其桑皮者牙色，礬光者可書。今則棉推興國、涇縣，敝邑桐城浮山左亦抄楮皮結香紙。邵、建則竹紙。順昌紙、柬紙，則廣信為佳，即秦本也。續錄曰：北紙橫簾，南紙豎簾。
○ In the Yongle period Jiangxi made lianqi paper and memorial paper, which came from Yanshan; placard paper came from Changshan in Zhe and Yingshan in Lu. In the fifth year of Xuande, jasmine paper was made, printed with gold-sprinkled paper, five-coloured gold powder and porcelain-blue waxed paper. Besides these, Xue Tao paper is alum-glazed with mica powder, and mirror-surface Korean paper is cocoon paper. The Chengxin Hall paper of the Later Tang is extremely rare. The Tan paper of Songjiang, or the imitation Song sutra paper, is Jingchuan lian paper soaked, pleated with bletilla, waxed and burnished. The Xuande paper with the Chen Qing mark is of white paper-mulberry bark, thick enough to be split into three or four sheets, with a mellow sound and a fibrous body. That of mulberry bark is ivory-coloured, and the alum-glossed kind can be written on. Nowadays for cotton paper Xingguo and Jing county are esteemed, and Fushanzuo in my own Tongcheng also makes paper-mulberry and edgeworthia paper. Shao and Jian make bamboo paper. For Shunchang paper and card paper, Guangxin's is best, which is the Qin paper. The Continued Record says: Northern paper has horizontal screen lines and southern paper vertical ones.

《器用類·抄紙法 Method of Making Paper》

1
○治楮者漚之，投黃葵之根，則釋而為淖糜，酌諸槽，抄之以簾，其薄者一再抄，厚至五六抄，覆諸夾牆，煏乾而揭之。或以石灰水浸楮，後滌其灰。偽者加竹料、草料，以粉取白，則沁不耐書矣。竹取笥初成竹，斷之，去青，謂之竹絲，浸而舂之，江西抄者粗。其抄草紙按尺者，煮當子藤葉，抄而累之，則番張不黏，或用榆皮。閩中抄竹紙、簡紙，取榔樹合圍者，鋸片舂碎，煮水抄簾，乃可笮之而番張烤煏也，或用大圓黃香樹皮。廣信用羊桃藤水。皆取其滑。
○ Those who prepare paper-mulberry soak it, add roots of yellow mallow, and it loosens into a soft pulp; this is ladled into the vat and lifted with a screen, the thin sheets with one or two dips and the thick with five or six; they are laid on a double wall, dried over fire, and peeled off. Or the paper-mulberry is soaked in limewater and the lime then washed out. Counterfeiters add bamboo and grass material and whiten it with powder, and then it bleeds and does not take writing. For bamboo, take bamboo just grown from the sheath, cut it, remove the green, which is called bamboo thread, soak and pound it; that made in Jiangxi is coarse. For grass paper made by the chi, boil dangzi vine leaves, and when the sheets are made and stacked they do not stick together; or elm bark is used. In Min, for making bamboo paper and card paper, langshu trunks a full embrace thick are sawn into pieces, pounded fine and boiled, and the water used in the screen, so that the sheets can be pressed and baked one by one; or the bark of the large round yellow-fragrance tree is used. Guangxin uses water of yangtao vine. All are used for their slipperiness.

《器用類·作故色紙 Making Antique-Coloured Paper》

1
○樺皮燒煙熏紙，作故色如泥。凡紙沁者，熏之不沁。續錄曰：紙自然故者，其表故色，其里必新。
○ Smoke paper with the smoke of burning birch bark, and it takes an antique colour like mud. Paper that bleeds will not bleed after smoking. The Continued Record says: In paper that is naturally old, the surface is antique in colour, but the inside is surely new.

《器用類·熿槌法 Method of Sizing and Beating》

1
○熿百幅，白礬倍黃明膠，滾化而稀水收之。槌紙以一濕一干合百為垛，用大石壓一伏時，乃槌三百下，以互間勻疊為度。米元章硾越竹如金版，入芨番覆。輟耕錄亦載藏經紙法，芨合石硾為光。
○ To size a hundred sheets, take white alum and twice as much clear yellow glue, dissolve them by boiling, and take them up in thin water. In beating paper, put one wet and one dry sheet together to make a stack of a hundred, press it for a double-hour under a large stone, then beat it three hundred strokes, until the alternate sheets are evenly stacked. Mi Yuanzhang beat Yue bamboo paper till it was like gold plate, putting in bletilla and turning it over. The Records Made while Resting from the Plough also record the method for sutra paper, burnishing it to a gloss with bletilla and a stone.

《器用類·筆法 Methods for Brushes》

1
○兔穎、鼠狼、雞雉、胎髮皆可為之。銳、齊、圓、健，其四德也。唐有筆匠鐵頭。宣州諸葛高、常州許穎，李端叔稱歷陽柳材，黃山谷言郎奇棗心散卓，今巨細純羊毫近之，以宛轉滿志也。妙手近推湖州，撫李家渡業遍天下，大率毫少，倍狸萵耳。鼠毫者，竹鼺尾毫也。兔毫惟前肩者尖健。壺中贅錄曰：養筆以硫黃酒舒其毫，取冬水，筆亦防蠹也。東坡書杜君懿藏諸葛筆，每百枝用汞粉沸湯研墨膠筆，不蠹不燥。又曰：生毫筆成，飯甑蒸出，懸水甕數月，乃可用。山谷用椒、檗煎湯磨松煤染筆，幹後收貯，或以靛染之。馮應京曰：洗筆宜熱水。
○ Rabbit hair, weasel, chicken and pheasant feathers and a baby's first hair can all be used. Sharp, even, round and firm are its four virtues. In the Tang there was the brushmaker Tietou. There were Zhuge Gao of Xuanzhou and Xu Ying of Changzhou; Li Duanshu praised Liu Cai of Liyang, and Huang Shangu spoke of Lang Qi's jujube-heart sanzhuo brushes, which today's pure goat-hair brushes, large and small, approach, because they turn as one wishes. The finest makers are now held to be in Huzhou, and the trade of Lijiadu in Fu spreads all over the empire; they generally use little hair, doubled with wildcat and lettuce. Rat hair is the tail hair of the bamboo rat. Of rabbit hair, only that from the forequarters is pointed and firm. The Superfluous Records from within the Pot says: To nurture brushes, loosen the hairs with sulphur wine, using winter water, which also protects them from insects. Dongpo wrote that Du Junyi stored Zhuge brushes, and for every hundred he ground ink with calomel and boiling water and sized the brushes with it, so that they neither got insects nor dried out. He also said: When raw-hair brushes are finished, steam them in a rice steamer, take them out and hang them over a water jar for several months, and then they can be used. Shangu dyed brushes with pine soot ground in a decoction of Sichuan pepper and phellodendron, and stored them after drying, or dyed them with indigo. Feng Yingjing says: Brushes should be washed in hot water.

《器用類·辯硯 Distinguishing Inkstones》

1
○硯譜：青州紅絲第一，柳公權用之。其實端第一，次歙，次臨洮，駝磯、廬山、常山不及也。熟鐵硯重，倭漆查硯輕。澄泥如銅雀瓦類，後仿而瘞泥生潤耳。端州，今肇慶，宋欽宗改名。斧柯山即靈羊峽，有小湘、後歷、石磐、砊水、岩西、下巖、大小秋風、桃花，其名也。宋宗室希鵠續錄曰：下巖惟漆黑、青花二種，扣之無聲，磨墨亦無聲。卵石去膘，有花點，吳淑硯賦所謂點滴青花，久在水底，難得矣。今分將軍、梅花、老坑、水坑，以蕉葉白、火納文辯水岩，亦因病而識之，張世南謂之火黯。外石號屏風背，多紫白綠鴝眼，絛絲有黃綠朱青數十重明潤者。朱子暇洽瞯守端曰：嫩鋒橫斷，發墨最神。似端者，九溪黎石、沅州黑石也。洮有猿頭斑、瓜皮黃、蚤紋。陸子淵得洮瓊。龍尾為李後主開，羊鬥嶺有棗心諸坑，刷絲、內裹、叢絲、馬尾、獨吐諸名，正視如洒墨，側觀刷絲燦然。中德曰：洗研宜蓮蓬穰、半夏切平，不可紙拭。中通曰：滾水洗，易乾，然泉浸所以養石，若墨膠久滑，當以烰炭略磨。
○ The Manual of Inkstones puts the red-thread stone of Qingzhou first, which Liu Gongquan used. In fact Duan is first, She next and Lintao next, while Tuoji, Lushan and Changshan do not match them. Wrought-iron inkstones are heavy, and Japanese lacquered zha inkstones light. Clay inkstones are like the tiles of the Bronze Bird Terrace, which were later imitated by burying clay so that it grew moist. Duanzhou is the present Zhaoqing, renamed by Emperor Qinzong of the Song. Mount Fuke is Lingyang gorge, and Xiaoxiang, Houli, Shipan, Kengshui, Yanxi, Xiayan, Greater and Lesser Autumn Wind and Peach Blossom are the names [of its pits]. Xigu, of the Song imperial clan, says in the Continued Record: Of the Xiayan there are only two kinds, lacquer-black and blue-flowered; they make no sound when tapped, and make no sound in grinding ink either. Pebble stones with the skin removed have flower spots, what Wu Shu's Rhapsody on Inkstones calls "drops of blue flowers"; they lie long under water and are hard to obtain. Nowadays they are divided into General, Plum Blossom, Old Pit and Water Pit, and the Shuiyan is distinguished by the banana-leaf white and fire-received patterns, which are also recognized by their flaws; Zhang Shinan calls them fire-darkening. The outer stone is called screen-back, with many purple, white and green mynah's eyes, and the bright and glossy kind with dozens of layers of yellow, green, red and blue banded threads. Zhu Zixia Qiaxian, prefect of Duan says: When the tender edge cuts across, its production of ink is most marvellous. Those resembling Duan are the Li stone of Jiuxi and the black stone of Yuanzhou. Tao has ape-head spots, melon-skin yellow and flea patterns. Lu Ziyuan obtained Tao jade. Longwei was opened for Li Houzhu; at Yangdou ridge there are the jujube-heart and other pits, and names such as brushed silk, inner wrapping, clustered silk, horse tail and single spit; seen straight on it looks sprinkled with ink, and seen from the side the brushed silk is brilliant. Zhongde says: For washing inkstones, lotus-pod pith and pinellia cut flat are suitable; they must not be wiped with paper. Zhongtong says: Washed in boiling water they dry easily, yet soaking in spring water nourishes the stone; if ink glue has long made it slippery, rub it lightly with light charcoal.

《器用類·假鴝眼 Imitation Mynah's Eyes》

1
○取龜溺和色，先炙石而塗之，直穿石底。郭林曰：縳龜以火煏之，又乍冷之，以聲驚之，彼即溺出。中通按：李士材云：以豬鬃鼻內戳之，即出。
○ Mix turtle urine with colour, first heat the stone and paint it on, and it penetrates right through to the bottom of the stone. Guo Lin says: Tie up a turtle and heat it with fire, then suddenly chill it and startle it with noise, and it will pass urine. Zhongtong notes: Li Shicai says that if one pokes inside its nose with a pig bristle, it comes out at once.

《器用類·仿宋硯 Imitation Song Inkstones》

1
○磚磨後置沙中，敲之則缺痕古，上墨塗之，浸水半日，洗去，幹復塗墨，久即上繡，加礬易上，然不如空墨水之有光。王肯堂曰：研瓦始唐人。邵伯溫曰：研中隆起如瓦，以不留墨為貴，百年後方可就平，今窪中示古，何為乎？
○ After the brick is ground, put it in sand and knock it so that the chips look old, smear it with ink, soak it in water for half a day, wash it off, and when dry smear on ink again; in time it takes on rust, more easily with alum added, but it does not have the gloss of the ink-water [left] empty. Wang Kentang says: Inkstone tiles began with the Tang. Shao Bowen says: An inkstone raised in the middle like a tile is valued for not holding ink, and only after a hundred years does it become flat; why then do people now hollow the middle to show age?

《器用類·墨法 Methods for Ink》

1
○石腦油煙作墨，堅重；以松煙者，疏而碧，今不必也。李廷珪上矣。近代程君房、方于魯、祝彥輔、羅小華、丁南羽、邵青丘、吳去塵、吳百昌、象玄、潘方凱、方回、嘉客、環山、方伯暗、敷遠，不惜萬金，故得合諸家秘法。君房、于魯以神宗見取名彰耳。法以蘇木煮草，去草性，燃煙。所製金紫照人。漆煙作墨，其色反白。獨用豬脂作墨，不成。君房以脂入頂煙六分之一，其墨細潤，書畫尚焉。其入金泥、朱沙、冰麝者，貴之也。水用葟山易泉。膠用鹿膠，阿膠次，廣膠天雨錐之不動，市上牛皮膠合者耎矣。膽用熊膽，次青魚膽。吳去塵獨用豬膽，為其易也。取其動而化膠，黑能增色也，然豬膽久之適如其質而已，不能增也。古藏墨以豹囊，然至燕地往往化粉，惟君房、伯暗兩家墨入燕，堅倍於南。昔君房、伯暗互相問而不言，各悟而後知，若合符節，良以北方風高，法以百日酎釀，筋用三銖，故不碎也。製以秋和之日，無風重房，萬杵乃成，所忌者天陰，陰則墨餲矣。試法：頂煙墨口可以截紙，投清水碗中一晝夜如故，熊膽力也。君房玄元靈氣、于魯青鱗髓、潘方凱石蓮秘寶、方回宜宜堂、嘉客客道人、吳百昌紫雪、程孟陽松圓閣、方伯暗寫經墨、澤遠一笏金、敷遠碧水神珠、廣居神隨，皆累試累驗之，其他亦不勝記矣。驗墨之成色與白金等，潘嘉客試墨帖言之甚詳。其磨各墨而浸觀其黑者，一端也。其以新而冒舊者，窌之石灰中一晝夜而出者也。儲泳袪疑曰：墨在製膠、煎膠、練杵鐵石臼中而已，區區秦皮、紫草，適足為累。中通曰：墨用膠輕，故須萬杵乃勻。
○ Ink made from petroleum soot is hard and heavy; that from pine soot is loose and blue, but is not now necessary. Li Tinggui was supreme. In recent times Cheng Junfang, Fang Yulu, Zhu Yanfu, Luo Xiaohua, Ding Nanyu, Shao Qingqiu, Wu Quchen, Wu Baichang, Xiangxuan, Pan Fangkai, Fang Hui, Jiake, Huanshan, Fang Bo'an and Fuyuan spared no expense, and so were able to combine the secret methods of all the schools. Junfang and Yulu became famous because they were taken up by Emperor Shenzong. The method is to boil the grass with sappanwood to remove the grassy nature, and burn it for soot. What they made shone with gold and purple. Ink made from lacquer soot is, contrary to expectation, whitish. Ink made with pig fat alone does not set. Junfang added fat to a sixth of top soot, and his ink was fine and moist, prized for writing and painting. Those with gold paste, cinnabar, borneol and musk added are valued. For water, spring water from Huangshan is used. For glue, deer glue is used, donkey glue next; Guang glue cannot be pierced with an awl in rainy weather, while the ox-hide glue mixed in the market is soft. For gall, bear gall is used, black-carp gall next. Wu Quchen alone used pig gall, because it is easy. It is used because it moves and dissolves the glue and its blackness increases the colour; but pig gall in time merely matches its substance and cannot increase it. Of old, ink was stored in leopard-skin bags, but on reaching the Yan region it often turned to powder; only the ink of the two houses of Junfang and Bo'an, on entering Yan, is twice as hard as in the south. Formerly Junfang and Bo'an asked each other but did not tell; each realized it himself, and afterwards they found they agreed like tallies; it was because the wind in the north is strong, and their method was to brew with hundred-day wine and use three zhu of sinew, so it does not break. It is made on a mild autumn day, in an inner room without wind, finished with ten thousand pounding strokes, and what is avoided is cloudy weather, for in cloudy weather the ink spoils. The test: the edge of top-soot ink can cut paper, and thrown into a bowl of clear water it is unchanged after a day and night, by the strength of the bear gall. Junfang's Xuanyuan Spiritual Qi, Yulu's Blue Scale Marrow, Pan Fangkai's Stone Lotus Secret Treasure, Fang Hui's Yiyi Hall, Jiake's Guest Daoist, Wu Baichang's Purple Snow, Cheng Mengyang's Pine Circle Pavilion, Fang Bo'an's Sutra-Copying Ink, Zeyuan's One Tablet of Gold, Fuyuan's Blue Water Divine Pearl and Guangju's Shensui have all been tested repeatedly and proved; others are too many to record. The grading of ink is like that of silver, and Pan Jiake's Notes on Testing Ink discuss it in great detail. Grinding various inks and soaking them to see which is blackest is one approach. New ink passed off as old has been buried in lime for a day and night and taken out. Chu Yong's Dispelling Doubts says: Ink depends only on making the glue, boiling the glue and pounding it in an iron or stone mortar; trifles like ash bark and gromwell are merely an encumbrance. Zhongtong says: Ink uses little glue, so it needs ten thousand pounding strokes to be even.

《器用類·冬硯 Inkstones in Winter》

1
○冬以楊花鋪硯槽，則不冰，曰春風膏。或鹽磨墨，或火酒。
○ In winter, if willow catkins are spread in the inkstone well it will not freeze; this is called spring-wind paste. Or grind ink with salt, or with distilled liquor.

《器用類·劣墨發光法 Methods of Making Poor Ink Lustrous》

1
○臘梅皮浸水磨墨，有光，用白糖則膠矣。兆兗曰：冬用米醋磨墨。
○ Ink ground in water in which wintersweet bark has been soaked has a lustre; with white sugar it becomes sticky. Zhaoyan says: In winter grind ink with rice vinegar.

《器用類·帖拓類 Rubbings》

1
○舊拓紙薄，久則磨光，而紙面無氣味，即謂之墨香可也。淳化閣帖，棗木刻，用匱紙，或用烏金拓，或蟬翼拓，有銀挺擐痕。太觀刻于太清樓。潘師且刻為絳帖。希白潭州帖。劉次莊戲魚堂。此外武岡、彭州、泉州諸本不一。若寶賢堂、東書堂，則周籓、晉籓拓也。陶九成言蘭亭百十七刻，定武褚臨，皆宋人臨者多矣。金石集于歐陽公、趙德甫，鐘鼎篆韻合於薛用敏，徽欽時上好下趣，容可盡信耶？漢魏一字、三字石經少矣。陝碑為唐鄭覃書。又有孟蜀石經、南宋石經，詳見通雅。餘全人曰：淳化閣帖，宋季南狩，遺于泉州，已湮池中，時出光怪，發之，即是馬蹄真跡，謂銀錠擐痕也。中通曰：拓先煮墨，絹包試而拓之，為蟬翼，須蠟一摩，北方用駱駝油，市上用雞子青。中履曰：或以燭油入墨礬。兆兗曰：墨亦礬調，待紙將乾，則勻而不枯不滲也。刷雞卵青者，天陰轉潮，須洗發團之，微用白蠟磨拓帖面，永遠發光，又可常用。
○ Old rubbings have thin paper, which in time is rubbed glossy, and the paper surface has no smell; this may be called the fragrance of ink. The Chunhua Pavilion Model Calligraphies were carved on jujube wood and printed on gui paper, either in black-gold rubbing or cicada-wing rubbing, with marks of silver ingot fastenings. The Taiguan [calligraphies] were carved at the Taiqing Tower. Pan Shidan carved them as the Jiang Model Calligraphies. Xibai made the Tanzhou Model Calligraphies. Liu Cizhuang made the Xiyu Hall [calligraphies]. Besides these, the editions of Wugang, Pengzhou, Quanzhou and others are various. The Baoxian Hall and Dongshu Hall are rubbings of the Zhou and Jin princely houses. Tao Jiucheng speaks of a hundred and seventeen carvings of the Orchid Pavilion; of the Dingwu and Chu copies, most are copies by Song people. Metal and stone inscriptions were collected by Lord Ouyang and Zhao Defu, and the seal script of bells and tripods brought together by Xue Yongmin; in the time of Huizong and Qinzong those below followed what those above loved — can it all be believed? Of the one-script and three-script stone classics of the Han and Wei, few remain. The Shaan stele [classics] were written by Zheng Tan of the Tang. There are also the stone classics of the Meng [house of] Shu and of the Southern Song, detailed in the Comprehensive Elegance. Yu Quanren says: The Chunhua Pavilion Model Calligraphies, when the Song fled south at the end, were lost at Quanzhou and long submerged in a pond, where strange lights sometimes appeared; when dug out, they were the true traces of the horse hoofs, meaning the marks of the silver ingot fastenings. Zhongtong says: In rubbing, first boil the ink, wrap it in silk, test it and then rub, which makes cicada wing; it must be given one rub with wax; in the north they use camel fat, and in the market egg white. Zhonglü says: Some put candle grease into the ink-alum. Zhaoyan says: Ink too is mixed with alum, and if one waits until the paper is nearly dry it is even and neither dry nor bleeding. Those brushed with egg white, when the weather turns damp, must be washed off and rolled up; rubbing the face of the rubbing lightly with white wax makes it lustrous for ever and usable all the time.

《器用類·書畫類 Calligraphy and Painting》

1
○書貴鐘王，而虞取其健秀，褚取其緊俊，歐顏取其矩法，後則分體擅長。黃山谷謂樂毅論為王著書小僧縳律，楊凝式為散僧入聖，獨服東坡之沉著，張旭書智雍壁，楷法妙絕，而草書顛逸，字字皆在法度中，此善評也。畫以小李思訓、李成、郭熙為北宗，王維以至董、巨、倪、黃為南宗，究不相掩，各臻其致。大約紙絹無過千年者，自黃長睿、董彥遠、米襄陽諸公所謂宋時已無晉物，今有宋元人摹仿筆，即是神品。唐絹粗扁，宋絹極細。新絲燒之則蹴然而就焦，焦作絲臭；年久者否。藏此者貴舒卷，即免濕蠹，然火燭、風日、不洗手，最忌觀玩。河東張愛賓論鑒識收藏，精矣。中德曰：松江畫絹三錢餘一方，方闊三尺，可以比宋。宣德雲鶴裱綾，亦有織者。南京則粗絺不精也。
○ In calligraphy Zhong [You] and Wang [Xizhi] are valued, while Yu [Shinan] is taken for his vigorous elegance, Chu [Suiliang] for his tight refinement, and Ouyang [Xun] and Yan [Zhenqing] for their rules and methods; later each excelled in a separate style. Huang Shangu said that Wang Zhu's writing of the Treatise on Yue Yi is a little monk bound by the precepts and Yang Ningshi a free monk entering sagehood, and he admired only Dongpo's steadiness; Zhang Xu's writing on the wall of Zhiyong had a wonderful standard script, yet his cursive was wild and free, every character within the rules — these are good judgments. In painting, the Lesser Li [Zhaodao], Li Sixun, Li Cheng and Guo Xi are taken as the Northern School, and Wang Wei down to Dong [Yuan], Ju[ran], Ni [Zan] and Huang [Gongwang] as the Southern School; in the end neither eclipses the other, and each reaches its own excellence. In general no paper or silk lasts more than a thousand years; Huang Changrui, Dong Yanyuan, Mi Xiangyang and others said that in the Song there were already no Jin objects, so copies by Song and Yuan people now are divine works. Tang silk is coarse and flat, Song silk very fine. New silk when burnt shrivels and chars at once, and the char smells of silk; old silk does not. In storing them, rolling and unrolling is important, to avoid damp and insects; but fire and candles, wind and sun, and unwashed hands are most to be avoided in viewing. Zhang Aibin of Hedong's discussion of connoisseurship and collecting is very fine. Zhongde says: Songjiang painting silk at three qian and more a square, the square three chi wide, can compare with the Song. The Xuande cloud-and-crane mounting damask is also woven. That of Nanjing is coarse gauze and not fine.

《器用類·裝潢法 Methods of Mounting》

1
○裱褙作池，故曰潢也。太約黏宜省漿，裱宜綿紙，多刷自沁，故懸壁不瓦。鑲攢必俟天潤。上壁貴其滋調，下壁則宜燥矣。裱冊挖紙，不在綾絹，續用芨背，縫忌當畫心，卷舒久之，必然有損。吳中湯強為精，強氏常住王弇州園，今顧元方稱莊希叔頡頏湯強，周江左嘗記之。其時貴秋冬，春中，夏下，忌暑溽也。春以皂角一挺，滑石、白礬、川椒、黃蠟、油各一兩；夏用蠟、礬、艾各一兩，金精石、木鱉子、秦艽、白芨、芫花各半兩，烏頭、信、巴豆少許，皂角一挺；秋加石燕少許；冬用黃蠟一兩，白礬五錢，礦砂二錢，茯苓三兩，鹽三錢，面一斤。其砑子者，用白芨餳并熬，以肥皂槎水一升，聽後用蠟一處同煎。蘇用撞裱，挖散，即無接縫高低。
○ Mounting and backing makes a pool, hence it is called huang [pool-dyeing]. In general the paste should be thin, the mounting should use cotton paper, and frequent brushing makes it soak in of itself, so that it does not warp when hung on the wall. Inlaying and joining must wait for humid weather. For putting it on the wall, moistness is valued, and for taking it down dryness is suitable. In mounting albums the cut paper should not be in the damask or silk; when joining, back them with bletilla, and the seams must avoid the centre of the painting, for when rolled and unrolled long they are sure to be damaged. In Wu, Tang Qiang was the finest, and Master Qiang lived constantly in the garden of Wang Yanzhou; now Gu Yuanfang says Zhuang Xishu rivals Tang Qiang, as Zhou Jiangzuo once recorded. As for the seasons, autumn and winter are best, spring middling and summer worst, because of summer heat and damp. In spring use a stick of honey locust and a tael each of talc, alum, Sichuan pepper, yellow wax and oil; in summer a tael each of wax, alum and mugwort, half a tael each of jinjing stone, momordica seed, gentiana, bletilla and genkwa, a little aconite, arsenic and croton, and a stick of honey locust; in autumn add a little stone swallow; in winter a tael of yellow wax, five qian of alum, two qian of ore sand, three taels of poria, three qian of salt and a jin of flour. For burnishing, use bletilla and malt sugar boiled together with a sheng of soapberry water, and then boil it together with wax. In Su they use butt-mounting, cutting the pieces apart, and then there are no uneven seams.

《器用類·黏法 Paste Methods》

1
○花椒湯去椒，以盆冷之，旋糝白麵，令其慢沈，不可攪動，明早攪勻，或浸數日，每日一攪，淋去原浸之湯，卻入白礬末、乳香少許，新水調和，冷鍋旋擂，不令結塊，方以慢熟之，切作塊，以原湯浸椒湯煮之，攪勻再煮，候熟，取置盆內，用冷水浸其面，常換水，可留數月用之。碑帖外殼，宜以芨、礬、蠟、椒、乳香三伏厚黏，曝而壓之，成器後須近人氣，如床榻上，待一年後藥性已定，其堅如石，永不蒸蛀矣。李如一曰：加楮汁更牢。或面和芨末，入水，不可將水入面也。中通曰：面作塊，入椒、礬、白蠟末，用水煮之，俟麵浮起為度，後入清水浸之，至省漿白泛即易水，待其泛盡取出，待乾，以入白芨汁作糊，永遠不受霉濕。欲以葫蘆褶紙，則用抄紙之漿滉之。
○ Remove the pepper from Sichuan pepper water, cool it in a basin, sprinkle in white flour gradually so that it sinks slowly without stirring, and stir it evenly the next morning; or soak it several days, stirring once a day, pour off the original soaking water, then add powdered alum and a little frankincense, mix with fresh water, grind it in a cold pan without letting it form lumps, then cook it slowly, cut it into pieces, boil it in the original pepper water, stir evenly and boil again until done, put it in a basin, cover the surface with cold water, change the water often, and it can be kept for several months. For the outer covers of rubbings, paste them thickly in the dog days with bletilla, alum, wax, pepper and frankincense, dry them in the sun and press them; once made, they must be kept near human warmth, as on a bed, and after a year, when the drugs have settled, they are hard as stone and never get damp or insects. Li Ruyi says: Adding paper-mulberry sap makes it stronger. Or mix the flour with powdered bletilla and put it into water; one must not pour the water onto the flour. Zhongtong says: Make the flour into lumps, add pepper, alum and powdered white wax, boil it in water until the flour floats up, then soak it in clear water, changing the water when the white starch rises, take it out when no more rises, dry it, and make it into paste with bletilla juice, and it will never be affected by mould or damp. To pleat paper with gourd [paste], wash it with the slurry used in making paper.

《器用類·畫色 Painting Colours》

1
○靛花、藤黃作綠。研赭合膠。朱用砂乳，謂之綽絳。其研飛大青、石綠者，後必托粉。花蕙用胭脂、碧蟬、秦皮。配色要鮮。水墨加色，浸沁顯青紙可也先沁黃水，宿夜沁之，乃藍。生紙細染，界處以礬筆襯之。磨墨待澄，用其浮者。遠山飽水，筆尖蘸淡墨抹之，噴濕烘染，深淺不沁。中通曰：朱砂不變色，銀朱、紅花久在風日則變。畫家乳朱砂，漂之，去浮，用中者乳而用之，標作丹赭用，印色用標，取其細也。石青難研，以耳塞粟許彈入，便成粉。
○ Indigo flowers and gamboge make green. Ground ochre is mixed with glue. Vermilion is made by milling cinnabar, which is called chuojiang. For ground and levigated azurite and malachite, the back must be supported with white. For flowers, rouge, blue-cicada and ash bark are used. Colour matching should be fresh. For ink-wash with colour added, soaking to show on blue paper will do first soak with yellow water, and after soaking overnight it becomes blue. For fine dyeing on raw paper, back the borders with an alum brush. Grind the ink and let it settle, using what floats. For distant mountains, saturate the brush with water and wipe on pale ink with the tip, spray it wet and dry-dye it, and the shades will not bleed. Zhongtong says: Cinnabar does not change colour, but vermilion and safflower change if long exposed to wind and sun. Painters mill cinnabar, levigate it, remove what floats, mill the middle part and use it, use the top part for red ochre, and use the top part for seal ink because it is fine. Azurite is hard to grind; flick in a millet-grain's worth of earwax and it becomes powder.

《器用類·異色 Strange Colours》

1
○雜俎言畫鬼神目隨人轉，以曾青和壁魚也。盛伯舍為錢仲馭取螢畫漁火，愚笑之。蓋禁方用梟精、螢尾、乳金，則夜有光，想合方別有法耳。宋高文虎蓼花洲錄云：徐諤得畫牛，晝在攔外，夜歸攔中。李煜獻太宗，遍問不知，惟僧贊寧曰：南倭海中方諸蚌淚，得之和色，則晝隱而夜見；沃焦風燒嵐石落，得之磨色，則晝見而夜晦。見張騫海外經。後杜鎬簡三館，果見之。物理久而愈出，信然。
○ The Miscellany says that painted ghosts and spirits whose eyes follow people are done with azurite mixed with silverfish. Sheng Boshe painted fishing fires with fireflies for Qian Zhongyu, and I laughed at him. For the secret prescription uses owl essence, firefly tails and milled gold, and then it shines at night; I suppose the compounding has another method. Gao Wenhu of the Song, in the Records of Polygonum Flower Isle, says: Xu E obtained a painting of an ox which by day was outside the pen and at night returned inside the pen. Li Yu presented it to Taizong, who asked everyone and none knew, except the monk Zanning, who said: In the sea of southern Wa there are the tears of the fangzhu mussel; mixed into colour, they make it hidden by day and visible by night; at Wojiao, when the wind burns, mountain stones fall, and ground into colour they make it visible by day and dark by night. This is seen in Zhang Qian's Classic of Lands beyond the Seas. Later Du Hao examined the three institutes and indeed found it. The principles of things come out more and more over time — truly so.

《器用類·寫影法 Method of Portraiture》

1
○首腰足三停，立則為首者七，坐則為首者五。自頂寧至下頷，以眼為中；自眼至頷，以准頭為中。其面或長或短，或仰鼻，或大口，眼更多變耳。視何等老少，在皴顴頦見勢，此其概也。畫家皆以紙板對臨，以草灰紙隔而砑其紋，乃以筆分之。愚見魏子一於扇上水墨為人寫真，一一逼肖，故是神穎。通曰：眼用墨圈藤黃而細破之。有玻鏡攝摹法。暄曰：畫色用膠蓋之，永不為塵侵。
○ The head, waist and feet are three divisions; standing, the figure is seven heads, and seated five heads. From the crown to the lower jaw, the eyes are the middle; from the eyes to the jaw, the tip of the nose is the middle. The face may be long or short, with an upturned nose or a large mouth, and the eyes are even more varied. See what age the person is, and the posture is seen in the wrinkles, cheekbones and chin; this is the general outline. Painters all copy facing a paper board, burnish the lines through a sheet of straw-ash paper, and then divide them with the brush. I saw Wei Ziyi paint portraits in ink on fans, each one lifelike — truly a divine talent. Tong says: For the eyes use an ink circle with gamboge, finely broken. There is a method of copying with a glass mirror. Xuan says: If painted colours are covered with glue, they are never invaded by dust.

《器用類·刻碑法 Method of Carving Steles》

1
○雙鉤過朱，難得入神。米元章謂顏魯公筆迹，家僮修改，波磔失真，惟廬山、吉州題名為不失也。神隱曰：樂毅論是就石書丹。張大復曰：章簡甫搬涉，其韻愈勝。而章林石田曰：但守鋒穎，不差毫髮，庶幾可嗣先民。
○ With double outlining and tracing in red, it is hard to capture the spirit. Mi Yuanzhang said that Yan Lugong's brush traces were altered by household servants and the strokes lost their truth; only the inscriptions at Lushan and Jizhou did not lose it. The Shenyin says: The Treatise on Yue Yi was written in red directly on the stone. Zhang Dafu says: Zhang Jianfu carried and copied it, and its resonance was all the better. And Zhang Lin of Shitian says: Only keep the brush tip without deviating a hair's breadth, and one may succeed the ancients.

《器用類·摹帖紙 Paper for Tracing Model Calligraphy》

1
○響拓槌蠟，明如魫角。次捶油紙。若熟桐油重塗棉紙，久則茶洗，可用數年。
○ For tracing against the light, paper beaten with wax is as clear as fish-head bone or horn. Next is beaten oiled paper. If cotton paper is coated several times with boiled tung oil, it can be washed with tea when old and used for several years.

《器用類·礬書白字 Alum Writing and White Characters》

1
○皂礬水寫字，入五倍子水中；鹽鹵寫紙，烘以火；草麻子油寫，撒紙灰或杏仁灰；俱可見。又曰：白芨研汁入礦灰，蘸礦灰書黃竹紙，俱如丹。
○ Writing with green-vitriol water, put into gallnut water; writing on paper with brine, heated over fire; writing with castor oil, sprinkled with paper ash or apricot-kernel ash — all can be seen. It is also said: Grind bletilla juice with quicklime, dip [the brush] in it and write on yellow bamboo paper, and all appears like cinnabar.

《器用類·寫不上者有法 Methods for Things That Will Not Take Writing》

1
○姜汁研墨，書布絹，不湮。石工以大蒜粘石。市鍋者以蘿卜書鍋。肥皂水磨墨，書油紙、畫燭。
○ Ink ground with ginger juice writes on cloth and silk without blotting. Stoneworkers stick to stone with garlic. Pot sellers write on pots with radish. Ink ground with soap water writes on oiled paper and paints candles.

《器用類·字浮水上法 Method of Making Characters Float on Water》

1
○黃檗、白礬就字書紙，乘濕入水沈之，去紙，則字畫脫浮於水面。或磨黃芩。
○ Write on paper over characters with phellodendron and alum, plunge it into water while wet, remove the paper, and the strokes come away and float on the surface of the water. Or grind scutellaria.

《器用類·洗畫上黑法 Methods of Washing Black from Paintings》

1
○畫上粉被黑，或硫煙熏，以石灰湯蘸洗二三次，則色復舊。中通曰：周江左言：用枇杷核洗畫上霉，錘，浸滾水，冷定洗之，則霉氣垢污盡去。亦可皂角。又須急以清水淋去枇核、皂角之餘氣。
○ When white pigment on a painting has turned black, or been smoked with sulphur fumes, dab and wash it two or three times with limewater, and the colour returns to its former state. Zhongtong says: Zhou Jiangzuo says that to wash mould from paintings, pound loquat stones, soak them in boiling water, wash with it when cooled, and the mould and grime all go. Honey locust also serves. Then one must quickly rinse off the remaining vapour of the loquat stones and honey locust with clear water.

《器用類·洗誤字法 Methods of Washing Out Mistaken Characters》

1
○宋久夫云：蔓荊子二錢，龍骨一錢，柏子五分，白丁香十條，定粉少許，俱為細末，點水於字上，取藥摻之，少頃拂去。一方：鷹糞白、馬珂螺粉洒之，數洗即去。中通曰：濕之，覆以紙，取瑪瑙砑之，則墨自去。防奸者寫成後以桐油沁之，則必不可洗補。
○ Song Jiufu says: Two qian of vitex seeds, one qian of dragon bone, five fen of arborvitae seeds, ten white cloves and a little lead white, all finely powdered; put water on the character, sprinkle the powder on, and brush it off after a moment. One prescription: sprinkle hawk-dung white and powdered horse-cowrie shell, and after a few washings it goes. Zhongtong says: Wet it, cover it with paper and burnish it with agate, and the ink comes away of itself. To guard against forgery, after writing soak it with tung oil, and then it can never be washed and altered.

《器用類·頃刻碑法 Method of Instant Steles》

1
○以鐵浸水，加礬，以白筆書之，拖墨硯上，則礬不受墨，成白字。又以此書紙上，不見字，浮水即見。舊傳烏鲗腹水立卷，久則不見，今試之，先書時不似墨，但變色耳。中履曰：以椑汁磨墨，拖礬書之紙。
○ Soak iron in water, add alum, write with a white brush, pass ink over it on the inkstone, and the alum does not take the ink, forming white characters. If one writes with this on paper, the characters are not visible, but floated on water they appear. It was formerly said that cuttlefish ink makes contracts that disappear in time; trying it now, when first written it does not look like ink but merely changes colour. Zhonglü says: Grind ink with persimmon juice and pass it over alum writing on paper.

《器用類·藏書闢蠹 Protecting Books from Insects》

1
○芸香即七里香，山谷謂之山礬，非楓膠香也。郿縣石魚、商山必慄香作書軸，白魚不犯，蓋沉、檀、降香作軸皆不蠹也。尋常宜用桐杉，膠則易蒸，糊則生炷，以苦楝子末生面粘之。犀玉雖貴，紙何以能勝耶？春宮圖謂之籠底書，以此辟蠹，乃厭之也。莽草、萵苣熏之，即去。愚者曰：竹紙有漿粉，故易生蠹，真綿紙書不生蠹。古用黃卷，以漬檗殺蟲也。中履曰：書廚可置樟腦、雄黃，以芨表之。
○ Rue is the seven-li fragrance, which Shangu called mountain alum; it is not sweetgum resin. Stone fish of Mei county and bilu fragrance of Shangshan used for book rollers are not touched by silverfish, and indeed rollers of aloeswood, sandalwood or rosewood are never insect-eaten. Ordinarily paulownia or fir should be used; with glue it easily gets damp, and with paste it breeds mould, so stick them with powdered chinaberry seed and raw flour. Rhinoceros horn and jade may be precious, but how can paper bear them? Erotic pictures are called "bottom-of-the-box books," because they ward off insects by suppressing them. Fumigated with illicium or lettuce, [insects] go at once. The Foolish One says: Bamboo paper has starch and powder, so it easily breeds insects, while books of true cotton paper do not. The ancients used yellow scrolls because soaking in phellodendron kills insects. Zhonglü says: Camphor and realgar can be placed in book cabinets, covered with bletilla.

《器用類·揭濕黏法 Method of Opening Damp-Stuck [Pages]》

1
○書邑水不開者，火酒燒過，含其剩水噴之，即開。黏而欲其易開者，豆粉黏也。
○ When a book is stuck by water and will not open, burn distilled liquor, take the remaining liquid in the mouth and spray it, and it opens at once. For sticking that is meant to open easily, stick with bean-flour paste.

《器用類·印色芝泥法 Methods for Seal Ink》

1
○茶油四兩，皂角九錢，白芨去皮毛五錢，金毛狗取毛一錢，老生姜去皮五錢，以武火熬，文火漫收之，滴珠為候，絹濾去滓，再煎數沸，艾絨四錢，銀朱四錢，油調之，再蒸再曬，聽用。朱太環曰：入乾姜熬油者，等其重，去其姜，而陽光所漉，濁者下矣，以之印紙易乾，冬用之不凍，與艾絨和而不殖。松雪齋用麻油一兩，入七萆麻肉，熬令色改，即漉去，投芨、椒、礬、牙皂，文武三沸，又濾去，俟油成珠，投黃蠟少許，濾去其滓，貯瓷瓶，瘞地月餘，用水花銀朱研淘加油，染煮帛、熟艾、鵝毛、吐絲之類，以絹囊縫固，內於印池。朱六錢加辰砂四錢為優。吳寧野紀用川山甲油，取其不滲。程穆倩加大青，取其發紫艷也。盛白含得錢仲馭法，以燈心調粉內之，碾則易碎，再以水澄其粉在下，以之受油，妙於艾絨。中德曰：埋萆麻油三年，取出，色白如冰，復曬之熟，每斤入黃蠟一錢，白礬一錢，芨二錢，貯之。研朱砂，水飛，去黃標與砂腳，用中間細者入碗，加燒酒，微火煮一炷香，取乾，又研，乃拌艾絨，艾絨以屢煮屢磨乃熟如綿。中通曰：油於印外者，加白斂。中履曰：蘇合油最斂，入附子則金扇面立乾，或入陀僧。謝在杭曰：瓶油合朱砂，印色隱起。
○ Four taels of tea oil, nine qian of honey locust, five qian of bletilla with skin and hairs removed, one qian of hair taken from golden-haired dog fern and five qian of old ginger peeled, boiled over a strong fire and gathered slowly over a gentle one, until it forms beads when dripped; strain out the dregs through silk, boil again several times, mix four qian of mugwort floss and four qian of vermilion with the oil, steam and sun it again, and it is ready for use. Zhu Taihuan says: For oil boiled with dried ginger, use an equal weight, remove the ginger, and filter it in the sun so that the turbid part settles; seals made with it dry easily on paper, do not freeze in winter, and mixed with mugwort floss it does not go bad. The Songxue Studio used a tael of sesame oil with seven castor beans added, boiled until the colour changed and then filtered, added bletilla, pepper, alum and soap pods, brought it to the boil three times over strong and gentle fire, filtered again, and when the oil formed beads added a little yellow wax, filtered out the dregs, stored it in a porcelain bottle buried in the ground for over a month, then used water-flower vermilion, ground and washed, with oil added, dyeing boiled silk, ripe mugwort, goose down, spun silk and the like, sewn up firmly in a silk bag and put in the seal-ink box. Six qian of vermilion with four qian of Chenzhou cinnabar is excellent. Wu Ningye's records use pangolin oil because it does not bleed. Cheng Muqian added azurite, to bring out a purplish brilliance. Sheng Baihan obtained Qian Zhongyu's method of mixing in powder with rush pith; when ground it breaks easily, and the powder is settled again with water at the bottom and used to take up the oil, better than mugwort floss. Zhongde says: Bury castor oil for three years and take it out, and it is white as ice; sun it again until ripe, add to each jin a qian of yellow wax, a qian of alum and two qian of bletilla, and store it. Grind cinnabar and levigate it, removing the yellow top and the sandy bottom, put the fine middle part in a bowl, add distilled liquor, boil over a gentle fire for the time of one incense stick, dry it, grind it again and mix it with mugwort floss, the floss being boiled and ground repeatedly until soft as cotton. Zhongtong says: If the oil spreads beyond the seal, add ampelopsis. Zhonglü says: Storax oil contracts it most; with aconite added the gold fan surface dries at once, or litharge may be added. Xie Zaihang says: Bottled oil combined with cinnabar makes the seal ink stand up in relief.

《器用類·香類 Incense》

1
○沉香出海南諸國，木類權櫸，凌冬不凋，橘葉蕤花，實如檳桄，自腐結香曰熟結，上也；斫鑿而結曰生結；水朽曰脫落；因蠹隙結曰蟲漏；各有沈棧，一作箋，即速也。黃熟，其剔取也。雞骨等名，其狀也角革、黃烏、水碗、青桂、蠟繭、菌芝、金絡、竹脫、機梭、馬蹄、牛頭、燕口、蝟刺、龍鱗、烏刺、虎脛、鷓斑、仙杖及杵臼、肘拳、鳳雀、龜蛇等。萬安黎母山東峒冠絕，警者雜黎中，秋月夜探之，香透林起，以朝陽幽醞也。奇南同類，自分陰陽。盧頤曰：沈，牝也，味苦性利，其香含藏，燒更芳烈，陰體陽用也；奇南，牡也，味辣，沾舌麻木，其香忽發，而性能閉二便，陽體陰用也。奇南有綠結、油糖、蜜結、金絲、虎斑等，鋸之其屑成團，舶來者佳。沈則瓊甜。金樓子俞益期一木五香，訛也。栴檀是檀香，雞舌是母丁香，薰陸滴垂是乳香，出波斯，類松，藿香是草，何乃以為一木耶？概言皆木出耳。陳懋仁曰：千年榕生伽南。劉薦叔曰：有力之木皆降皆結，其枝皆青托香，其屑皆黃屑也。安息、金顏、篤耨、亞濕、唵叭，皆木脂煉收者。龍腦出婆律、抹羅、短叱諸國，南海深山亦有之，因名波律。其木類杉，葉圓，皮皵，仁粒如縮砂蜜者肥而流膏，其無花實者瘦，有時噴香，人以帛敷地，驚墮如蝶，頃刻吸香入木，不易得也。斷之待乾，析理溢片，或鋸板劈取，有梅花腦、速腦、米腦，赤者曰父婆律。中通曰：吸煙瑣喉者，真。世多升樟腦作冰片。蘇合油能斂香。迷迭草燒之去鬼。龍涎則取諸海木上者，焚真龍涎，翠煙結空，坐可翦分香縷。外紀曰：龍涎是土中產，初流出如脂，至海凝塊，有千斤者。又云：有獸吐涎，曰龍涎香，惟黑人國與伯西兒海最多。泉州市舶稅課云：古城、賓達儂香多，三佛齊多黃檀、藥沈，古城出麝香木，暹羅出羅斛香、梨香、降真香，渤泥有金腳腦、水札腦，登流眉有薔薇水。
○ Aloeswood comes from the countries of the South Sea; the tree resembles the quan and zelkova, does not wither in winter, has orange-like leaves and drooping flowers and fruit like areca; when the resin forms by natural decay it is called ripe formation, the best; that formed after cutting is called raw formation; that rotted by water is called fallen-off; and that formed in insect holes is called worm-leak; each has its chen and zhan kinds, zhan also written jian, which is su. Yellow-ripe is what is picked out. Names like chicken bone describe its shapes horn-leather, yellow-black, water bowl, green cassia, wax cocoon, mushroom, golden net, bamboo slough, loom shuttle, horse hoof, ox head, swallow's mouth, hedgehog spine, dragon scale, black thorn, tiger shin, partridge spot, immortal's staff, and pestle-and-mortar, elbow-and-fist, phoenix-and-sparrow, tortoise-and-snake and so on. That from the eastern caves of Mount Limu in Wan'an is the very best; those who know it mingle among the Li and search for it on autumn moonlit nights, when the fragrance rises through the forest, because the morning sun has secretly brewed it. Qinan is of the same kind, and they are naturally divided into yin and yang. Lu Yi says: Chen is female, bitter in taste and free-flowing in nature, its fragrance stored within and all the more intense when burnt — yin in body and yang in use; qinan is male, pungent in taste, numbing the tongue, its fragrance bursting forth suddenly, and by nature it can close up both excretions — yang in body and yin in use. Qinan has green-formed, oil-sugar, honey-formed, golden-thread, tiger-spot and other kinds; when sawn the sawdust forms clumps; that brought by ship is best. Chen is sweet. The Jinlouzi's [citing of] Yu Yiqi's "one tree with five fragrances" is mistaken. Zhantan is sandalwood, chicken-tongue is mother-clove, xunlu drippings are frankincense, which comes from Persia and resembles pine, and patchouli is a herb — how could they be taken as one tree? He only meant in general that all come from trees. Chen Maoren says: Thousand-year banyans produce qienan. Liu Jianshu says: Strong trees all [yield] jiang and jie; their branches all have green-supported fragrance, and their shavings are all yellow shavings. Benzoin, jinyan, dunou, yashi and anba are all tree resins refined and gathered. Borneol comes from the countries of Polu, Malayu and Duanchi, and is also found deep in the mountains of the South Sea, hence called polu. The tree resembles the fir, with round leaves and rough bark; those with kernels like amomum are fat and flow with resin, while those without flowers or fruit are lean; at times it sprays fragrance, and people spread silk on the ground, and it falls startled like butterflies, but in a moment it sucks the fragrance back into the tree and is hard to obtain. The trunk is cut and left to dry, and flakes overflow along the grain, or it is sawn into boards and split to take it; there are plum-blossom borneol, su borneol and rice borneol, and the red kind is called father polu. Zhongtong says: That which makes the throat feel locked when inhaled is genuine. In the world camphor is often sublimed to pass for borneol. Storax oil can fix fragrance. Rosemary burnt drives away ghosts. Ambergris is taken from sea driftwood; when true ambergris is burnt, the blue-green smoke forms in the air, and one can sit and cut the threads of fragrance apart. The Record of the Outer Regions says: Ambergris is produced in the earth, flows out first like fat, and congeals into lumps on reaching the sea, some of a thousand jin. It also says: There is a beast whose spittle is called ambergris; it is most plentiful in the land of the black people and the sea of Brazil. The tax registers of the Quanzhou maritime trade office say: Champa and Bindanong have much incense, Srivijaya much yellow sandalwood and medicinal aloeswood; Champa produces musk wood; Siam produces Lohu incense, pear incense and rosewood incense; Brunei has golden-foot borneol and shuizha borneol; and Tambralinga has rosewater.

《器用類·奇制法 Methods of Strange Preparations》

1
○有曰金龜吐艷者，以柳灰、丹粉、芨為之，而腹含眾香也。欲其樓臺，則加乾荷葉，午日陰乾；欲見人物，則用男胎髮翦勻入內；欲見仙鶴，則加水秀才中通曰：水面長足蟲，午日收五十枚入之；欲見香球，則加寄生草；欲見白雲鋪地，則加鉛粉。其總藥，則甘松、白芷、零陵、稿本、霍香、大黃、玄參、香附、沉、檀、降、乳各五錢，末而蜜之，甲子日攢，丙子碾，戊子和，庚子丸，壬子盛，再甲子焚之。不作金龜，則為小餅。夷門廣牘曰：瓨內荷葉，五月時以蜜塗之，自有小蟲食其青翠，惟存本葉紗，枯，摘去其柄，暴乾為末，合香入少許，則煙盤於上，用箸分劃為雲篆字，皆可，是亦犛靬之幻也。聚香以艾納、酸棗仁、甲煎甲，螺掩也，取如龍耳者，灰汁酒漉，再以旨酒入蜜炒之，如金。
○ There is one called "golden tortoise spitting beauty," made of willow ash, red lead and bletilla, with all kinds of incense held in its belly. If one wants towers, add dried lotus leaves, dried in the shade at noon; to see figures, use a boy's first hair, cut evenly, put inside; to see immortal cranes, add water scholars Zhongtong says: long-legged insects on the water surface, fifty collected at noon and put in; to see incense balls, add mistletoe; to see white clouds spread over the ground, add lead white. The general ingredients are five qian each of nardostachys, angelica, lysimachia, ligusticum, patchouli, rhubarb, scrophularia, cyperus, aloeswood, sandalwood, rosewood and frankincense, powdered and mixed with honey, gathered on a jiazi day, ground on bingzi, mixed on wuzi, made into pills on gengzi, stored on renzi, and burnt on the next jiazi. If not made into a golden tortoise, they are made into small cakes. The Yimen Guangdu says: Smear the lotus leaves in a jar with honey in the fifth month, and small insects come of themselves to eat the green, leaving only the gauze of the leaf; when withered, remove the stems, dry them in the sun and powder them, and add a little when compounding incense, and the smoke coils above, and can be divided with chopsticks into cloud-seal characters at will — this too is the magic of Lixuan. For gathering incense, use ainafeng, sour jujube seed and jiajian jia is the operculum of a snail; take those like dragon's ears, filter them with lye and wine, then fry them with good wine and honey until golden.

《器用類·選香薰香 Selected Incense and Scenting》

1
○東莞斫白木椿，歲淖堆之，隔年鑿取一層，久則愈妙，今名牙香，亦名黃熟。其生香發青點者，曰選香白香四分一斤，選香斤且二兩。彫去白木，但存油結，坐書帷中，隔蒸其魂，此非淺躁人所領矣。熏香者，三伏包花同暴也，或以芳花同炭紙封曝之。寓簡曰：南方秉火，英華發外，草木多香。
○ In Dongguan they cut the white-wood stumps, heap them in the mud for a year, and the next year chip off a layer, which improves with age; it is now called tooth incense, or yellow-ripe. Raw incense showing green spots is called selected incense white incense is four fen a jin, selected incense a jin and two taels. Carve away the white wood leaving only the oily formations, sit within the study curtains, and steam out its soul through a screen; this is not something shallow and impatient people can appreciate. Scenting is done by wrapping flowers with it and sunning them together in the dog days, or by sealing fragrant flowers with charcoal in paper and sunning them. The Yujian says: The south holds fire, its splendour shows outwardly, and plants are mostly fragrant.

《器用類·合香易簡法 Simple Methods of Compounding Incense》

1
○合香不厭多。麝得蛇蛻，片腦得相思子，香益甚。其入沉、麝、蘇合者，瓷盒蠟封，瘞地月餘為妙。合香除煉蜜，則取圓樹汁似桂，或以榆皮，或黃香皮。嶺南北有青香樹，冬不凋者。范石湖取橄欖脂香、泡花柚花蒸香。其窮六和者，荔枝殼、甘蔗滓、乾柏葉、黃楝頭、梨棗核，任加松楓球江東因呼黃楝頭、楓脂為欖香。稻稈燒甘蔗，米泔浸大黃，再宿燒之，皆妙。
○ In compounding incense, more is never too much. Musk with snake slough and borneol with jequirity seeds become even more fragrant. Those containing aloeswood, musk and storax are best sealed with wax in porcelain boxes and buried in the ground for over a month. In compounding incense, besides refined honey, sap of the round tree like cassia is used, or elm bark, or yellow-fragrance bark. North of the Ling there is the green incense tree, which does not wither in winter. Fan Shihu used olive resin incense and steamed incense with pomelo blossoms pomelo flowers. For "poor man's liuhe," lychee shells, sugarcane dregs, dried cypress leaves, yellow-chinaberry heads and pear and jujube stones, with pine and maple balls added at will in Jiangdong they therefore call yellow-chinaberry heads and maple resin "olive incense". Burning sugarcane with rice straw, or rhubarb soaked in rice water and burnt after two nights, is excellent.

《器用類·焚香法 Methods of Burning Incense》

1
○煤餅之上香錢隔火，或玉片，或雲母，或銀，或砂。屠赤水言翦火浣布銀鑲為最。低几焚香，則煙穗遍化。綿紙裱室，以收香也。楊廷秀云：琢瓷作鼎碧于水，削銀為葉輕於紙。不文不武火力均，閉合垂簾風不起。宓山愚者曰：內經載香氣湊脾，首楞言水沈無令見火，此焚香埋火之昉也。麝、檀、夷香最熱，惟東莞選香養人，倉卒難致，惟窮六和耳。浮山句曰：窮六和香宜土屋，瓦爐茶餅晝夜足。木根野花曝三伏，山人不羨龍涎福。復銘之曰：香舍其身，用其餘魂，燒不見火，密室知恩。
○ Above the coal cake, the incense coin is separated from the fire by a piece of jade, mica, silver or sand. Tu Chishui says a silver-mounted piece of cut asbestos cloth is best. When incense is burnt on a low table, the plumes of smoke spread everywhere. Rooms are papered with cotton paper to hold the fragrance. Yang Tingxiu says: "Porcelain carved into a tripod bluer than water,
silver pared into leaves thinner than paper. Neither strong nor gentle, the fire is even;
shut tight with blinds lowered, no wind rises." The Foolish One of Mishan says: The Inner Classic records that fragrant qi gathers in the spleen, and the Surangama says that aloeswood must not be allowed to see fire; this is the origin of burning incense with buried fire. Musk, sandalwood and foreign incenses are hottest; only the selected incense of Dongguan nourishes people, but it is hard to obtain in a hurry, so there is only the poor man's liuhe. A line of Fushan says: "Poor man's liuhe incense suits an earthen house,
a tile censer and tea cakes suffice day and night. Tree roots and wild flowers sunned in the dog days —
the mountain man does not envy the luck of ambergris." He also inscribed: "Incense gives up its body
and uses its remaining soul;
burning without showing fire,
in a closed room one knows its kindness."

《器用類·琴法 Methods for the Qin》

1
○自臨岳及龍齦分周天度，四分用三。自一徽至四徽，猶九徽至十三也。七為中詳見時論。凡聲從中起，而上下應之，細分二十四徽。木用泡桐。宋久夫謂梧桐作琴，貞白謂剛桐作琴，皆非也。漆灰久則紋斷，有蛇腹、牛毛、梅花數種，其偽者，一火一煉之者也。細無痕者佳。神隱取劍鋒斷。東坡云：琴弦舊則聲暗，桑葉揩之，新，無如其青何。愚者曰：生天門冬煮琴絃，如玉。坡又云：木本實而末虛，惟桐小枝實而本皆中虛，故世貴孫枝也，實故絲中有木聲。愚者曰：高興語耳。琴取泡桐，虛木有聲，又削之而不毛。陽材琴暮清雨清，陰材琴旦清晴清，易時則濁。造琴家古稱雷文、張越池口有唇收聲，成化有豐城萬隆，弘治有杭州惠祥、高騰、祝海鶴。萬歷時，大內造琴五百張，具各種式，不過金玉寶石裝之耳。其大腹者音洪，楊大經云。神隱曰：范連州百納，非法也，為漆礙矣。桐木不宜太松。
○ From the bridge to the dragon's gums [the length] is divided as the degrees of the celestial circle, using three of four parts. From the first to the fourth stud corresponds to the ninth to the thirteenth. The seventh is the middle see the Shilun for details. All sounds arise from the middle, and above and below respond to it, finely divided into twenty-four studs. For wood, empress tree is used. Song Jiufu said the qin is made of wutong, and Zhenbai said of hard tong; both are wrong. When the lacquer and ash are old the pattern cracks, in several kinds, snake-belly, ox-hair and plum-blossom; counterfeits are made by heating and firing. Those that are fine and seamless are good. The Shenyin takes [the kind like] a sword-point break. Dongpo says: When qin strings are old, the sound is dull; rub them with mulberry leaves and they are new, but there is no helping their greenness. The Foolish One says: Strings boiled with raw asparagus root become like jade. Po also says: In trees the base is solid and the ends empty; only in the tong are the small branches solid while the base is hollow, so the world values grandchild branches; being solid, the wood sounds among the silk. The Foolish One says: These are words of high spirits. The qin uses empress-tree wood, which is hollow and resonant, and when shaved does not fluff. A qin of yang wood is clear in the evening and in rain, a qin of yin wood clear in the morning and in fine weather; at other times it is muddy. Of qin makers, antiquity names Lei Wen and Zhang Yue at Chikou there is a lip that gathers the sound; in the Chenghua period there was Wan Long of Fengcheng, and in the Hongzhi period Hui Xiang, Gao Teng and Zhu Haihe of Hangzhou. In the Wanli period the palace made five hundred qin of every style, merely decorated with gold, jade and gems. Those with large bellies have a grand tone, as Yang Dajing said. The Shenyin says: The hundred-patch qin of Fan Lianzhou is not the proper method, being hindered by lacquer. Tong wood should not be too loose.

《器用類·窯器本末 The Origins and Development of Ceramics》

1
○柴、汝、官、哥、定，宋窯之名也。元美曰：柴窯，柴世宗進御。葉填言錢鏐燒。陸龜蒙詠越窯：奪得千峰翠色來。則唐有矣。定州白磁有芒，遂命汝州造青窯器。均州五色，皆汝之類也。江南處州龍泉有窯。政和間，京師自置官窯。南宋邵成章提舉內窯，號邵局，最佳。餘則有烏泥、餘杭續窯、樞府窯之類。陸文裕曰：宋處州章生一、生二兄弟至龍泉之琉田窯，而生一陶者百淬碎而色淡，故名哥窯，以鐵足有聲為驗。仿定者曰象窯，亦苦無聲。格古錄曰：霍州器，元夾金匠彭君寶所置。高麗窯似龍泉。大食窯，則佛菻藥燒五色者也。麟洲窺天外乘曰：我朝專設于浮梁景德鎮。永樂、宣德內府燒造，以骔眼甜白為常，以蘇麻離青為飾，以鮮紅為寶。成化尚五色炫爛，而回青未有也。回青出外國，正德閒大璫鎮雲南得之，煉石為寶者也。嘉靖閒回青雖盛，鮮紅土斷絕，而上忽命燒大鋼，圍至六七尺。穆宗登極，詔發宣德鮮紅式造，撫臣徐栻言此土已絕，止可采礬紅。今上時且添造棋局矣。楙為九江分守，曾督運二鋼云。吉州永和窯，宋時開，至今有舒翁、舒嬌之器，土人傳文山時窯變，遂廢。建窯今在德化縣，土產程寺後山中，穴而取之，乃大塊白石，舂碎為粉，澄傾石井，始埴為器。白土性急，加泑不得滿，滿則璺，惟佛像滿泑者，以下空也。于司直曰：永樂壓手杯、宣德祭紅杯盤，發古未有，以西紅寶石末之入泑，凸起者也。茶巴鹵壺、匾罐、爐瓶、盤碟、敞口花尊、暗花坐墩，皆精。成窯草蟲可口、子母雞勸杯，曰雞釭。神廟、光宗皆尚前窯，故價最貴。嘉窯泡盃、壇盞甚佳。蓋永尚厚，成尚薄；宣青尚淡，嘉青尚濃。成青未若宣青，蘇渤泥青也；宣彩未若成彩，淺深入畫也。其同者，汁水瑩厚如堆脂，汁紋雞橘也；質料膩實，不易茅蔑也；磨弄歲深，火色退淨也。四番有乾提窯，無泑，作飲水器。中履曰：鮮紅止燒二碙十二盤，今盤片一塊值百金，紅光四射。
○ Chai, Ru, Guan, Ge and Ding are names of Song kilns. Yuanmei says: The Chai kiln supplied the court of Emperor Shizong, Chai Rong. Ye Tian says that Qian Liu fired it. Lu Guimeng wrote of the Yue kiln: "Seizing the green of a thousand peaks." So the Tang had it. Ding white porcelain had rough edges, so Ruzhou was ordered to make celadon wares. The five colours of Junzhou are all of the Ru kind. At Longquan in Chuzhou, Jiangnan, there were kilns. In the Zhenghe period the capital set up its own official kilns. In the Southern Song, Shao Chengzhang supervised the inner kilns, called the Shao Office, which were the best. Others include Wuni, the Yuhang continuation kiln and the Shufu kiln. Lu Wenyu says: In the Song, the brothers Zhang Shengyi and Zhang Sheng'er of Chuzhou came to the Liutian kiln at Longquan, and Shengyi's pottery had a hundred crackles and pale colour, hence called the Ge [elder brother] kiln, tested by its iron foot and its ring. Imitation Ding is called Xiang kiln, which also lacks ring. The Essential Criteria of Antiquities says: Huozhou ware was established by Peng Junbao, a Yuan gilding craftsman. The Korean kiln resembles Longquan. The Arab kiln is the five colours fired with Byzantine drugs. Linzhou's Further Glimpses of Heaven says: Our dynasty set up kilns exclusively at Jingdezhen in Fuliang. In the imperial manufactories of the Yongle and Xuande periods, sweet white with horsehair eyes was standard, Sumali blue was used for decoration, and fresh red was the treasure. The Chenghua period favoured brilliant five colours, and there was not yet Muslim blue. Muslim blue comes from abroad; a great eunuch garrisoning Yunnan in the Zhengde period obtained it; it is stone refined into a treasure. In the Jiajing period, though Muslim blue was plentiful, the fresh-red earth had run out, and the emperor suddenly ordered large vats to be fired, up to six or seven chi round. When Muzong ascended the throne, he issued Xuande fresh-red patterns for manufacture, but the governor Xu Shi said this earth was exhausted and only alum red could be used. In the present reign they have even added the making of chessboards. Mao, as Assistant Administrator of Jiujiang, once supervised the transport of two vats. The Yonghe kiln of Jizhou was opened in the Song, and there are still wares of Old Shu and Shu Jiao; the locals say that in the time of Wenshan the kiln transformed and so was abandoned. The Jian kiln is now in Dehua county; the earth is produced in the mountain behind the Cheng temple, dug from pits; it is large lumps of white stone, pounded to powder, settled and poured into stone wells, and only then moulded into wares. White earth is quick-natured, and glaze must not be applied fully, for if full it cracks; only Buddhist images are fully glazed, because they are hollow below. Yu Sizhi says: The Yongle press-hand cups and Xuande sacrificial-red cups and dishes were unprecedented, made with powdered Western rubies in the glaze, standing out in relief. The chabalu pots, flat jars, censers and vases, plates and dishes, wide-mouthed flower vases and stools with hidden decoration are all fine. The Chenghua kiln's grass-and-insect cups, pleasant to the mouth, and mother-and-chick wine cups are called chicken cups. Shenmiao and Guangzong both esteemed the earlier kilns, so their prices are highest. The Jiajing kiln's bubble cups and altar cups are very good. For Yongle favoured thickness and Chenghua thinness, Xuande blue favoured paleness and Jiajing blue richness. Chenghua blue does not equal Xuande blue, which is Sumali blue; Xuande colours do not equal Chenghua colours, whose shades are fit for painting. What they share is a glaze lustrous and thick like piled fat, with glaze patterns like chicken[-skin] and orange[-peel]; a body smooth and dense, not easily cracked; and, after years of handling, the fire colour completely gone. The Western barbarians have the Ganti kiln, without glaze, which makes drinking vessels. Zhonglü says: Fresh red was fired for only two vats and twelve plates; now a single shard of a plate is worth a hundred gold, with red light radiating on all sides.

《器用類·磁器之泑草木汁也 Porcelain Glaze Is the Juice of Plants》

1
○愚者訉饒人，皆從袁州市麻，燒而淋其灰汁，即泑也。其餘瓨窯亦取蘆萁柴燒之，而淋取其汁，但有精粗耳。瓨罐等泑，或取草灰汁。其黃色瓨泑，則常土壞而取赤土汁上之者也。中通曰：燒芝麻秸而淋其汁，以染色則成紫，淋灰以浣，亦取其滑也。楫曰：景德鎮燒器土，皆自婺源來，細埴而灰色，燒成乃白。吳強曰：麻倉窯有土可作泑，在婺源界。
○ The Foolish One asked people of Rao, who all buy hemp from Yuanzhou, burn it and leach the ash juice, which is the glaze. The other jar kilns also burn reed stalks and firewood and leach the juice; only there is fine and coarse. For the glaze of jars and pots, grass-ash juice is sometimes used. The glaze of yellow jars is ordinary unfired earth with red-earth juice applied. Zhongtong says: Burn sesame stalks and leach the juice, and dyeing with it gives purple; leaching ash for washing is also for its slipperiness. Ji says: The clay fired at Jingdezhen all comes from Wuyuan, fine-grained and grey, and becomes white only when fired. Wu Qiang says: The Macang kiln has earth that can be made into glaze, on the border of Wuyuan.

《器用類·窯器之青乃石土所畫也 The Blue of Ceramics Is Painted with Mineral Earth》

1
○廬陵、安福、新建出黑赭石，磨水以畫磁壞，初畫無色，入窯燒之，則成天藍。景德窯嘗取諸婺源，名曰畫燒青，一曰無名子。蘇渤泥青，則外國來者。
○ Luling, Anfu and Xinjian produce black ochre stone, which is ground with water to paint unfired porcelain; when first painted it has no colour, but fired in the kiln it becomes sky blue. The Jingde kilns once took it from Wuyuan, calling it painting-firing blue, or wumingzi. Sumali blue comes from abroad.

《器用類·琉璃窯 Glaze Kilns》

1
○北京燒琉璃磚瓦，在陽德門、登豐門，用坩子土、馬牙石入黑鉛燒成。劉晴川魁當嘉靖建雷殿時，曾以此事費用入諫。南京報恩寺琉璃塔，中具五色，則方山岡琉璃門暱土所作也。
○ In Beijing, glazed bricks and tiles are fired at the Yangde and Dengfeng gates, made of crucible earth and horse-tooth stone with black lead added. Liu Qingchuan Kui, when the Thunder Hall was built in the Jiajing period, once remonstrated about the expense of this matter. The glazed pagoda of the Bao'en temple in Nanjing, containing five colours, was made from the ni earth of the Glaze Gate at Fangshangang.

《器用類·黏磁器 Mending Porcelain》

1
○白芨、石灰為末，用雞子白調勻，碎處縛定，待幹，但不可見雞湯。粘官窯，以青竹燒瀝，合雞卵青，縛窯破處，湯內煮一二沸，放陰處三五日，其牢如釘。定窯則濃楮汁可黏。中履曰：生面筋入石灰，久杵，忽化水，可黏磁器，但不可於水內久浸。
○ Powder bletilla and lime, mix evenly with egg white, bind the broken parts firmly and let it dry; but it must not meet chicken broth. To stick official-kiln ware, combine the drippings of burnt green bamboo with egg white, bind the broken parts, boil it once or twice in water, and put it in the shade for three to five days, and it holds fast as if nailed. Ding ware can be stuck with thick paper-mulberry sap. Zhonglü says: Raw gluten with lime added, pounded a long time, suddenly turns liquid and can stick porcelain, but must not be soaked long in water.

《器用類·舟 Boats》

1
○桅之高，少舟之長五十分之一。檣枰之衡為舟之闊。其底深淺，視棤之棱。其柁與其底平。小舟之舵稈則可上可下。船諺曰：船有七飛禽，八走獸，九板一木。又有寸木不見天。此其概也。艌以竹青油灰，吳船歲一油之。溪船底平，江船底深，梭船底尖。江湖用桿榶，兩廣用陽橋。江湖帆在桅左，廣帆在桅右。蜀用布帆。洋船下有直木，皆重底也。其用輪者，古有之矣。袁褧曰：韓蘄王黃天蕩用飛輪八楫，踏車盤回江面。史紬曰：劉裕曾用之，舟內輪轉，外不見人。張燧曰：虞允文采石之役，舟中踏車行船發炮。
○ The height of the mast is a fiftieth less than the length of the boat. The width of the mast-step is the width of the boat. The depth of its bottom depends on the edge of the cross-beams. Its rudder is level with its bottom. The rudder stock of a small boat can be raised and lowered. The boatmen's proverb says: A boat has seven flying birds, eight running beasts, nine planks and one timber. There is also: "an inch of wood does not see the sky." This is the general outline. Caulking is done with bamboo shavings and tung-oil putty, and the boats of Wu are oiled once a year. Stream boats have flat bottoms, river boats deep bottoms, and shuttle boats pointed bottoms. On the rivers and lakes they use gantang, in the two Guang yangqiao. On the rivers and lakes the sail is to the left of the mast, in Guang to the right. Shu uses cloth sails. Ocean ships have straight timbers below, all double-bottomed. Those using wheels existed in antiquity. Yuan Jiong says: Han Qiwang at Huangtian Marsh used flying wheels with eight paddles, pedalled round on the river. Shi Chou says: Liu Yu once used them; the wheels turned inside the boat and no one was seen outside. Zhang Sui says: In Yu Yunwen's battle at Caishi, boats were driven by pedal wheels and fired cannon.

《器用類·洋舫 Western Ships》

1
○萬歷甲申七月，紅毛艘渡閩求市，沈士弘將軍遣之去。其入長身紅髮，深目藍睛，高鼻赤足，帶劍走舟上若飛，登岸不能疾行。舟長二十餘丈，雙底，木厚二尺，外瀝青錮之。桅三接，其帆用布，一桅堅樹，其二桅以候風之恬猛為升降。中橫一桿，桅上有斗，容四十人，繫繩若梯，上下了遠。下列發貢銃，前用木照，後用柁。黑鬼善沒，為之使。此李衷一所記也。利瑪竇言舶三等：小者舟腹自上達下僅留一孔，四圍點水不漏，下填以石，風濤起時，惟操舟者縛身檣桅，一日行千里；中者容數百人；大者上下八層，桅長十四丈，有舶師、歷師、將卒、客商千人。江船用鐵毛，洋舫用木碇，多則獨鹿木，其縫以椰索貫而瀝青石腦油塗之。
○ In the seventh month of the jiashen year of Wanli, Red-hair ships crossed to Min seeking trade, and General Shen Shihong sent them away. Those people were tall, with red hair, deep-set blue eyes, high noses and bare feet, carrying swords, running on the ship as if flying, but unable to walk fast on land. The ship was over twenty zhang long, double-bottomed, with timber two chi thick, sealed outside with pitch. The masts were in three sections and the sails of cloth; one mast was fixed firmly, and the other two were raised and lowered according to the calmness or fierceness of the wind. Across the middle was a spar, and on the mast was a top holding forty men, with ropes like ladders for climbing up and down to look far. Below were ranged cannon; in front they used a wooden sight and behind a rudder. Black devils, good divers, served them. This was recorded by Li Zhongyi. Matteo Ricci said that ships are of three grades: in the small ones the belly of the ship from top to bottom leaves only one opening, watertight all round, filled with stones below, and when storms arise only the helmsman ties himself to the mast, and they sail a thousand li a day; the middle ones hold several hundred people; and the large ones have eight decks above and below, masts fourteen zhang long, and carry a thousand people — shipmasters, calendar masters, officers, soldiers and merchants. River boats use iron anchors and ocean ships wooden anchors, often of dulu wood, their seams threaded with coir rope and coated with pitch and petroleum.

《器用類·屋 Houses》

1
○四阿兩下，榮楹楣棟，必不易也；榱桷拱斗、承塵藻井，致飾耳。棟之半為簷宇。七柱則上十五。宇高一尺，則光多一丈。准車工尺，周法也。枕梁交卯，磚山省翼，挑坊挂角，四兩千斤，謂之尺經。首簽卜禨。中椽宜偶，藏初柱腦，則破之矣。喻皓有營舍法。歐陽歸田錄：開寶寺塔，喻皓所造，勢傾西北，皓曰：風吹百年當正。中通曰：北隅層勢收分少耳，風無吹改理。西方以山牆開堂門。有無柱者。江陵有一柱觀，靈谷有無梁殿。盝頂、燕窩、複合、雪洞、軒廊、假石，入者迷出，有之矣。計凌滄有園亭書：多少朱門鎖空宅，主人到老未曾歸。暄曰：瓦深則風日難下，墻厚則寒暑不侵，藉地則燥濕無涉，此居室攝生之良法也。
○ The four-slope and two-slope roofs, the eave-ends, columns, lintels and ridgepoles are indispensable; rafters, brackets, ceilings and coffered ceilings are merely decoration. Half the ridgepole [length] makes the eaves. With seven columns, the upper is fifteen. If the eaves are a chi higher, the light extends a zhang more. The cart-maker's chi is the Zhou method. Pillow beams with crossing mortises, brick gables saving wings, cantilevered tie-beams and hanging corners, four taels bearing a thousand jin — this is called the chijing. The first signing is divined for good omens. The central rafters should be even in number, and if the top of the first column is hidden, it is broken. Yu Hao had a method of building houses. Ouyang's Records of Returning to the Fields: The pagoda of the Kaibao temple, built by Yu Hao, leaned northwest, and Hao said: After a hundred years of the wind blowing it will be upright. Zhongtong says: It was only that the storeys at the north corner were tapered less; there is no principle of the wind blowing it straight. In the West the hall doors are opened in the gable walls. There are some without columns. Jiangling has the One-Column Pavilion, and Linggu the Beamless Hall. There are truncated-pyramid roofs, swallow nests, double joins, snow caves, verandas and artificial rocks, so that those who enter lose their way out. Ji Lingcang wrote of gardens and pavilions: "How many vermilion gates lock empty houses,
whose owners never came home till old age." Xuan says: With deep tiles, wind and sun hardly come down; with thick walls, cold and heat do not invade; with a raised floor, dryness and damp do not reach — these are good methods of nurturing life in a dwelling.

《器用類·犀角器 Rhinoceros-Horn Ware》

1
○黑中黃花曰正透，黃中黑花曰倒透，黃中又黃、黑中又黑曰重透。分水犀能分水。駭雞犀白縷如絲。通天犀具物形，真宗賜寇準者。犀因玩月形生角，象被雷驚紋入牙，信矣。骨咄犀，碧色，蛇角也。暹羅犀皆有粟文，角腦後有高起阜，其鼻角亦佳，有全黃者。交趾有山牛角，曰毛犀，無粟紋，有花斑。凡製犀角器，初白久黃，有以礬入鳳仙根醩黃者，或以馬蹄偽之。宇文懋昭金志言：秀州周通直家有正透犀帶，其中一點白，以紙燈近之，即時滅，有濕氣，疑是水犀。贊寧曰：此獸夜有光。通天駭雞犀置屋上，百鳥不敢集，大霧露夜終不沾濡。角為釵導，闢塵。若行蠱毒鄉，食以角攪之，無患。箭中欲死，以刺瘡中，即沫出而愈。溫嶠燃之見水怪，燃水犀也。薄片夾腋下，碾之如粉，所謂人氣粉犀也。犀杯貯茶及塗胡桃油，色易枯。中通曰：匠作有規矩盃、乳盃、花草山水，蘇作為佳，今廣亦仿蘇作。
○ Yellow flowers in black are called true-through, black flowers in yellow reverse-through, and yellow within yellow or black within black double-through. The water-parting rhinoceros can part water. The chicken-startling rhinoceros has white threads like silk. The heaven-reaching rhinoceros has the shapes of things, like the one Zhenzong gave Kou Zhun. That the rhinoceros grows its horn from gazing at the moon, and the elephant's tusk gets its pattern from being startled by thunder, is true. The guduo rhinoceros is blue-green and is snake horn. Siamese rhinoceroses all have millet-grain patterns, with a raised mound behind the base of the horn, and their nose horns are also good, some entirely yellow. Jiaozhi has mountain-ox horn, called hairy rhinoceros, without millet patterns but with flowered spots. Rhinoceros-horn ware is white at first and yellow in time; some yellow it by steeping in alum with impatiens root, or counterfeit it with horse hoof. Yuwen Maozhao's Record of the Jin says: The family of Zhou Tongzhi of Xiuzhou had a true-through rhinoceros belt with one white spot in the middle, which vanished at once when a paper lantern was brought near and had a damp qi; it was suspected to be water rhinoceros. Zanning says: This beast glows at night. If the heaven-reaching chicken-startling rhinoceros horn is placed on a roof, no birds dare alight, and on nights of heavy fog and dew it is never wet. The horn made into hairpins wards off dust. If one travels in a region of gu poison, stirring food with the horn prevents harm. When someone hit by an arrow is about to die, if it is pricked into the wound, foam comes out and he recovers. Wen Jiao burned it and saw water monsters; it was water rhinoceros he burned. Thin slices held under the arm can be ground like powder; this is what is called human-qi powdered rhinoceros. If rhinoceros cups hold tea or are smeared with walnut oil, the colour easily withers. Zhongtong says: Craftsmen make regular cups, nipple cups and flower, grass and landscape cups; Su work is best, and now Guang too imitates Su work.

《器用類·象牙器 Ivory Ware》

1
○粉紅，有粟紋。象之板齒有花點。其曰殊角者，海象牙也。紅豬牙如蚌，棗色如象牙紋。魚牙如象齒。周禮榆貫象牙，能制水怪。老象有六牙，常自脫而埋之。煮象牙，用醋鹵煮之，自軟。一云：木賊軟牙。牙黃者，浸木賊水，復白；曲者直。又掘芭蕉中白，或用豆腐滓浸擦。暄曰：量刻痕，但以醋浸一七，亦軟。
○ It is pink with millet-grain patterns. The molars of the elephant have flower spots. What is called shujiao is walrus ivory. Red pig tusks are like clam, jujube-coloured like ivory patterns. Fish teeth are like elephant tusks. The Rites of Zhou's elm and ivory can control water monsters. Old elephants have six tusks, which they often shed and bury. To boil ivory, boil it in vinegar and brine and it softens by itself. One says: Horsetail softens ivory. Yellowed ivory soaked in horsetail water becomes white again, and crooked pieces straight. Also, dig into a banana plant and whiten it in the middle, or soak and rub it with bean-curd dregs. Xuan says: Having measured the carving lines, just soak it in vinegar for seven days and it also softens.

《器用類·海甲類 Shells of the Sea》

1
○玳瑁六葉，其厚而黃多、有物形者貴。其移斑火板，以石灰、鉛粉、鹼水點於牛角者，偽也。劉郁曰：撒八兒為玳瑁之遺精，蛟魚食之，吐出，年深結成，其價如金。龜同似玳瑁，有黃甲，無黑斑。鶴頂乃海中鶴魚之首，摩印其兩頰之紅于其頂骨也。鸚鵡盃乃螺也。魚枕則火鉗魚骨也。海魚鱗厚大者，亦可為飾。其曰海膽者，外狀痱磊，巨細不一，大及雞卵，亦可鑲為盃瓶之屬。海杖者，長數尺，大魚之須也。交趾蝦鬚有長七八尺可為柱杖者。奇方：搗驢骨爛，實于柔鐵圍之，同地榆、五加皮、急性子煮之，為器，紋如象牙。海槎錄曰：儋州紅蟹，殼形有十二點，堪作楪子。北尸曰：金龜甲蟲可以養粉。廣州市上甲丁蟲以為首飾。工人脫蝐筒等，倒懸潑醋即開。
○ Tortoiseshell comes in six plates; that thick and mostly yellow, with shapes of things, is valued. That with transferred spots and fire boards, made by dotting ox horn with lime, lead white and lye, is counterfeit. Liu Yu says: Sabar is the left-over essence of the hawksbill; sharks eat it and spit it out, and over the years it forms; its price is like gold. Guitong resembles tortoiseshell, with yellow shell and no black spots. Crane's crest is the head of the crane-fish of the sea, the red of its two cheeks impressed by rubbing onto its skull. Parrot cups are conches. Fish pillows are the bones of the tongs fish. The thick, large scales of sea fish can also be made into ornaments. What is called the sea urchin is warty outside, of varying size, as large as a hen's egg, and can also be mounted as cups and vases. Sea staffs, several chi long, are the whiskers of great fish. The shrimp whiskers of Jiaozhi are sometimes seven or eight chi long and can be made into walking sticks. A strange recipe: pound donkey bones to a pulp, pack them into a ring of soft iron, and boil them with burnet, acanthopanax and impatiens seed, and when made into objects the pattern is like ivory. The Records from the Sea Raft say: The red crabs of Danzhou have shells with twelve spots, fit for making small dishes. Beishi says: The golden-tortoise beetle can be kept in powder. In the markets of Guangzhou, jiading beetles are made into hair ornaments. When craftsmen take off [shells] of yi tubes and the like, they hang them upside down and pour on vinegar, and they open at once.

《器用類·火爆 Fireworks》

1
○火藥自外夷來。宋開寶二年，岳義方上火箭。張和仲記虞允文采石舟中發霹靂炮，乃紙為之，實以石灰、硫黃，墜水而火自水跳出。永樂立神機營，西塢以尺測量，精矣。唐有火樹銀花，想已用之耶？硝入杉灰則直發，硫則橫爆，加黃礬則硑烈，箬瓢灰則悄聲，碾西匡石粉則發時不先光。試之，堆相間丈而點一及諸堆者，萬杵者也。掌上然之，毫無所傷，以其疾也。入鐵蛾、樟腦則成花，今名煙火。禦銃者，濕絮、魚網、土囊，柔能制剛也。岳珂桯史曰：汴城舊多曲折，蔡京方之，黏罕、乾離不視城而笑，植炮四隅，隨方擊之，城既引直，一炮所望，皆不可立矣。魏子一曰：敵臺宜築三角附城，如菱芰，兩腋皆有小門可出，而外炮不能攻也。城址砌石，上即以土築之，炮子入土便陷不出。暄曰：末乾箬可代硫。
○ Gunpowder came from the foreign barbarians. In the second year of Kaibao in the Song, Yue Yifang presented fire arrows. Zhang Hezhong records that Yu Yunwen in the battle of Caishi fired thunderbolt bombs from his boats, made of paper filled with lime and sulphur, which fell into the water and the fire leapt out of the water. In the Yongle period the Divine Engine Battalion was established, and the Western dock measured with rulers — very precise. The Tang had "fiery trees and silver flowers" — I suppose they already used it? Saltpetre with fir ash goes off straight, with sulphur it bursts sideways, with yellow alum it explodes fiercely, with bamboo-sheath and gourd ash it is quiet, and with ground xikuang stone powder it gives no flash before going off. Test it: piles a zhang apart, when one is lit, and it reaches all the piles — it is pounded ten thousand times. Lit in the palm it does no harm at all, because it is so fast. With iron moths and camphor added it makes flowers, now called fireworks. Defences against guns are wet wadding, fishing nets and earth bags, the soft overcoming the hard. Yue Ke's Notes from Ting says: The walls of Bian were formerly very winding, and Cai Jing squared them; Nianhan and Ganlibu looked at the walls and laughed, set up catapults at the four corners and struck in each direction, and since the walls had been straightened, nothing within the view of one catapult could stand. Wei Ziyi says: Bastions should be built triangular, attached to the wall like water caltrops, with small doors in both flanks from which men can go out, while guns outside cannot attack them. The foundation of the wall is laid with stone, and above that built with earth, so that shot entering the earth sinks and does not come out. Xuan says: Powdered dry bamboo sheath can substitute for sulphur.

《器用類·剞劂法 Methods of Engraving》

1
○以旌德拳刀手為上，畫楷行草，方能生肖。金陵、江、廣皆雀刀，閩用挑刀。梨棗、白棗以油塗之，黏槁後洒石灰，潤水去瓤，點畫分明，易于下刀。刀貴閩淬，以得洋鐵也。中通曰：雖梨板亦止印二萬篇，則胡塗矣，故須煮板，能久，但下刀難耳。暄曰：用石灰鹽煮，易刻，耐久，且不生蟲。
○ The fist-knife carvers of Jingde are the best, and only they can make regular, running and cursive script lifelike. Jinling, Jiang and Guang all use sparrow knives, and Min uses pick knives. Pear and jujube or white jujube wood is smeared with oil, and after the copy is pasted down, lime is sprinkled on, it is moistened with water and the pulp removed, so that the strokes are distinct and easy to cut. Knives quenched in Min are valued, because they get foreign iron. Zhongtong says: Even pearwood blocks can print only twenty thousand impressions before they blur, so the blocks must be boiled to last, but then they are hard to cut. Xuan says: Boiled with lime and salt, they are easy to carve, durable and free of insects.

1

○ The drum recently presented as tribute from Luzon has its face covered with rattan, and when struck the sound trembles endlessly. A square-faced drum has also been seen, used in hunting. In antiquity drums made from the hide of an ox killed by lightning could overawe distant [foes] and ward off evil. Zhongtong says: Yang Ruosheng saw it himself.

1
○犍鐘者，先醒鐘，輕椎得聲，然後重椎，則聲發而鐘不釁。鑄鐘頂留孔，其多孔者鐘聲亦發。磬則氣上出，鐵磬亦留三眼。諺云：磬畏茈茹。
○ Those who strike bells first awaken the bell, getting a sound with a light stroke, and then strike heavily, so that the sound comes out and the bell does not crack. When a bell is cast a hole is left in the top, and bells with many holes also sound out. In chime stones the qi goes out upward, so iron chimes also leave three holes. The proverb says: Chime stones fear zi ru.

《器用類·金鋼鑽 Diamonds》

1
○金鋼鑽鈍，置瓦上燒赤，復銳。小者黑，大者白。抱朴子曰：生水底，如鐘乳，體似紫石英。惟安南高石山一角羚羊能碎金剛石。東壁曰：白貘骨能碎之，而羚羊角亦能碎貘骨。周密云如鼠屎，青黑色，鷹隼黏食，遺糞而人取之者，訛也。獨孤滔曰：紫背鉛能碎金剛鑽。虛舟曰：細島砂能磨之，使之長方成鋒，即金剛切玉刀矣。有極大者，瑩白放光，香山隩首勒嵌之，以此競貴。十洲記：西海流砂治昆吾作劍，則鐵光明如水晶。貘熊舐鐵，糞可鑄劍，駝、火鳥糞亦然，故周密說不明耳。凡寶石真者必堅，皆能刻磁，好水晶之棱亦能刻磁。
○ When a diamond drill is blunt, heat it red on a tile and it is sharp again. The small ones are black, the large ones white. Baopuzi says: It grows under water like stalactites, its body resembling amethyst. Only the one-horned antelope of the Gaoshi mountains in Annam can break diamonds. Dongbi says: White tapir bone can break it, and antelope horn can also break tapir bone. Zhou Mi's saying that it is like rat droppings, blue-black, eaten with sticky food by hawks and falcons, and gathered by people from their droppings, is mistaken. Dugu Tao says: Purple-backed lead can break diamond drills. Xuzhou says: Fine island sand can grind it, making it long and square to form an edge, which is the diamond jade-cutting knife. There are extremely large ones, lustrous white and radiating light, which the chiefs at Xiangshan'ao set in [rings], vying with one another in their value. The Record of the Ten Isles: If the flowing sand of the Western Sea is used to work Kunwu [iron] into swords, the iron is as bright as rock crystal. The tapir-bear licks iron, and its dung can be cast into swords, and so can the dung of the camel-fire bird; so Zhou Mi's account is unclear. All true gems are certainly hard and can engrave porcelain; the edges of good rock crystal can also engrave porcelain.

《器用類·淬刀法 Methods of Quenching Knives》

1
○山間水出而殷者曰繡水，淬刀刻玉，地溲也。一曰：虎骨、朴硝、醬，刀成之後，水赤而屢淬之。一以醬同硝塗鏨口，煆赤淬水。一以羊角、乳髮為末，調傅刀口。不必蟾酥塗而自然灰埋也。舊有詩曰：醬能雕白玉，鹽可濯黃金。夷門廣牘曰：地榆一兩，蔥、蒜汁各一碗，同煮二時，便可容刀。刀生鉒，以木賊草去之，或用炭磨。若新硎塗鸊鵜膏，則不生鉒。中通曰：服玉者以苦米地榆酒消之為水，苦酒者，醋也，是醬醋可刻玉。暄曰：塗藥煆刀，必豎鉗，否則偏。
○ Water that comes out in the mountains dark red is called embroidery water; knives quenched in it carve jade; it is earth-urine. One says: Tiger bone, mirabilite and soy paste — after the knife is made, when the water is red quench it repeatedly. One smears the edge of the chisel with soy paste and saltpetre, heats it red and quenches it in water. One powders sheep's horn and hair, mixes them and applies them to the knife edge. There is no need to smear toad venom, and it is naturally buried in ash. An old poem says: "Soy paste can carve white jade,
salt can wash yellow gold." The Yimen Guangdu says: A tael of burnet, boiled with a bowl each of scallion and garlic juice for two double-hours, can then admit the knife. When a knife rusts, remove it with horsetail, or polish it with charcoal. If a newly whetted [knife] is smeared with grebe fat, it does not rust. Zhongtong says: Those who take jade dissolve it into water with bitter rice, burnet and wine; bitter wine is vinegar, so soy paste and vinegar can carve jade. Xuan says: In forging a knife coated with drugs, the tongs must hold it upright, otherwise it goes askew.

1
○粉包之，或燒石灰。虛舟曰：胡桃燒灰，可藏針。或曰：石灰收針不滑，宜以油抹，用頭髮收。
○ Wrap them in powder, or burnt lime. Xuzhou says: Needles can be stored in burnt walnut ash. Some say: Needles kept in lime are not slippery; they should be wiped with oil and kept with hair.

《器用類·料絲 Glass Threads》

1
○滇金齒衛用瑪瑙、石英屑汁，以北方天花點之，乃凝，練為絲，以作燈。李西涯書作繚絲。大內青瑣即此物。亦能假琅玕珠，謂之瑟瑟。閩中乃以鉛煉。永昌燒圍棋子亦以鉛。料絲磨漿，能止血破血，猶之白定瓷末之能止鼻衄、塗瘡瘢也。近丹陽、松江能作料絲。
○ At the Jinchi garrison in Dian they use the juice of agate and quartz chips, set with northern tianhua, and it congeals, and is refined into threads to make lamps. Li Xiya writes it as liaosi. The blue lattices of the palace are this material. It can also imitate langgan beads, which are called sese. In Min it is refined with lead. The weiqi stones fired at Yongchang also use lead. Glass-thread ground into slurry can stop bleeding and break up blood, just as powdered white Ding porcelain can stop nosebleeds and be smeared on scars. Lately Danyang and Songjiang have been able to make glass threads.

《器用類·沙壺發光法 Method of Making Sand Teapots Lustrous》

1
○先掘土坑，下置沙盆，乃以細茶多用沸泡壺中，置沙盆上，復以沙盆蓋之，上覆以土，七日起之，如此者三，壺放光如鏡矣。
○ First dig a pit in the earth and put a sand basin at the bottom; then steep plenty of fine tea in boiling water in the teapot, set it on the sand basin, cover it with another sand basin and cover that with earth; take it out after seven days, and after doing this three times the teapot shines like a mirror.

《器用類·忍蠟炷 Long-Burning Wax Candles》

1
○黃蠟、松脂、槐花各一斤，浮石末四兩，一處鎔之，用燈心布澆，一日夜僅點一寸，此王西岩方。一曰：槐角倍白膠香，硫四之一，鎔化一處，以小竹筒置一燭心，將藥灌之，候冷去竹。中通曰：以綿紗繩澆蠟，則屈曲收之，隨用。又聖燈法：浮萍、瓦松、遠志、黃丹、蛤粉等末，每油一兩入藥一錢，點燈可照一月之久。口外人以樺皮卷蠟作燭，與山民燒松節竹片何異？
○ A jin each of yellow wax, rosin and pagoda-tree flowers and four taels of powdered pumice are melted together and poured over rush-pith cloth; it burns only an inch in a day and night; this is Wang Xiyan's prescription. One says: Twice as much white-glue incense as pagoda-tree pods and a quarter of sulphur, melted together; put a wick in a small bamboo tube, pour the mixture in, and remove the bamboo when cool. Zhongtong says: If wax is poured over cotton-yarn cord, it can be coiled up and used as needed. Also, the method of the holy lamp: powder duckweed, orostachys, polygala, red lead, clam-shell powder and so on, add a qian of the drug to each tael of oil, and the lamp will burn for a month. People beyond the passes roll wax in birch bark to make candles — how is that different from mountain folk burning pine knots and bamboo strips?

《器用類·燭 Candles》

1
○玉山桕油加蠟最堅，他處桕油蓋蠟，過冬則暗矣。木天蓼子亦可為燭。眉公曰：蜀葵點作火把，雨中不滅。川井油點火不畏雨，何必洞冥之豹髓、錫屑、蘇油乎？羊脂燭無煙，明角燈宜之。蜃脂為燭，煙成樓台。內方曰：乾漆、海金沙、火硝、硫各一兩，黑豆青、黃蠟各二兩，鎔化，以布燈心作條捻之，風前不滅。一曰：止用蜀葵秸，風中不滅。
○ The tallow-tree oil of Yushan with wax added is the hardest; tallow-tree oil from elsewhere covered with wax grows dim after the winter. The seeds of silvervine can also be made into candles. Meigong says: Hollyhock lit as a torch does not go out in the rain. Sichuan well oil when lit does not fear rain, so why need one the leopard marrow, tin filings and perilla oil of the Dongming [Record]? Mutton-tallow candles are smokeless and suit horn lanterns. Candles made of clam fat have smoke that forms towers. The Inner Prescriptions say: A tael each of dried lacquer, lygodium spores, saltpetre and sulphur and two taels each of black-bean green and yellow wax, melted, twisted into sticks with cloth wicks, do not go out before the wind. One says: Using only hollyhock stalks, they do not go out in the wind.

《器用類·止燭瀉法 Methods of Stopping Candles from Guttering》

1
○郎瑛曰：燭心散，以線縛之。愚者以鹽補，則瀉止，或以箸倚之。兆兗曰：燭用臘水浸過，雖三伏日不瀉。
○ Lang Ying says: When the wick spreads, tie it with thread. The Foolish One mends it with salt, and the guttering stops, or leans a chopstick against it. Zhaoyan says: Candles soaked in twelfth-month water do not gutter even in the dog days.

《器用類·補漏 Mending Leaks》

1
○缸裂縫，先箍定，置烈日中，加炭燒炙其縫，乘熱以好青塗弭，則不漏，近亦用鹽生補。又鹽可擦碗。酒瓶漏者，羊血擦之。油籠、漆籠漏者，以馬屁勃塞之，即止。劉伯溫多能鄙事云：油籠滲漏，剝穿山甲里面肉靨投入，自至漏處補住。又云：木香塞醋桶漏，醋調粉成泥塞鮓桶漏。又云：陶鋼雖厚，炙以艾，鑽之即徹。中履曰：磁瓦屑合芨、榆補漏。椎生黃豆與瓦屑，可補鍋。
○ For a cracked vat, first bind it with hoops, put it in the hot sun, heat the crack with charcoal, and while hot seal it with good qing, and it will not leak; lately raw salt has also been used to mend it. Salt can also be rubbed on bowls. Leaking wine bottles are rubbed with sheep's blood. Leaking oil baskets and lacquer baskets are plugged with puffball, and stop at once. Liu Bowen's Many Humble Skills says: When an oil basket seeps, peel off the dimpled flesh from inside a pangolin and throw it in, and it will go by itself to the leak and seal it. It also says: Costus root plugs leaking vinegar tubs, and powder mixed with vinegar into mud plugs leaking pickled-fish tubs. It also says: However thick a pottery vat, if heated with mugwort it can be drilled right through. Zhonglü says: Porcelain and tile fragments combined with bletilla and elm mend leaks. Pounded raw soybeans with tile fragments can mend pots.

《器用類·香爐灰 Incense-Burner Ash》

1
○米湯和紙灰，曬乾，煆紅，研之，三煆曰滿爐金。或一斗加礦灰二升煆之。尋常燒柚皮、千張灰足矣。冷爐以草紙、茄秸炭埋之，火亦自活。欲留宿火者，胡桃燒半紅，煖灰埋，三五日不燼。中履曰：茶油餅滓入爐，常燃。水經注：盧水聖子本炭不滅。
○ Mix paper ash with rice water, dry it in the sun, heat it red and grind it; heated three times it is called full-burner gold. Or to a dou add two sheng of quicklime and heat it. Ordinarily, ash of burnt pomelo peel and bean-curd sheets is enough. A cold burner, buried with charcoal of straw paper and eggplant stalks, comes alive again by itself. To keep fire overnight, burn a walnut half red and bury it in warm ash, and it will not burn out for three to five days. Zhonglü says: Tea-oil cake dregs put in a burner keep burning constantly. The Commentary on the Water Classic: The shengzi charcoal of the Lu River does not go out.

《器用類·香炭餅 Incense Charcoal Cakes》

1
○炭末一斤，針沙四兩磨針之鐵沙，搗蜀葵花、棗肉為餅，乾之。歐陽公言蔡君謨欲清泉香餅，清泉地出石炭，為香餅，終日不滅。其作腳爐炭餅，采蜀葵葉，搗烰炭、煤炭末，以化開石灰之濃汁和之，鐵圈捶實，暴乾。中通曰：江南細枝炭曰煤炭，非石炭也。蔚州石炭終日不滅。
○ A jin of powdered charcoal and four taels of needle sand the iron filings from grinding needles, pounded with hollyhock flowers and jujube flesh into cakes, and dried. Lord Ouyang said that Cai Junmo wanted Qingquan incense cakes; Qingquan produces mineral coal, which made into incense cakes burns all day without going out. To make charcoal cakes for foot warmers, gather hollyhock leaves, pound them with powdered light charcoal and coal, mix with the thick liquid of slaked lime, pound firm in iron rings and dry in the sun. Zhongtong says: In Jiangnan, fine twig charcoal is called meitan; it is not mineral coal. The mineral coal of Yuzhou burns all day without going out.

《器用類·獸炭 Animal-Shaped Charcoal》

1
○炭與鐵矢等分合搗，入芙蓉葉十之三，再搗，和糯麋，範獸形，乾之，鞁紅入爐，信宿不滅。
○ Pound equal parts of charcoal and iron dross, add three-tenths of hibiscus leaves, pound again, mix with glutinous gruel, mould into animal shapes and dry them; blown red with bellows and put in the burner, they last two nights without going out.

《器用類·燒炭忌糒 Charcoal Burning Avoids Bei Wood》

1
○五糒柴燒火無焰，成炭之後忽然自燒，故炭窯忌之。榕亦無焰，然其枝曝為點火繩。八月割茅燒之有聲，汁力也；九月割則枯矣。巴菽瀉松脂，則松柴無焰。中德曰：棕桄燒之澀鬱不著。櫟炭以畫木而燒之，則有墨處著，無墨處不著。凡木不著者，加硝黃。暄曰：燒炭而隱以沸水沃之。
○ Wubei firewood burns without flame, and after it has become charcoal suddenly burns of itself, so charcoal kilns avoid it. The banyan too has no flame, yet its branches are dried to make fuse ropes. Thatch cut in the eighth month crackles when burnt, because of the strength of its sap; cut in the ninth month it is withered. If croton drains away the pine resin, pine firewood has no flame. Zhongde says: Palm and sugar palm burn sluggishly and do not catch. If oak charcoal is used to draw on wood which is then burnt, the parts with ink catch and those without do not. For any wood that will not catch, add saltpetre and sulphur. Xuan says: In burning charcoal, it is secretly doused with boiling water.

Bamboo utensils, sunned and heated over fire to bring out the dirt and then wiped, become glossy.

○ Wash the clay, grind bricks fine, and combine them with zou sand to make vessels, and they are not damaged by fire. Large furnaces in the mountains should be made thus, and finished with golden-grain fire. Oyster and clam shells are extremely hard; in Hainan walls are built of them, and powdered and pounded into sandy earth they are harder still.

《器用類·去斑 Removing Marks》

1

So those who treat stains expose green cloth to the dew at night and wear it next to the body in the morning, and the stains fade.

《器用類·辨古銅器法 Methods of Judging Ancient Bronzes》

1
○銅入土千年青，入水綠千年，瑩如玉，午後翠欲滴，蝕處如蝸篆，或蝕薄，或鋤破，並不見銅色，翠綠徹骨，或有一線丹，然尚有銅聲。傳世古則紫褐而朱斑，沸湯煮之，斑愈明。偽者漆調朱也，或塗汞錫，加醋調硇，蘸而浸水變色者，熱手久摩，銅腥觸鼻矣。井水調泥礬，浸一周時，再烘熱銅器上，三度作腳色，候干，卻將硇砂浸化，淨筆蘸上，刷三四度，候日餘洗去，遇幹即洗，全在調停水洗候色，若入地窖藏之，即生朱砂斑，欲其色沈，以竹葉煙熏之，泥礬兩度，俟半日許溶，又頻浴之，即為茶褐色。古銅聲微而清，新銅濁而哄。或割口焊之，其聲變者。至鼎、彞、樽、觚、卮、觶，商金銀等款識，博古圖詳矣。博山下象海水，收香也。古綠匜洗可養目。秦鏡厚而背光，照之神清辟邪，多水銀。古海燕、蒲萄等，則漢鏡也。香爐畜火，莫如宣銅。宣廟命工煉十二次，溶下鋼格，謂內殿災金銀銅像渾者，非也。乳爐最宜，魚戟、蜒耳、象鼻、獸面皆佳，竹節、翻環、索耳、分襠下也。有鑄耳者，有鋈金者，有雞皮者。初仿宋燒斑，或蠟茶，末年淡者乃妙，望之黯然，迫視如肉，內色蘊火，其中彩爛善變。偽者銅氣自新，光不從內發也。嘉靖初有學道鑄，後有施家鑄，惟以銅重為價。蘇有蔡鑄，南京有甘鑄，先肯煉銅，略變其色，日夜蓄火十年，則亦可觀矣。陳旻昭言周鳴仲、王越石、孫淮橋以一見文王鼎分而為四，而主人亦不知其真贗也，不可不知。虛舟子曰：器不過適用而已，求之甚精，而顏以好古之目，亦為隱賑者化其俗耳。噫！天道好還，且令富貴之子以此揮霍，何非暗體天道之一端耶？盧坦之對杜黃裳是也。觚不觚錄曰：畫當重宋，而三十年來忽重元；窯器忽重宣、成，價增十倍，大抵吳人濫觴。今陸子剛之治玉，鮑天成之治犀，朱碧山之治銀，趙良璧之治錫，馬勳之治扇，呂愛山之治金，石小溪之治瑪瑙，蔣抱雲之治銅，皆比常價再倍。嗟乎！大臣無所嗜好，價自當平。焦澹園曰：道無形而器有象，如犧尊之重遲，蜼敦之智辨，黃目之清明，山罍之鎮靜，壺尊、著尊之質樸，使人指掌而意悟，目擊而道存，皆有不言之教焉。故曰：古之君子不必親相與言也，以禮樂示之而已。至若柱爵懲濫，饕餮戒貪，山觚防湎，觸事著警，凡以成德而礪行，抑其次也。原父謂禮家之制度、小學之文字、譜牒之謚系，靡不有資，猶為末務。乃上不能契道，下不以飭德，而持為耳目之玩，則宣和君臣之失也，適足為戒而已。
○ Bronze in the earth for a thousand years turns blue, and in water green; after a thousand years it is lustrous like jade, so kingfisher-green in the afternoon that it seems to drip; corroded places are like snail-trail seal script, some thinly corroded, some broken by hoes, with no copper colour showing at all, green right to the bone, or with a line of red, yet it still has the ring of bronze. Antiques handed down above ground are purple-brown with red spots, and boiled in water the spots become all the clearer. Counterfeits are lacquer mixed with vermilion, or coated with mercury and tin, mixed with vinegar and sal ammoniac, dipped and soaked to change colour; if rubbed long with a hot hand, the smell of copper strikes the nose. Mix mud and alum with well water, soak for a full day, then heat the bronze and apply it three times to make the foot colour; when dry, dissolve sal ammoniac, dip a clean brush in it and brush three or four times; after more than a day wash it off, washing it whenever it dries; everything depends on adjusting the washing and watching the colour; if stored in a cellar it grows cinnabar spots; to deepen the colour, smoke it with bamboo-leaf smoke, apply mud and alum twice, wait about half a day for it to dissolve and bathe it frequently, and it becomes tea-brown. Ancient bronze sounds faint and clear; new bronze muddy and booming. Some cut and solder the mouth, which changes the sound. As for the inscriptions on ding, yi, zun, gu, zhi and zhi vessels and the gold and silver inlays, the Illustrated Catalogue of Antiquities is detailed. The base of the boshan [censer] represents sea water, to collect the incense. Ancient green yi basins and washers can nourish the eyes. Qin mirrors are thick with glossy backs; looking into them clears the spirit and wards off evil; they contain much mercury. Those with ancient sea-swallow and grape [patterns] are Han mirrors. For incense burners holding fire, nothing equals Xuande bronze. The Xuande emperor ordered craftsmen to refine it twelve times, melting down the steel grids; the saying that it was the gold, silver and bronze images of a burnt palace hall mixed together is wrong. Nipple censers are best, and fish-halberd, snail-ear, elephant-trunk and beast-face are all good; bamboo-joint, turned-ring, rope-ear and divided-crotch are lower. There are cast ears, gilt ones and chicken-skin ones. At first they imitated Song fired spots or wax-tea colour; the pale ones of the last years are wonderful, dark when seen from afar and like flesh close up, the colour within holding fire, with brilliant colours that change well. In counterfeits the bronze looks naturally new, and the lustre does not come from within. At the beginning of Jiajing there were the Xuedao castings, later the Shi family castings, priced only by the weight of the bronze. Su had Cai castings and Nanjing Gan castings; they first took care to refine the bronze and slightly changed its colour, and if fire is kept in them day and night for ten years, they too become worth seeing. Chen Minzhao says that Zhou Mingzhong, Wang Yueshi and Sun Huaiqiao divided a King Wen tripod they saw into four, and the owner did not know which was genuine and which false — one must know this. Xuzhouzi says: Vessels need only be fit for use; seeking them with great refinement and labelling it "love of antiquity" also transforms the customs of those who hide wealth. Ah! Heaven's way loves to return, and if it makes the sons of the rich squander their wealth on these things, is this not one way in which the way of Heaven is secretly embodied? Lu Tan's answer to Du Huangshang is the case. The Records of the Gu That Is Not a Gu says: In painting the Song ought to be valued, but in the last thirty years Yuan has suddenly come to be valued; in porcelain Xuande and Chenghua have suddenly come to be valued, the price increasing tenfold; in general the people of Wu started it. Now Lu Zigang's jade, Bao Tiancheng's rhinoceros horn, Zhu Bishan's silver, Zhao Liangbi's tin, Ma Xun's fans, Lü Aishan's gold, Shi Xiaoxi's agate and Jiang Baoyun's bronze all cost twice the usual price. Alas! If great ministers had no such tastes, prices would naturally be level. Jiao Danyuan says: The Way has no form but vessels have images, such as the weightiness of the xi zun, the wisdom of the wei dui, the clarity of the yellow eyes, the steadiness of the shan lei, and the plainness of the hu zun and zhu zun, which make people understand as if pointing at the palm, and see the Way preserved at a glance; all have an unspoken teaching. Hence it is said: The gentlemen of antiquity did not need to speak to each other in person; they simply showed it by rites and music. As for the post-cup punishing excess, the taotie warning against greed, the mountain gu guarding against drunkenness — giving warnings in every matter, all to perfect virtue and whet conduct — that is secondary. Yuanfu says that the institutions of the ritualists, the characters of the philologists and the posthumous names and lineages of the genealogists all draw on them, but these are still minor matters. But if one cannot accord with the Way above, nor use them to refine virtue below, and holds them as playthings for eyes and ears, that was the fault of the ruler and ministers of the Xuanhe period, and serves only as a warning.

《器用類·用木法 Methods of Using Wood》

1
○凡伐木宜四月、七月，則不蟲而堅韌。榆莢下、桑椹落，亦其時也。至于木之有子實者，候其子實將熟，皆其時也。凡非時之木，水漚一月，或火煏取乾，蟲則不生。伐木以桐油灌之，即乾。買大松者貴其色頳，則鑿腦以蘇木、胡椒灌之，通身皆紅。
○ Trees should be felled in the fourth and seventh months, and then the wood does not get insects and is firm and tough. When the elm seeds come down and the mulberries fall is also the time. For trees that bear seeds or fruit, the time is when the seeds or fruit are about to ripen. Wood cut out of season, soaked in water for a month or dried over fire, will not breed insects. If felled timber is soaked with tung oil, it dries at once. Buyers of large pines value a red colour, so the top is chiselled and filled with sappanwood and pepper, and the whole trunk turns red.

《器用類·起重法 Methods of Lifting Weights》

1
○以剛鐵作蠡絲旋，旋入耎鐵方基中，既成，二物牝牡相合，左旋則入，右旋則出，乃以承重物，先左旋則縮之，後右旋而伸之，其漸長處寔之以楔，如此屢加，則起矣。凡引重用一轆轤，省力一倍；以筒筒圓木，入滑汁其中，以繩卷筒上，其力更省。宋治平中，河中府浮梁漲絕，鐵牛投河，募出之者，僧懷丙以二大舟實土，為木衡以鉤牛，乃除其土，舟浮牛出。轉運張燾以聞，賜之紫衣。暄曰：稱象者曾仿此法。
○ Make a spiral screw of hard iron that turns into a square base of soft iron; when made, the two fit together as male and female, entering when turned left and coming out when turned right; then to bear a heavy object, first turn it left to contract it and then turn it right to extend it, and fill the gradually lengthening part with wedges; adding thus repeatedly, the weight is lifted. For hauling weights, one windlass halves the effort; put a round log in a tube with lubricating liquid inside and wind the rope on the tube, and the effort is even less. In the Zhiping period of the Song, the floating bridge of Hezhong prefecture was cut by a flood and its iron oxen fell into the river; when a call went out for someone to retrieve them, the monk Huaibing filled two large boats with earth, made a wooden beam to hook the oxen, then removed the earth, and as the boats rose the oxen came out. The Transport Commissioner Zhang Tao reported it, and he was granted a purple robe. Xuan says: The weighing of the elephant once imitated this method.

《器用類·稱桿輪運 Steelyard Levers and Wheel Transport》

1
○凡運重石，先立稱桿，以木作架，架立衡輪，下復以輪轉之，隨所置而審之，其省力多。今之樹牌坊、造橋皆如是也。智癸亥隨老父在都，見運皇極殿磉，高闊以丈，特築黃土於御道，以眾繩牽之，不用下輪者，內司利于糜費也。
○ To move heavy stones, first set up a steelyard lever with a wooden frame, on which stands a balance wheel, with wheels turning below, adjusted according to where it is placed, which saves much effort. Today's erection of memorial arches and building of bridges are all done thus. In the guihai year I, Zhi, was with my father in the capital and saw the plinths of the Huangji Hall being moved, a zhang high and wide; yellow earth was specially laid on the imperial road and they were pulled with many ropes, without wheels underneath, because the inner office profited from the waste.

《器用類·轉水法 Methods of Raising Water》

1
○其來處何高，則所激之高可與之比。或故使之瀑下，就以筒承瀑，則水激而上出，上既出則流通而不止矣，如今過山龍之出酒也。然水器宜精密，勿使洩氣，初引以氣吸之，器大則以火生氣而抽發之。昔漢靈帝作番車渴兔，註云：渴兔為曲筒，以氣引吸上也。則漢時中國已知此法矣。池塘中用筒激水，亦可高數丈而出之，或下用輪激，此須制具，如東坡所云蜀井用竹筒抽汲之法，凡井皆可為也。灌田急宜講此。水碓礳，南方類為之，漢杜詩、魏韓暨之水排是也。用風帆六幅車水灌田者，淮揚海堧皆為之。暄曰：遠國用轉龍逆流水，以為已下也，而不知其已上也，與抽吸過關、濺沫成卉諸法，皆稱奇絕。
○ However high its source, the height to which it can be forced can match it. Or one deliberately makes it cascade down and catches the cascade with a tube, and the water is forced up and out, and once out it flows continuously without stopping, as the "dragon over the mountain" now draws wine. But water vessels must be precise and tight so as not to leak air; at first it is drawn by sucking with air, and if the vessel is large, fire is used to generate air and draw it up. Of old Emperor Ling of the Han made the fanche and kepi, and the commentary says: The kepi is a curved tube that draws up by sucking air. So in the Han China already knew this method. In ponds water can be forced with tubes to come out several zhang high, or forced with wheels below; this requires equipment, like the method of drawing water with bamboo tubes in the wells of Shu described by Dongpo, which can be done with any well. For irrigating fields this should be urgently studied. Water-powered pestles and mills are made everywhere in the south; the water bellows of Du Shi of the Han and Han Ji of the Wei are these. Using six sails to work water wheels for irrigating fields is done all along the sea coast of Huai and Yang. Xuan says: Distant countries use the turning dragon to make water flow backward, so that one thinks it has gone down without knowing it has gone up; this and the methods of suction through passes and spray forming plants are all called marvels.

《器用類·運機 Self-Moving Machines》

1
○不因流泉，不因風轉，而自運者，有機焉。縣甬而開小流，則漸輕而上，其機亦轉。有積沙以壓之而漏之者。此外則因乎始動而發，華陽國志一士獻木牛流馬于諸葛者是也。鍇鑰卷轉，七輪交催，約處十二齒，大者同天度。自張衡、一行、祖沖之，皆能作平儀，運機自動。蘇鶚載新羅獻萬佛山，木人稽首，其機在鐘。楓窗載太平興國中，張思訓上渾儀，七人直七政，十二神時，皆以機發。魏樸、馬鈞俱通此，亦人自悟耳。孫孺理寸自鳴鐘，乃訝之哉龍溪孫大娘稱奇工。
○ That which moves of itself, not by a flowing spring nor turned by wind, has a mechanism. Hang a vessel and open a small flow, and as it gradually lightens and rises, the mechanism also turns. There are those that press with accumulated sand and let it leak. Besides these, there are those set off by an initial movement, such as the wooden oxen and gliding horses presented to Zhuge [Liang] by a scholar in the Records of Huayang. Keys roll and turn, seven wheels urge one another on, with twelve teeth at the joints, the large one matching the degrees of heaven. From Zhang Heng, Yixing and Zu Chongzhi on, all could make armillary spheres with moving mechanisms that moved by themselves. Su E records that Silla presented a Ten-Thousand-Buddha Mountain whose wooden figures bowed their heads, the mechanism being in a bell. The Fengchuang records that in the Taiping Xingguo period Zhang Sixun presented an armillary sphere in which seven figures represented the seven luminaries and twelve spirits the hours, all set off by mechanism. Wei Pu and Ma Jun both understood this; it is also something people realize for themselves. Sun Ru made an inch-sized self-ringing clock — and people marvel at that Sun Daniang of Longxi was praised as a wonderful craftsman.

《器用類·築法 Methods of Rammed Earth》

1
○蒸土雜秫，則錐不能入，此赫連築統萬法也。凡滑條墐土，入炭屑、瓦屑，皆極堅，後可旁燒，先不必蒸也。麟州城中無井，惟以沙泉在城外，欲包展城之，其地善崩，謂之抽沙。熙寧中，呂公弼令鄧子喬視之，喬曰：古有拔軸法，謂掘去抽沙，實以炭末墐土，則城可築。如議城之，果堅不陷。一曰：以千年木釘樁，則為力更省，松柏皆可數百歲不壞。
○ Steamed earth mixed with sorghum cannot be pierced by an awl; this was Helian's method of building Tongwan. All smooth-strip plastered earth with charcoal chips and tile chips added is extremely hard, and can afterward be fired at the sides; there is no need to steam it first. Within the walls of Linzhou there were no wells, the only source being a sand spring outside the walls; they wanted to extend the walls to enclose it, but the ground was given to collapsing, which was called drawing sand. In the Xining period, Lü Gongbi ordered Deng Ziqiao to inspect it, and Qiao said: In antiquity there was the method of pulling the axle, which means digging out the drawing sand and filling it with charcoal powder and plastered earth, and then the wall can be built. They built it as he proposed, and indeed it was firm and did not collapse. One says: If piles are driven with thousand-year-old wood, the effort is even less; pine and cypress can last several hundred years without rotting.
