《飲食類 Food and Drink》

《飲食類·酒曲 Wine Ferment》

1
○麵百斤，菉豆斗二升，杏仁十二兩，夏擷辣蓼枝葉，煎汁以溲前三味，置箱壓實，每片以稻稈護，縣乾，醒晝暴夜露，足七日收，臘乃傳酵。北京內府曲則有紅蔻、藿香、白芷、草果仁、杏仁、竹葉、白蓮，以辣蓼、蒼耳、菉豆汁苴餅。又雪香曲以糯粉五斤，面六斤，和勻，計一升，布苴壓實為餅，至臘釀時，糯一石，曲一餅，貯潘瀾百二十斤。米友石家法：面和成甄，置酓二七，暴之為曲，初不用藥，凡釀米五斗，水四斗半，曲二斤半。
○ A hundred jin of flour, a dou and two sheng of mung beans and twelve taels of apricot kernels: in summer pluck the branches and leaves of water-pepper, boil them into a juice to knead the first three ingredients, put them in a box and press them firm, protect each cake with rice straw and hang it to dry, exposing it to the sun by day and the dew by night for a full seven days, then store it; in the twelfth month it is used to pass on the leaven. The palace ferment of Beijing has red cardamom, agastache, angelica, tsaoko, apricot kernels, bamboo leaves and white lotus, wrapped into cakes with juice of water-pepper, cocklebur and mung beans. Also, the Snow Fragrance ferment mixes five jin of glutinous rice flour and six jin of wheat flour evenly, about a sheng in all, wrapped in cloth and pressed firm into a cake; when brewing in the twelfth month, one shi of glutinous rice takes one cake of ferment, stored with a hundred and twenty jin of rice water. The household method of Mi Youshi: knead the flour into a cake, put it in a covered vessel for fourteen days, and dry it in the sun to make ferment, using no drugs at first; for brewing, five dou of rice, four and a half dou of water and two and a half jin of ferment.

《飲食類·釀 Brewing》

1
○重酘曰酎，浮蟻曰酵。凡醞，傷熱則酸，傷冷則甜。東風至而酒湛溢，故貴臘醅。煮酒少沸，不得過也。畢萬曰：新蒲漬酒味厚，以高梁莖煮酒色紅，以青枳煮酒氣香色碧，或以竹葉蠟入酒煮之。諸穀諸藥皆可入釀，以藥浸酒，不如以藥入曲。紫藤角仁熬香入酒，則不敗，不必砒甕也。韶粉入酒去酸，或以火灰，或入炒赤豆，或鎔鉛傾入酒中。虛舟曰：酒中置茄子柴灰，則酒到夜成水。凡酒中火焰，以青布拂之，滅。暄曰：冬釀酒難熟，以麥蒿浸溫，鋪覆缸內，酒母立至。
○ Twice-fermented wine is called zhou, and the floating ants are called jiao. In all brewing, if damaged by heat it turns sour, and if damaged by cold it turns sweet. When the east wind comes, wine overflows, so the wine of the twelfth month is valued. When wine is heated, it should boil only a little and not more. Bi Wan said: New cattail soaked in wine gives a rich flavour; heating wine with sorghum stalks makes it red, and heating it with green bitter orange makes it fragrant and blue-green; or bamboo-leaf wax is put in the wine and heated. All grains and drugs can go into brewing, but soaking drugs in wine is not as good as putting drugs into the ferment. If wisteria seed-kernels are roasted till fragrant and put into wine, it will not spoil, and there is no need for arsenic-[lined] jars. Shao powder put into wine removes sourness, or ash from the fire, or fried red beans, or molten lead poured into the wine. Xuzhou says: If ash of eggplant stalks is put into wine, the wine turns to water by night. Whenever a flame [appears] in wine, brush it with blue cloth and it goes out. Xuan says: Wine brewed in winter is hard to ripen; soak wheat straw warm and spread it over the vat, and the wine mother comes at once.

《飲食類·叩甕辨酒 Telling Wine by Tapping the Jar》

1
○聲清而長者佳，重而短者酒苦，不響者酒必壞。
○ If the sound is clear and long, it is good; if heavy and short, the wine is bitter; if there is no ring, the wine is surely spoiled.

《飲食類·燒酒 Distilled Liquor》

1
○元時始創其法，名阿剌吉。稻、黍、雜糧等皆可燒，先煮熟，鋪地，候冷，和曲蓋之，對晝則發熱炙手，攤之，取入壇中，泥封其口，或三朝，或七日，乃蒸而取其氣水。其三日者，曲藥迅也；七日者，君臣藥也。或以麥芽，如糖法，但不下水。雜記曰：醋入火酒，味如常酒，不復酸。飲膳正要云：葡萄燒酒出哈喇火者最烈。際波里島蒲萄酒可度入十年。燒酒畏鹽，鹽化燒酒為水。暄曰：紙蘸燒酒然之，火去而濕存，可徵雷升雨降之理。
○ The method was first created in the Yuan, and is called araki. Rice, millet and miscellaneous grains can all be distilled: first cook them, spread them on the ground, wait till cool, mix with ferment and cover; after a day and night it heats up enough to burn the hand; spread it out, put it in a jar and seal the mouth with mud, and after three mornings or seven days steam it and take its vapour-water. For three days, the ferment drugs are fast; for seven days, they are sovereign-and-minister drugs. Or use malt, as in making sugar, but without adding water. The Miscellaneous Records say: When vinegar is put into distilled liquor, it tastes like ordinary wine and is no longer sour. The Correct Essentials of Food and Drink says: Grape spirit from Qara-khoja is the strongest. The grape wine of the island of Cyprus can keep for ten years. Distilled liquor fears salt; salt turns distilled liquor into water. Xuan says: When paper dipped in distilled liquor is lit, the fire goes and the moisture remains, which can verify the principle of thunder rising and rain falling.

《飲食類·香酒法 Method of Scenting Wine》

1
○作格懸繫茉莉於甕口，離酒一指許，紙封之，旬日香徹矣。暹羅以香薰壇如漆而貯酒。
○ Make a frame and hang jasmine from the mouth of the jar, about a finger's breadth above the wine, seal it with paper, and in ten days the fragrance penetrates. In Siam they fumigate jars with incense until they are like lacquer and store wine in them.

《飲食類·賽葡萄釀 Imitation Grape Wine》

1
○黑豆去皮，磨碎，於銀器水煮，加數烏梅、少許明礬，熬冷，色黑，濾潔，調以酒蜜，而盛瓶中，密封一宿，飲。
○ Skin black beans, grind them, boil them in water in a silver vessel, add several smoked plums and a little alum, boil and cool; the colour is black; strain clean, mix with wine and honey, put in a bottle, seal tight overnight, and drink.

《飲食類·藥釀 Medicinal Wine》

1
○一分燒酒，二分窨，苧袋盛藥浸之，或煮三炷香，然不若藥和曲釀之妙也。秘法：羊藿、地黃、山羊血、五加入卵伏，如法合麵曲釀，以燒酒拍水，煮窨三年，妙甚，藥隨宜增。
○ One part distilled liquor and two parts cellared [wine], with the drugs soaked in a ramie bag, or heated for the time of three incense sticks; but this is not as good as mixing the drugs into the ferment for brewing. A secret method: epimedium, rehmannia, wild goat's blood and acanthopanax are put into an egg to be brooded, then combined with the flour ferment for brewing as usual, made with distilled liquor beaten with water, heated and cellared for three years — excellent; drugs may be added as suitable.

《飲食類·紅曲 Red Ferment》

1
○石半白粳飯，分十五處，入曲母三斤，揉勻，以帛密覆，熱即攤開，覺溫急堆起，又密覆。次朝日中又作三堆，過一時分作五堆，再一時合作一堆，又過一時分十五堆，稍溫又作一堆，如此數次。第三日，用大桶盛新汲水，以竹籮盛曲，作五六分蘸濕，完又作一堆，如前法。第四日，如前又蘸，若曲半沉半浮，再依前法作之，又蘸，若盡浮則成矣，曝乾收之。其米過心者，謂之生黃，入酒及鮓酢中，鮮紅可愛。一曰：造紅曲，用粳米煮熟爛飯，煎赤靈芝草汁溲為團，中穿一穴，上下擷辣蓼葉覆藉三五日，如未紅，再煎芝草汁常灌之，常浴之，仍以蓼覆，以紅為度，曬乾收，七八月可造。曲母即赤芝、辣蓼遏飯積多次之精者，此方近世乃出。向來瑞金造紅曲，福州古田最紅，其麵母出沙縣。李昌谷詩：酒滴珍珠紅。夏彥剛曰：江南人造紅曲酒，則古有之矣。中通曰：紅曲母法：蒸白粘米飯一斗，倍上曲拌勻，如造酒法，下甕，冬七日，春秋五日，夏三日，如酒熟為度，入盆中擂如稠糊。每粳米一斗，止用此母二升，此一料母可造紅曲石半。
○ Divide a shi and a half of cooked white non-glutinous rice into fifteen heaps, add three jin of ferment mother and knead evenly, cover tightly with silk, and when it heats spread it out; when it feels warm, quickly pile it up again and cover it tightly. The next morning, in the sun, make it into three heaps again; after a double-hour divide it into five heaps, after another double-hour combine it into one heap, after another double-hour divide it into fifteen heaps, and when slightly warm make it into one heap again — and so on several times. On the third day fill a large tub with freshly drawn water, put the ferment in a bamboo basket and dip it in five or six times to wet it, and when done make it into a heap again as before. On the fourth day dip it again as before; if the ferment half sinks and half floats, make it again by the previous method and dip it again, and if it all floats it is done; dry it in the sun and store it. That in which [the red] has passed through the heart of the grain is called raw yellow, and put into wine and pickled fish or vinegar it is a lovely bright red. One says: To make red ferment, cook non-glutinous rice to a soft mush, knead it into balls with a boiled juice of red lingzhi, pierce a hole through the middle, cover above and below with water-pepper leaves for three to five days; if not yet red, boil more lingzhi juice and water and bathe it often, still covering it with water-pepper, until red; dry and store; it can be made in the seventh and eighth months. The ferment mother is the essence of rice repeatedly treated with red lingzhi and water-pepper; this prescription only appeared in recent times. Hitherto red ferment was made at Ruijin, and that of Gutian in Fuzhou is the reddest, its flour mother coming from Sha County. Li Changgu's poem: "Wine drips pearl-red." Xia Yangang said: The red ferment wine made by people south of the Yangzi existed in antiquity. Zhongtong says: The method of the red ferment mother: steam a dou of white sticky rice, mix evenly with double the amount of superior ferment, and put it in a jar as in making wine, seven days in winter, five in spring and autumn and three in summer, until it is ripe like wine, then put it in a basin and grind it into a thick paste. For every dou of non-glutinous rice only two sheng of this mother is used, and one batch of mother can make a shi and a half of red ferment.

《飲食類·湭油 Lees Oil》

1
○酒釀腳十斤，加面八兩，川椒二兩，閉之數月，其浮者即為油。中德曰：宋其武年伯云：以罐蒙紗，入糟埕中，從春過夏，取出，在罐中者即為糟油。凡酒腳十斤，加酒面二斤。馮道濟曰：以竹作篘，置糟埕下，其在篘下者為油，亦須冬收過夏乃取。
○ To ten jin of wine-brewing dregs add eight taels of flour and two taels of Sichuan pepper, close it up for several months, and what floats is the oil. Zhongde says: Uncle Song Qiwu says: Cover a jar with gauze and put it into a jar of lees, take it out after spring and summer, and what is in the jar is lees oil. For every ten jin of wine dregs add two jin of wine flour. Feng Daoji says: Make a strainer of bamboo and put it at the bottom of the lees jar; what is under the strainer is the oil, which also must be collected in winter and taken out after summer.

《飲食類·蒸露法 Method of Steaming Floral Waters》

1
○銅鍋平底，牆高三寸，離底一寸作隔，花鑽之使通氣，外以錫作餾蓋蓋之，其狀如盔，其頂圩，使盛冷水，其邊為通槽，而以一咮流出其餾露也。作灶以磚二層，上鑿孔以安銅鍋，其深寸，鍋底置砂，砂在磚之土，薪火在磚之下，其花置隔上，故下不用水，而花露自出。凡薔薇、茉莉、柚花皆可蒸取之，收入磁瓶，蠟封，而日中暴之，干其三之一，露乃不壞。服一切藥，欲取精液，皆可以是蒸之。近法以磚上砌臼，置沙石厚一二寸，鋪花其上，而以錫盔蓋之，但以鹽泥泥其外縫。陳則梁曰：以重湯蒸錫甑取露，更無焦氣。中履曰：鍚甑頂作中低滴溜，甑中石子上置一罐接之，驗頂上冷水煖則起蓋取中，其花露盡矣。須白石圓徑五分者鋪底，熱則易之。
○ A copper pan with a flat bottom and walls three inches high, with a perforated partition an inch above the bottom to let the steam through, is covered outside with a tin still-head shaped like a helmet, its top concave to hold cold water, with a channel round its edge, from which a spout lets out the distillate. A stove is made of two layers of brick with a hole cut in the top to set the copper pan an inch deep; sand is put under the pan, the sand being above the brick and the firewood below the brick, and the flowers are placed on the partition, so no water is used below and the floral water comes out of itself. Roses, jasmine and pomelo flowers can all be steamed for it; it is put in porcelain bottles, sealed with wax and exposed to the sun until a third has dried away, and then the distillate does not spoil. Whenever one wants to extract the essence of any medicine, it can be steamed in this way. A recent method is to build a cavity on the bricks, put in a layer of sand and stones an inch or two thick, spread the flowers on it and cover with a tin helmet, only sealing its outer seam with salt-mud. Chen Zeliang says: Steaming in a tin steamer over a water bath to take the distillate gives no burnt smell at all. Zhonglü says: Make the top of the tin steamer low in the middle to drip, place a jar on the stones in the steamer to catch it, and when the cold water on top becomes warm, lift the lid and take out what is inside, and all the floral water has been extracted. White stones five fen in diameter must be spread on the bottom, and changed when hot.

《飲食類·餳 Malt Sugar》

1
○以一斗糯蒸飯，以十二兩干麥芽舂成麵和之，如拍酒式，飯上之水可厚三寸。造此者於釜中，灶用溫火，後漸加煖，蓋之，上更以綿絮壓之，凡二閱時而化，化則榨之，其糟不用，專取水煎之，以竹片攪之，攪不可歇。其成糖也，灶火宜細，取糖就熱牽之則白。一曰：臨熱以荸薺末洒之，則香脆。一曰：芝麻花末糝之，佳。于司直曰：內府窩絲上料細牽，次煉琥珀糖，加芨汁，滴水成珠為度。
○ Steam a dou of glutinous rice and mix it with twelve taels of dried malt pounded to flour, as in making wine, with water about three inches deep above the rice. Those who make it do so in a cauldron, using a gentle fire on the stove at first and gradually increasing the heat, with a cover pressed down with cotton floss; after about two double-hours it liquefies, and then it is pressed, the dregs not being used, and only the liquid is boiled, stirred with a bamboo slip without stopping. When the sugar is forming, the stove fire should be low, and if the sugar is pulled while hot it becomes white. One says: Sprinkle it with powdered water chestnut while hot, and it becomes fragrant and crisp. One says: Dusting it with powdered sesame flowers is good. Yu Sizhi said: The palace's nest-silk is made from superior material finely pulled, and next amber sugar is refined with bletilla juice until a drop in water forms a pearl.

《飲食類·造白糖法 Method of Making White Sugar》

1
○煮甘蔗汁，以石灰少許投調，成赤沙糖，再以竹器盛白土，以赤糖淋下鍋，煉成白沙糖，劈鴨卵攪之，使渣滓上浮。老學庵曰：聞茂德言中國無沙糖，唐太宗時外國貢至，問之，甘蔗汁煎，用其法，遂精。茂德乃宋敕局勘定官，餘郡人。
○ Boil sugarcane juice and stir in a little lime to make red sugar; then put white clay in a bamboo vessel, pour the red sugar through it into a pot and refine it into white sugar, breaking duck eggs into it and stirring so the dregs float to the top. The Studio of Old Learning says: I heard Maode say that China had no granulated sugar; in the time of Emperor Taizong of the Tang it came as tribute from abroad, and on enquiry it was boiled from sugarcane juice; the method was adopted and became refined. Maode was an examining official of the Song Imperial Bureau, a man of Yu commandery.

《飲食類·糖霜 Sugar Frost》

1
○唐大歷間，鄒和尚在蜀遂寧傘山始傳此法，今盛於閩廣。智聞餘賡之座師曰：雙清糖霜為上，瀵尾為下。十月濾蔗，其汁乃凝，入釜煮定，以銳底瓦罌穴其下而盛之，置大瓦中，俟穴下滴，而上以鮮黃土作餅蓋之，下滴久乃盡，其上之滓於是極白，是為雙清；次清屢滴，蓋除而餘者近黑，則所謂瀵尾。造皮糖者，甕置竹片，熬糖入之，反甕使滴，餘干於竹上者為皮糖。神隱曰：糖霜和鐙心收，則不潤。
○ In the Dali period of the Tang, the monk Zou first passed on this method at Sanshan in Suining in Shu; it now flourishes in Min and Guang. I, Zhi, heard from my examiner Yu Gengzhi: Double-clear sugar frost is the best and "spurting tail" the worst. In the tenth month the cane is strained and its juice congeals; it is boiled in a cauldron until settled and put into a pointed-bottomed earthenware jar with a hole at the bottom, placed in a large earthenware vessel, and when it drips from the hole, a cake of fresh yellow earth is put on top as a cover; after long dripping below, the residue on top becomes extremely white, and this is double-clear; the second clear drips repeatedly, and what remains when the cover is removed is almost black, which is what is called spurting tail. For making skin-sugar, bamboo slips are put in a jar, boiled sugar is poured in, the jar is inverted to let it drip, and what dries on the bamboo is skin-sugar. The Shenyin says: Sugar frost stored with lamp-rush does not get damp.

《飲食類·洗面筋法 Method of Washing Gluten》

1
○麵十斤，鹽兩半，半溫水和之，俟其發起，挼之挏之，牽開有筋，則入水洗之，可得半成，餘滓澄為小粉。無鹽則無筋矣。市以麩為之。入肥皂則不成。
○ Mix ten jin of flour and a tael and a half of salt with half-warm water, wait for it to rise, knead and pull it, and when it shows strands on being pulled apart, wash it in water; one gets about half, and the remaining sediment settles as starch. Without salt there are no strands. In the market it is made with bran. If soap is added, it does not form.

《飲食類·飯 Cooked Rice》

1
○入樸硝在內，則自各粒而不相粘。陳飯再蒸，不揭蓋，過夜，暑亦不餿。馮應京曰：生莧菜鋪飯上，經宿不。
○ If mirabilite is put in, the grains stay separate and do not stick. Old rice steamed again without lifting the lid keeps overnight without going off even in summer. Feng Yingjing said: If raw amaranth is spread on the rice overnight, it does not [spoil].

《飲食類·辟穀 Abstaining from Grain》

1
○王禎農書云：晉惠帝永寧二年，黃門侍郎劉景先奏：臣遇太白山隱民，傳濟饑方：用大黑豆五斗，淘淨，三蒸之，去皮；以大麻子三斗，浸一宿，亦三蒸之，去殼取仁；各搗為末，作團如拳，入甑再蒸，戌蒸寅出，正午暴乾，未而服，飽不得食一切物。第一頓七日不饑，四頓則永不饑也。口渴，即煎大麻子湯飲之。若欲更食，用葵子三合研，煎冷服，取下藥如金色，任食諸物。類方用黑豆五斗，大麻子三升，蒸曬如前，但加糯米三升作粥，和餅，甑蒸一宿，乾末之，用紅棗五斗煮膏和塊，再蒸服之，渴飲黑芝麻水。左慈法用大豆粒細者，手挼令光暖徹豆內，先日不食，以冷水頓服之。黃山谷法用黑豆、貫眾各一升，煮熟，去貫眾，曬乾，每日空心啖五七粒，食百草枝葉皆有味。肘後方取白茅根，曬乾，搗末，水服。枕中記：茯苓辟穀，服三年，使鬼神。葛洪言五萬日能立亡，日中無影，果然乎？松脂茯苓辟穀方，即松梅丸。愚者家傳方以牛乳制茯苓，可漸辟穀。嵩高記用酒浸茯苓和蜜服，梁敕給陶隱居茯苓白蜜，即此意也。暄曰：西山歐陽仿服食秘法七方，與吞草餌地肥，俱可救荒。
○ Wang Zhen's Book of Agriculture says: In the second year of Yongning of Emperor Hui of the Jin, Liu Jingxian, Gentleman-in-Waiting of the Yellow Gate, memorialized: I met a recluse of Mount Taibai who passed on a method of relieving famine: take five dou of large black beans, wash them clean, steam them three times and skin them; soak three dou of hemp seeds overnight, steam them three times too, husk them and take the kernels; pound each to powder, make balls the size of a fist, put them in a steamer and steam again, steaming from the xu hour to the yin hour, dry them in the sun at noon, and take them at the wei hour, eating one's fill and then eating nothing else. After the first meal one is not hungry for seven days, and after four meals never hungry again. If thirsty, boil hemp seeds and drink the water. If one wishes to eat again, grind three ge of mallow seed, boil it and take it cold, and it brings down the medicine, golden in colour, after which one may eat anything. The Classified Prescriptions use five dou of black beans and three sheng of hemp seed, steamed and dried as before, but add three sheng of glutinous rice made into gruel, mix it into cakes, steam them overnight in a steamer, dry and powder them, boil five dou of red jujubes into a paste and mix into lumps, steam them again and take them; when thirsty, drink black-sesame water. Zuo Ci's method uses the small grains of soybeans, rubbed with the hand until glossy and warmed right through; fast the day before, then take them all at once with cold water. Huang Shangu's method boils a sheng each of black beans and cyrtomium until done, removes the cyrtomium and dries [the beans] in the sun; eat five or seven grains on an empty stomach each day, and the branches and leaves of all plants will taste good. The Prescriptions Kept Up the Sleeve take cogon grass root, dry it in the sun, pound it to powder and take it with water. The Record in the Pillow: abstaining from grain with poria and taking it for three years, one can command ghosts and spirits. Ge Hong says that after fifty thousand days one can vanish where one stands and cast no shadow at noon — is it so? The prescription of abstaining from grain with pine resin and poria is the Pine and Plum Pills. The Foolish One's family prescription prepares poria with cow's milk, with which one can gradually abstain from grain. The Record of Song and Gao takes poria soaked in wine with honey; the Liang edict giving the recluse Tao poria and white honey had this meaning. Xuan says: Ouyang Fang of the Western Hills's seven secret prescriptions for diet, together with swallowing grasses and eating earth-fat, can all relieve famine.

《飲食類·咽津辟穀 Abstaining from Grain by Swallowing Saliva》

1
○澥上三珠曰：舌柱上齶，攪上下齒，取津液咽之，一晝夜咽三四百，或頻呷一二匙水，水能止饑，但不可多，多則肚疼。又不可飲白湯，白湯令人饑。又勿食鹽醋物，令人少津液。久之欲食，須先薄粥而後飯，以腸約，勿頓飽也。最上伏氣。暄曰：伏氣須學龜息，能答厭會，若入咽以津蓋之，如是致滿，亦可辟穀。
○ The Three Pearls of Xieshang says: Press the tongue against the upper palate, stir it round the upper and lower teeth, take the saliva and swallow it, three or four hundred times in a day and night; or frequently sip a spoonful or two of water, for water can stop hunger, but not too much, for too much gives stomach ache. Also, one must not drink plain boiled water, which makes one hungry. Also, do not eat salty or sour things, which reduce the saliva. After a long time, when one wants to eat, one must first take thin gruel and then rice, because the intestines have shrunk, and not eat one's fill at once. Best of all is subduing the qi. Xuan says: Subduing the qi requires learning the tortoise's breathing, being able to answer [the qi] at the meeting [of the epiglottis]; when it enters the throat, cover it with saliva, and so until full — this too allows abstaining from grain.

1
○茶載神農食經，古荼即茶漢志荼陵音茶，詳通雅。韓翃謝茶啟云：吳主置茗，晉人分茶。晏子三茗，自古已然，惟桑苧以製顯耳。宓山約之曰：種以多子，稍長即移大即難移，灌汁遠沁，山宜西南，以受露多也，采宜穀雨前後，羅介立夏開園。中複曰：雨前芽嫩，摘以指甲，勿傷老節，夏不如春，或秋一摘，俗盡下其老葉，以明年再生也。樹老則燒之，其根自發。宜糠與焦土種，惡停水。製有三法：摘葉貴晴，候其發香，熱鍋搗青，使人旁扇，傾出煩挼，再焙至三而燥；一法：沸湯微燖，晾乾，綿紙藉而焙之；一法：蒸葉晾干，再以火焙。收貴錫瓶，或箬藉，或沙甕，礬黏入炭，箬封固，倒庋閣上，承以新磚，以濕蒸自上而下也。紙、木、香、藥、食、檗諸氣，近則受染，慎之哉！中通曰：箬能隔濕，下路方磚奠沙，沙下鋪箬，乃不上潮，故竹絲編箬盛茶，外黏封，不受煙，而近灶閣之不壞。甕泑亦生潤，故用沙甕，以礬礜黏其外，最妙。是處產茶，焙製斯異。名茶皆炒，岕以蒸焙。趙長白茶史載事耳，龍團鳳餅、紫茸驚芽，何為乎？松蘿去尖與柄與筋，畏其先焦也，炒薪宜枝，不用幹葉，文火武催，急翻，半熟為度，生則黑矣。旁扇袪熱，乃免黃褐，掀出磁盤，尤須急扇，乃重揉之，再以文炒，或三乃乾，帶潤覆之，則氣罨鬱，更一焙焉，待冷上霜。優劣定於始鐺，清濁係乎末火，確矣。馮可賓曰：白巖、烏瞻、青東、顧渚、筱浦皆岕，而羅氏居小秦王廟後洞山，向陽，蒸以葉之老嫩定蒸之遲速，皮梗碎色帶赤，其候也，大熟失鮮。茶焙歲修，別茶熏乾，焙簾勿用新竹，煙炭剔去，上搖大扇，火氣旋轉乃勻耳。中履曰：林確齋梅田種茶，亦製圿片。北源藏溪法：六安貢尖近亦能用諸法。大圿征燖後泡，今先洗葉炒之，香亦相似。山泉上，江水中，井下。山足泉重於頂，砂冽於石。永叔曰：張又新載劉伯芻七等，陸羽語李季卿二十種，多反茶經，辨南零特怪，其又新附之耶？王禹偁贊谷簾湯中能稱三疊，茶經忌湍瀑，此非瀑邪？其收潭邪？甕置溪石，紗覆承露，梅雨投伏龍肝，變則火洗可也。芷園日記曰：天水都好，須器中久俟其色變，蟲去，色香味始妙，山泉留亦變也。湯最忌煙，紅炭瓦鐺，急扇勿停，小砂壺數具，時分三投冬下投，春中投，夏上投，何計銀銚龔春邪？蘇廙十六湯，文瀾俊語也。李南金用背二涉三為合量，羅鶴林欲於松風澗水後移瓶去火而瀹之，羅廩曰：湯老矣，去火何救哉？愚者曰：上池水以淡為甘，山泉重冽，宜多一沸，沸之與止，代錯無窮，各取當機耳。色白香沖，味恬而滑，絕非草氣，不較花芳，此最上乘。雲林香花收茶清閟集曰：早取將開蕊入茶封之，烘乾，取茶入瓶，久夫綃紙苴花，納茶潤藉，紙裛［或瓶收花香，去花貯茶，皆第二義也。許次抒、葉清臣、邢士襄、聞龍、張源諸公箋錄，各圖快語，灑濯玄味，須遇其人，自非眠雲漱石，何暇領此哉？姚士麟曰：關中油酥點飲，永順烹以草果，番人膜拜迎茶。此茶馬御史所以設也。馬眼光照人，全身齒少毛，附掌粘者無病，上馬給茶百二十斤，中七十，下五十；商每芽引三錢，葉引二錢，闌出者死。漢中野茶長簍曰篦，上馬給茶十五篦，常馬不過六篦。吳麟長曰：南楚熏曝成茶，且雜樹葉，出塞即香，蓋地寒則草香發，膻酪賴此去病，故貴之。苦澄出廣，蒙頂乃苔也。普洱茶蒸之成團，狗西番市之，最能化物，與六安同。修江出欒茶，治頭風，乃石楠葉也。

Han Hong's letter of thanks for tea says: The ruler of Wu set out tea, and the men of Jin shared tea. Mishan summarizes: in planting, use many seeds and transplant as soon as they grow a little when large they are hard to transplant; water with juice that seeps far; the hills should face southwest, to receive more dew; picking should be around Grain Rain, and in Luojie the gardens are opened at the Beginning of Summer. Zhongfu says: Before the rains the buds are tender; pick them with the fingernails without harming the old nodes; summer [tea] is not as good as spring, or there is one picking in autumn; it is the custom to strip all the old leaves so they grow again the next year. When the bushes are old, burn them, and the roots put out shoots of themselves. They should be planted in bran and burnt soil, and hate standing water. There are three methods of preparation: pick the leaves preferably on a fine day, wait till they give off fragrance, and press them green in a hot pan with someone fanning beside, pour them out and knead them repeatedly, and roast again up to three times until dry; another method is to scald them slightly in boiling water, air-dry them and roast them on cotton paper; another is to steam the leaves, air-dry them and then roast them over fire. For storage, tin bottles are best, or lining with bamboo leaves, or sand jars with alum stuck on and charcoal put in, sealed tightly with bamboo leaves, stored upside down on a shelf on new bricks, because damp steams from above downward. Paper, wood, incense, drugs, food and phellodendron all have qi which taints it if near — beware! Zhongtong says: Bamboo leaves keep out damp: lay square bricks below, spread sand on them and bamboo leaves under the sand, and the damp will not rise; so tea is kept in bamboo-weave lined with bamboo leaves, sealed outside, and it takes no smoke and does not spoil even stored near the stove. Glazed jars also produce damp, so sand jars are used, with alum or arsenolite stuck on the outside — best of all. Tea is produced everywhere, and the roasting and preparation differ. Famous teas are all fried; jie tea is steamed and roasted. Zhao Changbai's History of Tea only records events; what of Dragon Ball and Phoenix Cake, Purple Down and Startled Bud? For Songluo, the tips, stalks and veins are removed, for fear they scorch first; for frying, the fuel should be twigs, not trunks or leaves; a gentle fire is urged on to a strong one, turning quickly, and half-cooked is the measure, for raw it turns black. Fanning beside removes the heat and so avoids yellow-brown; when tipped out into a porcelain dish, it must be fanned all the more quickly, then kneaded again and fried again gently, perhaps three times, until dry; covered while still moist, the qi is stifled, so it is roasted once more and, when cool, frost appears. Quality is fixed in the first pan, and clarity or turbidity depends on the last fire — that is certain. The tea-roasting stove is repaired every year, and other teas are smoked dry; for roasting screens do not use new bamboo; smoky charcoal is picked out; a large fan is waved above so that the heat circulates evenly. Zhonglü says: Lin Quezhai grows tea at Meitian and also makes jie flakes. The method of Beiyuan and Cangxi: Liu'an tribute tips have lately also used these methods. The great jie [teas] were formerly scalded and then steeped; now the leaves are first washed and fried, and the fragrance is similar. Mountain spring water is best, river water next, and well water last. A spring at the foot of a mountain is heavier than one at the top, and one from sand is colder than one from rock. Yongshu said: Zhang Youxin recorded Liu Bochu's seven grades and Lu Yu's telling Li Jiqing of twenty kinds, which mostly contradict the Classic of Tea; his distinction of the Southern Ling is particularly strange — did Youxin add it himself? Wang Yucheng praised the Gulian [spring] for being able to show three layers in the hot water, but the Classic of Tea avoids rapids and waterfalls — is this not a waterfall? Or was it collected in the pool? Put stones from the stream in the jar and cover it with gauze to catch dew; in the plum rains put in stove-earth; if it changes, it may be washed with fire. The Diary of the Zhi Garden says: Rain water is always good, but it must be kept long in a vessel until its colour changes and the insects go, and only then are its colour, fragrance and taste wonderful; mountain spring water kept also changes. Hot water most fears smoke; with red charcoal and an earthenware pot, fan quickly without stopping, have several small sand teapots, and put [the tea] in at three different times in winter put it in below, in spring in the middle, in summer on top — why bother about silver kettles and Gongchun pots? Su Yi's Sixteen Waters is fine and brilliant writing. Li Nanjin used "past the second and approaching the third [boil]" as the proper measure, and Luo Helin wanted to move the kettle off the fire after the sound of wind in the pines and the mountain stream and then steep it; Luo Lin said: The water is already too old — what good is removing it from the fire? The Foolish One says: Water from the upper pool takes blandness as sweetness, while mountain spring water is heavy and cold and should be boiled once more; boiling and stopping alternate endlessly, each taking what suits the moment. Colour white, fragrance penetrating, taste mild and smooth, with no grassy taste and not competing with the fragrance of flowers — this is the very best. Yunlin's storing tea with fragrant flowers the Qingbi Collection says: early in the morning take buds about to open, put them in the tea and seal it, roast dry, take the tea out and put it in bottles, and after a long time wrap the flowers in silk paper and put them in with the tea to moisten it, the paper wrapping [it]; or store the flower fragrance in a bottle, remove the flowers and keep the tea is all second-rate. The notes of Xu Cishu, Ye Qingchen, Xing Shixiang, Wen Long, Zhang Yuan and others each seek brilliant phrases and wash out the mysterious flavour; one must meet the right person, for who but one who sleeps among the clouds and rinses his mouth with stones has leisure to appreciate this? Yao Shilin said: In Guanzhong they drink it with butter, in Yongshun they boil it with tsaoko, and the foreign peoples bow to welcome tea. This is why the Censor for Tea and Horses was established. A horse whose eyes shine at a person, with few teeth in its whole body, little hair, and hooves that stick to the palm is free of illness; for a superior horse a hundred and twenty jin of tea is given, a middling one seventy and an inferior one fifty; merchants pay three qian per bud-permit and two qian per leaf-permit, and those who smuggle it out are put to death. Wild tea of Hanzhong in long baskets is called bi; a superior horse is given fifteen bi of tea, and an ordinary horse no more than six bi. Wu Linchang said: In southern Chu they smoke and sun-dry tea mixed with tree leaves, and once beyond the passes it is fragrant, for in cold lands the fragrance of plants comes out; people who live on mutton and curds depend on it to get rid of illness, so they value it. Kuchen comes from Guang, and Mengding is moss. Pu'er tea is steamed into balls; the Western barbarians buy it, and it is best at dissolving food, like Liu'an. Xiujiang produces luan tea, which cures headache from wind; it is photinia leaves.

《飲食類·醍醐、酥酪、抱螺 Tihu, Butter, Curds and Baoluo》

1

○ Put cow's milk in a jar, set up a cross-shaped wooden churn, and have two people pull it from opposite sides to bring out the essence; ladle off what is on top, pad up the thick part, boil it, skim off the scorched foam, and it congeals into butter. The clear part that congeals a little is called tihu, which can only be kept without leaking in eggshells and gourds. Some wrap white sugar in it to make cakes, some make curds, or add a little mutton fat, warm and mix it with honey, and drip it swirling into water, which is called baoluo. All are made in the cold months; cut one or two slices of radish to remove the rank smell.

《飲食類·豆腐 Bean Curd》

1

my strength was spent and my heart already rotten. Had I known the art of Huainan earlier,
I could have sat at ease and gained a spring of coins." For the Materia Medica says that bean curd was made by Liu An, the Prince of Huainan. To a dou of soybeans add a sheng of mung beans, grind, mix with oil, drain off the dregs, boil, and coagulate with gypsum — that is excellent. If one or two slices of radish are put in, it will not form curd. If the milk is mixed with salt and vinegar and put in a cauldron, it turns into red water. Dried bean-curd skins are first soaked in rice water and then heated until soft. Zhonglü says: Old ones are dyed with lin powder or malt crumbs and boiled soft; mouldy ones do not soften; peeled and rolled up, they are called bean-curd candles.

1

《飲食類·筍供 Offerings of Bamboo Shoots》

1
○林洪曰：霍如庵以方片筍和白米煮，因舉濟顛曰：拖油盤裡專黃金，和米鐺中煮白玉。然參玉版禪，正在獨味，謂之傍林鮮。文與可守臨川，正煨筍午飯，忽得坡詩：想見清貧饞太守，渭川千畝在胸中。不覺噴飯滿案。山齋供曰：沃土種竹，土發十坼，即鉏出之，勿見風，以布封還，拭之，蒸熟，最佳。煮久，湯積為油，入少鹽，炭煏之，其味獨全，山陰筍鯗是其類也。天目、廬山拳筍，乃小黃竹筍也。南岳搗筍而乾之，亦勝閩之沙筍。中通曰：杭州筍大如桶，墜地即碎，用竹刀切。廬山筍青而瘦，南岳筍黑而肥，有汁，故搗之為餅。中履曰：筍譜：甌越菡隋筍七八月生；縉雲南多互筍，七月生，至十月味稍苦；芭竹筍九月生；雪竹筍五月生；方竹筍八月生。大抵浙閩以南四時有筍。鬻筍有出汗法，遠行乃不壞：先平列立尖，以黃土覆之，燖湯潑之，一夕出汗，乃取入簍。最畏糯米糖酸之類，香櫞柑橘皆然。

with rice in the pan, boiling white jade." But to partake of the meditation of the jade slab lies in the single flavour, which is called "freshness beside the grove." When Wen Yuke was prefect of Linchuan, he was just roasting bamboo shoots for lunch when he suddenly received Dongpo's poem: "I imagine the poor and greedy prefect
with a thousand mu of the Wei River [bamboo] in his breast." Before he knew it, he had sprayed his rice all over the table. The Offerings of the Mountain Studio say: Plant bamboo in rich soil, and when the soil cracks in ten places, hoe [the shoots] out at once, without letting them meet the wind, wrap them in cloth to bring them home, wipe them and steam them — the best. Boiled long, the broth accumulates into oil; add a little salt and dry them over charcoal, and their flavour alone is preserved; the dried bamboo shoots of Shanyin are of this kind. The fist shoots of Tianmu and Lushan are the shoots of small yellow bamboo. At Nanyue they pound bamboo shoots and dry them, which also surpass the sand shoots of Min. Zhongtong says: The bamboo shoots of Hangzhou are as large as buckets and break when they fall to the ground; they are cut with a bamboo knife. 《飲食類·爛物法 Methods of Softening Things》

1
○枳實烹鮮，骨耎；薄荷不腥；蜜塗郭索，煮之則青，柿蒂同煮亦青；醉見燈沙，宜置皂角，或醋，或蒜，或用茱萸一粒置蟹厴中，經年不沙；杏仁麋羊；胡桃去臊；銅器煮則男損陽，女暴下，酒灌封燒可也；荷花蒂煮肉，精者浮，肥者沉；急性熟鵝；白梅瀹雞；灶取瓦一片同煮，老物皆爛。神隱曰：羊，滾湯下，蓋定，慢火；獐鹿，冷水下，不蓋；鵝用櫻桃葉數片。陸游家世舊聞曰：楚公陸佃農師北使，攜貔歸，為隙光所射，輒死，性能糜肉。燕山錄曰：以鼺煮鱉，以蚊煮犬，省力易熟。俗詮曰：舊籬篾入鍋熟物，或楮實同煮，封口易爛。今庖用沙仁以益脾也。其驛遞官廚有秘方，則乾蛆末也。凡大小溲最爛物，而蛆又其化者。其曰沆瀣漿，乃以甘蔗、蘿卜切塊煮爛者也。至于鐵烙鵝掌，艾炙鱉腹，何乃為腴口計而暴殄若是乎？古人遇物理則記之耳，切忌侈靡為飲食之人。
○ Cooking fresh fish with bitter orange softens the bones; mint removes the fishy smell; crabs smeared with honey turn green when boiled, and boiled with persimmon calyxes they also turn green; drunken crabs go sandy when they see a lamp, so put in soap-pod, or vinegar, or garlic, or put an evodia seed under the crab's apron, and they will not go sandy for a year; apricot kernels soften mutton; walnuts remove the rank smell; boiling in copper vessels harms the yang of men and causes women's sudden discharges, but sealing with wine poured in and roasting is all right; when meat is boiled with lotus calyxes, the lean floats and the fat sinks; balsam seed cooks goose; smoked plums for boiling chicken; take a tile from the stove and boil it with old animals, and they all become tender. Lu You's Old Things Heard of My Family says: When Lord Chu, Lu Dian (Nongshi), went as envoy to the north he brought back a pi beast, which died when the light from a crack shone on it; its nature could soften meat. The Record of Yanshan says: Boil turtles with a flying squirrel and dogs with mosquitoes, and they cook easily with less effort. The Popular Explanations say: Old bamboo fence strips put in the pot cook things, or boiling with paper-mulberry fruit and sealing the mouth softens them easily. Urine and faeces soften things most, and maggots are their transformation. What is called hangxie broth is sugarcane and radish cut into pieces and boiled soft. As for searing geese's feet with iron and burning turtles' bellies with mugwort, why waste things like this merely to fatten the mouth? The ancients merely recorded principles of things as they met them; one must strictly avoid becoming a person given over to extravagance in eating and drinking.

《飲食類·省柴法 Methods of Saving Fuel》

1
○南京以三蘆炊一頓飯。馮道濟言：四兩柴可熟。以上閉氣，而灶中四圍以濕草鞋立之，細柴燒釜臍也。蒸物用少水，堅閉，自爛。其悶飯，洗米一碗，水二碗，則不必撇湯，但遏火而自乾矣。
○ In Nanjing they cook a whole meal with three reeds. Feng Daoji says: Four taels of firewood can cook it. This works by keeping in the heat: around the inside of the stove wet straw sandals are stood up, and the fine firewood burns at the navel of the cauldron. In steaming things, use little water and close it tightly, and they soften of themselves. For stewed rice, one bowl of washed rice to two bowls of water, so there is no need to skim off the water; just damp down the fire and it dries by itself.

《飲食類·炒白果 Roasting Ginkgo Nuts》

1
○白果用粗糠炒，即易熟而不焦。
○ If ginkgo nuts are roasted with coarse bran, they cook easily without scorching.

《飲食類·用椒法 Method of Using Pepper》

1
○會菜將成，擂胡椒灑之，即辣；若蓋其釜，則雖多不辣。
○ When a mixed dish is nearly done, grind pepper and sprinkle it on, and it is hot; if the cauldron is covered, it is not hot however much is used.

《飲食類·用芥 Using Mustard》

1
○陳芥子入細辛少許，醋與蜜同研，則極辣。
○ Grind old mustard seed with a little asarum, vinegar and honey, and it is extremely hot.

《飲食類·脫栗皮 Removing Chestnut Skins》

1
○煮銀杏、栗子，用油紙捻在內，則皮自脫。破栗臍，灰燒不爆，或曰眉上埒之。
○ When boiling ginkgo nuts or chestnuts, put a twist of oiled paper in, and the skins come off by themselves. If the navel of a chestnut is broken, it does not burst when roasted in ash; some say [mark it] on the brow.

《飲食類·菱 Water Caltrop》

1
○煮過，以礬湯綽之，紅綠如生。煮菱要青，用石灰水拌過，先洗去灰，煮則青。舒州李惟熙曰：芡花向日，菱花背日，故寒，然幹菱米正平，不寒也。
○ After boiling, scald them in alum water, and they are red and green as if fresh. To keep water caltrops green when boiled, mix them with limewater, first wash off the lime, and then they are green when boiled. Li Weixi of Shuzhou says: The flowers of the foxnut face the sun and those of the water caltrop turn away from it, so it is cold; but dried water-caltrop kernels are neutral and not cold.

《飲食類·杏仁 Apricot Kernels》

1
○蘿苩梗煮杏仁，無苦味。北京以巴旦杏仁作酪。
○ Apricot kernels boiled with radish stalks have no bitter taste. In Beijing they make a curd from badan almonds.

《飲食類·煮麵 Boiling Noodles》

1
○煮面令湯清，北方用花鹼，南方用糖醋撮。切面堅韌者，以雞卵白，或以菉豆粉。
○ To keep the water clear when boiling noodles, in the north they use flower-alkali and in the south a pinch of sugar and vinegar. For cut noodles that are firm and chewy, use egg white or mung-bean flour.

《飲食類·煮芋 Boiling Taro》

1
○宜冬月食，灰煮之則蘇，草置釜底，奠芋，蓋悶，亦佳。
○ It is suitable to eat in winter; boiled with ash it becomes crumbly; or put straw at the bottom of the cauldron, set the taro on it and stew it covered, which is also good.

《飲食類·菉豆 Mung Beans》

1
○煮菉豆，揭蓋則綠。其煮而堅者，碓豆也。豆將熟時，逐日摘之，老則急裂墮地，不與別豆同。
○ When boiling mung beans, lift the lid and they stay green. Those that remain hard after boiling are pestle beans. When the beans are nearly ripe, pick them day by day, for when old they split suddenly and fall to the ground, unlike other beans.

《飲食類·黑豆 Black Beans》

1
○荷花梗煮黑豆，豆易糜，賣豆者用此法。中履曰：清晨吞生黑豆者，用馬料豆。
○ If black beans are boiled with lotus stalks, they easily become mushy; bean sellers use this method. Zhonglü says: Those who swallow raw black beans in the early morning use horse-fodder beans.

《飲食類·乾山藥法 Method of Drying Chinese Yam》

1
○大山中者，堅細味香，沙上種者不如也。刮皮，以厚紙裹，掛於風中，最良。又置焙籠中，鋪茅數寸，以微火烘之，作湯點，如新。
○ Those from deep in the mountains are firm, fine and fragrant; those grown on sand are not as good. Pare off the skin, wrap them in thick paper and hang them in the wind — best of all. Or put them in a drying basket on a layer of cogon grass several inches thick and dry them over a gentle fire; made into soup they are as if fresh.

《飲食類·炒落花生法 Method of Roasting Peanuts》

1
○生則瀉人，焦則不堪，須以紙苴水浸，然後入釜炒之，則內熟而不焦，其香如松子。
○ Raw they give one diarrhoea, scorched they are inedible; one must wrap them in paper soaked in water and then roast them in the pan, and they are cooked inside without scorching, with a fragrance like pine nuts.

《飲食類·藏鮮果法 Method of Storing Fresh Fruit》

1
○入新瓷，雜薄荷、鹽、礬，水浸，以油紙同木蓋、竹箬堅其口，置清冷泉內，雖久如新。必立夏時置井中，至立冬時置地間。每斤果，鹽一兩，明礬六錢，水用滿。又曰：六月六日儲水，浸諸鮮果，不壞。用木桶閉氣亦可。鮮果忌風。
○ Put them in new porcelain mixed with mint, salt and alum, soak them in water, seal the mouth firmly with oiled paper, a wooden lid and bamboo leaves, and put it in a clear cold spring, and even after a long time they are as if fresh. They must be put in a well at the Beginning of Summer and placed on the ground at the Beginning of Winter. For each jin of fruit, a tael of salt and six qian of alum, with the water filled to the top. It is also said: Water stored on the sixth day of the sixth month keeps fresh fruits soaked in it from spoiling. A wooden tub sealed airtight may also be used. Fresh fruit dreads the wind.

《飲食類·收栗梨 Storing Chestnuts and Pears》

1
○收栗不蛀，以慄蒲燒灰，淋汁澆，二宿出之，候乾，置盆中，用沙覆之。收梨，以草覆之，令出汗，乃入桶中，最畏風，惟青皮梨稍不畏風。或曰：梨以數顆插一顆上，不損。
○ To store chestnuts without worms, burn chestnut burs to ash, pour on water and use the lye to wet them, take them out after two nights, wait till they are dry, put them in a basin and cover them with sand. To store pears, cover them with straw to make them sweat, then put them in a tub; they dread the wind most, and only green-skinned pears are a little less afraid of it. Some say: If several pears are stuck onto one [radish], they do not spoil.

《飲食類·收雞頭 Storing Foxnuts》

1
○曬乾，入瓶，紙蒙而埋之地中。雞頭一斗，用防風四雨，換水浸之。陳彥和曰：果以防風水浸之，經月不壞。
○ Dry them in the sun, put them in a bottle, cover with paper and bury it in the ground. For a dou of foxnuts, use four taels of saposhnikovia and soak them, changing the water. Chen Yanhe says: Fruit soaked in saposhnikovia water does not spoil for a month.

《飲食類·藏柑 Storing Mandarins》

1
○以盆盛，用乾潮沙蓋之，土瓜同法。一曰：宜菉豆同收。
○ Put them in a basin and cover them with dried damp sand; snake gourds are kept the same way. One [source] says: They should be stored with mung beans.

《飲食類·收湘橘 Storing Xiang Tangerines》

1
○用煮湯錫瓶收之，經年不壞。神隱曰：橘以薄竹刀就其柄處微勒一匝，則味不枯，久留。橘枯，可剌多眼，以蜜水浸之，則鮮。
○ Store them in a tin bottle that has had water boiled in it, and they do not spoil for a year. The Shenyin says: If a thin bamboo knife is drawn lightly once round a tangerine at the stem, its flavour does not dry out and it keeps long. If a tangerine has dried, prick it with many holes and soak it in honey water, and it becomes fresh.

《飲食類·藏胡桃 Storing Walnuts》

1
○不可焙，焙則油。其兩合處有縫，掌拍之可碎。
○ Do not dry them over fire, for then they become oily. Where the two halves meet there is a seam, and a slap of the palm will crack them.

《飲食類·生姜 Ginger》

1
○生姜社前收，無筋。製姜用蟬蛻，則無筋。以梅與姜相間作糖，則梅不酸，姜不辣。
○ Ginger harvested before the she festival has no fibres. If ginger is prepared with cicada slough, it has no fibres. If plums and ginger are candied in alternate layers, the plums are not sour and the ginger not hot.

《飲食類·收茄 Storing Eggplants》

1
○以爐灰藏之，可至四五月。若煮過，石壓，先暴瓦熱而列茄，略乾，則永藏至正二月，和物調食，味如新。又可作糖乾。
○ Stored in stove ash they last until the fourth or fifth month. If they are boiled, pressed with a stone, and laid out on tiles that have first been heated in the sun until slightly dry, they keep indefinitely until the first or second month, and when mixed with other things and seasoned they taste as if fresh. They can also be made into candied dried [eggplant].

《飲食類·醋 Vinegar》

1
○社醋、七醋，皆以麵面和之，浸熱而酸。神仙醋則以火逼連攪成者。米醋內入炒鹽，則不生白衣。孕婦造醋，必苦。山查、棗子皆可作醋，最養人。愚者于醬醋悟變化之理焉。中通曰：米醋結蓋，取出以鹽淹之，可治發背。陳景昭曰：蒸糯飯，熱入埕中，上半糊飯四圍而中空之，汲冷水浸二七，乃以柳枝攪，至三七即成白醋，再鍋熬之，即成紅醋，伏天最佳，四季皆可。
○ She vinegar and seventh-day vinegar are both mixed with wheat flour and turn sour when steeped warm. Immortal's vinegar is made by forcing it over fire with continual stirring. If fried salt is put into rice vinegar, it does not grow a white film. Vinegar made by a pregnant woman is always bitter. Hawthorn and jujubes can both be made into vinegar, which is most nourishing. The Foolish One gained insight into the principle of transformation from soy sauce and vinegar. Zhongtong says: When rice vinegar forms a cover, take it out and pickle it in salt, and it can treat abscesses of the back. Chen Jingzhao says: Steam glutinous rice and put it hot into a jar, packing the rice round the upper half and leaving the middle empty; draw cold water and steep it for fourteen days, then stir it with a willow branch, and by the twenty-first day it becomes white vinegar; boil it again in a pan and it becomes red vinegar; the dog days are best, but it can be done in all four seasons.

《飲食類·醬 Soy Paste》

1
○煮豆和麵，覆楮，生毛，暴燥，曰醬黃，與瓜菜同入甕而泥之，以暴三伏，開時味妙。瓜不可鹽曬入醬，但拭淨，以石灰水浸之，取同醬黃、香料入甕。豆醬取油，餘仍充醬。諺曰：伏水宜醬，臘水宜酒。小蕪荑醞醬，山榆仁也，能殺蟲。又曰：伏中合醬，曲不生蟲；日未出汲水下醬，不引蠅子；清明柳條止醬醋潮溢；醬內生蟲，以馬鞭草碎切入之，或川椒、百部，蟲即死；雷不覆醬，食之腹鳴；月上下弦觸醬，醬壞；以熟水下醬，味香；以生水下醬，則雨點不妨。中通曰：大抵水少則不酸。黃子攤薄則不發熱，且色黃，若厚則黑爛矣。下缸後遇陰雨，小棓撐起缸蓋，以出其氣也。鹽停冷，摻其面，天晴一二日便翻轉，令白，傾翻令勻，且出熱氣。
○ Boil beans and mix with flour, cover with paper-mulberry leaves until mould grows, and dry it in the sun — this is called paste-yellow; put it in a jar together with melons and vegetables, seal it with mud, expose it to the sun through the dog days, and when opened the flavour is wonderful. Melons must not be salted and sun-dried before going into the paste; just wipe them clean, soak them in limewater and put them in the jar with the paste-yellow and spices. Oil is taken from the bean paste, and the rest still serves as paste. The proverb says: Water of the dog days is good for paste, water of the twelfth month for wine. Small wuyi used in brewing paste is the kernel of the mountain elm, and can kill insects. It is also said: if paste is made in the dog days, the ferment breeds no insects; if the water for the paste is drawn before sunrise, it does not attract flies; willow branches at Qingming stop paste and vinegar from frothing over; if insects breed in paste, chop vervain finely and put it in, or Sichuan pepper or stemona, and the insects die at once; if paste is not covered during thunder, eating it makes the belly rumble; if paste is touched at the first or last quarter of the moon, it spoils; paste made with boiled water is fragrant; if made with raw water, then drops of rain do no harm. Zhongtong says: In general, with little water it does not turn sour. If the yellow is spread thin it does not heat up and its colour is yellow; if thick, it goes black and rotten. If it meets cloudy rain after going into the vat, prop up the vat lid with a small stick to let out the vapour. When the salt has cooled, sprinkle it over the surface, and after one or two fine days turn it over, so that it whitens, tipping and turning it so that it is even and the hot vapour escapes.

《飲食類·蝦米 Dried Shrimp》

1
○閩出龍蝦，大次則對蝦，海豐土蝦肉黑，如小兒臂，俱宜酒醋烹之，則香。靖江小蝦色白，最香，竟可乾食。清波雜志曰：淮南蝦米用席裹入京，皆枯黑無味，以便溺浸一宿，水洗去，則紅潤如新。
○ Min produces lobsters, the next largest being prawns; the earth shrimps of Haifeng have black flesh and are as large as a child's arm; all are best cooked with wine and vinegar, which makes them fragrant. The small shrimps of Jingjiang are white and most fragrant, and can even be eaten dry. The Miscellaneous Records of Qingbo say: Dried shrimps of Huainan wrapped in mats to go to the capital all become shrivelled, black and tasteless; soak them overnight in urine and wash it off with water, and they become red and glossy as if fresh.

《飲食類·冥果 Preserved Fruit》

1
○用銅青煮之，則色綠。永嘉雕梅，謂之梅籃，先浸石灰，後用銅器瀹之，乃以蜜暴收之。礬水濯青梅，及鹽鹵梅醬淹桂花，不變色。梅鹵可醃梔子花、萱草花、牽牛花、林擒、藕、冬瓜、葫蘆，更入紫蘇葉，妙。凡蜜惟夏蜜甘，煎果覺酸，當換蜜。收藏者以細辛放頂上。
○ Boiled with verdigris, they become green. The carved plums of Yongjia, called plum baskets, are first soaked in lime, then scalded in copper vessels, and then stored in honey after drying in the sun. Green plums rinsed in alum water, and osmanthus flowers pickled in salted-brine plum paste, do not change colour. Plum brine can be used to pickle gardenia flowers, daylily flowers, morning-glory flowers, crab-apples, lotus root, wax gourd and bottle gourd, and adding perilla leaves is wonderful. Of all honeys only summer honey is sweet; if candied fruit seems sour, the honey should be changed. Those who store them put asarum on top.

○ Combining green plums with roses makes them all the redder.

《飲食類·酵饅頭 Leavened Steamed Buns》

1
○醅面俟老烈用之，或用糯醅，冬置煁側，或入生酒，妙。別以鹼發者遜之。饅開首者曰橐駝臍，吳下呼為腦膪音腦胙，言熟食之肥也。長曰繭，斜曰桃。

Buns that open at the top are called camel's navel, and in Wu they are called naozhuo pronounced naozuo, meaning the fat of cooked food. Long ones are called cocoons, and slanting ones peaches.

《飲食類·解梅酸牙 Relieving Teeth Set on Edge by Plums》

1

《飲食類·蓴 Water Shield》

1
○荇類，葉如馬踶有鈌者，蓴也。李長蘅曰：袁石公盛稱湘湖蓴羹，不知湘湖無蓴，皆從西湖采去；又謂非湘湖水浸不佳，不知蓴初摘時必浸之經宿，乃愈肥，不必湘湖也，然西湖。在胸則服升麻吐之，在腹服鬱金下之。虛舟曰：嘗白礬末不澀而覺其甘，食黑豆不腥，乃中金蠶蠱毒也，即濃煎石榴皮根飲，立吐出一蟲。李晦之曰：冷水飲白礬、牙茶，解中毒。又曰：宜用蝟皮。中履曰：剌蝟能制金蠶蠱。暄曰：廣西、雲、貴以元旦及二月二、三月三計月造蠱，蠱有五種，皆以馬兜鈴三兩，每煎一兩，空心服，不吐再服。
○ Of the floating-heart kind, the one with leaves like a horse's hoof with a notch is water shield. Li Changheng said: Yuan Shigong highly praised the water-shield soup of Xiang Lake, not knowing that Xiang Lake has no water shield, it all being gathered from West Lake; he also said it is not good unless steeped in Xiang Lake water, not knowing that water shield must be steeped overnight when first picked, and then it is plumper, so it need not be Xiang Lake, but rather West Lake. [If poison] is in the chest, take cimicifuga to vomit it; if in the belly, take turmeric to purge it. Xuzhou says: If powdered alum tastes not astringent but sweet, and black beans eaten do not taste raw, one has been struck by golden-silkworm gu poison; at once boil pomegranate bark and root strong and drink it, and one will vomit up a worm. Li Huizhi says: Taking alum and bud tea in cold water cures poisoning. It is also said: Hedgehog skin should be used. Zhonglü says: The hedgehog can master golden-silkworm gu. Xuan says: In Guangxi, Yunnan and Guizhou they make gu at New Year and on the second of the second month and third of the third month, counting by the months; there are five kinds of gu, and for all, take three taels of birthwort, decocting one tael at a time, on an empty stomach, and if one does not vomit, take it again.

《飲食類·韭汁 Leek Juice》

1
○生取汁，能通竅化物，可除痞積胸痰。韭性不寒，又不壞人，但生汁辣耳。
○ Juice taken raw can open the orifices and dissolve things, and remove obstructions, accumulations and phlegm in the chest. The nature of leeks is not cold, and does not harm a person; only the raw juice is hot.

《飲食類·辨蕈毒 Detecting Poisonous Mushrooms》

1
○以燈心草和蕈煮，或以銀簪淬之，燈心與簪色黑，即有毒。
○ Boil the mushrooms with rush pith, or dip a silver hairpin in them; if the pith or the hairpin turns black, they are poisonous.

《飲食類·曬取瓜子法 Method of Drying Melon Seeds》

1
○楊梅核、西瓜子拌杮漆渣，曬乾，皆自開。西瓜子以面糊粘版上，日爆之，自然裂。中履曰：汴梁瓜子大，金井闌干；江西者小。
○ Waxberry stones and watermelon seeds mixed with persimmon-lacquer dregs and dried in the sun all open by themselves. Watermelon seeds stuck on a board with flour paste and exposed to the sun split open naturally. Zhonglü says: The melon seeds of Bianliang are large, [called] golden well railings; those of Jiangxi are small.

《飲食類·解松子油法 Method of Remedying Rancid Pine Nuts》

1
○松子仁油壞者，攤竹紙上焙之，如新。凡仁類皆用此法。
○ Pine-nut kernels that have gone rancid, spread on bamboo paper and roasted, are as if fresh. This method can be used for all kinds of kernels.

《飲食類·水沫 Water Foam》

1
○溪中水沫，取起令乾，為末，入湯中，即冷而不沸，食之則轉失氣。暄曰：以此沫調水，令人飲之，則瀉不止，多服甘草乃解。
○ Foam from a stream, taken out, dried and powdered, put into hot water makes it cold at once and it will not boil; eaten, it makes one break wind. Xuan says: If this foam is mixed with water and given to someone to drink, they will have unceasing diarrhoea, relieved only by taking much licorice.

1

○ Those drunk from eating too many lychees can be relieved by drinking water in which the shells have been soaked. The people of Yue use bitter melon, or honey, or salt.

《飲食類·解傷茶 Relieving Harm from Tea》

1
○飲茶多而腹脹者，以醋解之，或以姜豉。
○ Those whose bellies swell from drinking too much tea are relieved with vinegar, or with ginger and fermented beans.

《飲食類·炙不溢油法 Method of Roasting without Oil Dripping》

1

《飲食類·用鹽 Using Salt》

《飲食類·飲食禁忌 Prohibitions in Food and Drink》

Do not eat anything that has been kept long. If well water bubbles, do not take it. Do not drink wine or sauce that casts no reflection. The five-mineral powder, that is the cold-food powder, means that if one takes metals and minerals and eats hot food, the two heats provoke each other. "Mineral outbreak" is the outbreak of the poison of metals and minerals. Pei Xiu of the Jin took cold-food powder, and ought to have drunk hot wine, but drank cold wine and died — why? The Thousand Gold Prescriptions say: All food is taken cold, but wine must be warm. So the biography of Pei Xiu's saying that he ought to have drunk hot wine is also wrong. Sun Simiao said: Do not eat raw honey in the seventh month, for it causes sudden diarrhoea. Eating the blue-green, red and sour kind causes vexation of the heart. It must not be eaten with raw onions or lettuce, which causes diarrhoea. Eating salted fish after a full meal of honey causes sudden death. What, then, is one to make of Zhongshu and Dongpo sitting together all day eating bean curd and gluten soaked in honey? I once saw someone who ate honeyed things with rice; I cannot even take tea with honey. For bodies differ in disliking damp and disliking dryness. Xi Shuye said: Strong-smelling pungent foods harm the eyes, garlic most of all. Scallion whites must not be eaten with honey. Garlic and inula flowers damage the eyes. If someone taking atractylodes pills also eats garlic, their eyes grow dim at once. The hibiscus is the [?]-seed flower, and children must not play with it. Taken long, lovage causes sudden death, yet Daozhou and Yongzhou make it into tea snacks. Hong Mai said: Drinking schizonepeta decoction after eating yellow-cheek catfish brings instant death. Yellow croaker must not be eaten with buckwheat, for it makes one lose one's voice. Peaches must not be eaten with turtle. Chuogeng says that in eating puffer fish one must avoid schizonepeta, yet Zanning says puffer fish boiled with schizonepeta, changing the water three or five times, is free of poison — is this not contradictory? Birds that die of themselves with eyes unclosed, ducks with white eyes, birds with four spurs, and eggs marked with the character eight all kill people. Chengshi says: A turtle with hair under its belly, or with the characters five or ten under its belly, must not be eaten.

《飲食類·稻 Rice》

1
○稻以午時華，夜露自莖而上，入苞中，則含之而成實。種以百數，約則秥、粳、秫，秫即糯也。清明浸曬，種則同；然蒔耘之後有鐋抓法，又不用牛，則吳下泥沃也。南海田乘桴播刈，與湖湘之圩漫撒同法。高州一歲三種，赤道之下兩度春秋，則穀草隨之；北遠日地，有五穀不生者矣。粟亦有秥糯。往謂宋真宗向占城求早穀種，故曰秥。阮一閱詩話載唐人有紅蓮早稻之句，則唐有矣。香稻亦古有，非自外來也。吳下香粳七月熟，南京南鄉銀條米亦香，而香稻置雜稻倉，倉中皆香，是秈香也。種須肥耨，而結實少于常米，故民不種也。江右粒短，下江粒長。晚米長腰，遏黃曰冬舂，冬舂不生蟲，食之易化。八月熟曰觀音秈、菊花新、愛日黃、桃花米，有油潤紅曰紅蓮心，其燥者蝦須紅，烏稻殼黑米皮紅也，不可勝記。
○ Rice flowers at noon, and the night dew rises up the stem into the husk, which holds it and forms the grain. The varieties number in the hundreds, but in brief they are the non-glutinous, the japonica and the shu, shu being the glutinous. Steeping and drying the seed at Qingming is the same everywhere, but after transplanting and weeding there is the tang-harrow method, without the use of oxen, because the mud of Wu is rich. The fields of the South Sea are sown and reaped from rafts, the same method as the scattered sowing of the polders of Hu and Xiang. Gaozhou plants three crops a year, and under the equator there are two springs and autumns, so the grain and grasses follow them; in the far north, far from the sun, there are lands where the five grains do not grow. Millet too has non-glutinous and glutinous kinds. It used to be said that Emperor Zhenzong of the Song sought early rice seed from Champa, hence the name zhan. Ruan Yiyue's Remarks on Poetry records a line by a Tang poet about "red-lotus early rice," so the Tang already had it. Fragrant rice too existed in antiquity and did not come from abroad. The fragrant japonica of Wu ripens in the seventh month, and the silver-strip rice of the southern countryside of Nanjing is also fragrant; when fragrant rice is put in a granary with mixed rice, the whole granary becomes fragrant — this is fragrant indica. It must be planted in rich soil and weeded, and yields less than ordinary rice, so the people do not grow it. The grains of Jiangxi are short, and those of the lower Yangzi long. Late rice has a long waist, and that kept from yellowing is called winter-pounded; winter-pounded rice breeds no insects and is easily digested. Those ripening in the eighth month are called Guanyin indica, Chrysanthemum New, Sun-Loving Yellow and Peach-Blossom rice; the oily, glossy red one is called Red Lotus Heart, the dry one Shrimp-Whisker Red, and black rice has a black husk and red bran — more than can be recorded.

《飲食類·麥 Wheat》

1
○麥入土百四十日秀，秀後六十日成，蓋歷四時，兼寒熱之性也。大麥有須，小麥光。孟夏麥秋至，麥以此時而秋成也。與麻互為候：麥黃種麻，麻黃種麥。北麥凍枯而復發，故云南麥不可多食。北人非麵不飽。今治瘧忌面，然北人不忌，亦無妨。稻百日而麥倍之，天熱則易成也。稻米作飯，埋乃化蟲，而麥得溫即為蛾，以陽鬱易發也。
○ Wheat ears a hundred and forty days after going into the ground, and ripens sixty days after earing, for it passes through all four seasons and combines the natures of cold and heat. Barley has awns and wheat is smooth. In the first month of summer "the wheat's autumn arrives," because wheat ripens at this time as if in autumn. It and hemp serve as signs for each other: when wheat yellows, sow hemp; when hemp yellows, sow wheat. Northern wheat is frozen and withers and then sprouts again, hence the saying that southern wheat must not be eaten in quantity. Northerners do not feel full without wheat food. Nowadays wheat food is avoided in treating malaria, but northerners do not avoid it and come to no harm. Rice takes a hundred days and wheat twice as long; when the weather is hot it ripens easily. Rice cooked and buried turns into worms, while wheat becomes moths when warmed, because the yang is pent up and easily breaks out.

《飲食類·番豆 Foreign Beans》

Sun Mian says: Jiangxie is the foreign bean. Guo Lin says: Eaten raw they are oily and also cause diarrhoea.

《飲食類·瓜 Melons and Gourds》

1
○南瓜不結，於其根去地七尺，刀穿入磁片，則結實。冬瓜蔓，於日午苕帚打之，實多。摘下冬瓜，見日影則芽，觸苕帚風則爛，貓踏之則沙，切食所餘，糝灰不壞。又去瓤，納新菊花，封其口，久留如新，然必於所生之上下置之。視其灣弓貼肉者為母，留其子種之。此瓜俟其皮上白霜乃美，非經霜也。葉十去八。瓜生後以土安之，則大。甜瓜未熟，插鯗魚剌蒂上，經宿便熟。瓜傍煮漆，瓜死；瓠燒穰，亦死。種諸瓜，種以布包之，下浸以水，上覆以灰，晝近日，夜置溫所，方用而種。
○ If a pumpkin does not set fruit, pierce its root seven chi from the ground with a knife and insert a piece of porcelain, and it will fruit. If the vines of the wax gourd are beaten with a broom at noon, it fruits abundantly. When a picked wax gourd sees the sun's shadow it sprouts, touched by the wind of a broom it rots, and trodden on by a cat it goes mealy; when cut and partly eaten, the rest sprinkled with ash does not spoil. Also, remove the pulp, put in fresh chrysanthemum flowers and seal the opening, and it keeps long as if fresh, but it must be placed with the end that grew upward still up. The one with the bowed curve close to the flesh is the mother, and its seeds are kept for planting. This gourd is good only when white frost forms on its skin, not because it has been through frost. Remove eight of every ten leaves. After the gourds form, set them on earth, and they grow large. If a fishbone from dried fish is stuck into the stem of an unripe muskmelon, it ripens overnight. If lacquer is boiled near melons, they die; if gourds are burnt with straw, they too die. In planting all melons, wrap the seeds in cloth, soak them in water below and cover them with ash above, keep them near the sun by day and in a warm place by night, and then they are ready to plant.

《飲食類·西瓜 Watermelon》

1
○性惡香，忌麝，深畦遠離而種，一糞之間五日灌水，凡二水而一糞。河南子大，江西子小。建昌有紅瓤瓜，西昌有枕頭瓜。黃瓜，胡瓜也，有綠色、白色。竹匕開其根跗，納以大麥，則瓜生久而大。黃瓜出土，根上開花生瓜，有斑白長短種。熟瓜有青，有斑，有棱，有圓，有長，有金色、銀色。造架與依，則結體圓。絲瓜宜背陽向陰，或謂之紡線。
○ By nature it dislikes fragrance and avoids musk; plant it in deep beds far apart, water it every five days between manurings, two waterings to one manuring. The seeds of Henan are large, and those of Jiangxi small. Jianchang has red-fleshed melons, and Xichang has pillow melons. The cucumber is the hu melon, and comes in green and white. If the base of its root is opened with a bamboo spoon and barley put in, the melons grow long and large. The cucumber flowers on the root as soon as it comes out of the ground and bears fruit, and there are spotted, white, long and short kinds. Ripe melons are blue-green, spotted, ridged, round or long, gold-coloured or silver-coloured. If a trellis is built for them to lean on, the fruit grows round. The loofah should have its back to the sun and face the shade; some call it the spinning thread.

《飲食類·結瓠法 Methods of Fruiting Gourds》

1
○根以竹根分之，實多。瓢結時，剖藤跗，插巴豆，二三日後，瓢柔可紐，隨去巴豆，瓢復鮮活。又以筆蘸芥辣界瓢，其界處永不長，如刻成然。欲空其瓤，開瓠頂，納巴豆水，蝕去之。假成式曰：牛踐苖，則子苦。生壺盧時，盆水照之，則結者圓。虔州山以種苦瓠為業，其子亦充果食，如西瓜子。中通曰：今以牛糞澆瓠及胡蘆，皆苦，不可食，驢、馬、豕糞皆宜。
○ If the root is divided with a bamboo root, the fruits are many. When the gourd forms, split the base of the vine and insert a croton seed, and after two or three days the gourd is soft and can be twisted; then remove the croton, and the gourd becomes fresh and lively again. Also, if one draws lines on a gourd with a brush dipped in hot mustard, the lines never grow, as though carved. To hollow out its pulp, open the top of the gourd, put in croton water, and it eats it away. Duan Chengshi says: If oxen tread on the shoots, the fruit is bitter. When the bottle gourd is forming, if a basin of water reflects it, the fruit grows round. In the hills of Qianzhou they make a living by growing bitter gourds, and the seeds are also eaten as snacks like watermelon seeds. Zhongtong says: Nowadays gourds and bottle gourds watered with cow dung are all bitter and inedible; dung of donkeys, horses and pigs all suits them.

《飲食類·大胡蘆法 Method of Growing Large Bottle Gourds》

1
○方五尺，種四本，每兩近處，竹刮其半，交泥封，俟其活，除去一穗；復取此兩大本相併，如前法，則一斗之種變為一石。老長柄胡蘆，合萆麻子煮，乘軟結其柄，乾之，如生成。神隱曰：坑填油麻、菉豆葉及爛草，一重糞土，一重草。
○ In a square of five chi plant four plants, and at the near side of each pair scrape away half [the stems] with bamboo, join them, seal them with mud, and when they have taken, remove one shoot; then take these two large plants and join them in the same way, and seed of a dou becomes a shi. Old long-handled bottle gourds boiled with castor seeds have their handles knotted while soft, and when dried they look as if grown that way. The Shenyin says: Fill the pit with sesame and mung-bean leaves and rotten straw, a layer of manured earth and a layer of straw.

《飲食類·蔬法 Methods for Vegetables》

1
○治沃壤，必三糞鋤之。播子宜昧爽，易於勾萌。子須陳久，不癩。種于乾壤，而後灌水。墨娥錄曰：用桐油腳入糞內，一順攪均，澆菜根，橫蟲不生，或以山砒、蒼葉末散之。中覆曰：以油菜子之外皮作灰淹土，即不生蟲。
○ In preparing rich soil, one must manure and hoe it three times. Sowing should be done at dawn, when it sprouts easily. The seed must be old, and then it does not blight. Sow it in dry soil and then water it. The Record of Mo'e says: Put dregs of tung oil into the manure, stir evenly in one direction and water the roots of the vegetables, and pests will not breed; or scatter powdered mountain arsenic and cocklebur leaves on them. Zhongfu says: If the outer husks of rapeseed are burnt to ash and mixed into the soil, insects do not breed.

《飲食類·白菜 Bok Choy》

1
○七月種者，冬菜；九月杵淺坑種，四月收子榨油，名油菜。冬以雞鵝糞或芝麻莘覆根，則春盛。摘其薹心食之，枝遂旁發，結子繁衍。西江不摘薹，以不糞也。其曰羊角菜，似白菜而葉捲，性柔，四時宜種，榨油較多，霜蕤甚適口，春薹最肥。其曰黃矮菜，似白菜而短。蓮花白菜，似黃矮而色白，性柔葉密，九月播子，十月移種。寒心菜，似蓮花菜而葉又密。又有夏白頭菜、冬白頭菜。京中箭簳白，最肥大，宜蔬而子小，又夏種之，名夏菁菜。北地黃芽菜，則黃矮也。瓢兒菜，似夏菁菜，色微黃。菘菜莖闊白，葉端青，碎蹙而密。埤雅曰：以其凌冬如松，故名菘也。在北為蔓青，有根；在南為菘，為菁，無根。芥菜有大葉、碎葉、晚早、青紫之別，三月收子為芥醬，需堪榨油。其子白色，曰白芥菜。一頭旁發數頭者，曰百頭芥菜。似芥而色藍者，曰芥藍菜。其曰冬夏菾菜，則莙薘也。
○ That sown in the seventh month is the winter vegetable; that sown in shallow pits made with a pestle in the ninth month has its seeds collected in the fourth month and pressed for oil, and is called the oil vegetable. If its roots are covered with chicken or goose dung or sesame stalks in winter, it flourishes in spring. If the heart of the flower stalk is picked to eat, branches spring from the side and seeds multiply. In Xijiang they do not pick the stalks because they do not manure. What is called goat-horn vegetable resembles bok choy but has curled leaves and is soft; it can be planted in all four seasons, gives more oil when pressed, is very palatable after frost, and its spring stalks are the plumpest. What is called yellow dwarf vegetable resembles bok choy but is short. Lotus-flower white vegetable resembles the yellow dwarf but is white, soft and dense-leaved, sown in the ninth month and transplanted in the tenth. Cold-heart vegetable resembles the lotus-flower vegetable but its leaves are denser still. There are also summer white-head and winter white-head vegetables. The arrow-stem white of the capital is the plumpest and largest, good as a vegetable but with small seeds, and when sown in summer it is called summer jing vegetable. The yellow-sprout vegetable of the north is the yellow dwarf. The ladle vegetable resembles the summer jing vegetable and is slightly yellow. Song vegetable has broad white stems, leaves green at the tips, crinkled and dense. The Piya says: Because it braves the winter like the pine (song), it is called song. In the north it is the turnip, with a root; in the south it is song or jing, without a root. Mustard greens are distinguished as large-leaved and fine-leaved, late and early, green and purple; the seeds are collected in the third month for mustard sauce, and can be pressed for oil. Those with white seeds are called white mustard. One that puts out several heads beside one head is called hundred-head mustard. One resembling mustard but blue in colour is called Chinese kale. What is called winter and summer tian vegetable is chard.

《飲食類·萊菔 Radish》

○ If purple song is hoed with the dew on it, it breeds insects. For replanting, keep the heart shoots, remove the large leaves, turn the radish over, remove its fine roots, put it into the earth and heap soil round it, and it puts out new clumps; collect the seeds and sow them again, and they grow large. Those of Baixia have red skins, those of Jinxi are as big as a dou, and in Guyuan there are some of which a cart carries only two. Zhongtong says: Cut a large pear, remove the core like a jar, fill it with radish seeds, cover with the top of the pear and bury it in the earth; when the pear dries or rots, take out the radish seeds and sow them, and they will be round like pears and taste of pear.

《飲食類·頃刻菜法 Method of Instant Vegetables》

1
○久夫曰：用新雞首生子，從頂中小竅之，去黃白，納菜子，紙封極固，與雞伏四十九日，一雞伏不足，以他雞足數，取濕地種播之，須臾菜出。凡種瓜瓠皆如此。一法：三伏中曬瓜菜子，須雜麻莖內心播之，頃刻即出。曬一年長一寸，曬過三年即長三寸，若五七年即長五七寸。
○ Jiufu says: Take a new hen's first-laid egg, make a small hole in the top, remove the yolk and white, put in vegetable seeds, seal it very firmly with paper, and give it to a hen to brood for forty-nine days, making up the count with another hen if one is not enough; then sow the seeds in damp soil and the vegetables appear in a moment. All melons and gourds can be grown like this. One method: dry melon and vegetable seeds in the sun in the dog days, sow them mixed with the pith of hemp stalks, and they come up instantly. Dried one year, they grow an inch; dried for three years, three inches; for five or seven years, five or seven inches.

《飲食類·姜 Ginger》

1
○賈思勰齊民要術曰：種姜宜白沙地，少與糞和，熟耕如麻地，縱橫七遍尤善。三月種之，先種耬耩，尋壟下姜，一尺一科，令上土厚三寸，數鋤之。六月作葦屋覆之。九月掘出，置屋中。愚按：種姜亦時時發瘟，瘟則連畦皆壞，蓋秋熱則壞，姜增棚貯水可解。窖姜亦宜挑沙養之，窖中土濕則爛。姜葉曰冬葉，廣交以苞物，特久。
○ Jia Sixie's Essential Techniques for the Common People says: Ginger should be planted in white sandy soil with a little manure mixed in, tilled thoroughly as for hemp, preferably seven times lengthwise and crosswise. Plant it in the third month, first furrowing with a seed drill, setting the ginger along the ridges one plant to a chi, with three inches of soil on top, and hoe it several times. In the sixth month make a reed shelter to cover it. In the ninth month dig it out and put it in the house. The Foolish One notes: Ginger plants also often get blight, and when they do whole beds are spoiled, for autumn heat spoils ginger; adding a shed and storing water can remedy this. Ginger in cellars should also be kept in sand, for if the cellar earth is damp it rots. Ginger leaves are called winter leaves; in Guang and Jiao they are used to wrap things, which keep especially long.

《飲食類·菌栭 Mushrooms and Tree Fungi》

1
○羊肚、磨菰、天花、猴頭、雲南雞堫，皆菌菰類也。高腳傘頭，或紅黃白，背開細縫者，皆可椒瀹之。其背光者，朝菌，有夜光者，煮不熟者，湯照人無影者，殺人，以夏秋間毒蠱行吐涎生也。異苑言：交趾有菌，其葉塗人，舉體菌生，隨即腐爛，食之即笑不止，松葉解之。外紀：諳厄利亞有草名榝而多泥，人食之輒笑死。凡木耳曰栭，春生者薄，夏生者厚。馮應京曰：爛木及葉埋肥地，常澆米泔，即生。愚訉山野人：冬至斷大木，砍之，日澆淅水，至春生蕈，圓樹楮榆為上，椐柳亦可，雪凍則愈出。雷時生曰雷蕈，閩中以斧驚之，亦雷意也。中德曰：德外舅孫魯山言：宣大外邊榆肉最脆，榆上之癭也。中通曰：斷木細斫，爛草蒙而泔淋之，乃生蕈；埋糯稈，草覆，泔淋，則生蘑菰。孝陵松菌至美，得椒無畏。中履曰：菌栭濕蒸，性滑，陳之能治疥。榆肉出口外龍門所一帶，今燕京價至三十兩一斤。
○ Sheep's stomach, mogu, heavenly flower, monkey head and the jizong of Yunnan are all of the mushroom kind. Those with tall stems and umbrella heads, red, yellow or white, with fine slits opening underneath, can all be blanched with pepper. Those with smooth undersides, morning mushrooms, those that shine at night, those that do not cook when boiled, and those whose broth casts no reflection of a person kill people, because they grow from the slaver spat by poisonous creatures moving about in summer and autumn. The Garden of Marvels says: In Jiaozhi there is a mushroom whose leaves, smeared on a person, make mushrooms grow over the whole body, which rots at once; eaten, it causes unstoppable laughter, and pine needles cure it. The Record of the Outer Regions: In England there is a herb called sardonia, and those who eat it laugh themselves to death. All tree fungi are called er; those growing in spring are thin and those in summer thick. Feng Yingjing says: Bury rotten wood and leaves in rich soil and water them regularly with rice-washing water, and they grow. The Foolish One asked mountain folk: at the winter solstice they cut down large trees, hack them and water them daily with rice-washing water, and in spring mushrooms grow; round trees, paper mulberry and elm are best, and ju and willow will also do; with snow and frost even more come out. Those growing in thunder are called thunder mushrooms; in Min they startle them with axes, which also imitates thunder. Zhongde says: My father-in-law Sun Lushan said that elm flesh from outside Xuan[hua] and Da[tong] is the crispest; it is a gall on the elm. Zhongtong says: Cut wood into small pieces, cover with rotten straw and soak with rice-washing water, and mushrooms grow; bury glutinous-rice straw, cover it with straw and soak with rice-washing water, and mogu grow. The pine mushrooms of the Xiao Mausoleum are most delicious, and with pepper there is nothing to fear. Zhonglü says: Mushrooms and tree fungi are damp-steamy and slippery in nature; kept till old, they can cure scabies. Elm flesh comes from the area of Longmen beyond the passes, and now costs up to thirty taels a jin in Yanjing.

《飲食類·種山藥 Planting Chinese Yam》

《飲食類·苜蓿非藜 Alfalfa Is Not Goosefoot》

○ Song brocade was as thick as a coin; today Shu brocade compares with it, and Jiaxing imitates it, with woven gold, locked gold, shot colours and figured patterns. Fei Zhu's Manual of Shu Brocade lists eight-[?]-halo and six-[?]-halo brocades, large-medallion horse and ball brocade, and double-medallion cloud and goose brocade. The Records Made while Resting from the Plough list ginger-sprout, square arch, interlocked lozenge, pavilion and [?]-cattail brocades. The Western Ocean has wide single-piece brocade quilts. The ice-silkworm brocade of Mount Yuanqiao does not fear water or fire. The cave brocade of Chen and Yuan and the brocade of the Li people are cloth with raised pile. Xiangshan's poem: "Red sleeves weave damask, boasting of persimmon calyxes." Persimmon calyxes are its pattern. The ingenious products of the weaving rooms include kesi tapestry, pulled weave, cut velvet, gong thread and couched patterns. What is called jisun has five-coloured roundels; it was Yuan dress, and is now the dress of commandants. Shoe damask, pongee and tabby, floss pongee, gauze and leno are in common use. The crepe of Huzhou is called walnut gauze. The crepe of Nanjing does not crinkle; Hangzhou's therefore does. For autumn leno and crepe floss pongee, there is local pongee everywhere, but the pepper cocoons of Qing and Lai are the best, and do not stain with oil. Henan has coarse silk. The pongee of Tongcheng can be used to shroud corpses and lasts long. The silk of Jiaozhi is used raw and suits hot weather. The ice gauze of Nanjing is the ancient ice-white square-hole gauze. The Dongyang red cloud gauze comes from Zhangzhou; Dongyang is a person's name. For wool serge, the Gugu wool of Lanzhou is the best; its sheep are very large; camel wool is also used, and soiling oil comes out of itself; the next grade is called hejian. Liu Kesheng says: A nun of Lanzhou made it over many years; it is white and can take a hundred jin of safflower, and today all is imitation. Suofu comes from the country of Herat and is made of bird down; also written suofu with another character, in Guangdong it is made of calendered silk, which also raises cloud patterns. Poluo and saqalat are woven thick with wool pile, of the class of pulu and rugs. The fire-washed stone wool is made from the hair of the fire rat of the burning isles, or from saxaul wood or the unburnable herb.

《衣服類·綿花布類 Cotton Cloth》

1
○古惟枲布，老者衣帛。鄧潛谷曰：元入中國，非也。禹貢已著卉服，梁武用木綿帳，唐有木棉詩，陶九成記烏泥涇始盛耳。廣有木棉高樹，即斑枝花之大者，外域所謂吉貝也。吉終為白氎布。今洋來者闊白，而有厚薄二種。有彌牟絞布，甚狹，彼中編作耎床。棉花有白、紫、藍三色，紫者煖而耐久。嘉靖時，新安孝子方勵齋創製紫飛花布，雲間乃後仿者，因有兼絲納文。常州有東門闊，金齒有桐花布。
○ In antiquity there was only hemp cloth, and the old wore silk. Deng Qiangu says it entered China in the Yuan, which is wrong. The Tribute of Yu already records grass garments, Emperor Wu of the Liang used cotton curtains, and the Tang had poems on the cotton tree; Tao Jiucheng records only that it began to flourish at Wunijing. In Guang there is the tall kapok tree, the large kind of the speckled-branch flower, which foreign lands call kapas. Kapas finally became white die cloth. That which now comes from overseas is wide and white, and comes in thick and thin kinds. There is mimou twisted cloth, very narrow, which they weave into soft beds. Cotton comes in three colours, white, purple and blue, the purple being warm and durable. In the Jiajing period Fang Lizhai, a filial son of Xin'an, created purple flying-flower cloth, which Yunjian later imitated, and so there came to be silk-mixed couched patterns. Changzhou has the Dongmen broadcloth, and Jinchi has paulownia-flower cloth.

1

《衣服類·識葛法 Method of Identifying Kudzu Cloth》

1
○挼布聲響，水濕明亮者，真。梅葉洗蕉葛衣，不脆。暄曰：濕之亦響。會昌雙絲者，或以蕉，以紵，以麻兼，最難別識，須退開視之，雙線皆葛而嫩為上。
○ Cloth that rustles when rubbed and is bright when wet is genuine. Banana and kudzu clothes washed with plum leaves do not become brittle. Xuan says: When wet it also rustles. The double-thread kind of Huichang is sometimes mixed with banana, ramie or hemp, and is most hard to tell apart; one must pull it apart to look, and that in which both threads are kudzu and supple is the best.

《衣服類·裝核法 Method of Padding with Cotton》

1
○以絹作夾，木綿核之，先漿以杏仁，則綿不食絹。布衣裝綿，伏中無綿珠，秋冬則有。綿花置燈心少許，即無珠。于湖有槌絲綿如絨氈者，可作通裁，以杭絮表之。
○ When silk is made into a lined garment and padded with cotton, first starch it with apricot kernels, and the cotton will not eat into the silk. Cloth garments padded with cotton have no cotton pills in the dog days, but get them in autumn and winter. If a little rush pith is put in with the cotton, there are no pills. At Yuhu there is beaten silk floss like felt, which can be cut whole and faced with Hangzhou wadding.

《衣服類·花機 The Draw Loom》

1
○筘率千二百齒，度經過糊，齒有兼縷，紗經三千二百縷，綾經五千六百縷。古八十縷為升，今之綢殆六十升也。嘉湖出口出水乾絲為經，則任從提挈，不憂斷接。機長丈六，起花樓，掘地藏足，中托衢盤用千八百竹條，提花坐樓，以的杠卷絲，用兩疊助木尖插筘兩頭。疊助者，羅空在耎綜袞頭，兩扇結綜，一耎一堅，紗亦在袞頭制定也。織綾綢則去此兩扇，而用桄綜八扇。兩手交織曰縐紗。兩梭□一梭重曰秋羅。先染絲織者，鞋屯絹也。綾絹以浮輕見花，紗羅以糾緯見花。綾絹一梭一提，紗羅來梭提，往梭不提。其獨織者，浪梅小花，視素羅加桄二扇。潞油、小雲、溫州方錦，皆獨織者。倭鞋則斫綿夾藏經面，織過刮成黑光者也。白下仿倭鞋，先緯鐵絲而後刮之。蜀錦刻絲與通身盤龍盤枝，則截梭斗合，節節換法矣。機杼之巧，殆天工乎？治絲提緒，入星丁，乃由送絲簽以登大車。調絲用絡篤，過竹鉤為活頭焉。緯絡紡車既籰，有溜眼、掌扇、輕耙、印架，乃扱于筘中此非織筘，妙于交竹一度，然後過黍胡，用小粉麵筋洗下。其染紗者，用明膠刷之，曰清膠紗。偽重綢分兩者，或糖或粉。絲貴吳絲，日本皆市此。川、楚、閩、廣、齊、豫各有繭。
○ The reed has about twelve hundred dents; the warp is sized before being passed through, with several threads to a dent: a gauze warp has three thousand two hundred threads and a damask warp five thousand six hundred. In antiquity eighty threads made a sheng, so present-day silk is about sixty sheng. If silk dried by the "out of the mouth" and "out of the water" methods of Jia[xing] and Hu[zhou] is used for the warp, it can be pulled and lifted at will without fear of breaking. The loom is sixteen chi long, with a raised figure-tower and a pit dug in the ground for the feet, supporting in the middle the qu frame using eighteen hundred bamboo rods; the pattern is lifted by someone sitting in the tower, the silk is wound on the warp beam, and two beater-bars with tapered wood are inserted at both ends of the reed. As for the beater, the leno openings are at the head of the soft heddles, with two sets of tied heddles, one soft and one hard, and gauze too is fixed at the head of the roller. For weaving damask and silk, these two sets are removed and eight sets of shafts are used. Weaving with the hands alternating is called crepe. Two shuttles [?] and one shuttle heavy is called autumn leno. That woven from silk dyed beforehand is xietun silk. Damask and tabby show their patterns by light floats, gauze and leno by twisted wefts. For damask and tabby, each shuttle pass has a lift; for gauze and leno, the outgoing pass is lifted and the return pass is not. For those woven singly, such as wave-plum and small flowers, two shafts are added compared with plain leno. Lu oil, small cloud and the square brocade of Wenzhou are all woven singly. Japanese [satin] is woven with cut floss hidden between the warp faces and then scraped to a black gloss. The imitation Japanese [satin] of Baixia first has iron wires as weft and then is scraped. For Shu brocade, kesi and all-over coiled-dragon and coiled-branch patterns, the shuttle is cut and joined, changing the method section by section. The ingenuity of the loom — is it not almost the work of Heaven? In preparing silk, the ends are picked up and put through the star nails, then passed by the silk-feeding rod onto the great reel. For winding silk a winder is used, and passing over a bamboo hook makes the live end. When the weft has been wound on the spinning wheel and the reel, there are the sliding eye, palm fan, light rake and stamp frame, and then it is drawn into the reed this is not the weaving reed, with a clever crossing of bamboos once, after which it goes through the shuhu and small starch washed out from gluten is used. For dyed gauze, it is brushed with clear glue and called clear-glue gauze. Those who counterfeit heavy silk by weight use sugar or starch. Of silks, Wu silk is valued, and Japan buys it all. Sichuan, Chu, Min, Guang, Qi and Yu each have their own cocoons.

《衣服類·染紅 Dyeing Red》

1
○河水浸紅花，次日囊盛，洗去黃水，又溫洗之，又以豆萁灰淋水洗之，乃泡烏梅湯點槌烏梅與乾紅花等分。帛藉黃檗而染紅，或炒槐花入蘇木藉白下號喬紅。蘭絨洋布可受斤金之繎紅，入夜發光。福建胭脂亦重受紅花者。肇慶山崦小花叢生，含苞可作胭脂葉似藍，花似蓼。鄭虔曰：山榴花作胭脂。茜草染紫。又有樹茜，終葵子汁如胭脂，江北曰架菜子，後退色。紫礦、血竭為胡胭脂，礦出蟻壞，血竭其樹脂也。中通曰：凡紅色花皆可取汁作胭脂，但有深淺，如鳳仙染甲之類。杭州夜色紅用重受胭脂，碧用碧蟬藍胭脂，以朱砂、大青皆重，不可作夜色。蘇木蠹亦可作紫礦。朱虯庵曰：廣西太平府有紅草，可染紅。
○ Soak safflower in river water, the next day put it in a bag and wash out the yellow liquid, wash it again warm, then wash it in lye of bean-stalk ash, and then fix it with a steeping of smoked-plum water beaten smoked plums and dried safflower in equal parts. Silk is dyed red over a ground of phellodendron, or with fried pagoda-tree flowers and a ground of sappanwood in Baixia this is called Qiao red. Blue velvet and foreign cloth can take the gold-costly flame red, which glows at night. The rouge of Fujian also takes the safflower heavily. In the mountain hollows of Zhaoqing small flowers grow in clumps, whose buds can be made into rouge the leaves like indigo, the flowers like knotweed. Zheng Qian says: Rouge is made from mountain pomegranate flowers. Madder dyes purple. There is also tree madder, the juice of whose zhongkui seeds is like rouge; north of the Yangzi it is called jiacai seed, and later the colour fades. Lac and dragon's blood are the foreign rouge; lac comes from ant nests, and dragon's blood is the resin of a tree. Zhongtong says: Juice can be taken from all red flowers to make rouge, only deeper or lighter, like impatiens used to dye the nails. The Hangzhou night-colour red uses heavily-taken rouge, and the blue uses blue-cicada indigo rouge, because cinnabar and azurite are both heavy and cannot make night colours. Sappanwood borers can also make lac. Zhu Qiu'an says: Taiping prefecture in Guangxi has a red herb that can dye red.

《衣服類·雜染 Miscellaneous Dyes》

1
○黃槐，青靛，綠則合之，紫則青紅合，淺色則視輕重加減之。若椒褐、茶褐、荊茄色，有兼櫨黃者。墨水者，皂礬、五倍子，易毀布帛，今不用矣。梔子染黃，久而色脫，不如槐花，或用櫨蘗。紅莧菜煮生麻布，則色白如苧。荷葉煮布為褐色，布作荷香。櫸柳皮可染黑。不獨布也，沙尾鬘帽黃者白者，以櫸葉同煮即黑。一作櫃。凡梣、楓、樺、烏桕、檗、楊桐皆可染，染必加礬，不則入汙泥而黑矣。入泥者，其布易壞；染布而乾之，再入泥，則不壞布。茜紅以烏梅湯退，紅以石灰水退，後茜不失銖兩。虛舟曰：鴿糞煮黃絹，色變白，或以雞糞煮白之。儲糧藤似山藥，結實如小瓜，以之染葛作汗衫，則不近膚而爽，本草赭魁、鵝抱之類乎？今或以椑漆染麻衫，最爽汗。
○ Pagoda-tree [flowers give] yellow and indigo blue; green is made by combining them, purple by combining blue and red, and light colours by adding or reducing amounts as needed. Such colours as pepper-brown, tea-brown and thorn-eggplant include smoke-tree yellow. For ink-black, green vitriol and gallnuts are used, which easily damage cloth and silk, so they are no longer used. Gardenia dyes yellow, but the colour fades in time; it is not as good as pagoda-tree flowers, or smoke tree and phellodendron may be used. Raw hemp cloth boiled with red amaranth becomes white like ramie. Cloth boiled with lotus leaves turns brown and smells of lotus. Zelkova bark can dye black. Not only cloth: yellow and white shawei hair-hats boiled with zelkova leaves turn black. One version writes gui. Ash, maple, birch, tallow tree, phellodendron and yangtong can all dye, but alum must be added in dyeing, otherwise put into mud they turn black. Cloth put into mud is easily spoiled; if cloth is dyed and dried and then put into mud, it is not spoiled. Madder red is stripped with smoked-plum water, and [safflower] red with limewater, after which the madder loses not a jot. Xuzhou says: Yellow silk boiled with pigeon dung turns white, or it is whitened by boiling with chicken dung. The grain-storing vine resembles Chinese yam and bears fruit like small melons; kudzu cloth dyed with it and made into undershirts stands off the skin and is cool — is it of the same kind as the zhekui and e'bao of the Materia Medica? Nowadays some dye hemp shirts with persimmon lacquer, which is coolest against sweat.

《衣服類·各種污衣洗法 Methods of Washing Various Stains from Clothes》

1
○綠礬、百藥煎污衣，烏梅湯洗。槐花污衣，酸梅湯洗。楊梅、蘇木污衣，硫黃煙熏，然後洗之。染漆，以香油洗，更以蘿卜煩潤之。酒、醋、醬污衣，藕洗之。酒污衣，井水澆洗，日曬無痕。糞污衣，埋土內一時，取出水洗。蚊蠅糞污衣，燈心蘸水擦之。暄曰：黃泥污衣，生姜汁挼，以水擺之，又如常洗。各種衣亦有法：彩色衣，牛膠水浸半日，溫湯洗；皂衣，濃煎梔子湯洗；白衣，菖蒲未攪水擺之，又法蘿卜汁洗，皂角不及；洗蕉葛，清水揉梅葉汁洗，泡湯亦可；羅絹衣，皂廁溫湯洗，垢淨，復過別器另湯浸，又洗，其折浸不揉，反覆拍洗，帶水搭竿上，候水滴乾，乃展攤，與洗葛同法。
○ Clothes stained with green vitriol or fermented gallnut are washed with smoked-plum water. Clothes stained with pagoda-tree flowers are washed with sour-plum water. Clothes stained with waxberries or sappanwood are fumigated with sulphur smoke and then washed. Lacquer stains are washed with sesame oil and then rubbed repeatedly with radish. Clothes stained with wine, vinegar or soy paste are washed with lotus root. Clothes stained with wine are washed by pouring well water over them, and dried in the sun leave no mark. Clothes soiled with dung are buried in earth for a double-hour, taken out and washed with water. Clothes soiled by the droppings of mosquitoes and flies are rubbed with rush pith dipped in water. Xuan says: Clothes stained with yellow mud are rubbed with raw ginger juice, rinsed in water, and then washed as usual. Various clothes also have their methods: coloured clothes are soaked half a day in ox-glue water and washed in warm water; black clothes are washed in strong gardenia decoction; white clothes are rinsed in water stirred with powdered sweet flag, or washed with radish juice, which soap-pods do not equal; banana and kudzu cloth are washed in clear water with rubbed plum-leaf juice, or steeped in hot water; leno and silk clothes are washed in warm water with soap-pod, and when the dirt is gone, transferred to another vessel and soaked in fresh water, washed again, soaked folded without rubbing, patted and washed repeatedly, hung dripping on a pole until the water has dripped dry, and then spread out — the same method as for washing kudzu.

1
