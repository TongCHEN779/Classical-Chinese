《草木類 Plants and Trees》

《草木類·松 Pine》

1
○酉陽雜俎：命根遇石成偃蓋。蘇伯材去中根，留四傍以造之。香花有甘苦二種，皮薄而白者甘，皮黑者苦，巴豆瀉香，勿服也。沙漉茅蒸，酒煮數十次，如牽糖法，則白矣。五鬣、七鬣為一朵葉者，剔牙栝子松也；兩鬣而細，則常松也。栝子松膚青，內長而外遂浮脫，結一房數十葉，一葉球一子，子殼內有仁，甘美。滇南種子色黑，遼子色黃。其性喜乾，初種惡蟻。其闊瓣厚葉者，羅漢松也，樹老結實，長四五分，底平上銳，色紫黑，乾之味甘，補腎，其香益肺，本草綱目所未載也。高山石岩水松亦數丈，葉如簪，盤如菊而厚，夏結子如豆大，赭色，亦所未載也。南岳、天臺落葉松，一名金錢松。天台山亦有落葉松。本草水松，水中苔也。閩水松乃水楊之類。黃山、武功千年松，高寸許，石上草耳。中通曰：種松有流脂不止者，以大糞塗之，即止。中履曰：采臣言遼北松塔高數尺。履按：既未臘國松子如荔枝。
○ The Miscellany of Youyang: When the taproot meets rock, [the pine] forms a spreading canopy. Su Bocai removed the central root and kept the four side roots to produce this. Pine pollen is of two kinds, sweet and bitter; that with thin white bark is sweet and that with black bark bitter; croton purges the pollen, so do not take it. Filtered through sand, steamed with cogon grass and boiled in wine several dozen times, like pulling sugar, it becomes white. Those with five or seven needles in a bunch are the toothpick kuo-seed pine; those with two fine needles are the ordinary pine. The kuo-seed pine has a blue-green skin, growing inside while the outside floats loose and peels off; each cone has dozens of scales, each scale holds one seed, and inside the seed shell is a kernel, sweet and delicious. The seeds of southern Dian are black, and those of Liao yellow. By nature it likes dryness, and when first planted it hates ants. The one with broad, thick leaves is the arhat pine; old trees bear fruit four or five fen long, flat at the base and pointed at the top, purple-black, sweet when dried, tonifying the kidneys, and its fragrance benefits the lungs; this is not recorded in the Compendium of Materia Medica. The water pine of high mountain rocks is also several zhang tall, with leaves like hairpins, whorled like chrysanthemums but thick, bearing in summer seeds the size of beans, ochre-coloured; this too is not recorded. The larch of Nanyue and Tiantai is also called the golden-coin pine. Mount Tiantai also has larches. The water pine of the Materia Medica is a moss in water. The water pine of Min is of the water-willow kind. The thousand-year pines of the Yellow Mountains and Wugong, an inch or so high, are only grasses on the rocks. Zhongtong says: When a planted pine runs resin without stopping, smear it with night soil and it stops at once. Zhonglü says: Caichen says that the pine cones north of Liao are several chi high. I, Lü, note: The pine seeds of Java are like lychees.

《草木類·茯苓可種 Poria Can Be Cultivated》

1
○松上結苓，則下有茯。龜策傳曰：雨霽無風，夜燒兔絲，罩燭而火滅者，明即掘之。本草載泰華終南，而今貴雲南苓。堅折松之橛曰茯苓撥，錐刺地，不可拔者，掘之，離根皆生。近有棄松柴種苓者，留樁三尺，切茯苓置根下，其松氣液不上，則下注，五年遂如拳矣。中履曰：楓之豬苓，竹之雷丸，猶松之茯也，多年則堅，餘氣傳精，即此可信。暄曰：採者椿上揭皮，易開，內有白膜，則知下已結苓矣。
○ Where poria forms on a pine, there is fu below. The Biographies of Diviners says: After rain has cleared and there is no wind, burn dodder at night; where a candle covered over goes out, dig there the next morning. The Materia Medica records [poria] of Taihua and Zhongnan, but now that of Yunnan is valued. The stumps of firmly broken pines are called poria-sticks; prick the ground with an awl, and where it cannot be pulled out, dig, and [poria] grows all along the roots. Lately there are those who cultivate poria on discarded pine wood, leaving stumps three chi high and placing cut poria under the roots; the pine's vital fluids, not rising, flow downward, and in five years it is as big as a fist. Zhonglü says: The zhuling of the maple and the leiwan of the bamboo are like the poria of the pine; after many years they harden, the remaining qi transmitting the essence, and this is credible. Xuan says: Those who gather it peel the bark on the stump, which opens easily; if there is a white membrane inside, one knows that poria has formed below.

《草木類·柏 Cypress》

1
○埤雅曰：柏性指西，猶磁之指南也。擦柏葉小而岐。蓊柏即側柏。瓔珞、杉柏可寄枝。刺者，檜也。柏利於造舟。敝苓為合香用。宜以子種。
○ The Piya says: The cypress by nature points west, just as the lodestone points south. The cha cypress has small, forked leaves. The weng cypress is the flat cypress. The yingluo and fir cypress can be grafted. The prickly one is the juniper. Cypress is useful for building boats. Decayed [cypress] is used for compounding incense. It should be grown from seed.

《草木類·桐 Tung》

1
○剛桐，油桐也；襯桐，青梧桐也。宋詡謂二者作琴，非也。琴用白桐，乃泡桐也。世傳桐斷地脈，乃油桐也，油能殺蟲枯木。有多年結子者，有三年結子者。南方有赬桐、刺桐，泉州號刺桐城。海桐可為籓障，可接楊梅，別一種也。
○ Hard tung is the oil tung; chen tung is the green parasol tree. Song Xu's saying that the qin is made from these two is wrong. The qin uses white tung, which is the empress tree. The tung that is commonly said to cut the earth's veins is the oil tung, whose oil can kill insects and wither trees. Some bear seeds after many years, some after three years. The south has the red tung and the coral tree, and Quanzhou is called the City of Coral Trees. The sea tung can be used for hedges and can be grafted with waxberry; it is another kind.

《草木類·杉 Fir》

1
○杉煔不宜水壤，種之亦發，然挺茂不久，焦枯也。溫杉葉細，刺杉葉粗。一種桱木，似杉而硬。宜芒種時遇雨斬肄插之。廬山大林寶樹，即杉松也。［暄曰：閩有水杉，葉似垂柏，其材剝之可卷作甑盤等器，名曰卷木，甚輕。
○ Fir does not suit waterlogged soil; planted there it grows, but its tall luxuriance does not last, and it withers. The warm fir has fine leaves and the prickly fir coarse leaves. One kind, the jing tree, resembles the fir but is harder. At Grain in Ear, when it rains, cut shoots and plant them. The precious tree of the Great Forest on Mount Lu is the fir-pine. [Xuan says: Min has the water fir, with leaves like a weeping cypress; its wood can be peeled and rolled into steamers, trays and other utensils, and is called rolled wood; it is very light.

《草木類·楝樹 Chinaberry》

1
○楝為器不裂。聯子密種，則駢結如垣墻然，宜芒種時。有成紫花者。其子浸臘水，貯甕中，至夏月滌器，蠅永不近。取其子曬乾，雜冬舂米內，不生蛀蟲，居家必用。曰黃楝芽，可為木蓼。乘雅曰：獬豸食之，火獸也，喜其潔；蛟龍畏之，木蟲也，激其怒。
○ Utensils of chinaberry do not crack. If the seeds are planted close together they grow joined like a wall; it should be done at Grain in Ear. Some have purple flowers. If its seeds are soaked in twelfth-month water and stored in a jar, and utensils are washed with it in summer, flies never come near. If its seeds are dried and mixed with winter-pounded rice, it breeds no weevils; every household should use it. It is called yellow chinaberry shoot, and can be used as tree knotweed. The Chengya says: The xiezhi eats it, being a fire beast that loves its cleanness; dragons fear it, being wood creatures it provokes their anger.

《草木類·棕 Palm》

1
○栟櫚春種子，勿裁之。大早木盛時，春秋二分各裁六葉。其子未透而苞者，名曰木魚，肥稚堪菹，東坡云：贈我木魚三百尾。畏雞糞，經之則朽。
○ Sow hemp palm seeds in spring and do not trim them. When the trees are fully grown, trim six leaves each at the spring and autumn equinoxes. The seeds still enclosed in their sheaths are called wood-fish, plump and tender and fit for pickling; Dongpo says: "He gave me three hundred wood-fish." It fears chicken dung, which rots it.

《草木類·桑 Mulberry》

1
○春斷枝瘞之，生條，臘月移種，平其根，翦去，俟次年離跗四五寸翦去，後年亦如法翦，始成木。灌宜鬱棲，不宜糞。桑根下埋龜甲，則茂盛不蠹。壓桑條宜燥土，則根易生，濕則條爛矣。抱朴言桑為箕精，能助藥力。愚按：夏季取桑柘火，以木黃而綿，則火氣長，故利關節，拔氣奪邪也。惟燒肉忌桑柴。
○ In spring cut branches and bury them, and they put out shoots; in the twelfth month transplant them, level the roots and cut them back; wait till the next year and cut four or five inches above the base, and the year after cut likewise, and then it becomes a tree. It should be watered with compost, not with dung. If a tortoise shell is buried under the roots of the mulberry, it flourishes and does not get borers. Layering mulberry branches should be in dry soil, and then roots grow easily; if damp the branches rot. Baopu says that the mulberry is the essence of the Winnowing Basket and can strengthen drugs. The Foolish One notes: In summer fire is taken from mulberry and cudrania, because the wood is yellow and soft, so the fire qi lasts long; therefore it benefits the joints, draws out qi and expels evil. Only in roasting meat is mulberry firewood avoided.

《草木類·火蕉 The Fire Banana》

1
○日照火蕉，則其葉上脊縫一條無影，而兩傍垂翣者有影；月中則皆有影。燒釘釘之則茂，彼火性盛，故合日也。厭勝謂之辟火。千年則結棗，大如瓜。凡數十年而花，數十年而實。
○ When the sun shines on the fire banana, the line along the ridge of its leaves casts no shadow, while the drooping fans on either side do; in moonlight all cast shadows. If it is nailed with heated nails it flourishes, for its fire nature is strong and so accords with the sun. In apotropaic magic it is called fire-averter. After a thousand years it bears jujubes as large as melons. Every few decades it flowers, and every few decades it fruits.

《草木類·木蜜 Raisin Tree》

1
○名枳椇，木不宜近酒室，使酒味薄。材作枕，醉者易醒。語曰厲漢指頭，中州呼拐棗，一名槏構。
○ It is called zhiju; the tree should not be near a wine room, for it makes the wine thin. Its wood made into a pillow sobers the drunk easily. The saying calls it "the brute's fingers"; in the Central Province it is called crutch jujube, and it is also called qiangou.

《草木類·鹽敷樹 The Salt-Spread Tree》

1
○生五棓子之鹽敷樹，燒炭而火性不死，少頃於炭中復焰，故燒炭者簡去此木。虎被藥箭，尋遇此樹食之，則毒藥性解。後魏書云：勿吉國水氣咸凝，鹽生樹上。今按真臘國有咸平樹。山海經：橐山多糒。郭璞注：七八月穗有鹽粉，可酢羹。是五糒也。
○ The salt-spread tree on which gallnuts grow, when burnt to charcoal, its fire nature does not die, and after a moment flares up again in the charcoal, so charcoal burners pick out this wood. When a tiger is hit by a poisoned arrow, it seeks out this tree and eats it, and the poison is neutralized. The History of the Later Wei says: In the country of Wuji the watery vapours congeal salt, and salt grows on the trees. I now note that Cambodia has the salty-level tree. The Classic of Mountains and Seas: Mount Tuo has much bei. Guo Pu's commentary: In the seventh and eighth months its ears have salt powder, which can be used to sour broth. This is the wubei.

《草木類·糙葉樹 The Rough-Leaf Tree》

1
○物志謂之枯條，葉性澀，可以止滑，一名樸樹，其子可種，即白駁木，榆類也。葉沙磨器，細於木賊。蠟梅葉亦沙，可用。
○ The Record of Things calls it the withered strip; its leaves are astringent and can stop slipperiness; it is also called the pu tree; its seeds can be planted; it is the white-mottled tree, of the elm kind. Its leaves are sandy and polish utensils finer than horsetail. Wintersweet leaves are also sandy and can be used.

《草木類·蠟樹 The Wax Tree》

1
○水冬青葉細於女貞，五月養蠟子，七月收蠟，不宜盡采，至來年四月又得生子。其蠟曬乾，以越布蒙甑口，置蠟布上，置甑釜中，水沸蠟融，入器凝白，可為燭材。其滓以囊投熱油中，則蠟盡，油遂可為燭。凡養蠟子經三年，停亦三年，或伐木，旺生新條，養一年，停一年。江南有水蠟樹，則不似冬青，至冬落葉，又一種也。蠟燭用桕油者堅。又水槿葉細花黃，一名水椐，冬斬條插，易成大木，可以放蠟，即水蠟一類。中通曰：嶺北蠟樹初生葉近紅，老則皮節香。
○ The water holly has leaves finer than glossy privet; wax insects are raised in the fifth month and the wax gathered in the seventh; it should not all be gathered, so that in the fourth month of the next year it produces insects again. The wax is dried in the sun, Yue cloth is stretched over the mouth of a steamer, the wax is put on the cloth and the steamer set in a cauldron; when the water boils the wax melts into the vessel and congeals white, and can be used as candle material. The dregs, put in a bag and thrown into hot oil, give up all their wax, and the oil can then be made into candles. Wax insects are raised for three years and then rested for three; or the trees are felled and put out vigorous new shoots, and are raised one year and rested one year. Jiangnan has the water wax tree, which does not resemble the holly, and sheds its leaves in winter; it is another kind. Wax candles made with tallow-tree oil are hard. Also, the water hibiscus, with fine leaves and yellow flowers, also called water ju, if branches are cut and planted in winter, easily becomes a large tree, on which wax insects can be put; it is of the water wax kind. Zhongtong says: The wax trees north of the Ling have nearly red leaves when young, and when old the bark and nodes are fragrant.

《草木類·桕 Tallow Tree》

1
○烏桕宜接。其子外白膜，蒸鎔之，凝為燭材。其子內肉杵榨之為清油，然不宜食，以之逐水則可。堅木入水不朽。
○ The Chinese tallow tree should be grafted. The white membrane outside its seeds is steamed and melted, and congeals into candle material. The flesh inside its seeds, pounded and pressed, gives a clear oil, which is not fit to eat but can be used to purge water. Hard woods do not rot in water.
2
○墨是可有堅木曰則獨鹿，入水千年不朽。利未亞堅木有文，入水土不壞。勿榒祭亞之國，城建於海中，亦以千年木為樁。約皆椰桄類也。顧玠曰：初栽椰，用鹽一二斗置根下，其木最堅。偲儡木漬鹽水中，百年不朽。中通曰：穆公云：勃露有樹生脂膏，極香烈，名拔爾撒摩，傅諸傷損，一日夕則肉復合，塗痘不瘢，以塗屍，千年不朽壞。
○ Mexico has a hard wood called zedulu, which does not rot in water for a thousand years. Libya has a hard wood with patterns which does not rot in water or earth. The city of Venice is built in the sea, also on piles of thousand-year wood. These are all roughly of the coconut and sugar-palm kind. Gu Jie says: When first planting coconut, put one or two dou of salt under the roots, and the wood becomes very hard. Sileiwood soaked in brine does not rot for a hundred years. Zhongtong says: Mugong says that in Peru there is a tree that produces a resin, extremely fragrant and strong, called balsam; applied to wounds, the flesh closes again in a day and night; smeared on pox it leaves no scars, and smeared on corpses keeps them from rotting for a thousand years.

《草木類·榆 Elm》

1
○榆長則皮脫，甚潔，其材綿而堅，其汁滑。有倒垂者。椐榆則枌屬也。北方食其莢。有小樹似烏飯，葉而背白，潤其紋可照，啖之最滑，如犁頭草，嶺北號曰小榆。盧氏曰：南方無榆而有杉。愚按：無止方之大榆耳，楖榆、山榆皆有。山榆即楩，其實曰蕪荑。
○ As the elm grows its bark peels off and it is very clean; its wood is soft yet hard, and its sap slippery. Some are weeping. The ju elm is of the fen kind. In the north they eat its samaras. There is a small tree like the wufan, with leaves white beneath, whose lines when moistened can reflect; eaten it is very slippery, like pear-head grass; north of the Ling it is called little elm. Master Lu says: The south has no elm but has fir. The Foolish One notes: It lacks only the great elm of the [north]; the ji elm and mountain elm are both found. The mountain elm is the pian, whose fruit is called wuyi.

《草木類·梓楸 Catalpa and Qiu》

1
○梓種於林，則令諸木內拱。斷根瘞之，遂長條，枚取以分種。或曰：檟即檟也。爾雅：大而皵，楸；小而皵，檟。臘月種之。材理有雲朵紋。物理所云：梓木為柱，在下則有聲，云木爭位。又曰：梓木為舟，起蜃，造舶不用。
○ If catalpa is planted in a forest, it makes all the other trees bow inward. Cut roots buried put out long shoots, which are taken one by one and planted separately. Some say: The jia is the jia. The Erya: Large with rough bark is the qiu; small with rough bark is the jia. Plant it in the twelfth month. The wood has cloud-patch grain. The Studio of the Principles of Things says: When catalpa is used for columns and placed lowest, there is a sound, said to be the trees contending for position. It also says: Boats of catalpa raise mirages, so it is not used for building ships.

《草木類·楠 Nanmu》

1
○楠宜臘月種子。赤者材堅，子白者材脆。年深向陽，結成旋紋者，曰斗柏楠。又一種曰石楠，可辟白蟻。
○ Nanmu should be sown in the twelfth month. Those with red seeds have hard wood, and those with white seeds brittle wood. That which over many years facing the sun forms spiral patterns is called dou-cypress nanmu. Another kind is called photinia, which can ward off termites.

《草木類·栩 Xu Oak》

1
○栩即柞櫟，伐則灌生，而葉大可包物也。其斗為染皂材，俗名黃慄，可為腐。夏統採梠，杜甫食橡，皆一物也。又或書為櫧，而後分之櫧平聲。
○ The xu is the zuo oak; when felled it grows as a bush, and its leaves are large enough to wrap things. Its cupules are material for dyeing black; it is commonly called yellow chestnut, and can be made into curd. Xia Tong's gathering of lü and Du Fu's eating of acorns refer to the same thing. It is also sometimes written zhu, which was later distinguished as zhu level tone.

《草木類·櫧 Zhu》

1
○小葉似茶，木色似楠，少黑，結實小，狀似栗，救蝟自裹，味有甘苦二種，可作腐食，材最堅，白蟻不能嚙。中履曰：江北呼圓櫧，金陵人呼為椐。
○ Small leaves like tea, wood coloured like nanmu but slightly black, bearing small fruit shaped like chestnuts, wrapped like a hedgehog; the taste is of two kinds, sweet and bitter, and it can be made into curd to eat; the wood is hardest of all, and termites cannot gnaw it. Zhonglü says: North of the Yangzi it is called round zhu, and people of Jinling call it ju.

《草木類·楮 Paper Mulberry》

1
○楮易生，其汁最黏，其實補人，其皮作紙，一名穀，或呼為構。舊說碓葉無椏。愚者曰：葉肥則圓，瘦則椏，然一樹而有椏圓，何邪？當以結實皮白為雌，皮斑無實為雄。或呼穀桑。中通曰：楮子、麻子同撒，秋冬留麻為楮作煖，來春刈麻，放火燒之，楮乃盛發，臘月斫皮，其梗可薪。
○ The paper mulberry grows easily; its sap is very sticky, its fruit nourishing, and its bark is made into paper; it is also called gu, or gou. The old saying is that the dui has unforked leaves. The Foolish One says: When the leaves are fat they are round and when thin forked; but how can one tree have both forked and round? One should take those bearing fruit with white bark as female, and those with spotted bark and no fruit as male. Some call it grain-mulberry. Zhongtong says: Sow paper-mulberry and hemp seeds together; in autumn and winter leave the hemp to keep the paper mulberry warm, and the next spring cut the hemp and burn it, and the paper mulberry flourishes; in the twelfth month cut the bark, and the stems can be used as firewood.

《草木類·楓 Sweetgum》

1
○分根側條種。脂為白膠香。忽生瘤，為楓人。深山傳有不落葉之楓，則樹似楓而開椏者，其中仍多兩椏，或不開椏者。韓雨公曰：楓松皆可造乳者，鑽入蓖、巴菽，可令成癭。張程曰：箕峰有楓，開兩岐紅花，其葉莖亦紅。
○ It is planted by dividing side shoots from the roots. Its resin is white-glue incense. When it suddenly grows burls, they are maple men. It is said that deep in the mountains there are sweetgums that do not shed their leaves; these are trees like the sweetgum with forked leaves, mostly with two forks, or unforked. Han Yugong says: Both sweetgum and pine can be made to produce burls; drill holes and put in castor or croton, and they form galls. Zhang Cheng says: At Jifeng there is a sweetgum with red flowers in two forks, whose leaves and stems are also red.

《草木類·槐 Pagoda Tree》

1
○材堅。其芽可芼。結子角如豆，可種。其花蕊和灰蒸之，供染。初生槐弱，以麻脅之。其倒垂槐曰龍爪槐，枝脆，靈谷寺門者是也。爾雅注：葉大而黑者曰欀槐，葉晝聶合而夜炕布者曰守宮槐。雜記曰：老槐生火。膠撓水則清。
○ The wood is hard. Its buds can be eaten as greens. It bears pods like beans, which can be planted. Its flower buds, steamed with ash, are used for dyeing. When first planted the pagoda tree is weak and is supported with hemp. The weeping pagoda tree is called dragon-claw pagoda tree, with brittle branches; that at the gate of Linggu temple is one. The commentary on the Erya: That with large black leaves is called the xiang pagoda tree, and that whose leaves fold together by day and spread out at night is called the palace-guard pagoda tree. The Miscellaneous Records say: Old pagoda trees produce fire. Its gum stirred in water clears it.

《草木類·椿 Toon》

1
○樗櫟疏而臭，其葉脫處有白，故樗蒲錦以此稱之。椿木實，色赤而葉香，芽可茹，亦可生食。根側分小木種之。其材為船底，不利為船。臘則稱。為梁，燕不來。虛舟子曰：以柏作東戶，而樁楣其上，則爭位有聲。
○ The tree-of-heaven and the oak are loose-grained and smelly; where its leaves fall a white mark remains, hence the chupu brocade is named after it. The toon has solid wood, red in colour, with fragrant leaves; its shoots can be eaten, raw as well. It is planted by dividing small trees from beside the roots. Its wood is used for ship bottoms, but is not good for boats. The twelfth month is suitable [for felling]. Used as a beam, swallows do not come. Xuzhouzi says: If the east door is made of cypress and toon is used for the lintel above it, they contend for position with a noise.

《草木類·楊柳 Poplar and Willow》

1
○喬者楊，弱者柳。楊有青、白、赤，赤近水生，根須可栵以護壖堤。柳有大葉、長葉，倒垂者曰宮柳。臘月斬大乾，燔下焦而深插之，梯則歲可髡而復條肄也。諺云：根宜焦，埋到腰，一株死，有百株。楊青白髡頭，赤須長，膚青白者材可雕刻，又名櫸。杞柳白，皂葉亦似楊，材供剞劂。櫸葉染皂，材中切版。
○ The tall one is the poplar, the weak one the willow. Poplars are blue-green, white or red; the red grows near water, and its roots can be planted in rows to protect banks and dikes. Willows have large leaves or long leaves; the weeping one is called palace willow. In the twelfth month cut large dry [poles], scorch the lower end and plant them deep, and the ladders can be pollarded every year and sprout again. The proverb says: "Scorch the root,
bury it to the waist;
where one dies,
there are a hundred." Poplars are pollarded when blue-green and white, and have long red whiskers; those with blue-white skin have wood that can be carved, also called zelkova. The basket willow is white, and the black-leaved kind also resembles the poplar; its wood is used for engraving. Zelkova leaves dye black, and the wood is fit for cutting blocks.

《草木類·柘 Cudrania》

1
○柘葉可飼蠶，其絲為弦，清響。隔年不採者，春再生，必毒蠶，故夏月宜擊脫之。柘木以醋調礦灰塗之一宿，作問道烏木。
○ Cudrania leaves can feed silkworms, and its silk makes strings with a clear ring. Those not picked for a year sprout again in spring and are sure to poison the silkworms, so in summer they should be beaten off. Cudrania wood smeared overnight with quicklime mixed with vinegar makes imitation ebony.

《草木類·樟樹 Camphor Tree》

1
○春易葉，初秋復發葉，子見霜黑，可作香。韶州鋸之升腦，江右名豫章而不取腦，其土別乎？豫章老則出火自焚，種不宜近家室。矮腳樟即烏藥，冬不凋，結紅子，名雪裡紅。
○ It changes its leaves in spring and puts out leaves again in early autumn; the seeds turn black after frost and can be used as incense. In Shaozhou they saw it to sublime camphor, while in Jiangxi it is called yuzhang and camphor is not extracted — is the soil different? Old camphor trees produce fire and burn themselves, so they should not be planted near houses. The dwarf camphor is lindera; it does not wither in winter and bears red berries, called red-in-the-snow.

《草木類·漆 Lacquer》

1
○漆不取液則自斃，惟霜降後取者良。樊重先為人嗤，後得其用者也。種宜臘月。朝鮮國有黃漆樹，似棕，六月取汁，漆物如金。今廣漆則黃，江漆則黑。中德曰：舊說五更露中取漆，可以多得，日出則無漆矣。凡取漆者耗神。
○ If the lacquer tree is not tapped it dies of itself; only that tapped after Frost's Descent is good. Fan Zhong was first laughed at, and later got the use of it. It should be planted in the twelfth month. Korea has the yellow lacquer tree, like a palm; its sap is taken in the sixth month and lacquers things like gold. Now Guang lacquer is yellow and Jiang lacquer black. Zhongde says: The old saying is that lacquer tapped in the dew at the fifth watch yields more, and after sunrise there is no lacquer. All who tap lacquer exhaust their spirits.

《草木類·草木膠 Plant Glues》

1
○金漆、椑漆之外，有貓頭刺汁以粘雀，雞腸草汁以黏蟬，芨、楮、榆皮、滑條皆黏。粵有石葦，如萵苣而肥厚，邊有鋸刺，女以膠髮。又有霸王鞭刺，作籬，斷汁甚濃，猶澤漆、茹蕳、薜荔之斷汁也。蜀羊泉，即漆姑，野蜂采汁為巢蒂者。外域塊香，皆樹汁也。樹汁皆可煉膠。中通曰：入漆者油其手，合粘者水中為之。
○ Besides gold lacquer and persimmon lacquer, there is the sap of cat's-head thorn for catching sparrows and the sap of chickweed for catching cicadas, and bletilla, paper mulberry, elm bark and slippery strips are all sticky. Yue has a pyrrosia like lettuce but fat and thick, with saw-tooth edges, which women use to glue their hair. There is also the overlord's-whip thorn, made into hedges, whose sap when cut is very thick, like the cut sap of sun spurge, rujian and creeping fig. Shuyangquan is lacquer-aunt, whose sap wild bees gather to make the stems of their nests. The lump incenses of foreign lands are all tree saps. All tree saps can be refined into glue. Zhongtong says: Those working with lacquer oil their hands, and gluing is done in water.

《草木類·皂角 Honey Locust》

1
○肥皂子短而肥，色紫。皂角長如刀，色黑，木有長刺，名天丁。豬牙皂小如豬牙，色黑。香皂子圓小而香，色白。四木形葉不相似，惟子氣味同。取皂以篾箍樹，加楔之，則皂角盡落。中履曰：皂角不結實，則鑿孔入鐵五斤，泥封之，次年實倍。悟知皂角入鍋爆落，固相感相制而用也。
○ Soapberries are short and plump and purple. Honey-locust pods are long like knives and black, and the tree has long thorns called heavenly nails. Pig-tooth soap pods are small like pigs' teeth and black. Fragrant soap seeds are small, round and fragrant, and white. The four trees do not resemble each other in form or leaf; only their seeds are alike in smell and taste. To take the pods, bind the tree with bamboo hoops and drive in wedges, and all the pods fall. Zhonglü says: If a honey locust bears no fruit, chisel a hole, insert five jin of iron and seal it with mud, and the next year the fruit doubles. I realized that honey-locust pods put into a pan burst and fall, certainly used by mutual influence and control.

《草木類·黃楝頭 Yellow Chinaberry Head》

1
○黃楝頭樹分葉如椿，大者合抱，春采其葉，味苦而甘，一名回味，可治痢，而治霍亂尤效，皮可合香，本草綱目遺此未收。俗訛呼黃連頭。
○ The yellow chinaberry head tree has divided leaves like the toon, the large ones a full embrace; its leaves are gathered in spring, bitter yet sweet, also called "returning flavour"; it can cure dysentery and is especially effective for cholera; its bark can be used in incense; the Compendium of Materia Medica omits it. It is commonly miscalled yellow-coptis head.

《草木類·椒 Sichuan Pepper》

1
○椒氣下達，無花而實，精勝在膚。須燒地潑醋，隔紙鋪椒，以盆覆之，謂之出汗。孟詵曰：五月食椒，令人多忘。而生地椒紅丸服三年，則精自秘，吳猛有訣，局方有詩證之。但今江廣土椒閉氣，川椒口白，其目黑光如漆。有蔓生者，爾雅所謂椒榝醜梂也。秦椒大如指，開六出，白光，檓也。胡椒出番國，亦係蔓生，有白色者，或曰即畢澄茄。酉陽言胡椒結子相對，葉晨開暮合，合則卷其子於葉中。中通曰：椒喜麻糝、灰糞土，忌水浸根，以發纏樹根，或種白芷、生菜，皆避蛇食椒。中履曰：中秋後晴天帶露收椒，陰一日，曬三日，則紅而裂。
○ The qi of pepper reaches downward; it fruits without flowering, and its essence lies in the skin. One must burn the ground, sprinkle vinegar, spread the pepper on paper over it and cover it with a basin; this is called sweating. Meng Shen says: Eating pepper in the fifth month makes one forgetful. But taking pills of raw rehmannia and red pepper for three years makes the essence retained by itself; Wu Meng had a formula, and the Bureau Prescriptions have a poem to prove it. But nowadays the local pepper of Jiang and Guang blocks the qi, while Sichuan pepper has white mouths and black glossy eyes like lacquer. There is a vine-growing kind, what the Erya calls the jiao-sha's chou qiu. Qin pepper is as large as a finger, opens six-lobed, white and glossy; it is the hui. Black pepper comes from foreign lands, also a vine, and there is a white kind; some say it is cubeb. Youyang says that black pepper bears seeds in pairs, its leaves open in the morning and close in the evening, and when closed wrap the seeds within the leaves. Zhongtong says: Pepper likes hemp dregs and ash-dung earth and avoids water soaking its roots; wind hair round the roots, or plant angelica or lettuce, and both keep snakes from eating the pepper. Zhonglü says: After mid-autumn gather pepper on a fine day with the dew on it, keep it in the shade for a day and sun it for three, and it turns red and splits.

《草木類·梅 Plum》

1
○有鶴頂梅、消梅、冠城梅，皆實大，五月熟時梅，有六月熟者。早梅四月熟。冬梅花白實小。有玉蝶梅、綠萼梅、紅梅，再接即重臺。春分前、秋分後接，用桃杏體及本體。紅梅白葉，一花三子，曰品字梅。紫梗疏條。蠟梅別一種，其磬口者佳。芒種時種其子。
○ There are crane-crest plums, xiao plums and guancheng plums, all with large fruit, ripening in the fifth month as seasonal plums, and some ripening in the sixth month. Early plums ripen in the fourth month. Winter plums have white flowers and small fruit. There are jade-butterfly plums, green-calyx plums and red plums, which grafted again become double-flowered. They are grafted before the spring equinox or after the autumn equinox, on peach or apricot stock or their own stock. The red plum with white leaves, with three fruits to a flower, is called pin-character plum. Purple stems and sparse branches. Wintersweet is another kind, of which the chime-mouth is best. Its seeds are sown at Grain in Ear.

《草木類·桃 Peach》

1
○金桃金色，上有朱點。銀桃白。王母桃、半斤桃、鷹嘴桃、綿桃皆甘。其矬者曰瑞香桃。皮黑肉脂曰墨桃。無毛曰杏桃。有匾者。緋桃正紅。孩兒桃肉紅。瑪瑙桃紅白間。碧桃白。皆有千葉，千葉者不實。春分前、秋分後接於本體。畏水。十年脂凝而枯，刀劙而流之，又延十年。深山有山桃，四月花。燕有玉桃，則實似櫻桃而肉紅色者。外域有桃大如斗者，其核充蟠桃核。謝肇浙曰：黔中日月桃，一枝分紅白。
○ The golden peach is gold-coloured with red spots. The silver peach is white. The Queen Mother peach, half-jin peach, eagle-beak peach and cotton peach are all sweet. The dwarf one is called daphne peach. That with black skin and fatty flesh is called ink peach. The hairless one is called apricot peach. There are flat ones. The crimson peach is true red. The baby peach has red flesh. The agate peach is red and white mixed. The jasper peach is white. All have double-flowered forms, and the double-flowered ones do not fruit. They are grafted on their own stock before the spring equinox or after the autumn equinox. They fear water. After ten years the gum congeals and the tree withers; slit it with a knife to let it flow, and it lives another ten years. Deep in the mountains there are mountain peaches, flowering in the fourth month. Yan has the jade peach, whose fruit is like a cherry with red flesh. Abroad there are peaches as large as a dou, whose stones pass for the stones of the immortal peach. Xie Zhaozhe says: The sun-and-moon peach of Qianzhong has red and white on one branch.

《草木類·李 Plum (Li)》

1
○有御黃李、金沙李、蜜李、青脆李、白李、紫李、早李、晚李、苦李。李直方以綠李為首，號曰嘉慶。今嘉興有醉李，古云槜李，以是名邪？分根種接與桃同。壅宜豕通。埤雅云：性頗難老，老雖棘枯，子亦不細。大名府南樂縣有枝頭幹，如李而干熟於樹。肇慶有沙棠果，則如李而無核者也。徐勃曰：惟李核無仁，古謂王戎鑽李核，殆冤。
○ There are imperial yellow plums, golden-sand plums, honey plums, green crisp plums, white plums, purple plums, early plums, late plums and bitter plums. Li Zhifang put the green plum first and called it Jiaqing. Now Jiaxing has the drunken plum, anciently called zui li — was it named after this? Dividing roots, planting and grafting are the same as for the peach. It should be banked with pig dung. The Piya says: By nature it ages with difficulty, and though old and thorny and withered, its fruit is not small. Nanle county in Daming prefecture has the zhitougan, like a plum but drying ripe on the tree. Zhaoqing has the shatang fruit, which is like a plum without a stone. Xu Bo says: Only plum stones have no kernel, so the ancient story of Wang Rong drilling plum stones probably wronged him.

《草木類·櫻桃 Cherry》

1
○深紅紫者早熟，淺紅遲熟，曰蠟桃。月令仲夏含桃薦寢廟，則早熟也。枝節間有根須下垂，春前插之。子漿時得糞氣，則易熟。潤州櫻桃核而不仁。其曰脆櫻桃，則郁李也。信陽州有半紅白者，曰瑪瑙櫻桃。
○ The deep red and purple ripen early, and the pale red ripen late and are called wax peaches. The Monthly Ordinances' offering of hantao in the ancestral temple in midsummer means they ripen early. Root whiskers hang down from between the nodes of the branches, and they are planted before spring. When the fruit is juicy, if it gets the qi of dung it ripens easily. The cherries of Runzhou have stones without kernels. What is called the crisp cherry is the bush cherry. Xinyang prefecture has half red and white ones, called agate cherries.

《草木類·柰林檎頻婆榅桲 Nai, Crab-apple, Pinpo and Quince》

1
○柰有紅、黃、白，北方呼火刺賓。陳子良曰：大長者柰，圓者林檎。一名來檎，俗名花紅，紅多曰海紅。頻婆果似花紅而大，燕地如拳，可留至夏。此種秋分後接以棠梨，春以草稕懸於枝端。樹易生蠹，秋前藏木杪可剖之，過時則入根跗矣。關陝榅桲木如林檎，鐵梗棠而花白綠色，其實似樝，但膚慢而多毛。慶陽楸子似柰而小。同林檎搗汁，熬為果單。
○ The nai is red, yellow or white, and in the north is called halibin. Chen Ziliang says: The large and long are nai, the round are linqin. It is also called laiqin, and commonly flower-red; that with much red is called sea-red. The pinpo fruit is like the flower-red but larger, in Yan as large as a fist, and can be kept till summer. This kind is grafted on birchleaf pear after the autumn equinox, and in spring straw is hung on the ends of the branches. The tree easily gets borers, which before autumn are hidden in the treetop and can be cut out, but after that time they enter the base of the roots. The quince of Guan and Shaan has a tree like the crab-apple, iron stems like the tang, and white-green flowers; its fruit is like the hawthorn, but the skin is loose and very hairy. The qiuzi of Qingyang is like the nai but smaller. Pounded into juice together with crab-apples and boiled, it makes fruit leather.

《草木類·枇杷 Loquat》

1
○以葉長而名。接則核少，不接者壽。壅宜水淋淡草灰，不宜糞穢。向以為盧橘，按相如賦盧橘與枇杷並稱，則非一物矣。肇慶優曇缽似枇杷，無花而實。粵閩黃彈則似枇杷而無核。枇杷核能去霉垢，故能化痰。
○ It is named for its long leaves. Grafted, it has few stones; ungrafted, it lives long. It should be banked with water-leached weak grass ash, not with dung. Formerly it was taken to be the lu orange, but [Sima] Xiangru's rhapsody mentions lu oranges and loquats side by side, so they are not the same. The udumbara of Zhaoqing resembles the loquat, and fruits without flowering. The wampee of Yue and Min resembles the loquat but has no stone. Loquat stones can remove mould and grime, so they can dissolve phlegm.

《草木類·楊梅 Waxberry》

1
○紫者，楊誠齋所謂玉肌半醉生紅粟、墨暈微深染紫裳者也。白者，楊州所呼聖僧也。春分前接，用海桐。壅宜水淋淡草灰。吳下者大。贊寧曰：楊梅核碎必八塊。北戶錄曰：潘州有白楊梅。俗以火酒浸之。
○ The purple ones are what Yang Chengzhai described as "jade flesh half-drunk grows red millet,
ink halo slightly deep dyes a purple robe." The white ones are what Yangzhou calls holy monks. They are grafted before the spring equinox on sea tung. They should be banked with water-leached weak grass ash. Those of Wu are large. Zanning says: A waxberry stone when broken always gives eight pieces. The Record of the Northern Doors says: Panzhou has white waxberries. They are commonly steeped in distilled liquor.

《草木類·蔗 Sugarcane》

1
○容齋五筆曰：蔗有四色。廣川一種，數年生，如大樹，長丈餘。赤者名昆崙蔗，嶺以北皆種之。曼倩神異經云：甘蔗節蛔蟲，即甘蔗。生汁寒，煎糖熱，少食糖水甘養胃，多食則動濕熱。
○ The Fifth Series of Notes from the Rong Studio says: Sugarcane comes in four colours. One kind at Guangchuan grows for several years and becomes like a large tree over a zhang tall. The red kind is called Kunlun cane, and is grown everywhere north of the Ling. Manqian's Classic of Marvels says: The sugarcane joint-worm is sugarcane. The raw juice is cold and boiled into sugar it is hot; a little sugar water is sweet and nourishes the stomach, but too much stirs up damp heat.

《草木類·蕉子 Bananas》

1
○閩粵版焦結子大而味淡，佛手蕉小而甜，有羊角蕉、牛乳蕉，有四季結子者，生食甘冷，蒸而乾之最佳。開紅花曰美人蕉，嶺北即不易結子。蕉皮可為布，葉蒸而乾之，可以苴物。全人曰：截蕉花頭，則結實大。
○ The banban banana of Min and Yue bears large fruit with a bland taste; the Buddha's-hand banana is small and sweet; there are goat-horn bananas and cow's-milk bananas, and some bear fruit in all four seasons; eaten raw they are sweet and cooling, and steamed and dried they are best. The one with red flowers is called the beauty canna, which north of the Ling does not easily fruit. Banana fibre can be made into cloth, and the leaves steamed and dried can wrap things. Quanren says: If the flower head of the banana is cut off, the fruit is large.

《草木類·蘘荷 Myoga Ginger》

1
○升庵謂蘘荷即甘露，以急就注也。東壁謂蘘荷即上林猼且，而不知猼且乃芭蕉之轉聲也。蘘荷似蕉而小，又似蘆稷，三月開紅花，夏結綠刺房，內有黑子，其根似姜，可菹，蛇不喜此，故又治蠱。
○ Sheng'an says that myoga is ganlu, following the commentary on the Jijiu. Dongbi says that myoga is the bozuo of Shanglin, not knowing that bozuo is a sound-change of bajiao. Myoga is like the banana but smaller, and also like sorghum; it puts out red flowers in the third month and in summer bears green prickly capsules with black seeds inside; its root is like ginger and can be pickled; snakes dislike it, so it also cures gu poison.

《草木類·山茶 Camellia》

1
○紅白寶珠，肉紅曰楊妃，有磬口、鬯口、黃白、四季者，可以寄枝。白曰玉茗。坡詩：葉厚有棱犀甲健，花深少態鶴頭丹。詠寶珠也。馮時可滇記：滇多茶花，大於牡丹，木高十丈餘，沐府有寶珠樓。又一種曰茶梅，其萼如山茶而小，凌冬不雕。
○ Red and white jewel camellias, the flesh-red called Yang Guifei, and those with chime mouths, open mouths, yellow-white and four-season flowering, can be grafted. The white is called jade tea. Po's poem: "Leaves thick and ridged, strong as rhinoceros armour;
flowers deep, with little grace, red as a crane's crest." This describes the jewel camellia. Feng Shike's Record of Dian: Dian has many camellias, larger than peonies, the trees over ten zhang tall, and the Mu residence has a Jewel Tower. Another kind called tea-plum has calyxes like the camellia but smaller, and does not wither in winter.

《草木類·海棠 Crab-apple Blossom》

1
○梅聖俞云：胭脂色欲滴，紫蠟蒂何長。詠垂絲也，可寄枝於櫻桃。坡詩：日暖風輕春睡足。詠西府也，臘月開，插之亦生。其不花者，以已花之木為鍼，內其根跗，則花矣。鐵梗有四季花者，貼梗叢生。洪適詠黃海棠。李詡曰：冬至以糟水澆海棠根，則盛而鮮。壺中贅錄曰：世謂海棠無香，蜀潼川、昌州海棠獨香。賈耽言大足縣產者香。
○ Mei Shengyu says: "Rouge colour about to drip,
how long the purple-wax stems!" This describes the hanging-thread kind, which can be grafted on cherry. Po's poem: "Sun warm, wind light, spring sleep satisfied." This describes the Xifu kind, which flowers in the twelfth month and can also be grown from cuttings. For those that do not flower, make a needle of wood from one that has flowered and insert it at the base of the roots, and it will flower. The iron-stem kind has four-season flowers, growing in clusters close to the stems. Hong Gua wrote of the yellow crab-apple. Li Xu says: If crab-apple roots are watered with lees water at the winter solstice, they flourish brightly. The Superfluous Records from within the Pot say: It is said that crab-apple blossoms have no fragrance, yet those of Tongchuan and Changzhou in Shu alone are fragrant. Jia Dan says those produced in Dazu county are fragrant.

《草木類·金松 Golden Pine》

1
○台州金松垂條結子，如碧珠，三年子乃一熟，每歲生者相續，璀璨相間，子治腸風。
○ The golden pine of Taizhou has drooping branches bearing seeds like jasper beads, which ripen only once in three years, and those growing each year follow one another, glittering together; the seeds cure intestinal wind.

《草木類·木筆玉蘭 Magnolia Liliiflora and Yulan》

1
○木筆即辛夷，宜斬條插之。玉蘭即木蘭。大理府志：木蓮花樹高大，花如蓮，有青、黃、紅、白四種。白香山言忠州有木蓮花，王元美謂即玉蘭。智按：玉蘭春初開，木蓮四月花，蓋亦有別。五雜俎曰：木蘭去皮不死。
○ The mubi is the xinyi, and should be grown from cut branches. The yulan is the mulan. The Gazetteer of Dali Prefecture: The wood-lotus tree is tall, with flowers like lotus in four colours, blue, yellow, red and white. Bai Xiangshan says Zhongzhou has wood-lotus flowers, and Wang Yuanmei says they are the yulan. I, Zhi, note: The yulan blooms in early spring and the wood-lotus in the fourth month, so they are different. The Five Miscellanies says: The mulan does not die when its bark is removed.

《草木類·橘 Tangerine》

1
○沙橘大，即福橘。衢皮薄。琭橘最美。又有蜜橘、小橘、穿橘。太湖朱橘小於福橘，而甘同之。又有綠橘、匾橘，小為金橘，又小為金豆。柑有香柑、甜柑、蜜曇柑、牛乳柑、波斯柑、青柑、廣柑、酒柑。若佛手柑，則香櫞、香橘一類也。皆以四月前接，接宜枸橘，畏幹，喜豕通，而易生蠹，蠹化蝶，蝶胎子還育於樹為孩蟲，必探去之。浸鼠溺瓨中，浮者埋橘下，則茂，涅槃經如橘得鼠是也。臨江買蟻養於樹下，蟻自能食其蠹。柑初生如豆，亦多落下，收煮其苦水，糖蒸作果，頗佳。橘餅則旋去外皮，裂去其核，煮去其苦，石灰水浸之，後以糖煮成。凡橘、柑、橙之青時，皆可為青皮，化食下氣。［中履曰：橘蟲青色，口吐紅賴，岐頭入醋中，亦可抽絲。張苟仲曰：接四次，則橘無核。盧碩曰：橘種子者不結實，實亦長，俗呼柚子。廣柚則圞大。
○ The large sand tangerine is the Fu tangerine. That of Qu has thin skin. The lu tangerine is the finest. There are also honey tangerines, small tangerines and chuan tangerines. The red tangerines of Taihu are smaller than Fu tangerines but equally sweet. There are also green tangerines and flat tangerines, the smaller called kumquat, and smaller still golden bean. Of mandarins there are fragrant, sweet, honey-tan, cow's-milk, Persian, green, Guang and wine mandarins. The Buddha's-hand citron is of the same kind as the citron and fragrant tangerine. All are grafted before the fourth month, preferably on trifoliate orange; they fear dryness and like pig dung, but easily get borers, which turn into butterflies, whose eggs hatch on the tree again as larvae, and must be removed. Soak rats in urine in a jar, and bury those that float under the tangerines, and they flourish; this is what the Nirvana Sutra means by "like a tangerine getting a rat." At Linjiang they buy ants and keep them under the trees, and the ants eat the borers. When mandarins first form like beans many fall; these are gathered, boiled to remove the bitter water and steamed with sugar into sweets, which are quite good. For tangerine cakes, pare off the outer skin, split and remove the seeds, boil out the bitterness, soak in limewater, and then boil with sugar. All tangerines, mandarins and oranges when green can be made into green peel, which aids digestion and brings down qi. [Zhonglü says: The tangerine caterpillar is green, its mouth spits red lai; with its forked head dipped in vinegar it can also be drawn into silk. Zhang Gouzhong says: Grafted four times, the tangerine has no seeds. Lu Shuo says: Tangerines grown from seed do not fruit, or if they fruit the fruit is elongated, commonly called youzi. The pomelo of Guang is round and large.

《草木類·枸橘 Trifoliate Orange》

1
○枸橘穰綯間溝泥種，成栵，髡頭今發肄，棘針成屏翳。中德曰：枸橘與枸榾葉嫩時，皆可作茶。
○ The trifoliate orange is planted in ditch mud with straw rope, forming rows; pollarded, it puts out shoots, and its thorns form a screen. Zhongde says: The tender leaves of the trifoliate orange and the gouhu can both be made into tea.

《草木類·石榴 Pomegranate》

1
○有紅花、肉紅花、四季花、並蒂花、黃花、白花者。芒種取小版插之，雖深暑烈日中，亦宜淖以水糞。以石置於枝間，則結實。虛舟曰：榴宜骨磊其下，五色線曰石榴宜石膏糞，恐是骨石之訛。火榴則宜盆玩者也。曹州岡榴圍尺餘，晶榴內止十八子。
○ There are red-flowered, flesh-red-flowered, four-season-flowered, twin-flowered, yellow-flowered and white-flowered kinds. At Grain in Ear plant small cuttings; even in the height of summer under the blazing sun, they should be soaked with water and manure. If stones are placed among the branches, it fruits. Xuzhou says: The pomegranate likes bones heaped below it; the saying in the Five-Coloured Thread that the pomegranate likes gypsum and dung is probably an error for bone and stone. The fire pomegranate is suitable as a potted plant. The Gang pomegranate of Caozhou is over a chi round, and the crystal pomegranate has only eighteen seeds inside.

《草木類·木瓜 Chinese Quince》

1
○埤雅曰：楙，木瓜，百益一損。翦紙作花，粘實，得日露之氣乃紅，其文如生。一種香柦，色黃，生壓汁，和玄參、甘松作濕香。秋分後根側分小條種之，喜陰喜肥。廣志：木瓜枝一尺有百二十節。
○ The Piya says: The mao is the Chinese quince, with a hundred benefits and one harm. Cut paper into flowers and stick them on the fruit, and as it receives the qi of sun and dew it reddens, the patterns as if natural. One kind, the fragrant zha, is yellow; its raw juice pressed out and mixed with scrophularia and nardostachys makes moist incense. After the autumn equinox, small shoots are divided from beside the roots and planted; it likes shade and fertile soil. The Guangzhi: A chi of quince branch has a hundred and twenty nodes.

《草木類·牡丹 Tree Peony》

1
○高簇曰樓子，單葉曰山丹，可以芍藥匕頭接之，非社前即中秋可移，須淨洗其根，酒洗尤妙。神隱曰：記取南枝栽，勿足踏也。如種子，六月取黑而沉水者，拌土盛，至八月種之，來春生芽。凡澆牡丹，水不喜多，春宜間數日，秋不宜澆。若知有蠹，以白蘞、砒、芫為末，布於根下，近止以生桕油入土寸許，蟲即死。譜有鶴翎、鹿胎、檀心、粉奴、魏紫、姚黃百餘名，愈接愈幻。霏雪錄云：張茂卿嘗接牡丹於椿樹之杪，開時燕客推樓玩焉。入藥用紅單葉者。餘全人曰：漳潮不宜牡丹，有桶土移之者，現蕊尚開，明年即無花矣。
○ The tall clustered kind is called tower-form, and the single-petalled kind mountain red; it can be grafted using herbaceous peony shoots; it can be transplanted only before the she festival or at mid-autumn, and its roots must be washed clean, best of all with wine. The Shenyin says: Remember to plant it with its south-facing branch [to the south], and do not tread on it. To grow it from seed, take in the sixth month those that are black and sink in water, mix them with earth and keep them, sow them in the eighth month, and they sprout the next spring. In watering tree peonies, they do not like much water; in spring water every few days, and in autumn not at all. If one finds borers, powder ampelopsis, arsenic and genkwa and spread it under the roots; lately one simply puts raw tallow-tree oil an inch into the soil, and the insects die at once. The manuals list over a hundred names, such as crane-feather, deer-fawn, sandalwood-heart, powder-slave, Wei purple and Yao yellow, and the more they are grafted the more fantastic they become. The Records of Falling Snow say: Zhang Maoqing once grafted a tree peony onto the top of a toon tree, and when it bloomed he feasted guests and pushed up a tower to view it. For medicine the red single-petalled kind is used. Yu Quanren says: Zhang and Chao do not suit tree peonies; some transplant them with tubs of earth, and the buds then present still open, but the next year there are no flowers.

《草木類·瑞香 Daphne》

1
○有黃、紫、白、樓子、串子者，又有一體紅白者。帶花枝於背日處，或秋插水稻側，生根，移種之。侯延慶曰：廬山出瑞香，麝觸之萎，惟瀹茶灌其根，則不為蟲所蛀。結香似之，但可作紙。
○ There are yellow, purple, white, tower-form and string-form kinds, and also some red and white on one plant. Branches with flowers are planted in a place facing away from the sun, or inserted in autumn beside rice paddies, and when rooted they are transplanted. Hou Yanqing says: Mount Lu produces daphne, which wilts when touched by musk; only if its roots are watered with steeped tea is it not eaten by insects. Edgeworthia resembles it, but can only be used for paper.

《草木類·桂 Cassia》

1
○官桂四月花，無實，其樹高數丈，出邕交。嵇含曰：桂自踞嶺為林，葉如枇杷葉，鋸邊，表裏白毛者，為肉桂；臨嵓如竹，其葉似柿，無鋸齒者，為菌桂。其秋開者，木犀也，以木肉赤，有縱文，凌冬香辣，故皆以桂名。木犀有結實者，四季開者無實，而冬季花尤繁。王麟洲言有子者桂，無子者木樨，訛也。木犀結子如豆，今呼月桂，木犀呼岩桂。不宜灌糞。花時移，屈其條壓土中，能自生根，亦可以冬青接。寧都赤面石桂葉乃大五六寸，四圍鋸齒如刺。張邦基曰：湖南呼九里香，江東曰岩桂，浙人曰木犀，引王以寧九里香詩為證。郭青螺曰：西昌山桂二月開白花，叢開，其香似月季花，樹近於木犀，江北謂之九里香，其葉似禾，樹冬不落，新安謂之春桂，色有紅白二種，紅者肥而稀得，性喜野而畏俗，人家植之，九死一活。智按：此九里香非一種矣。
○ Official cassia flowers in the fourth month and bears no fruit; the tree is several zhang tall and comes from Yong and Jiao. Ji Han says: Cassia occupies ridges forming forests by itself; that with leaves like loquat leaves, saw-edged and white-haired on both sides, is cinnamon; that by cliffs like bamboo, with leaves like persimmon and no saw-teeth, is junshui cassia. That which blooms in autumn is osmanthus; because its wood is red with lengthwise lines, and it is fragrant and pungent through the winter, they are all named cassia. Some osmanthus bear fruit; those blooming in all four seasons have no fruit, and flower most profusely in winter. Wang Linzhou's saying that those with seeds are cassia and those without are osmanthus is mistaken. Osmanthus bears seeds like beans; what is now called moon cassia, and osmanthus called rock cassia. It should not be watered with dung. It is transplanted when flowering, or its branches bent down and pressed into the earth, where they root by themselves; it can also be grafted on holly. The red-faced stone cassia of Ningdu has leaves five or six inches long, saw-toothed all round like thorns. Zhang Bangji says: In Hunan it is called nine-li fragrance, in Jiangdong rock cassia, and by the people of Zhe osmanthus, citing Wang Yining's poem on the nine-li fragrance as proof. Guo Qingluo says: The mountain cassia of Xichang puts out white flowers in clusters in the second month, with a fragrance like the China rose, and the tree resembles osmanthus; north of the Yangzi it is called nine-li fragrance; its leaves are like grain and the tree does not shed them in winter; in Xin'an it is called spring cassia, in two colours, red and white, the red plump and rarely obtained; by nature it likes the wild and fears the vulgar, and of those planted in households nine die for one that lives. I, Zhi, note: These nine-li fragrances are not one kind.

《草木類·胡桃 Walnut》

1
○胡桃十月種，宜大石重壓其根，使實生，子不脫落。其核外肉最爛手皮。其核分二瓣者，山核桃也。占卑國出扁核桃，形如半月。
○ Walnuts are planted in the tenth month, and their roots should be pressed heavily with a large stone so that they set firmly and the nuts do not drop. The flesh outside the nut badly stains the skin of the hands. Those whose nuts divide into two halves are hickories. Jambi produces flat walnuts, shaped like half-moons.

《草木類·橄欖 Chinese Olive》

1
○青橄欖無仁。烏橄外肉杵碎，乾放，則自有霜堆起，如白鹽，曰欖醬。取時開孔納鹽，則欖自落。魚最畏之。嶺北一種，似大欖而圓撱。餘甘子形似山查，而味似橄欖。
○ The green olive has no kernel. If the outer flesh of black olives is pounded and left to dry, frost piles up on it by itself like white salt, which is called olive paste. When gathering, cut a hole and put in salt, and the olives fall by themselves. Fish fear it most. North of the Ling there is a kind like a large olive but round and oval. The emblic is shaped like the hawthorn but tastes like the olive.

《草木類·慄 Chestnut》

1
○房駭慄零，種其栗楔中枚方匾，他日生栗豐滿。一曰：和梂種之，易結而大。芽茁三年即實，成樹後根下原慄尚在，橡慄亦然，他木之核則爛矣，當取去原慄。諸暨慄大如拳。必接乃大，茅慄、錐慄皆可接也。埋慄臍向下，彼自生而向上，故眉抹其臍。掐臍吹氣，則不爆而皮易脫，固其理也。
○ When the burs split open and the chestnuts fall, plant the wedge-shaped nuts the middle one, square and flat, and the chestnuts later produced will be plump. One says: Planted with the bur, they fruit easily and large. Three years after sprouting they fruit, and after the tree has grown the original chestnut is still at the root; the same is true of acorns, while the stones of other trees rot; the original chestnut should be removed. The chestnuts of Zhuji are as large as fists. They must be grafted to be large; thatch chestnuts and cone chestnuts can both be grafted. Chestnuts buried with the navel downward grow upward of themselves, so one marks the brow and smears the navel. If the navel is pinched and blown into, it does not burst and the skin comes off easily; this is indeed the principle.

《草木類·榕 Banyan》

1
○閩廣多榕，垂條及地則復成根，久則擁腫，因名不死木。桂林有榕樹門，長溪有榕樹庵。為薪無焰，但細枝爆乾可為火繩。范正敏曰：衛西榕幹大如屋，韓少卿為郡，樹為吐煙，蓋樹老生火耳，久亦結香。中通曰：蕭伯玉年祖移植春浮園，遇雪則槁。
○ Min and Guang have many banyans; their drooping branches reach the ground and form roots again, and in time they swell, hence they are called undying trees. Guilin has the Banyan Gate, and Changxi the Banyan Hermitage. As firewood it gives no flame, but the fine branches dried by fire can be made into fuses. Fan Zhengmin says: The banyan trunks west of Wei are as large as houses; when Han Shaoqing governed the commandery, the trees gave out smoke, because old trees produce fire; in time they also form incense. Zhongtong says: My senior Xiao Boyu transplanted them to the Chunfu Garden, and they withered when it snowed.

《草木類·荔枝 Lychee》

1
○楓亭驛荔枝甲天下，長樂縣次之，廣以黑葉為美。多食作熱，以蜜解之。蔡忠惠公譜荔枝，徐勃廣之，曰：荔枝，蘆生者也。凡樹枝堅者可作蘆，福州方言也，言其非以核種也。有虎皮、駝踶、金棕、江家綠、一品紅，而火山最下。林崇壁曰：陳紫、宋香為尤。暄曰：興化狀元紅實小而肉滿，今楓亭無一株矣。
○ The lychees of Fengting post station are the best in the empire, and those of Changle county next; in Guang the black-leaf is esteemed. Eaten in quantity they cause heat, which is relieved with honey. Lord Cai Zhonghui wrote a manual of lychees, which Xu Bo expanded, saying: The lychee is reed-grown. Any tree with firm branches can be made "reed," which is a dialect word of Fuzhou, meaning that it is not grown from the stone. There are tiger-skin, camel-hoof, golden palm, Jiang family green and first-rank red, and huoshan is the poorest. Lin Chongbi says: Chen purple and Song fragrance are the finest. Xuan says: The top-graduate red of Xinghua has small fruit with full flesh; now there is not a single tree of it at Fengting.

《草木類·梨 Pear》

1
○有香青、消梨、瓶梨、綿梨、鵝梨、金花、寒雪、紫水、黃紅諸名。張子賢所云厭沙寺梨，謂之御園，其接以棠梨接棗樹上。按今用春分前接，接用桑體或棠梨，其實大，以油紙護之。梨名快果，百損一益。梨核中子多，種之少生梨，多生杜。安南有庵羅果，似北梨，四五月間熟，多食無害。
○ There are names such as fragrant green, xiao pear, bottle pear, cotton pear, goose pear, golden flower, cold snow, purple water and yellow-red. The pears of the Yansha temple mentioned by Zhang Zixian were called the imperial garden, and were grafted with birchleaf pear on jujube trees. I note that now they are grafted before the spring equinox, on mulberry or birchleaf pear stock, and the fruit is large and protected with oiled paper. The pear is called the quick fruit, with a hundred harms and one benefit. There are many seeds in the core, but few planted produce pears; most produce du pears. Annam has the anluo fruit, like the northern pear, which ripens in the fourth and fifth months and does no harm even if eaten in quantity.

《草木類·棗 Jujube》

1
○山束棗園至白露日，根下遍堆草焚之，以辟露氣也，不爾則多干落。正月元旦日出時，反斧斑駁椎，曰嫁棗。候大蠶入簇，以杖擊其枝，其年實多。紅棗，樹上熟而曬乾者也。黑棗，蒸熟而曬乾者也。有摘青而曬乾者。濟南府齊河縣有無核棗。棗不接者壽。有瓢棗、酸棗、雞心、細核、長棗、圓棗、大小棗。龍棗卷曲，結實不堪食。南棗出蘭溪，搖而知之，其肉離核。木中細剞劂。贊寧曰：棗木為匙，取其不餿不粘飯也。
○ In the jujube orchards of Shandong, on the day of White Dew, grass is piled under the roots everywhere and burned, to ward off the dew qi; otherwise many drop dried up. On New Year's morning at sunrise, they strike the trunk all over with the back of an axe, which is called marrying the jujube. When the great silkworms go into the frames, they beat the branches with sticks, and that year the fruit is plentiful. Red jujubes are ripened on the tree and dried in the sun. Black jujubes are steamed and dried in the sun. Some are picked green and dried in the sun. Qihe county in Jinan prefecture has stoneless jujubes. Ungrafted jujubes live long. There are gourd jujubes, sour jujubes, chicken-heart, fine-stone, long jujubes, round jujubes and large and small jujubes. The dragon jujube is curled and its fruit is inedible. Southern jujubes come from Lanxi; one knows them by shaking, for the flesh comes away from the stone. The wood is fit for fine engraving. Zanning says: Jujube wood is made into spoons because it does not go sour and rice does not stick to it.

《草木類·銀杏 Ginkgo》

1
○核兩頭尖，三棱者雄，二棱者雌，雌雄同種，相望乃結實。或雌木不結實，則鑿孔納雄木一塊，泥之，亦結。
○ The nuts are pointed at both ends; those with three ridges are male and those with two female; male and female must be planted together and face each other to bear fruit. If a female tree bears no fruit, chisel a hole, insert a piece of male wood and seal it with mud, and it will fruit.

《草木類·柿 Persimmon》

1
○歸田錄云：百十柿以一榠樝置其中，則紅而熟爛，榅桲亦可，土人謂之烘柿。今但取青者入甕中，自然紅熟。其曰海門、銅盆、方蒂、匾花，皆其名也。海門經久雨則蒂脫。綠柿耐久藏，即間居賦梁侯烏侯之柿也。春分接於本體。白露時摘下，碎搗，每升水半斤，釀四五時，柿液造雨蓋及紙扇用之。當結實時雨多，從跗至尺，以膚間橫裁之，不脫。
○ The Records of Returning to the Fields say: Put one Chinese quince among a hundred or so persimmons, and they turn red and ripen soft; quince will also do; the locals call them baked persimmons. Nowadays they just put green ones in a jar, and they turn red and ripe of themselves. Haimen, copper basin, square calyx and flat flower are all names of them. The Haimen kind loses its calyx after long rain. The green persimmon keeps long; it is the persimmon of Lord Liang and Lord Wu in the Rhapsody on Living in Idleness. It is grafted on its own stock at the spring equinox. Picked at White Dew and pounded, with half a jin of water to each sheng, and fermented for four or five double-hours, the persimmon juice is used for making umbrellas and paper fans. When there is much rain at fruiting time, cut across the skin from the base up to a chi, and the fruit will not drop.

《草木類·蒲萄 Grape》

1
○蒲萄三五月斬其藤，插於芋菔種之，一日灌冷茶，間兩日復以水，又以肥肉汁。蒲萄穿棗木而生，最甘。入麝於其皮，則蒲萄盡作香味。陝有白、綠、瑣瑣三種，西番以釀，甚妙。白大，而綠無核子。瑣瑣至細，兒食痘稀。
○ Cut grape vines in the third and fifth months and insert them in taro or radish to plant; water once with cold tea, after two days again with water, and also with fat meat broth. Grapes grown through jujube wood are sweetest. If musk is put into the bark, all the grapes take on its fragrance. Shaan has three kinds, white, green and suosuo, which the Western foreigners make into very fine wine. The white are large, and the green have no seeds. The suosuo are extremely small, and children who eat them have fewer pox.

《草木類·苧 Ramie》

1
○二月種，五月刈，七月再刈，九月又刈。績者以首苧柔，末苧脆，萵則惟繩索需。稚苧可芼。袁州種佳。燒灰淋汁，足為磁泑。
○ It is planted in the second month, cut in the fifth, cut again in the seventh and again in the ninth. For spinning, the first ramie is soft and the last brittle, and wo is needed only for rope. Young ramie can be eaten as greens. The Yuanzhou kind is good. Burnt to ash and leached, it is sufficient for porcelain glaze.

《草木類·綿花 Cotton》

1
○夏前濕子，染以草灰，播於鉏熟旱地，為溝宜深，當芒種時三耘則茂。秋前摘去散頭，則花繁子多。花三出，結鈴亦三瓣，紡布以此。其子仁可榨油，為黏舟用。北方綿花韌，南方綿花松。中通曰：綿花逢雨便薅，不薅則土結皮，故綿花嘗鋤數十次，惟種蕎麥不必多鋤。
○ Before summer, wet the seeds, coat them with grass ash and sow them on dry land well hoed, making the furrows deep; weeded three times at Grain in Ear, it flourishes. Before autumn pinch off the loose heads, and the bolls are many and the seeds plentiful. The flowers are three-lobed and the bolls also three-valved, and cloth is spun from them. The kernels of the seeds can be pressed for oil, used for caulking boats. Northern cotton is tough and southern cotton loose. Zhongtong says: Cotton must be weeded after every rain, for otherwise the soil forms a crust; so cotton is hoed dozens of times, but where buckwheat is planted it need not be hoed so often.

《草木類·罌粟花 Opium Poppy》

1
○廣義：重九種子，或曰中秋午種。花後囊如瓶，有細米，可粥，可以取油。其殼入藥，主澀斂。其結青苞時，以針刺十數眼，其津液自出，收人瓷器，用紙封口，暴二七日，即成鴉片，最能澀精。
○ The Guangyi: The seeds are sown on the ninth of the ninth month; some say at noon at mid-autumn. After flowering the capsules are like bottles, with fine grains that can be made into gruel or pressed for oil. The husks are used in medicine, chiefly astringent and contracting. When it forms green capsules, prick a dozen or so holes with a needle, and the juice comes out by itself; collect it in porcelain, seal the mouth with paper and expose it to the sun for fourteen days, and it becomes opium, which is most effective in retaining the essence.

《草木類·蘭 Orchids》

1
○廬山幽蘭有一干十二花者。王逸以楚辭之蘭為都梁，朱子從之；方虛谷定為醒頭香，瀕湖從之。傳曰蘭有國香，而以醒頭草專之乎？今貴建蘭，一乾九花，以葉直起者為上，其斜披者，章蘭也。二月割去，以茅燒之，令其再發，則直起而青潤矣。傷水葉生虱，亦宜燒去，而忌酷日苦雨。盆下宜碎瓦儭之，上乃土培。馮夢禎快雪堂漫錄曰：山土火煨，羅過，種，澆清水，以干土得水脹而抱根也。蘭根甘而生蟻，宜浸根於盆水，取出以溺澆之，復置水中，俟蟻浮水為度。今或置伏龍肝，或置人髮，皆制蟲也。茶油枯滓為妙。一曰：盆底用烰炭屑，則利水而蚓不來。陶石蕢曰：蘭喜清而惡肥。此誤說也。愚者曰：深澗幽蘭，誰灌之哉？庭蓄建蘭，蓋有道焉。蘭灌肥則易生蟲，須別處燒草皮以肥沃之，至春分時取此土以培蘭，四周作溝，以豬毛水澆之，則沿溝起箭。喜陰惡日。或燖雞鴨毛水，不宜多也。根密則茂，分之亦以春秋分。其品以玉魫為貴。椏蘭，發箭開椏者也。九節箭，其盛也。報春先，一干止一花，臘底發箭。蘭結子曰蘭筍，長房八棱，秋霜裂而子飛去，預護之，熟亦可種也。魚子蘭，木本也，長條細蕊如粟，小者曰賽蘭，香易入物，然性有毒。幽蘭、建蘭根甘，宜入藥，其花可茹，葉以浸油黑髮。都梁蘭根名土續斷，此亦宜名。武功山有五色蘭。葉似蘭而稍薄。紫蘭葉似仙茅。兆兗曰：幽蘭花有十瓣者，兗曾見之。黃山有瓔珞蘭，木本而香色俱不減。魚魫蘭莖弱。
○ The secluded orchid of Mount Lu has one stem with twelve flowers. Wang Yi took the lan of the Chuci to be dulianghua, and Master Zhu followed him; Fang Xugu settled it as the head-reviving herb, and Binhu followed him. The Zuo zhuan says the orchid has a fragrance of the state — would it be monopolized by the head-reviving herb? Now the Jian orchid is valued, with nine flowers on one stem; those with leaves standing upright are best, and those spreading obliquely are Zhang orchids. Cut them back in the second month and burn them with cogon grass to make them sprout again, and they stand upright, green and glossy. If water-damaged leaves get lice, they too should be burnt off; they dislike fierce sun and heavy rain. The bottom of the pot should be lined with broken tiles, and earth banked on top. Feng Mengzhen's Casual Records of the Kuaixue Hall says: Mountain earth roasted in fire and sieved is used for planting, watered with clear water, because dry earth swells when it gets water and embraces the roots. Orchid roots are sweet and breed ants; soak the roots in a basin of water, take them out and water them with urine, then put them back in the water until the ants float on the water. Now some put in stove earth and some human hair, all to control insects. Tea-oil cake dregs are excellent. One says: Light charcoal chips at the bottom of the pot help drainage, and earthworms do not come. Tao Shikui says: Orchids like clearness and hate fertilizer. This is a mistaken saying. The Foolish One says: Who waters the secluded orchids of deep ravines? But for Jian orchids kept in a courtyard there is a way. Orchids watered with fertilizer easily breed insects; one must burn turf elsewhere to enrich it, and at the spring equinox take this earth to bank the orchids, make a ditch around them, and water with pig-bristle water, and shoots rise along the ditch. They like shade and dislike sun. Or water used for scalding chicken and duck feathers, but not too much. When the roots are dense they flourish; they are divided at the spring or autumn equinox. Of the varieties, jade fish-head bone is most valued. The forked orchid is one whose shoots open in forks. The nine-joint shoot is its fullest. The baochunxian has only one flower to a stem, and sends up its shoot at the end of the twelfth month. The seeds the orchid bears are called orchid shoots, in long eight-ridged capsules, which split in autumn frost and the seeds fly away; protect them beforehand, and when ripe they can also be planted. The fish-roe orchid is woody, with long stems and fine buds like millet; the small kind is called rival-orchid; its fragrance easily enters things, but it is poisonous by nature. The roots of secluded and Jian orchids are sweet and fit for medicine; the flowers can be eaten, and the leaves are steeped in oil to blacken the hair. The root of the duliang orchid is called earth dipsacus; this too is a fitting name. Mount Wugong has five-coloured orchids. Their leaves are like orchids but slightly thinner. The purple orchid has leaves like curculigo. Zhaoyan says: There are secluded orchids with ten petals; I have seen them. The Yellow Mountains have the yingluo orchid, woody, yet no less in fragrance and colour. The fish-head-bone orchid has weak stems.

《草木類·茉莉 Jasmine》

1
○即悉那茗。花瓣長者，素馨也。蘇門、瓜哇有摩梨柰花，即茉莉成樹者。嶺北江南霜後糊室收之，最畏隙風，晴則取出受日，以午澆之。每大瓨可插猶鬃三百。至春煖後置露天下，晴則三日一澆水，五月則日一澆，燥更倍之，性喜曬，澆亦宜午後，六月則澆二三次。立夏至仲秋，皆灌窌汁糞水埋者，五日一灌，灌汁後忌水，俟次日下水，大花汁與水均，小花七水三汁可也。摘嫩枝使再發，則岐繁矣。六月可插枝種。根下生蟻，澆烏豆冷湯。保生錄：茉莉花勿置床頭，能引蜈蚣。
○ It is the xinaming. Those with long petals are sambac. Sumatra and Java have the molihua, which is jasmine grown into trees. North of the Ling and in Jiangnan, after frost it is kept in rooms with papered windows, fearing most the draughts through cracks; on fine days it is taken out to get sun and watered at noon. Each large jar can take three hundred cuttings. When the spring has warmed, it is put out in the open; on fine days watered every three days, in the fifth month once a day, twice as much when dry; by nature it likes sun, and should also be watered after noon; in the sixth month two or three times. From the Beginning of Summer to mid-autumn, water it with cellar juice buried dung-water every five days; after the juice avoid water until the next day; for large flowers equal parts juice and water, for small flowers seven of water to three of juice. Pinch the tender branches so that they sprout again, and they branch profusely. In the sixth month cuttings can be planted. If ants breed under the roots, water with cold black-bean broth. The Records of Protecting Life: Do not put jasmine flowers at the head of the bed, for they attract centipedes.

《草木類·種蓮 Planting Lotus》

1
○夏前淺池腴土築實，臥藕覆泥，疏種，露萌而浸之。瓨種以糟查、皮屑壅之。女栽則萎。周益公曰：羊角入池，栽荷立成。寧獻王曰：酒甕頭泥種，即開花。建寧、饒州蓮子大而矬。墨蛾錄曰：蓮子入雞卵，伏如法，取出，以天門冬為末，和肥泥安盆中，將蓮實磨穿一頭種之，勿令水乾，自然生葉開花，如錢。極惡桐油。又有睡蓮，北戶錄曰：睡蓮如荇而大，沈於水面，花數莖，五色，當夏自開，夜縮入水底，晝復出。乃是萍蓬草類。
○ Before summer, in a shallow pond with rich soil, ram it firm, lay the rhizomes flat, cover with mud, plant them sparsely, and when the shoots appear flood them. In jars they are planted and banked with lees and skin shavings. Planted by women, they wilt. Lord Zhou Yi says: Put sheep's horns in the pond, and lotus planted will thrive at once. Prince Xian of Ning says: Planted with mud from the top of a wine jar, they flower at once. The lotus seeds of Jianning and Raozhou are large and squat. The Record of Mo'e says: Put lotus seeds in a hen's egg, brood it in the proper way, take them out, powder asparagus root and mix it with rich mud in a pot, grind through one end of the lotus seed and plant it, not letting the water dry up, and it will naturally grow leaves and flowers the size of coins. It detests tung oil. There is also the water lily; the Record of the Northern Doors says: The water lily is like the floating-heart but larger, sinking to the water surface, with several stems of flowers in five colours, which open by themselves in summer, shrink into the bottom of the water at night, and come out again by day. It is of the pingpeng-grass kind.

《草木類·別有蓮心 A Separate Lotus Heart》

1
○蓮心一名茶菱，出建寧及寧州，五月淺水種子，角柔肉甘香，非蓮花之心也。
○ Lotus heart, also called tea-caltrop, comes from Jianning and Ningzhou; its seeds are sown in shallow water in the fifth month, with soft horns and sweet, fragrant flesh; it is not the heart of the lotus flower.

《草木類·水仙 Narcissus》

1
○六月根囊出土，取懸簷下，風吹又煙熏，逾月浸糞而復簷吹，十月盆栽，沙石水養，不用泥土，花高於葉，金盞銀臺。南京安德門華嚴寺前花匠畜水仙，云：但取大如卵者，夏出土懸之，九月積石子栽，數年乃大，大則開花。
○ In the sixth month the bulbs come out of the ground; take them and hang them under the eaves, exposed to wind and smoke; after a month soak them in dung and again expose them under the eaves; in the tenth month plant them in pots, nourished with sand, stones and water, without mud; the flowers stand above the leaves, golden cups on silver platforms. A flower grower in front of the Huayan temple at the Ande Gate of Nanjing who raised narcissus said: Just take those as large as eggs, lift them in summer and hang them, plant them in the ninth month in heaped pebbles, and after several years they become large, and when large they flower.

《草木類·菊 Chrysanthemum》

1
○北種最繁，怯木喜陰，五月肥沃之則高，摘蕊少留者花大。若有癃頭菊蟻，傍置鱉甲，引出棄之。有黑蚰瘠其枝，以麻裹箸頭捋出之。蚯蚓傷其根，以石灰水灌河木解之。象乾蟲賊其葉，磨鐵線穴搜之。劉蒙泉、范至能、史正忠譜至百種，有龍腦、新羅、玉金、萬鈴、合蟬、銀臺諸名，物有以人為而愈變者。入藥取甘菊，苖亦可芼。歲時記：夏至菊灰止小麥蠹。田汝成曰：蟈氏焚牡菊禁蛙，不驗。
○ The northern kinds are the most numerous; it dreads wood and likes shade; enriched in the fifth month it grows tall, and if the buds are pinched leaving few, the flowers are large. If there are lunghead chrysanthemum ants, put a turtle shell beside them to draw them out and throw them away. If black youchong weaken the branches, wrap a chopstick end in hemp and strip them off. If earthworms damage the roots, water with limewater or river wood to remedy it. If the xianggan insect damages the leaves, search them out in their holes with a ground iron wire. The manuals of Liu Mengquan, Fan Zhineng and Shi Zhengzhong reach a hundred kinds, with names such as Dragon Brain, Silla, Jade Gold, Ten Thousand Bells, Joined Cicadas and Silver Platform; there are things that change more and more through human effort. For medicine the sweet chrysanthemum is used, and its shoots can also be eaten as greens. The Record of the Seasons: Chrysanthemum ash at the summer solstice stops weevils in wheat. Tian Rucheng says: The Guo officer's burning male chrysanthemum to keep off frogs does not work.

《草木類·木芙蓉 Cotton Rose》

1
○江北木芙蓉冬槁，浙閩廣皆成樹，七月開，十月止，故名拒霜。有紅、黃、白花，有大紅者，四面者。粵西添色芙蓉，花朝開正白，午後微紅，夜深紅，此則轉觀花也。冬髡條，置向南日下，覆以干壤，候春見萌，則為深坎插之。取皮風戾之，漚池為綆，甚能勝水。中通曰：取皮作線為汗衫，不臭。
○ North of the Yangzi the cotton rose withers in winter, while in Zhe, Min and Guang it becomes a tree, blooming from the seventh month until the tenth, hence the name "resisting frost." There are red, yellow and white flowers, scarlet ones, and four-sided ones. The colour-adding cotton rose of western Yue has flowers pure white in the morning, slightly red after noon and deep red at night; this is the colour-changing flower. In winter pollard the branches, put them facing south in the sun covered with dry earth, and when buds appear in spring, plant them in deep holes. The bark taken and dried in the wind, retted in a pond and made into ropes, holds up very well in water. Zhongtong says: Thread made from the bark and made into undershirts does not smell.

《草木類·鳳仙花 Balsam》

1
○子名急性。苖可芼。藥不生蟲蠹。子能爛物。根花同礬，能糟骨角，變絳色。最易種也。又有金鳳花，乃金雀花，灌生細葉黃花者也，俗混呼之。
○ Its seeds are called "impatient." Its shoots can be eaten as greens. Medicines [with it] do not get insects. The seeds can rot things. The roots and flowers with alum can pickle bone and horn and turn them crimson. It is very easy to grow. There is also the golden phoenix flower, which is the golden sparrow flower, a shrub with fine leaves and yellow flowers; they are commonly confused.

《草木類·玉蕊瓊花 Jade Stamen and Qiong Flower》

1
○瓊花觀在楊州新城，一名后土祠，政和中改蕃釐院。唐李衛公詠唐昌玉蕊，是也。海陵南下，徙種北去，未幾故株復生。周益公曰：蔓如荼縻，冬凋春茂，柘葉紫莖，三月八出，須如垂絲，上綴金粟，花心後一英出眾須上，散為十餘蕊。王建云：一樹玲瓏玉刻成。楊巨源云：蕊珠宮上玉花香。曾慥謂瑒花，山谷謂山礬，皆臆也。八仙花體似繡球，白花八朵為叢，結子，瓣薄而不香；瓊花不結子，瓣厚而香；元瑞辨之。滇有鄧花，亦八蕊，有五色銀球，八面五出，而佛齊者八面四出。然曰瓊曰玉，未嘗不可通名。
○ The Qiong Flower Temple is in the new city of Yangzhou, also called the Houtu Shrine, renamed the Fanli Cloister in the Zhenghe period. What Lord Li Wei of the Tang celebrated as the jade stamen of Tangchang is this. When [Wanyan Liang of] Hailing came south, it was transplanted to the north, but before long the old stump grew again. Lord Zhou Yi says: Its vine is like the rose of Sharon, withering in winter and flourishing in spring, with cudrania-like leaves and purple stems, putting out eight-lobed flowers in the third month, with stamens like hanging threads, dotted with golden grains, and after the heart of the flower a single bloom rises above the stamens and disperses into a dozen or so buds. Wang Jian says: "A whole tree, exquisite, carved of jade." Yang Juyuan says: "Fragrant jade flowers on the Palace of Stamen Pearls." Zeng Zao calls it the yang flower and Shangu mountain alum; both are guesses. The eight-immortals flower resembles the snowball, its white flowers in clusters of eight, bearing seed, with thin and unfragrant petals; the qiong flower bears no seed and has thick, fragrant petals; Yuanrui distinguished them. Dian has the Deng flower, also with eight stamens, and five-coloured silver balls, eight-faced and five-lobed, while that of Srivijaya is eight-faced and four-lobed. But whether called qiong or jade, they may well share the name.

《草木類·長生草 Herbs of Long Life》

1
○金釵石斛，一名長生草，開小白花，甚香，若根荄間纏以人髮，鐵絲絡之，懸風日中，自生。又挂蘭挂之亦生。石崖千年松、菊、卷柏，可紙收笥，得水蓊然，皆長生草。本經石長生，丹沙草，葉似蕨而細，如龍須，黑如光漆，冬不凋。圖經紅花草，名長生。又通泉草、禿瘡花，一名長生，葉似地丁，中抽莖，白花，摘下經年不槁。深山木石上生雲霧草，如琴絃亂絲，無花葉，有黑如髮者，黃如金者。
○ Golden-hairpin dendrobium, also called long-life herb, puts out small white flowers, very fragrant; if hair is wound round the roots and it is wrapped in iron wire and hung in the wind and sun, it grows by itself. Air orchids too grow when hung up. The thousand-year pines, chrysanthemums and spikemoss of stone cliffs can be kept in paper in a basket, and on getting water become lush; all are long-life herbs. The Classic's stone long-life is the cinnabar grass, with leaves like fern but finer, like dragon's beard, black as glossy lacquer, not withering in winter. The Illustrated Classic's red-flower herb is called long-life. Also the spring-reaching herb and baldness flower, also called long-life, have leaves like violets with a stem rising in the middle and white flowers, and when picked do not wither for a year. Deep in the mountains on trees and rocks grows the cloud-and-mist herb, like qin strings or tangled silk, without flowers or leaves, some black like hair and some yellow like gold.

《草木類·杜鵑花 Azalea》

1
○即躑躅類，有大紅、粉紅、黃者，千葉可珍，喜陰，畏油煙。映山紅有紅白深淺，皆單葉，紅者取汁可染。
○ It is of the zhizhu kind, with scarlet, pink and yellow flowers; the double-flowered are precious; it likes shade and fears oil smoke. The mountain-reflecting red comes in deep and pale red and white, all single-flowered, and juice taken from the red can dye.

《草木類·梔子 Gardenia》

1
○有單花、重花、四季花，皆六出。芒種時穴腐版，泥塗翦枝種之，浮水面，候根生而栵種之。入藥取小者，染取大者，然易脫色。周吉甫所言白雲寺鄭強墓地之簷卜，則外紫內淡黃者也。景煥言青城申天師進紅梔子種。象踶花、含笑花，則似梔子者也。
○ There are single-flowered, double-flowered and four-season kinds, all six-lobed. At Grain in Ear, make holes in a rotten board, smear cut branches with mud and plant them, float it on the water, and when roots grow plant them in rows. For medicine the small are taken and for dyeing the large, but the colour fades easily. The zhanbo at Zheng Qiang's tomb at the Baiyun temple mentioned by Zhou Jifu is purple outside and pale yellow inside. Jing Huan says that Heavenly Master Shen of Qingcheng presented red gardenia seeds. The elephant-hoof flower and banana shrub resemble the gardenia.

《草木類·芝 Lingzhi》

1
○芝有五色，服食成仙者，溢言耳。木威喜芝類，即松脂入地所化，其上生小木，若蓮花，夜有光，持之滑，燒之不焦，佩之辟兵。蔡絛云：政和間，芝動二三萬本，蘄黃間一鋪二十五里遍野而出。王元美言嘉靖中各省進五色芝者無萬數也，多藥成者，上好祥瑞，何難紫脫朱草滿天下邪？潛草曰：搗糯米飯、雄黃及鹿頭血，或碎其角，加諸色藥，包暴乾，冬筍冬至日埋之，或灌藥入老樹爛樁，明春雷雨，即生五色芝。高山生水晶蘭，獨枝白瑩，乃白芝也。
○ Lingzhi come in five colours; that eating them makes one an immortal is exaggeration. The tree weixi fungus kind is formed from pine resin that has gone into the ground; on it grows a small tree like a lotus flower, which shines at night, is slippery to hold, does not char when burnt, and worn on the person wards off weapons. Cai Tao says: In the Zhenghe period, lingzhi came up in twenty or thirty thousand stems, spreading over the fields for twenty-five li between Qi and Huang. Wang Yuanmei says that in the Jiajing period countless five-coloured lingzhi were presented by the provinces, many of them made with drugs; when the ruler loves auspicious omens, how hard is it to fill the empire with zituo and red grass? Qiancao says: Pound glutinous rice with realgar and deer-head blood, or broken antler, add various coloured drugs, wrap and dry in the sun, and bury it with winter bamboo shoots at the winter solstice; or pour the drugs into the rotten stumps of old trees, and with the spring thunder and rain the next year five-coloured lingzhi grow. High mountains produce the crystal orchid, a single white lustrous stem; this is the white lingzhi.

《草木類·穿心排草 Heart-Piercing Paicao》

1
○本草以排草為熏。愚者見廣西香薦，即零陵香，在平樂山中見為蒿本。若嶺北有香草，對節生葉，兩葉相連，長五六寸，而幹穿其中，土人呼為排草，亦呼穿心草。又有夏開細粉紫花，如蒔蘿，乾之香，亦名排草。
○ The Materia Medica takes paicao to be xun. The Foolish One saw the incense mats of Guangxi, which are lysimachia, and in the mountains of Pingle saw that it was of the artemisia kind. North of the Ling there is a fragrant herb with leaves growing opposite at the nodes, the two leaves joined, five or six inches long, with the stem piercing through them; the locals call it paicao, or heart-piercing herb. There is also one that puts out fine pinkish-purple flowers in summer, like dill, fragrant when dried, also called paicao.

《草木類·斷腸草 Heartbreak Grass》

1
○一枝三葉，葉大如蔞，食之輒死。秋海棠亦名斷腸草，亦毒人。宋顧文薦船窗夜話曰：斷腸草近人則葉動，人誤食之，則以伏卵不生者研和香油灌之。爇麻亦斷腸，枝葉近膚皆壞，惟人溺可解。古巴島有異草毒木，人持其枝葉即死。東雲雛言：爇麻如麻葉而長，碎有刺，高三尺，耀州延綏包手取以飼豕，即肥。
○ One stem with three leaves, the leaves as large as trichosanthes; whoever eats it dies. The begonia is also called heartbreak grass, and is also poisonous. Gu Wenjian of the Song, in the Night Talks by a Boat Window, says: Heartbreak grass moves its leaves when people come near; if someone eats it by mistake, grind an egg that failed to hatch with sesame oil and pour it down him. Stinging nettle also breaks the gut; its branches and leaves ruin skin they touch, and only human urine can relieve it. The island of Cuba has a strange herb and poisonous tree; whoever holds its branches or leaves dies. Dong Yunchu says: The nettle is like hemp leaves but longer, finely prickly, three chi tall; in Yaozhou and Yansui they gather it with wrapped hands to feed pigs, which grow fat.

《草木類·桃金娘 Downy Rose Myrtle》

1
○桂林野花，叢開，似梅而末微銳，似桃而色更頳，中莖純紫，絲綴深黃如金粟，土人呼為桃金娘。八九月實熟，青紺若牛乳狀，味甘，入脾養血。其花能行血。
○ A wild flower of Guilin, blooming in clusters, like the plum but slightly pointed at the tips, like the peach but redder, with pure purple central stems and deep yellow threads strung like golden grains; the locals call it peach-gold-lady. The fruit ripens in the eighth and ninth months, dark blue like cow's teats, sweet in taste, entering the spleen and nourishing the blood. Its flowers can move the blood.

《草木類·龍鬚草 Dragon's-Beard Grass》

1
○芒種勿壅龍須草，壅則生蟲退色。此與燈心草皆莞也，可以為席。今肇慶金渡專以龍須草織席為市。
○ At Grain in Ear do not bank dragon's-beard grass, for if banked it breeds insects and loses colour. This and rush are both guan, and can be made into mats. Now at Jindu in Zhaoqing they make a trade of weaving mats from dragon's-beard grass.

《草木類·淡把姑煙草 Tobacco》

1
○萬歷末有攜至漳泉者，馬氏造之，曰淡肉果，漸傳至九邊，皆銜長管而火點吞吐之，有醉仆者。崇禎時嚴禁之，不止。其本似春不老而葉大於菜，暴乾，以火酒炒之，曰金絲煙。北人呼為淡把姑，或呼擔不歸。可以袪濕發散，然久服則肺焦，諸藥多不效，其症忽吐黃水而死。中履曰：瀕湖載金絲草，或曰即煙。履按：金絲草出慶陽，治諸血惡瘡，涼血，不言作煙食，其性亦異。
○ At the end of the Wanli period someone brought it to Zhang and Quan; the Ma family manufactured it and called it tanrouguo; it gradually spread to the nine frontiers, where everyone holds a long pipe in the mouth, lights it with fire and inhales and exhales it; some fall down drunk. In the Chongzhen period it was strictly prohibited, but it did not stop. The plant resembles the evergreen chunbulao but its leaves are larger than vegetables; dried in the sun and roasted with distilled liquor, it is called golden-thread smoke. Northerners call it danbagu, or "carried and not returned." It can dispel damp and disperse [pathogens], but taken for long it scorches the lungs, many drugs are ineffective, and the symptom is suddenly vomiting yellow water and dying. Zhonglü says: Binhu records the golden-thread herb, and some say it is tobacco. I, Lü, note: The golden-thread herb comes from Qingyang and treats bleeding and malignant sores and cools the blood; it is not said to be smoked, and its nature is also different.

《草木類·靛 Indigo》

1
○藍有數種：槐靛葉似槐，以五月種，畏水浸之；蓼靛葉團；大葉藍似苦檟；惟山靛染易上青，葉似賽蘭，三月插條種之。然大青難種，小青可數刈。諺曰：靛無擔。愚者嘗訉之，田父曰：擔乾靛者，遠路搖之，則復稀如粥，置之復堅。成簍時以地灰充之，多是八成。其浸靛者，對日取出而撞水，必力士一氣三百餘杵則成，緩歇一杵，挏水無聲，則靛不佳。染家用青黛，染紙用靛花。其瓨以火溫之，冷則發瘟。
○ There are several kinds of indigo: pagoda-tree indigo has leaves like the pagoda tree, is planted in the fifth month, and fears being soaked in water; knotweed indigo has round leaves; large-leaf indigo resembles the bitter jia; only mountain indigo dyes easily to blue, with leaves like the rival orchid, planted from cuttings in the third month. But great blue is hard to grow, while small blue can be cut several times. The proverb says: Indigo cannot be carried. The Foolish One once asked about it, and an old farmer said: When dried indigo is carried, shaken on a long road it becomes thin like gruel again, and left standing it hardens again. When it is packed in baskets it is padded out with earth ash, and is mostly eighty percent. Those who steep indigo take it out facing the sun and beat the water; a strong man must pound it three hundred times in one breath for it to form; if he slows or pauses for one stroke, and the water is stirred without sound, the indigo is not good. Dyers use indigo pigment, and paper dyers use indigo flower. The jars are warmed with fire, for if cold they "catch the plague."

《草木類·萍 Duckweed》

1
○天問：靡萍九衢。王逸注：生九衢中。謬也。即田字草、破銅錢，醫雞蒙眼最驗。神農本經：水萍勝酒。故周禮萍氏謹酒，謂萍不溺也。此一夕九子之小萍。
○ The Heavenly Questions: "The duckweed of nine crossroads." Wang Yi's commentary: growing in the nine crossroads. This is absurd. It is the tian-character herb, the broken copper coin, most effective in curing chickens' clouded eyes. Shennong's Classic of Materia Medica: Water duckweed overcomes wine. So the Duckweed Officer of the Rites of Zhou regulated wine, meaning that duckweed does not sink. This is the small duckweed that produces nine offspring in a night.

《草木類·紅豆樹 Red Bean Tree》

1
○滇南紅豆鮮紅堅實，或嵌骰子，或留銀囊，俗以為吉祥。嶺南樹似槐，寸可琵琶槽，秋開花，二三月莢枯子老。千金方治貓鬼病，吐之。乘雅以紅豆與高良姜、紅豆蔻為一物，則非矣。半截紅半截黑為相思子，段公路云蔓生，其別見一種乎？嶺南北娑羅樹，高大，皮青，葉似大青，對生而厚，八月結角如刀豆，裂開有紅豆，永久不壞，非高座寺門之娑羅也。中通曰：望春樹子亦紅，而久則黑。
○ The red beans of southern Dian are bright red and hard; some inlay them in dice and some keep them in silver pouches, considering them auspicious. In Lingnan the tree resembles the pagoda tree, and an inch of its wood can make a pipa sound box; it flowers in autumn, and in the second and third months the pods wither and the seeds mature. The Thousand Gold Prescriptions treat cat-ghost illness by making one vomit it. The Chengya's taking red beans, galangal and red cardamom to be one thing is wrong. Those half red and half black are jequirity seeds; Duan Gonglu says it is a vine — is that a separate kind? North of the Ling there is the sal tree, tall, with green bark, leaves like great blue, opposite and thick, bearing in the eighth month pods like sword beans, which split to show red beans that never decay; it is not the sal at the gate of the Gaozuo temple. Zhongtong says: The seeds of the wangchun tree are also red but turn black in time.

《草木類·蒲 Cattail》

1
○刈蒲不逼跗。花可食，可褥。精在蒻，黃，醫用散血速。
○ In cutting cattail, do not cut close to the base. The flowers can be eaten and used to stuff mattresses. Its essence lies in the young shoots, and the pollen is used by physicians to disperse blood quickly.

《草木類·各種取油 Extracting Oil from Various Things》

1
○皮油，烏桕之皮；煖油，桕子連皮所熬之汁也。桕子核中有油，曰子油。墨煙宜桐油，入藥宜生榨之脂麻油，食芼宜茶子油、豆油，點燈便菜子油，若桕子油更經點也。玉山桕油蠟燭通宵，甚堅；嶺北桕油燭止可冬點，過春則不明。莊綽曰：芝麻八拗，雨暘時薄收，大旱時大熟，開花向下，結子向上，炒焦壓榨，才得生油，槁車則滑，鑽針乃澀。宗奭曰：炒熟熱壓之，生油但可點照，再煎煉之，熟油始可食，而不中點照。一曰：麻油出炒熱者曰生油，出生研者曰熟油。虛舟曰：脂麻炒熟，入沸湯，油悉浮，可取用。紅花子油、蔓青子油亦可作燈。萊菔子油、番豆仁取油皆佳。山東以蒼耳子作油，治瘋。臥子曰：蓖麻壓油，以罌埋土中一年取出，則白，作印色油佳。又法：先以熱水浸之，其色黑，再浸之數次，則水白，然後榨取油，可食，最甘。罌粟油固精。甘蕉油、蕪菁油黑髮。紫蘇子榨油，燃燈甚明，可油器物，又能桑五金八石。凡榨油，先炒子，磨之，又碾之，乃蒸之，草苴入鐵圍上榨，蒸不可太過，太過則有水氣。中通曰：讀書點麻油，無煙，不損眼，然易燥，每斤以桐油三兩和之，或入少鹽，亦可省油。以生姜擦盞邊，不生滓暈。中履曰：菜子幹二石，榨油八十斤；飽芝麻二石，可百二十斤，白麻不能也。黃豆潤者一石取十八斤，榨木壓之可二十二斤。暄曰：豆油、巴油紙作字，塵染不滅。
○ Bark oil comes from the bark of the tallow tree; warm oil is the juice boiled from tallow seeds with their coats. The oil in the kernels of tallow seeds is called seed oil. For ink soot tung oil is suitable, for medicine raw-pressed sesame oil, for cooking camellia-seed oil and bean oil, and for lamps rapeseed oil; tallow-seed oil burns even longer. Candles of Yushan tallow oil burn all night and are very hard; candles of tallow oil north of the Ling can be burnt only in winter, and after spring are not bright. Zhuang Chuo says: Sesame has eight contrarieties: when rain and sun are seasonable the harvest is thin, in great drought it ripens abundantly, it flowers facing down and seeds facing up, it gives raw oil only when roasted and pressed, it makes a dry cart slippery, and makes a drill bit stiff. Zongshi says: Roasted and pressed hot, the raw oil is only fit for lamps; boiled and refined again, the cooked oil can at last be eaten, but is not fit for lamps. One says: Sesame oil from roasted seeds is called raw oil, and that from raw ground seeds cooked oil. Xuzhou says: Sesame roasted and put into boiling water has all its oil float, which can be taken and used. Safflower-seed oil and turnip-seed oil can also be used for lamps. Oil taken from radish seeds and peanut kernels is all good. In Shandong they make oil from cocklebur seeds to treat leprosy. Wozi says: Castor oil pressed and buried in a jar in the earth for a year comes out white, and is good for seal-ink oil. Another method: first soak in hot water, which turns black; soak several times more until the water is clear, then press the oil, which is edible and very sweet. Poppy oil retains the essence. Banana oil and turnip oil blacken the hair. Perilla-seed oil burns very bright in lamps, can oil utensils, and can also sang the five metals and eight minerals. In pressing oil, first roast the seeds, grind them, crush them and steam them, wrap them in straw and put them in iron rings for the press; the steaming must not be overdone, for if overdone there is water vapour. Zhongtong says: For reading, burn sesame oil, which gives no smoke and does not harm the eyes, but dries out easily; mix three taels of tung oil with each jin, or add a little salt, which also saves oil. Rub the rim of the lamp dish with raw ginger and no residue halo forms. Zhonglü says: Two shi of dry rapeseed press out eighty jin of oil; two shi of full sesame can give a hundred and twenty jin, which white sesame cannot. A shi of moist soybeans yields eighteen jin, and pressed with a wooden press can give twenty-two jin. Xuan says: Characters written on paper with bean oil or croton oil are not obliterated by dust.

《草木類·竹 Bamboo》

1
○初節歧枝者雌，孕筍也。記其南枝，宜雨種之。或去枝，或架扶。便人糞，不便豕通。其生花者，斫而灌糞可也。結實斑文，兩兩相比，謂之竹米亦可下積。趙德麟曰：六十年紂稪，斫而以子種，六年而復。瘞以死貓，能引過墻。防過則溝之，臥芝麻稈，以竹忌也。周子充曰：以雜細糖和土種竹，茂。或以麥拌土。宜椎之，忌足踏。冬宜掘。旁行筍則直根遠行，挑出筍時，腐草覆之，又灌以糞，則茂。畏水，又畏大旱。山種苖竹向下，坡種挂竹向上。糞壤筍美，瘠地質堅。槿、苦、淡三種入藥。戴凱之譜六十一，後人侈載，頗有名異實同者。員丘衛竹、昆崙尋竹、神異篩□、扶南雲母竹，節可為船；羅浮龍公竹、臨賀竹有十抱者，漢竹節受一斛，交趾梧竹為甑，皆大簜空簢類也。黃濟植竻竹，東坡詠澀勒，即笆籬竹有刺者，服傷竹、挲摩竹、海南木竹、箛朵竹，皆實心者。繇衛叢生，□蛸根截案面，則慈孝類也。定海、靈峰、灃州、成都、安成、奉新、澄州皆產方竹，八月出筍，節圍小刺。又有二棱竹、人面竹。廬山有匾竹。交趾篥竹刺虎。南方葸簩為矛，即筋竹，篻篣之類。閩有古散竹，皮似棕。杜臺卿淮賦有檳榔竹，即棕竹類。羅陽竹似芭蕉，蕉棕本一類也。吳都賦筼簹、箖箊，即越女試劍竹，葉廣，遼箬類也。建安筼簹，一名簞竹，中似人形。北戶錄：溱州通竹無節。馬觀曰：古城觀音竹如藤，長丈八尺，色如鐵，每寸二三節。利竹亦蔓生若藤。弓竹不能自立。雲南有垂絲竹。襄州臥龍山武侯祠箖竹百尺，稍上生葉。狹竹即邛，腫節遼葉。紫竹、斑竹先青後變。齊民要術蕪竹黑皮，竹污有文。澄州有黑竹。道州瀧中多丹竹。宜都飛魚口有紅竹。黔陽有赤岡竹。雷州電斑竹。瑞筠山竹色如爛銀。箿竹頭有文。廣西雪竹，大斑紅暈。梅露，細斑也。菡隋竹蟲蛀籜如繡。咸都黃竹，溝青。安思苦竹四色。當塗縣慈姥山簫管竹，皮白如霜。蓋竹亦白。皆以色名。廣州相迷竹、鏞竹皆生黃。甘竹殼薄，即江南竹。簡簣竹，無錫僕，如苦竹。永嘉大羅山龍牙竹，稀節。箘簬、般笴、射筒，皆箭筱也。九嶷有雙稍竹，浙有天親竹，末皆兩歧。臨海向竹，毛在竹間。雪峰有奯竹，漢陽有□箤竹。薛翊記蘇麻竹。桃枝可為席，籚竹可為笛，箄竹筍煮為布，顧微所云包竹也。會稽公孫竹高不盈尺，奉化新嶺五寸白竹，辰州龍絲竹盈尺如針，鳳栖崖小墨竹，皆瀟湘鳳尾類也。越王竹乃沙箸。石竹、碧蟬、疑波竹，花如榴，皆葉似耳。暘谷漫錄：安福有十二時字竹，殆藥書之乎？
○ Those whose first joint branches in a fork are female and bear shoots. Mark the south-facing branches, and plant it in rain. Some remove the branches, some support it with frames. It benefits from human manure, not from pig dung. Those that flower should be cut down and watered with dung. The fruit, with mottled patterns, paired side by side, is called bamboo rice it can also relieve accumulations. Zhao Delin says: After sixty years it withers; cut it down and plant the seeds, and in six years it recovers. If a dead cat is buried, it can draw [bamboo] across a wall. To prevent it crossing, dig a ditch and lay sesame stalks in it, which bamboo avoids. Zhou Zichong says: Bamboo planted in earth mixed with fine sugar flourishes. Or mix wheat into the earth. It should be pounded firm, and must not be trodden on. In winter it should be dug. If the shoots run sideways, the roots travel far straight; when shoots are dug out, cover with rotten straw and water with dung, and it flourishes. It fears water, and also fears great drought. On mountains plant mao bamboo facing downhill, on slopes plant gua bamboo facing uphill. On manured soil the shoots are delicious, and on poor soil the culms are hard. Three kinds, jin, bitter and mild, are used in medicine. Dai Kaizhi's manual lists sixty-one; later people listed extravagantly, with many different names for the same thing. The wei bamboo of Yuanqiu, the xun bamboo of Kunlun, the shai[?] of the Classic of Marvels and the mica bamboo of Funan, whose joints can be made into boats; the Longgong bamboo of Luofu and the bamboo of Linhe ten embraces thick, the Han bamboo whose joint holds a hu, and the wu bamboo of Jiaozhi used for steamers, are all of the great dang hollow-min kind. The le bamboo planted by Huang Ji and the se le of Dongpo's poem are hedge bamboos with thorns; the fushang bamboo, suomo bamboo, Hainan wood bamboo and guduo bamboo are all solid. The youwei grows in clumps, and [?]-shao root cut for desk surfaces is of the cixiao kind. Dinghai, Lingfeng, Lizhou, Chengdu, Ancheng, Fengxin and Chengzhou all produce square bamboo, which puts out shoots in the eighth month, with small thorns round the joints. There are also two-ridged bamboo and human-face bamboo. Mount Lu has flat bamboo. The li bamboo of Jiaozhi can stab tigers. In the south the xilao is used for spears, which is the sinew bamboo, of the piaopang kind. Min has the gusan bamboo, with bark like palm. Du Taiqing's Rhapsody on the Huai has areca bamboo, which is of the palm-bamboo kind. The Luoyang bamboo resembles the banana; banana and palm are originally of one kind. The yundang and linyu of the Rhapsody on the Wu Capital are the Yue maiden's sword-testing bamboo, with broad leaves, of the Liao bamboo-leaf kind. The yundang of Jian'an, also called dan bamboo, has a human shape inside. The Record of the Northern Doors: The through bamboo of Qinzhou has no joints. Ma Guan says: The Guanyin bamboo of Champa is like rattan, eighteen chi long, iron-coloured, with two or three joints to the inch. The li bamboo too is a vine like rattan. The bow bamboo cannot stand by itself. Yunnan has hanging-thread bamboo. At the shrine of the Marquis Wu on Mount Wolong in Xiangzhou, the lin bamboo is a hundred chi tall, with leaves only at the top. The narrow bamboo is the qiong, with swollen joints and Liao leaves. Purple bamboo and spotted bamboo are green at first and change later. The Essential Techniques for the Common People: the wu bamboo has black bark, and the zhuwu has patterns. Chengzhou has black bamboo. In the rapids of Daozhou there is much cinnabar bamboo. At Feiyukou in Yidu there is red bamboo. Qianyang has red-ridge bamboo. Leizhou has lightning-spotted bamboo. The bamboo of Mount Ruiyun is coloured like gleaming silver. The ji bamboo has patterns at the top. The snow bamboo of Guangxi has large spots with red halos. Plum-dew has fine spots. The handui bamboo has its sheaths eaten by insects like embroidery. The yellow bamboo of Xiandu has green grooves. The bitter bamboo of Ansi comes in four colours. The flute bamboo of Mount Cimu in Dangtu county has bark as white as frost. The gai bamboo is also white. All are named for their colours. The xiangmi bamboo and yong bamboo of Guangzhou both grow yellow. The gan bamboo has thin sheaths; it is the Jiangnan bamboo. The jiankui bamboo of Wuxi is like the bitter bamboo. The dragon-tooth bamboo of Mount Daluo in Yongjia has sparse joints. Junlu, bange and shetong are all arrow bamboos. Jiuyi has double-tip bamboo and Zhe heavenly-kin bamboo, whose ends both fork in two. The xiang bamboo of Linhai has hairs among the bamboo. Xuefeng has huo bamboo, and Hanyang [?]-cui bamboo. Xue Yi records the suma bamboo. Taozhi can be made into mats, lu bamboo into flutes, and the shoots of bi bamboo boiled to make cloth, which is what Gu Wei calls bao bamboo. The gongsun bamboo of Kuaiji, less than a chi tall, the five-inch white bamboo of Xinling in Fenghua, the dragon-thread bamboo of Chenzhou, a chi long and like needles, and the small ink bamboo of Fengqiya are all of the Xiaoxiang phoenix-tail kind. The King of Yue's bamboo is the sand chopstick. Pinks, blue cicada and yibo bamboo, with flowers like pomegranates, all have ear-like leaves. The Casual Records of Yanggu: Anfu has bamboo marked with the characters of the twelve hours — was it written with drugs?

《草木類·種地黃 Planting Rehmannia》

1
○懷慶東灣最佳。畦坑糞土，正月種之，以冬爛草覆之，候芽稍出，以火燒草，去其苗，再生葉肥，根益壯，有一斤三十枝者。餘或揉，或以菊花心為貴。本草言二八月采，不如冬月采妙。浙中亦種之。歐陽獻萬以西山麓田種之，獲數倍於稻。乘雅曰：種芐，次年種牛膝，次種芝麻，十年乃種芐，芐收地汁也。
○ That of Donggwan in Huaiqing is the best. Prepare beds and pits with manured earth, plant it in the first month and cover it with rotten winter straw; when the shoots begin to appear, burn the straw to remove the shoots, and the leaves that grow again are fat and the roots all the stronger, some with thirty pieces to the jin. The rest is kneaded, or the chrysanthemum-heart kind is valued. The Materia Medica says it is gathered in the second and eighth months, but gathering in winter is better. It is also grown in Zhe. Ouyang Xianwan planted it in fields at the foot of the Western Hills and got several times the yield of rice. The Chengya says: Plant rehmannia, the next year achyranthes, then sesame, and only after ten years rehmannia again, for rehmannia takes up the juice of the earth.

《草木類·種芍 Planting Peony Root》

1
○春沃土種之，過三年則大，取單葉花者。赤白舊以花分，或以火酒浸根一宿而辨之。今按：山中種芍者，采根曝乾，即赤芍；刮其根皮而蒸乾，為白芍。
○ It is planted in rich soil in spring, and after three years becomes large; the single-flowered kind is taken. Red and white were formerly distinguished by the flowers, or by soaking the roots overnight in distilled liquor. I now note: Those who grow peony in the mountains gather the roots and dry them in the sun, which is red peony; scraping off the root bark and steaming and drying it makes white peony.

《草木類·種術 Planting Atractylodes》

1
○汜勝之曰：榆廁雨時，高地疆土可種術。馬踶為佳。切而種之，猶芋薯也。忌肥，妨蟲，最宜金汁。馮應京曰：仲冬栽術，忌青色物相裹及澆灌，又忌用婦糞，則虛心而黑。
○ Fan Shengzhi says: When the elm seeds are falling and it rains, atractylodes can be planted in firm soil on high ground. Horse-hoof is best. It is cut and planted like taro and yam. It avoids fertilizer and harbours insects, and gold juice suits it best. Feng Yingjing says: Atractylodes planted in midwinter must not be wrapped in green things or watered, and must not have women's manure used on it, or it will be hollow and black.

《草木類·牛膝 Achyranthes》

1
○秋收子，春種，肥地深耕，土松易長。翦苗食，如翦韭法。旱則鋤蕓，荒則澆水。秋亦可種。其節腫，故名牛膝；其根直下一尺餘，有力如牛也。淮膝細，川膝及各地根粗。
○ Collect the seeds in autumn and plant in spring, in fertile land deeply ploughed, where the soil is loose and it grows easily. The shoots are cut to eat, in the same way as cutting chives. In drought hoe and weed it, and when parched water it. It can also be planted in autumn. Its joints are swollen, hence it is called ox-knee; its root goes straight down over a chi, as strong as an ox. Huai achyranthes is fine, while that of Chuan and elsewhere has coarse roots.

《草木類·石菖蒲 Rock Sweet Flag》

1
○生水石，不沾土，辛入心者也。一寸九節。屑瓦栽之，以夏布幕其上，則細而短，收煙明目。黎美周言得羅浮石菖，一寸十二節。王石攻言武功山有十五六節。
○ It grows on rocks in water without touching earth, and its pungency enters the heart. It has nine joints to the inch. Planted in tile fragments with summer cloth stretched over it, it becomes fine and short, and gathers smoke to brighten the eyes. Li Meizhou says he obtained rock sweet flag from Luofu with twelve joints to the inch. Wang Shigong says that on Mount Wugong there are some with fifteen or sixteen.

《草木類·靈壽木 Lingshou Wood》

1
○綱目言似竹，長八九尺，圍三四寸，有刺，可為杖及馬鞭，漢賜孔光者。藏器云：楤丈餘，莖有刺，山人取頭茹食，謂之吻頭。智見乃蕉棕本也，凌冬不凋，葉如山姜，葉邊有細刺，枝有大刺，發枝有棕，直幹節節拔生，秋結子成珠，如竹實之紋，味酸可食，謂之姜酢子。其乾，則京師四品以上開棍用之，號甘蔗棍。陸元恪曰：椐即樻也，即今靈壽。
○ The Compendium says it resembles bamboo, eight or nine chi long and three or four inches round, with thorns, and can be made into staffs and horse whips; it was what the Han bestowed on Kong Guang. Cangqi says: The cong is over a zhang tall with thorny stems; mountain people take its tops to eat, calling them wentou. I, Zhi, saw that it is a banana-palm stem, not withering in winter, with leaves like mountain ginger, finely thorned at the edges, large thorns on the branches, palm fibre where the branches sprout, and a straight trunk shooting up joint by joint; in autumn it bears seeds forming beads, with patterns like bamboo fruit, sour and edible, called ginger-vinegar seeds. Its dried stems are used in the capital by officials of the fourth rank and above for clearing the way, and are called sugarcane staffs. Lu Yuanke says: The ju is the kui, which is the present lingshou.

《草木類·種枸杞 Planting Goji》

1
○西寧子少而味甘，他處子多。惠安堡枸杞遍野，秋熟最盛。今各處可種，以三月朔截作段，先以草糞，而牛糞蓋之，又土蓋之，出則勤澆，一年可五翦。枸檵，言易生也。
○ That of Xining has few seeds and a sweet taste; elsewhere it has many seeds. At Hui'anbao goji covers the fields, and is most abundant when ripe in autumn. Now it can be planted everywhere: on the first of the third month cut it into sections, first [manure] with grass dung, cover with cow dung and then with earth, and when it comes up water it diligently; it can be cut five times a year. Gouji means that it grows easily.

《草木類·接木法 Methods of Grafting Trees》

1
○陳瑩中曰：色紅可使紫，葉單可使重，花小可使大，少可使繁，則人力之用天也。或將發生時，或將黃落時，春分前、秋分後，是其候也。執刀端直，不至重傷，則易成。大生至一二年者可接，其接枝亦翦其已生果二年有旺氣者，過脈乃善。凡腰接木之盈地者，近跗一尺，細齒鋸之為枯，又以利刀裁枯使光，則不沁水，遂從枯之一旁裁開其皮，微連其膜，約一小寸，先使翦下之枝裁去兩旁，而置口含之使熱，連唾插掩，紙封，箬封，麻縛，而以干壤少潤，復不宜灌，又遮日暴，俟成體乃可開也。七頭接者，木枝與根各斜裁其半，以人唾粘掩之，以瓦規土灌使少潤，晴陰皆以器覆之，要須同氣味相合，如芍藥與牡丹，方可七頭接也。其寄枝者，先裁其旁，春分交而組接之，俟活，乃各裁其大木之枝。其壓枝者，春秋壓其枝於地，封之，俟苞生根，則斷半而移焉。脫木者，和牛羊之滓包其鶴膝處，先微傷其膚而縛之，復以杖支之，隔年根生，則斷而移焉。善木者，臘月側坎而探，釘斷其中根，止留四散生者，覆而灌之，則生子碩大也。修木者，亦以秋冬，裁痕向上，則雨不食心，盡其枯朽，則不引蛀。其欲木直者，視其不足處，以刀劙其膚，氣行則傷痕先滿，而身直矣。北人云：以胡桃條接於柳木，易活而速實。郎仁寶曰：冬青樹上接梅，則開洒墨梅；桑上接楊梅，則不酸。若生癩，以甘草針之，則無。約法曰：樹以皮行汁，斜斷相交則生，用泥泥之，或以銳皮連木者插所砍之木心而泥封之。倒插而生者，析其皮而緒之也。至於性情所宜，畏冷如橘，畏熱如梨，松宜幹，檜宜濕，茉莉喜日，姜苖畏日，桑柳宜髡，且用其肄，間年歇枝，多種分半而摘其實，可也。南北高下，先後早晚，各有其宜。北蔓青有根，南菁菜惟葉。橘遇淮則為枳矣。榛松子盛於胡遼，龍荔在閩粵，茄子、甘草在南有成樹者，南詔梅李食於初春，梧桐子長大而撱，既未蠟之松子大於荔枝。若夫鬱養強熟，京師百菜初春獻焉，不可不知。
○ Chen Yingzhong says: Red can be made purple, single leaves double, small flowers large and few many; this is human effort using Heaven. Either when about to sprout or when about to turn yellow and fall, before the spring equinox or after the autumn equinox, is the time. If the knife is held straight and does not do heavy damage, it takes easily. Stocks grown one or two years can be grafted; the scions too are cut from trees that have borne fruit for two years and are vigorous, and are best when past the vein. In waist-grafting a tree that fills the ground, saw it with a fine-toothed saw a chi from the base to make a stump, trim the stump smooth with a sharp knife so that water does not seep in, then cut the bark open at one side of the stump, slightly attached to the membrane, about an inch long; first trim the two sides of the cut scion, warm it in the mouth, insert it with the saliva and cover it, seal with paper and bamboo leaves, bind with hemp and moisten slightly with dry earth; it should not be watered and must be shaded from the sun, and only when it has taken can it be uncovered. In seven-head grafting, the branch and the root are each cut obliquely by half, stuck and covered with human saliva, enclosed with a tile, watered with earth to keep slightly moist, and covered with a vessel in sun or shade; they must be of the same qi and taste, like herbaceous and tree peony, before they can be seven-head grafted. For approach grafting, first cut the side, join and bind them at the spring equinox, and when they have taken, cut the branches of each large tree. For layering, press the branches to the ground in spring or autumn and seal them, and when the buds have rooted, cut halfway and transplant. For air-layering, wrap the crane's-knee with cattle or sheep dung, first slightly wounding the skin, bind it and support it with a stick, and when it has rooted the next year cut it off and transplant. To improve a tree, in the twelfth month dig a pit at the side, probe and cut its central root with a nail, leaving only those spreading on all sides, cover and water it, and the fruit will be large. For pruning, also in autumn and winter, cut with the scar facing up, so that rain does not eat the heart, and remove all dead and rotten parts so that they do not attract borers. To make a tree straight, look where it is deficient and slit the skin with a knife; as the qi flows, the wound fills out first and the trunk becomes straight. Northerners say: Walnut scions grafted onto willow take easily and fruit quickly. Lang Renbao says: Plum grafted on holly opens ink-splashed plum blossoms; waxberry grafted on mulberry is not sour. If it gets scabs, prick it with licorice and they go. A general rule: trees move sap through the bark, and when cut obliquely and joined they grow; plaster them with mud, or insert a sharp piece of bark attached to wood into the heart of the cut tree and seal with mud. Those that grow when planted upside down do so by splitting the bark and joining it. As for what suits their natures — the orange fears cold, the pear fears heat, pine likes dryness, juniper dampness, jasmine likes sun, ginger shoots fear sun, mulberry and willow should be pollarded and their shoots used, branches rested in alternate years, and of many planted half the fruit picked — that will do. North and south, high and low, earlier and later, each has its suitability. The turnip of the north has a root, the jing vegetable of the south only leaves. The orange crossing the Huai becomes a trifoliate orange. Hazelnuts and pine nuts flourish among the Hu and in Liao, longans and lychees in Min and Yue, eggplant and licorice grow into trees in the south, the plums of Nanzhao are eaten in early spring, the seeds of the parasol tree are long, large and oval, and the pine nuts of Java are larger than lychees. As for forcing ripeness by warm cultivation, the hundred vegetables of the capital presented in early spring — this one must know.

《草木類·插花法 Methods of Arranging Cut Flowers》

1
○折木花，用火燔其折處，花不易謝。海棠花束薄荷於折處。梔子花折處槌碎之，以鹽入器中，不用水。牡丹用蜜，不用水。梅花用煮肉汁去油，俟冷養之。插梅，每旦當刺以湯。插芙蓉，當以沸湯閉以葉，少頃。插蓮，當先花而後水。插牡丹、芍藥、蜀葵、萱草，皆當燒枝。虛舟子曰：花瓶中水，燒瓦片投之，不臭。冬月濃灰汁入酒，不凍。鮮肉凍汁插花，耐久。
○ For cut woody flowers, scorch the cut end with fire, and the flowers do not fade easily. For crab-apple, bind mint around the cut end. For gardenia, pound the cut end and put it in a vessel with salt, not water. Tree peony uses honey, not water. Plum blossom is kept in meat broth with the fat removed, once cooled. For plum blossom, prick it with hot water each morning. For cotton rose, seal it with leaves in boiling water for a moment. For lotus, put in the flowers first and the water after. For tree peony, herbaceous peony, hollyhock and daylily, the stems should all be scorched. Xuzhouzi says: If burnt tile fragments are put in the water of a flower vase, it does not stink. In winter, strong lye with wine added does not freeze. Flowers put in jelly from fresh meat last long.

《草木類·枯樹活樹 Killing and Reviving Trees》

1
○木插肉桂而枯，以甘草灌之，復榮。烏賊骨釘之則翳，狗膽解之，復榮，或曰鱗魚幹樹，即墨魚海螵蛸也，或曰河豚骨，或曰邵陽魚刺，日西時樹陰即死。一云：桂碇木上則茂，碇木下則枯。大榕樹以蘇木作釘釘其根，則死。柚樹以一株阿魏入其內，則立乾。蒲萄以甘草鍼鍼之，即死。以肉桂屑布地，草不生。穴果樹以鐘乳粉納之，則實多味美，納於老樹根皮，則瘁者復茂。人溺□麻，即萎。豆汁澆鼠莽根，即爛。蘿汁滴野葛，即死。暄曰：或云凡樹離根三尺斧其皮，納巴豆數粒，則汁瀉而死。
○ A tree pegged with cinnamon withers, and watered with licorice it flourishes again. Pegged with cuttlefish bone it wilts, and dog's gall relieves it so that it flourishes again; some say the scale-fish dries trees, which is the cuttlefish bone; some say puffer-fish bone, some say Shaoyang fish spine; in the shade of the tree when the sun is in the west it dies at once. One says: With cassia pegged above, it flourishes; pegged below, it withers. A large banyan dies if its roots are nailed with sappanwood nails. A pomelo dries up at once if a piece of asafoetida is put inside it. A grapevine dies at once if pricked with a licorice needle. If cinnamon shavings are spread on the ground, grass will not grow. If stalactite powder is put into holes in fruit trees, the fruit is plentiful and delicious; put into the root bark of old trees, the ailing flourish again. If human urine [?] hemp, it wilts. If bean juice is poured on the roots of shumang, they rot. If radish juice is dripped on wild kudzu, it dies. Xuan says: Some say that if the bark of any tree is cut with an axe three chi from the root and a few croton seeds inserted, the sap drains out and it dies.

《草木類·移木轉垛法 Methods of Transplanting Trees with the Root Ball》

1
○先一年斷木之四周，曰轉垛。若木大者，先三年，每年輪開一方，乃可移種。果木宜疏，當以丈二為距。八月至正月皆可種之。白下蒔人畜此，待好事者之價，雖酷暑中可頃刻成花園也。
○ A year beforehand, cut [the roots] all round the tree; this is called turning the ball. For large trees, three years beforehand open one side in turn each year, and then it can be transplanted. Fruit trees should be spaced widely, twelve chi apart. They can be planted from the eighth month to the first month. The gardeners of Baixia keep these, waiting for a price from enthusiasts, and even in the fierce heat can make a garden in a moment.

《草木類·伐木留花皮者不裂 Logs Felled with the Bark Scored Do Not Split》

1
○凡木筏至蕪揚者，斬木必花其皮，不去皮則不定其性，全去則易裂，清流木皆如是。若在江西以上流，近斬近用，則全去其皮。江楚兜子、橦子松久則從內壤出，清流木堅，久則從外爛入。其浸水透者二分而止，久亦不裂。
○ For all timber rafts going to Wu and Yang, the logs when felled must have their bark scored; if the bark is not removed, their nature does not settle, and if it is entirely removed they split easily; all Qingliu timber is like this. If above Jiangxi, felled near and used near, the bark is removed entirely. The douzi and tongzi pines of Jiang and Chu in time decay from the inside out, while Qingliu timber is hard and in time rots from the outside in. That soaked through in water to two-tenths stops there, and does not split even after a long time.

《草木類·灌法 Methods of Watering》

1
○發萌時勿灌，灌則災翳。灌亦宜晚涼之候。其糞及撏豬湯、退雞鵝翎湯，皆不宜親木跗，必生蛙。凡雞鵝翎湯，投以荔枝、龍眼之核，則翎腐。當果實時灌之，摘食後灌之，臘前通灌之。糞必久宿，雜以水。澆花用塘中之積水，佳於新水，以其水煖而壯故也。凡花蔬宜日未出時澆，惟茉莉與莧菜、甕菜喜日中澆。中通曰：馬通和水，可催花早開幾日。
○ Do not water when the buds are sprouting, for if watered they suffer blight. Watering too should be done in the cool of evening. Dung, water from scalding pigs and water from plucking chickens and geese should all not touch the base of the tree, for it will surely breed frogs. If lychee or longan stones are put in water from plucking chickens and geese, the feathers rot. Water when fruit is forming, after picking, and generally before the twelfth month. Dung must be kept long and mixed with water. For watering flowers, standing water from a pond is better than fresh water, because the water is warm and strong. Flowers and vegetables should be watered before sunrise; only jasmine, amaranth and water spinach like watering at midday. Zhongtong says: Horse dung mixed with water can make flowers bloom a few days early.

《草木類·養花通理 General Principles of Cultivating Flowers》

1
○養花木事，當家人以消遣心為之，故善；不更事者欲以急就心為之，安得其理？孔子曰：吾不如老圃。修花般乾者，穴乾，納巴豆，則枝節柔軟，可結花。被麝沖，急用艾、雄黃於上風燒之，立解。盆內欲竹木小者，以溺浸大炭多年，穿穴種之，則活而不長。
○ The work of cultivating flowers and trees is done well by householders with a leisurely mind; how could those without experience who want quick results grasp its principles? Confucius said: I am not as good as an old gardener. For trimming flowers into dry [shapes], make a hole in the trunk and put in croton, and the branches become soft and can be tied into flowers. If struck by musk, quickly burn mugwort and realgar upwind, and it recovers at once. To keep bamboo or trees small in pots, soak a large piece of charcoal in urine for many years, make a hole in it and plant in it, and it lives but does not grow.

《草木類·草木好惡 Likes and Dislikes of Plants》

1
○牡丹得鐘乳粉而茂，海棠得糟水而花鮮。牡丹花根下置術，諸種顏色皆是腰金。凡栽花，根下置白斂末，辟蟲；穴中點硫黃，殺蠹。海棠花蕊用薄荷水浸，則開。養石菖蒲無力而黃者，根下宜鼠糞。惟枇杷不宜糞。凡花紅者令白，以硫黃燒煙，熏盞蓋花，則白。建蘭葉喜人捋之，水仙惡人捋之。種果木宜望日前，而月晦日晚可種莧。果熟，人盜其一，則鳥來啄之。
○ Tree peony flourishes with stalactite powder, and crab-apple with lees water has bright flowers. If atractylodes is put under the roots of tree peony, flowers of all colours are gold-waisted. In planting flowers, powdered ampelopsis under the roots wards off insects, and sulphur dotted in the hole kills borers. Crab-apple buds soaked in mint water open. Rock sweet flag that is weak and yellow should have rat droppings under its roots. Only the loquat should not be manured. To make red flowers white, burn sulphur and smoke them with a cup covering the flower, and they become white. The leaves of the Jian orchid like to be stroked by people, while narcissus hates it. Fruit trees should be planted before the full moon, and amaranth can be planted late on the last day of the month. When fruit is ripe, if people steal one, birds come to peck it.

《草木類·治花木蟲 Treating Insects on Flowers and Trees》

1
○去果木之蠹蠍蛣屈者，以杉木為鍼，關塞其氣，則自亡，或以硫黃末塞之，或以焰硝、硫作紙線，紝穴中焚之，走其臭，皆亡，或坋百部內穴中，亦然。殺蟲或治魚腥水灑蔬果等，癩蟲自消，海魚尤勝池魚。蟲能飛動者，取江橘黐以膠焉。或種蟻以食柑蟲。或順風燒油簍，以驅松蟲。或用桐油腳入糞澆菜果。樹掛竹燈架，去青蟲。桃樹蛀者，煮豬首湯，冷澆之。樹有蠹孔，孔邊彈竹篾如蒲蟲聲，蠹自出，可悟啄木取蟲亦此理也。林禽生毛蟲，埋蠶蛾於下，或以洗魚水澆之。西風雨能殺毛蟲。槐樹生蟲，擂鼓於下，則盡落。芝麻柴掛樹上，無蓑衣蟲。中履曰：坑中置甘草、蒜，則種樹不生蟲。
○ To remove borers and grubs from fruit trees, make needles of fir to plug and block their air, and they die by themselves; or plug them with powdered sulphur, or make paper fuses with saltpetre and sulphur, insert them in the holes and burn them, driving the smell in, and they all die; or put powdered stemona into the holes, with the same result. To kill insects, or sprinkle fishy water on vegetables and fruit, and the scab insects disappear of themselves, sea fish being better than pond fish. For insects that can fly, take river-orange birdlime to catch them. Or ants are kept to eat the mandarin insects. Or oil baskets are burnt upwind to drive off pine insects. Or tung-oil dregs are put into manure to water vegetables and fruit. Bamboo lamp frames hung in trees remove green caterpillars. For peach trees with borers, boil a pig's head and pour the broth on cold. When a tree has borer holes, flick a bamboo strip beside the hole to make a sound like a cattail insect, and the borers come out by themselves; one can see that the woodpecker extracts insects on the same principle. When crab-apples get hairy caterpillars, bury silkworm moths underneath, or water with water used for washing fish. West wind and rain can kill hairy caterpillars. When the pagoda tree gets insects, beat drums beneath it and they all fall. Sesame stalks hung on trees keep off bagworms. Zhonglü says: If licorice and garlic are placed in the pit, trees planted there do not get insects.

《草木類·制花耐久法 Methods of Preserving Flowers》

1
○林洪曰：十月後用竹刀取梅蕊將開者，蘸以蠟，投尊缶中，夏月以熱湯就盞泡之，花即綻，香可愛。張邦基木犀記曰：搗女貞子汁，拌岩桂半開者，入磁瓶中，幕之，至無花時密室置盤，其香裛裛，藏可留久。顧元夢云：花摘半含半放者，三停茶葉一停花，罐罌收之，一層茶一層花，間投至滿，紙箬縶固，入鍋重湯煮之，取出待冷，用紙封固，裹置火上焙乾，收用。中德曰：冪盛樹下擊木犀等花，收下，石椎令□築實於新瓶內，用乾荷葉數層鋪之，復以枯竹片壓之，乃汲井水浸之，冬月五日一易水，春秋三日，夏一日，任意取用，收之如前，經年不壞。
○ Lin Hong says: After the tenth month, take plum buds about to open with a bamboo knife, dip them in wax and put them in a jar; in summer steep them in a cup of hot water, and the flowers open at once, with a lovely fragrance. Zhang Bangji's Record of Osmanthus says: Pound privet-seed juice, mix it with half-open rock cassia, put it in a porcelain bottle and cover it, and when there are no flowers set it on a tray in a closed room, and its fragrance pervades; stored, it keeps long. Gu Yuanmeng says: Pick flowers half closed and half open, three parts tea leaves to one part flowers, and store them in a jar, a layer of tea and a layer of flowers alternately until full, seal tightly with paper and bamboo leaves, boil in a pot in a water bath, take out and let cool, seal with paper, wrap and dry over fire, and store for use. Zhongde says: Spread a cover under the tree and beat down osmanthus and other flowers, collect them, pound them firm with a stone pestle into a new jar, lay several layers of dried lotus leaves over them, press with withered bamboo strips, and fill with well water; in winter change the water every five days, in spring and autumn every three, in summer every day; take out as desired and store as before, and they keep for a year.

《草木類·留果法 Methods of Keeping Fruit》

1
○韓彥直橘譜云：金橘景祐中始至汴，溫成嗜之，藏金橘於菉豆中。梨同蘿卜收，皆久。凡梨蒂、香圓蒂插萊菔上，可留。佛手蒜罨其蒂，蒂壞以蠟彌之。風栗同龍眼縣，芡實同防風貯，山查同烰炭貯，橙橘同木瓜貯，皆不壞。異物志曰：香櫞浸汁浣苧，勝似酸槳。
○ Han Yanzhi's Manual of Oranges says: Kumquats first reached Bian in the Jingyou period; the Consort Wencheng loved them, and kumquats were stored in mung beans. Pears kept with radishes both last long. Pear stems and citron stems stuck into radishes keep. The stems of Buddha's-hand citrons are covered with garlic, and when the stem rots it is sealed with wax. Wind-dried chestnuts are hung with longans, foxnuts stored with saposhnikovia, hawthorns with light charcoal, and oranges and tangerines with Chinese quince, and none spoil. The Record of Strange Things says: Ramie washed in water in which citron has been soaked is better than with sour starch-water.

《草木類·草木通理 General Principles of Plants》

1
○草木感東方春氣而發，梅獨先者，酸為木性，得氣之正。晁迥客語曰：釋謂火行為變化性，如甘草遇火則熱，油麻入火成油則冷，甘蔗蒸糖則熱，揉水成湯則冷。蘭花午時發香，荷花向午則斂，蕨以猿啼盛發，芭蕉聞雷而長，橘以尸榮而彫於北徙，榴因骸茂而鬱於東移。繁露曰：蕪荑生於燕，枳榛死於荊，其地氣然也。春分鐵烙梨枝而栽，三月朔種枸杞，四月硫掐茄根，五月斫桑枝，不則來春不生，因乎時也。構汁可以團丹砂，狗骨皮汁可以粘雀，雞腸菜汁可以粘蟬，茯苓碎瓦，薜荔壓樹，杞版受刀復合，杮木畫皮生文。世傳紫薇花畏搔癢，蓋其枝易動也。納鹽落欖，惡鹽也。欖汁泥船隙，如漆，入水益乾，蓋欖之膠也。皂角樹以篾箍之，以木砧箍，盡落。皂角不實者，鑿孔入鐵泥三斤封之。鐵鍋煮皂莢，則爆落。火釘番蕉，則茂。梧桐砍皮則大。藤芋畏指，指即落。莧汁伴鱉肉，隔夜即成小鱉，不驗，蓋為其成鱉瘕也。蜜之忌蔥。生萊菔與地黃相反，熟則無害。凡人壯者少食無傷，如孟德啖野葛是也。東坡曰：桃杏花雙仁殺人，其花本五出，六出必雙。舊說草木花皆五出，惟梔子與雪花六出。愚按：水仙、秦椒花、仙茅花亦六出。桃杏六出雙仁殺人者，失常故也。木實之蠹者必不沙爛，沙爛者必不蠹而能浮，不浮者亦殺人。木望前移植，多實。社日杵根，實大。社酒澆根，實繁。元日火照，名嫁果樹。上元著磚樹岐，李繁。斧槌樹，實不落。
○ Plants respond to the spring qi of the east and sprout, and the plum alone comes first because sourness is the nature of wood, receiving the qi in its correctness. Chao Jiong's Remarks to Guests says: The Buddhists say that the fire phase has the nature of transformation, as licorice becomes hot when it meets fire, sesame put in fire becomes oil and is cold, sugarcane steamed into sugar is hot, and kneaded with water into syrup is cold. Orchids give off fragrance at noon, lotus flowers close toward noon, bracken flourishes at the cry of gibbons, the banana grows on hearing thunder, the orange flourishes with corpses and withers when moved north, and the pomegranate thrives on bones and pines when moved east. The Luxuriant Dew says: Wuyi grows in Yan, and trifoliate orange and hazel die in Jing, because the qi of the land is so. At the spring equinox pear branches are branded with iron and planted, on the first of the third month goji is planted, in the fourth month eggplant roots are pinched with sulphur, and in the fifth month mulberry branches are cut, otherwise they do not grow the next spring; this is according to the season. Paper-mulberry sap can bind cinnabar into balls, the bark sap of the dog-bone [tree] can catch sparrows, the sap of chickweed can catch cicadas, poria breaks tiles, creeping fig weighs down trees, boards of goji close again after the knife, and persimmon wood with the bark drawn on grows patterns. It is said that the crape myrtle fears tickling; that is because its branches move easily. Putting in salt makes olives fall, because they hate salt. Olive sap smeared on the seams of boats is like lacquer and gets drier in water, for it is the gum of the olive. If the honey locust is hooped with bamboo strips or wooden blocks, [the pods] all fall. If a honey locust does not fruit, chisel a hole, put in three jin of iron and seal it with mud. Honey-locust pods boiled in an iron pot burst and fall. The sago palm flourishes if hot nails are driven in. The parasol tree grows large if its bark is cut. The rattan taro fears fingers, and when pointed at its [fruit] falls. Amaranth juice mixed with turtle meat is said to turn into small turtles overnight; this is not borne out, but it may cause turtle concretions. Honey avoids scallion. Raw radish is antagonistic to rehmannia, but cooked there is no harm. Robust people suffer no harm from eating a little, as when Mengde ate wild kudzu. Dongpo says: Peach and apricot [stones] with double kernels kill people; their flowers are originally five-petalled, and the six-petalled must have double [kernels]. The old saying is that the flowers of all plants are five-petalled, and only the gardenia and snowflakes are six-petalled. The Foolish One notes: Narcissus, Qin pepper flowers and curculigo flowers are also six-petalled. Six-petalled peaches and apricots whose double kernels kill people do so because they have lost their constancy. Tree fruits that have borers will surely not go mealy and rotten, and those that go mealy and rotten will surely not have borers and can float; those that do not float also kill people. Trees transplanted before the full moon fruit abundantly. If the roots are pounded on the she day, the fruit is large. If the roots are watered with she wine, the fruit is abundant. Shining fire on them at New Year is called marrying the fruit trees. At the Lantern Festival put bricks in the forks of trees, and plums are abundant. Beat the trees with an axe, and the fruit does not fall.

《草木類·應月而生之通理 The General Principle of Growing in Response to the Moon》

1
○朱草日生一葉，半月即落，謂蓂莢，此猶芋奶、葄實應月生子也。虛舟子曰：梧桐遇閏益葉，藕過閏益節，黃楊遇閏短，謂之厄閏。
○ The red grass grows one leaf a day and they fall after half a month; it is called mingjia; this is like taro corms and zuo fruit producing offspring in response to the moon. Xuzhouzi says: The parasol tree adds a leaf in an intercalary month, the lotus root adds a joint, and the boxwood shrinks in an intercalary year, which is called the intercalary misfortune.

《草木類·藤刺之用 Uses of Vines and Thorns》

1
○蔓草皆左旋，蓋體天左旋也，陰而承陽也。藤能入筋，象筋也。有刺者辛而走竅。夜合、楊梅刺其葉。夜合辛而屬陰，其性破血。
○ All climbing plants turn leftward, because they embody Heaven's leftward turning, and are yin receiving yang. Vines can enter the sinews, because they resemble sinews. Those with thorns are pungent and open the orifices. Silk tree and waxberry have thorny leaves. The silk tree is pungent and belongs to yin, and by nature breaks up blood.

《草木類·木理實有文者 Woods with Real Patterns in the Grain》

1
○鸂鶒木，半紫褐，有蟹爪紋，半紀黑，如烏（門焦）。烏樠木即（門焦）也。有虎斑木，有夜明木，有雞翅木。花櫚結癭，猶楠斗斑也。楠榴，謂癭瘤也張弦有楠榴杭賦。櫚木後訛為花梨。綟木有側戾文。紫榆、花楸亦似紫檀。海橉時亦結癭。桄榔如棕竹而大，與邪欏等皆可車鏃作器。達達靴皮木亦有斑。鐵力木重，海船作碇。金剛木結實，可為念珠，其小者貴。無患木子黑曰鐵槵，牛首人先取鉗之曰銅槵。所云番萻提，則車旋桄檳子也。金剛椒乃藤子。欒華木子、忒當、鬼見愁子，皆為念珠，可觀。
○ Mandarin-duck wood is half purple-brown with crab-claw patterns and half black, like wu[?]. Black man wood is [?]. There are tiger-stripe wood, night-shining wood and chicken-wing wood. Huali forms burls, like the dou spots of nanmu. Nanliu means burls Zhang Xian has a Rhapsody on Nanliu Cups. Lü wood was later miscalled huali. Li wood has slanting patterns. Purple elm and flowered qiu also resemble red sandalwood. Hailin sometimes also forms burls. The sugar palm is like palm bamboo but larger, and like the xieluo can be turned and carved into utensils. Dada boot-bark wood also has spots. Ironwood is heavy, and used for anchors on ocean ships. The diamond tree bears fruit that can be made into rosary beads, the small ones being valued. The black seeds of the soapberry tree are called iron huan, and those that the people of Niushou first take and clamp are called copper huan. What is called the foreign bodhi is the lathe-turned seed of the sugar palm and areca. Diamond pepper is a vine seed. Golden-rain-tree seeds, tedang and gui-jian-chou seeds all make rosary beads worth seeing.

《草木類·金荊榴 Golden Jing Burl》

1
○金色木也，文致盤蹙。隋煬時朱寬征南，得此木數十片，作枕及案。
○ It is a golden-coloured wood with intricately coiled patterns. In the time of Emperor Yang of the Sui, Zhu Kuan on his southern campaign obtained several dozen pieces of this wood and made pillows and desks.

《草木類·相思木 Acacia Wood》

1
○即紅豆樹，大者鋸版，有花文，其近皮數寸無文也。南京以作扇邊骨。多年數圍者，可為書案，亦稱雞翅木，猶香樃之呼鸂鶒木也，以紋似耳。匠斜鋸之，其花較顯。或以黃紫分稱。
○ It is the red-bean tree; large ones sawn into boards show flowered patterns, but the several inches near the bark have none. In Nanjing it is made into fan ribs. Those many years old and several spans round can be made into desks, also called chicken-wing wood, just as the xiangsi is called mandarin-duck wood, because of the resemblance of the patterns. When craftsmen saw it obliquely, the patterns are more prominent. Or they are named separately by yellow and purple.
